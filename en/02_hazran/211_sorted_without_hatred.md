# Episode 211. Sorted Without Hatred

When they reached the Empire's perimeter, what caught the eye first was not a great wall, flashing surveillance devices, or armed soldiers.

It was daily life, too unremarkable.

After the ship hid itself deep, Sion's group walked in along the end of an old perimeter entry line that Sern guided.

The Empire imagined from outside should have been a more overtly suffocating place.

But the perimeter outpost they actually set foot in was closer to an excessively organized kind of daily life.

The streets were clean. The buildings were all matched in height. Even the awning lengths on every window were oddly similar.

Signs were visible but did not stand out. People were busy but not bustling. Noise was certainly present, yet strangely never crossed a line.

Scenes of someone laughing loudly, fighting loudly, or stopping in the middle of a street to chat for too long were rarely seen.

Everyone was busy with their own affairs, and everyone seemed like people who already knew how far they were supposed to move.

Sion found that order more uncomfortable somehow.

A chaotic, rough place would have been easier to stay alert in.

But here, everything was running too well.

The kind of tidiness that made you think first—not who benefits, but whose erasure and suppression keeps it maintained.

Seorin said it low.

"Quieter than I thought."

Kael received right away.

"Not quiet. Organized."

Sern walked looking only ahead.

"This is the perimeter. Less sealed than the center. But people's eyes find familiar categories faster here."

Luhai heard that and adjusted his posture for no particular reason.

"Familiar categories?"

Sern did not bother turning.

"Things like who is nobility, who is an attendant, who is someone you may speak to."

"Ah."

Luhai did not look displeased.

Sion saw that and nearly pinched the bridge of his nose.

Deeper into the outpost, they saw Myojok too.

Strangely, their presence was not treated as particularly noteworthy.

No overt contempt. No atmosphere of openly driving them away.

Instead, something more uncomfortable.

No one bothered to look for long.

A human shopkeeper sorting goods in front of a store quoted the same price to a Myojok customer but never quite made eye contact. People walking by did not swerve dramatically when a Myojok passed, but neither did they initiate conversation.

Even children, instead of staring with curiosity, behaved as though they had been separated from the start—with practiced familiarity.

Not because of hatred.

Closer to ignoring, so naturally, from having lived that way for so long that it felt like nothing.

Sion found that colder.

Ater wore a face that did not bother counting those gazes one by one.

Sion now felt: this person must have known this kind of air from very long ago.

Lines within courtesy. Order within dismissal. And within kind words, a grain that never seats you at the same table to the end.

Sern said nothing further.

He seemed less like someone reading people's gazes and more like someone reading how this entire street operated first.

Who guarded where. Who pretended to be indifferent and up to what point. Which stores were genuinely only doing business. Which buildings had strangely few footprints in front.

Since entering the Empire, Sern's silence felt as though it had grown older and harder.

That was when it happened.

Across the street, a middle-aged woman saw Luhai and stepped aside—very naturally.

And almost reflexively, bowed her head.

"Young master."

Luhai stopped.

"Huh?"

The woman glanced at Aka, then added as though correcting her expression.

"And the young lady."

This time Aka stopped.

"What?"

The woman, looking as though she had said nothing strange, bowed once more and simply went her way.

A brief silence.

Luhai was the first to raise the corner of his mouth.

"Did you hear that?"

Aka wore a face of genuine revulsion.

"I am going to pretend I did not."

"No but that was really natural, was it not? She said young master. And young lady too."

"Do not bring that up."

Luhai's eyes only grew more excited.

"What? Objectively, I must give off young-noble vibes."

Seorin cut in short.

"You do not."

Kael joined.

"You do not."

"Are you both jealous?"

"No. Stating reality."

Luhai was not deterred at all.

"But you just saw it. Clearly to people here, I look like—"

Aka cut him off.

"Enough. And why am I the young lady."

Sion ultimately swallowed a laugh.

Just moments ago, the city had felt suffocating for being too organized. But Luhai alone seemed to have punched a strange airhole through it.

Even Sern closed and opened his eyes, very faintly.

Impossible to tell whether it was exhaustion, familiarity, or both.

But Luhai's luck did not end there.

A little deeper into a side alley, an old man sitting in front of a small textile shop said with an even more convinced face.

"Your betrothal came quite early, young master."

This time Aka truly stopped.

"Betrothal?"

Luhai was nearly bursting.

"No, I mean, do we really look like that?"

"You do not at all." Kael said immediately.

But the old man glanced at Kael, Sion, and Sern's side, then looked even more convinced.

"Quite the entourage. Must be a fine house."

At that point, Luhai actually straightened his posture.

His speech even lowered for no reason.

"Hm. That could be."

Aka turned with a face of disbelief.

"Hey."

Luhai whispered.

"Hold on. This is very important right now."

"What is important."

"My dignity."

Seorin finally let out a hollow laugh.

Jiwoo turned her head away, shoulders shaking just barely.

Only then did Sion get a fuller sense of how the people in this city were reading them.

Not accurately.

They were roughly fitting them into familiar frames.

One person who looked like nobility. A child beside them who looked like a young betrothed. Attendants. Guards. And a silent figure like a private soldier.

Once categorized that far, they did not wonder deeper.

That was fortunate and strangely chilling at the same time.

A society that had long ignored Myojok treated outsiders in a similar way.

Rather than knowing precisely, it slotted them roughly into familiar ranks and moved on.

That was how they could pass through unremarkably.

Then a young clerk carrying goods on the street glanced at Serakion and immediately pulled back his gaze.

Startled, but not startled for long.

The kind of reaction to seeing an unusual private soldier or special guard that some noble kept around.

Sion did not miss it.

Serakion was still walking without a word.

Some people looked at him strangely, but no one held their gaze for long.

The Empire does not understand people finely.

Instead, it deploys them quickly into a familiar hierarchy and passes.

Sern said it very low.

"We keep going."

Luhai's mouth corner was still raised.

"But is it not genuinely funny? I might actually have the constitution for this place—"

"No." Aka cut immediately. "And I am not a betrothed."

Luhai snickered.

"Yeah, I know that too. But if everyone sees it that way, what can you do."

"Do you want to die?"

"Could you say that a little less roughly? You are ruining the young-noble image."

This time Sion truly laughed.

Brief, but that laughter released more than expected.

In streets too organized. In discrimination too familiar. In indifference too natural. Only that short commotion seemed to show they had not yet been fully swallowed into that order.

But the laughter did not last.

Sern stopped right after.

In front of a building that looked like an ordinary warehouse on the surface.

The sign was not large. Not many people came and went.

But Sern paused there—very briefly. Truly, very briefly.

Ater saw it too.

Sion did not miss both their faces hardening at the same time.

The playfulness from moments ago cooled with that single brief pause.

Sern said it low.

"From here."
