# Episode 93. The Heavier One That Arrived Next

When Sion first heard that something big was coming, he thought it only meant more of them.

It didn't.

What was coming this time was different not in number but in weight and role.

The metal sound from beyond the outer passage on the right was clearly different from the lines that had been pushing into the market until now. The rhythm was slower, but every time it rang, it pressed down beneath the ground with it.

Sion took the vibration through the soles of his feet first. He was standing on the ramp, and what shook was not the ramp but the sand beneath it.

Ater spoke briefly.

"A breaching unit."

It didn't need a long explanation.

The lines coming in until now had been the kind that made gaps, read directions, and tore through one space at a time. If a breaching unit came in, it meant crushing that very calculation by force and breaking through.

"Then the passages stop mattering,"
Sion said.

"Yes."

"Harun's barriers too?"

"Those were only ever good for one time."

Sion knew that answer was not comfort. Ater was not using any way of softening bad news right now. It meant there was no time for it.

Han Jiwoo straightened up from the hull's outer wall.

"If it hits here, it's over."

Even as she said it, her hands were already doing the next thing. She laid the ramp's angle half a hand-span flatter, tightened one joint again, and pushed with her foot to shift the metal plate Kael had stood up earlier.

"How many runs left?"
she asked.

"Nine."

"Nine."

She repeated the number, and then, for the first time, she swore.

Sion knew the curse wasn't aimed at the enemy.

"Building something like that out of machines,"
she said.

"What do you mean?"

"That thing was made to break, wasn't it."

"Isn't everything?"

"No."
Han Jiwoo cut him off.
"Most things are made to do something. To carry, to lift, to dig. Breaking is what happens along the way."

She lifted her chin a little toward the sound outside.

"That thing was built with its own breaking counted in from the start."

Listening to that, Sion understood why she was so angry.

Han Jiwoo was the hand that kept dying craft alive. Even on the first day they dropped into Hazran, she had picked the one that was dying most honestly, and when she tore off with her own hands the piece she had attached herself, she had said the ship doesn't hurt. And now, in front of that person, a thing built on the premise of its own breaking was walking toward her.

"That thing doesn't hurt either,"
Sion said.

"That's why I'm angry."

Aka had been listening toward the outside for a while now.

Sion saw that her hand was off the hull. It was the hand that had stayed pressed to the outer wall until a moment ago. Now it just hung at her side.

"Aka."

No answer.

"Aka."

"Mm."

"What do you hear?"

After a long while, Aka spoke.

"It has no breath."

That was something Aka had said before. What she had said when she first saw those things. Sion had heard it then too.

"I know. It never had any."

"Mm."

"But."

Aka didn't say what came next.

Sion looked at her profile.

She wasn't crying. It wasn't a frightened face either. Nor was it the face Aka had worn until now when she gave a verdict.

It was the face of someone who had understood something and stopped because she didn't know what it was.

"Aka."

"It's horrible,"
she said.

"Yeah."

"But."

It broke off again.

This time Sion didn't hurry her. He waited.

"It's strange,"
Aka finally said.
"I feel sorry for it."

Sion thought he had heard wrong.

"For what?"

"That."

"You feel sorry for that?"

"Mm."

Sion didn't know what to say.

That thing was coming this way right now, and when it came people would die, and the twenty-something here and the hundred and sixty-eight and the nine runs would all be shaken because of it. It was no time to feel sorry for anything.

"Why?"
he asked.

Aka shook her head.

"I don't know."

"You don't know, and you feel sorry for it?"

"Mm."

"Does that make sense?"

For the first time, Aka looked at Sion.

"No."

Then she looked outside again.

Sion looked at Aka's profile.

Until now this child had never said she didn't know. Whether something was a door or not, whether it was real or an imitation, how far it was alive and where it was dead—she had known all of it. There had been times she knew and didn't say, but never a time she said she didn't know.

Just now, for the first time, she hadn't known.

And she had grieved over the thing she didn't know.

Han Jiwoo had heard half of the conversation.

"What's she saying?"

Sion hesitated for a moment.

"She says she feels sorry for it."

Han Jiwoo's hands stopped.

"For what?"

"That thing."

Han Jiwoo listened once to the sound outside, looked once toward Aka, and lowered her hands to the joint again.

"…Okay."

That was all. She didn't argue, and she didn't say she understood.

The reaction struck Sion as strange, so he asked.

"Aren't you angry?"

"I am."

"Then why aren't you saying anything?"

Han Jiwoo answered without stopping the hand that was tightening.

"What I'm angry at is the hand that made that thing."

"And her?"

"She's looking at the thing itself."

Sion understood the difference half a beat late.

Han Jiwoo looked at what that thing had been made into. Aka looked at what that thing was. The two of them were looking at the same thing, standing in different places.

Outside, the sound pressed down once more.

Closer than before.

Ater spoke briefly.

"We need to look at the lower left joint line first. The upper body is built to be thrown away."

"Thrown away?"
Sion asked.

"Yes. It gives up the upper body and comes in."

Hearing that, Sion looked toward Aka again.

Aka wasn't saying anything. But her hand had dropped lower than before.

Sion looked at that hand for a long time.

He still didn't know what it meant. Aka had said she didn't know either. Here and now, there was neither a way nor the time to find out.

So Sion decided not to ask anything.

Instead he decided to remember the moment.

So that if the day ever came when Aka knew the reason, Sion could tell her he had been there with her.

And the black thing, for the first time, brought its weight to the end of the passage.

The first impression was that even the word humanoid didn't fit it.

Low,
thick,
and the center of it coming forward was strangely heavy. If the things they had seen until now were an unbroken line, this was like a head set in front of that line. Like a stone laid in front of a wave made of water.

The joints were thicker, and the front armor was built to open a way even if it had to take the cutting and scraping to do it. The place that should have looked like a face was even less a face. A black surface made only for impact and pressure.

With that single step, the debris covering the floor grated low beneath it.

Hearing that sound, Sion understood one thing.

Everything Hazran had used until now had been things to keep the other side from building speed. The laid-down frames, the plates set with gaps, the floor torn up. Kill the speed and you kill the angle, and kill the angle and people buy a beat.

That thing didn't build speed.

It came without building any to begin with, so there was no speed to kill.

"Han Jiwoo."

"What."

"When that comes, how many barriers do we use?"

Han Jiwoo didn't answer. Instead she stopped the hand that was tightening, went down below the ramp, and pulled the metal plate she had pushed aside earlier back with her foot. About a hand-span farther in.

"Why are you moving it?"

"So I'm the one who decides where it hits."

Sion looked that way.

She already knew she couldn't stop that thing. So instead of stopping it, she had moved on to choosing for herself where it would hit.

Beside her, Aka spoke very low.

"If it hits there, it doesn't die."

"Where does it die, then?"

"Next to it."

Han Jiwoo moved the plate another half span.

"Here?"

"Mm."

"Thanks."

Aka didn't answer. She was still looking outside.

Watching that short exchange, Sion felt something strange.

Just now, Aka had felt sorry for that thing and, at the same time, told them exactly where it would break things. Grieving and calculating weren't colliding at all inside the same person.

Sion didn't know how that was possible.

But the thought did come to him that it might be the way this child had lived until now, deep inside Hazran.
