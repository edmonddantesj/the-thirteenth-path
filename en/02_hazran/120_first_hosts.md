# Episode 120. The First Welcome

When the ship came to a full stop, the first thing to arrive was silence.

It was different from Hazran's silence. That had been the stillness left after collapse; here, it was the stillness of nothing having collapsed. There wasn't even the sound of the hull cooling, so Han Jiwoo waited for that sound, but it never came.

Without taking her hands off the helm, she looked out the viewport for a long time.

Inside, the ring structure was wide enough to hold ten hulls, and inside it was this one ship. Where it had stopped was the exact center of that width, and Han Jiwoo had never lined up the helm like that. The angle at which the hull sat, the length of silver floor visible below, all looked measured with a ruler.

"I'm not the one who stopped us here."

Han Jiwoo said.

"I know."

Seorin answered.

Luhai muttered, low,

"Even the parking here feels wrong."

No one laughed. Kael started to, then didn't, so the corners of his mouth went up and came back down.

Sern was reading the outer values when he spoke briefly.

"We are fixed in place."

Ater asked.

"Are we held?"

Sern read a moment longer before he answered.

"Not physically. But if we try to leave, we will be read at once."

Han Jiwoo tried pulling the helm back a little, but the hull didn't move, and pulling harder made no difference. When she let go of the helm, her palms were wet.

"It won't move."

"We are not held."

Sern said it again.

"There is simply no path that will take us."

Han Jiwoo looked at Sern, but Sern was looking at the record panel.

Aka looked out the viewport and spoke, very low.

"It's let us in."

Sion looked at her.

Without turning her eyes, Aka went on.

"We haven't said a word yet, and it feels like it's already put us inside."

When the Hazran people who had been sitting at the mouth of the passage stood up, Seorin raised a hand that meant sit. They didn't sit, so Seorin lowered her hand.

Just then, deep inside the ring structure, long, thin beams of light came on once more, one after another.

One at a time.
At the same interval.

The path they laid on the silver floor ended right below this ship's hatch.

And through that light, machines of a form they had never seen walked slowly out.

There were six, in three rows of two. Their feet made no sound on the floor, and no weapons were visible. There were no seams on their surfaces, and their arms didn't swing as they walked.

Sern counted.

"Six. Unarmed. As far as can be seen."

"As far as can be seen."

Ater repeated it.

The machine standing at the head had proportions close to a long, thin build, if it had been a person, and it stood a head taller than a person. Where a face should have been, it was smooth. A grain of light flowed slowly across it, stopped, and flowed again.

Luhai looked at that face, then looked away.

"It's got no eyes, but it feels like it's looking."

Kael took it up.

"It probably is looking."

Seorin said, very low,

"Like a blade that only learned how to smile."

Han Jiwoo looked at Seorin. Seorin was looking at the viewport, so Han Jiwoo looked at it again too, and the spot where the lead machine had stopped was exactly ten paces from beneath the hatch.

The lead machine bowed its head toward the ship. Not deeply, not shallowly either, but the time between bowing and rising again was long.

And a low, smooth voice carried all the way into the hull. It had not come through the hull's broadcast net, so when Sern looked at the record panel, there was no reception indicator.

"To the outside vessel that has reached Serakion,"

The voice spoke.

"We extend a delayed greeting."

A short silence.

Aka's lips moved in the shape of "Serakion," but she made no sound.

"Your vessel reached the threshold by an unscheduled method. Nevertheless, its entry has been permitted at present."

Luhai's face hardened.

"Permitted."

That one word was all he said aloud.

Han Jiwoo took her hands completely off the helm and set both hands on her knees. There was strength in her fingertips, so she opened her hands, but they closed again on their own.

The lead machine went on.

"Until stability checks on your vessel and the entities aboard are complete, we will guide you to the designated holding area."

Kael muttered, very low,

"Holding?"

Ater's eyes went to Sern, but Sern was looking at the record panel.

"It knows how many entities are aboard."

Sern said.

"Sixteen is showing on the record panel. It has been from the start."

No one had said sixteen. When Han Jiwoo looked back at the people at the mouth of the passage, they looked at one another, and a child spread out a hand of fingers, started counting, then stopped.

Sern read the values briefly, then said, almost in a whisper,

"That sentence does not allow for a refusal signal."

"Send one."

Seorin said.

While Sern tapped the record panel and waited, the machine outside held its bowed posture. There was no response.

"It is not receiving."

Sern said.

"It has not refused. It appears not to know that such a signal exists."

Aka said, very low,

"It isn't being considerate. It's pretending to be finished."

Sion watched the grain of light flowing across the lead machine's face outside the viewport. When he laid his hand over his pocket, the corner of the metal plate pressed against his palm.

A moment later the lead machine stepped back one pace and raised an arm to one side. The time its arm took to rise was the same as the time it took to come down.

At the same time, a very thin line of light came on, long, across the silver floor outside the ship.

It started below the hatch and ran into the dark deep inside the ring structure, and its end was out of sight. It was wide enough for two people to walk side by side, and no wider.

"For arriving outside entities,"

The voice rang out quietly again.

"Serakion provides the necessary degree of courtesy."

Luhai let out a breath that was close to a laugh.

"Courtesy, it says."

Seorin looked out the viewport and said, very low,

"That kind of line scares me most."

She put her hand to the hilt at her waist and took it away, and after that she didn't put it back.

Han Jiwoo took a tool pouch out from under the helm and strapped it to her waist. She pulled out a screwdriver, held it, then put it back.

"Do we have to go down?"

Kael asked.

While no one answered, Sern looked into the record panel.

"The air outside the hull is breathable. The temperature has been set as well."

"For us?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"It has been set for people."

Sern answered.

"It is exactly human body temperature."

Han Jiwoo rubbed her arms. She wasn't cold. That was why she rubbed them.

Ater went to the mouth of the passage and stood in front of the Hazran people.

"Please disembark last."

He told them.

"We will stand in front."

The old man nodded and took the child's hand.

Seorin stood in front of the hatch and put her hand on the handle, but she didn't turn it; she looked back. While Sion came up beside her, Han Jiwoo tightened her tool pouch once more, and Luhai switched off his flashlight. Outside, it was bright.

Seorin turned the handle.

The door made a sound as it opened. It was this ship's rough sound, the first they had heard since coming here, and at it Han Jiwoo squared her shoulders.

When the air outside came in, Sion knew it had no scent at all. Hazran's air always had something in it, but here, there was nothing.

The line of light was waiting just below the hatch.

Seorin set her foot down first onto the silver floor, and it made no sound. She lifted her foot and set it down again, and still nothing.

Sion stood behind her. Han Jiwoo behind him. Sern, Ater, Kael, Luhai. When Aka took her place at the very end of the party, the Hazran people lined up behind her.

A child holding an adult's hand stood on the threshold. Inside the threshold was this ship's floor; outside was silver. The child looked down for a long while, then stepped down, turned, and looked back at this ship. Seen from outside, this ship was small, and every solder mark showed.

The child took the adult's hand and turned away. The line of light began right underfoot. When the child stepped to the side so as not to tread on the line, the adult pulled the child back onto it.

A grain of light flowed once across the lead machine's face.
