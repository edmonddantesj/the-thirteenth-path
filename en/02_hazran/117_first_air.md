# Episode 117. The Air That Came First

After the shock passed, what was left inside the ship was not cheering but checking.

No one called what had just happened a miracle. While Han Jiwoo went back over the state of the hull, Sern read through the outer watch and the residual traces, and Kael and Luhai swept the inner passages first in case some section was still taking the shock.

While Luhai held his flashlight right up to a seam in the wall at the end of the passage and peered in, Kael went along pressing the floor with his foot, one plate at a time. The two of them came back without a word and only shook their heads.

The Hazran people sat pressed against the inner wall of the passage. They were the rest of the sixteen, the ones who were not the party, and even after the shock had passed, no one stood up. An old man looked up at Luhai.

"Where is this?"

"I don't know. Not yet."

Luhai answered that, set down a water bottle, and moved on.

Instead of picking up the bottle, the man pushed it to the child beside him. Only when the child had drunk half and pushed it back did the man drink.

Kael stopped as he passed them.

"Are you hurt anywhere?"

When the man shook his head, Kael touched the child's head once and moved on. Then the woman beside them touched her own child's head too, and the man sitting behind them laid a hand on his own child, once.

Han Jiwoo peered into the open panel for a long time. With the flashlight clamped in her mouth, she lifted the wiring with both hands and let it fall, then lifted it somewhere else, over and over for a long while, before she finally took the flashlight out of her mouth.

"The damage hasn't spread."

Kael looked back.

"That's good, isn't it?"

Han Jiwoo, her hand still inside the panel, couldn't nod right away.

"Not good, exactly…"

Her voice was low.

"It's stranger that it came through a reaction like that in one piece."

She ran her hand along the seam between the hull plates they had joined together on Hazran. Until a few days ago it had rung the moment a hand touched it; now it didn't ring. She knocked twice, and the sound came back even, and only then did she take her hand away.

Sion heard the sound, but he stayed leaning against the wall, turned the other way. His eyes were closed, and he was facing exactly the direction the ship had caught hold of first a moment ago.

It was still there. Very far off, like faint air.

It wasn't a scent, and it wasn't light, and it was different from Hazran too. Hazran's air had always carried dust and sweat and oil mixed into it, but here, nothing was mixed in at all.

Without meaning to, Sion frowned a little as he opened his eyes. Seorin caught it first.

"Feel something?"

Sion hesitated a moment, then spoke.

"We're not there yet. It's like only the air's getting here first."

Luhai's brows drew together.

"Air?"

Sion pushed off the wall and went to the viewport. Outside, it was still black. He stood in front of it without putting a hand on it.

"It's nothing like Hazran. It feels like a place where people live, but… not like a place made to fit people."

Luhai started to say something, then shut his mouth. Kael looked over at him, but Luhai didn't even shrug.

Seorin came over beside Sion.

"Like on Hazran?"

"No."

Sion answered, his eyes still on the viewport.

"Back then, something was there. Now…"

He broke off.

"I can feel what isn't there."

Seorin didn't ask anything more; she looked at Sion's face reflected in the viewport.

This time Aka looked straight over at Sion. She hadn't said a word for a long while, and when she spoke, it was very low.

"Maybe it's an orderly place."

Seorin asked back.

"In a good way?"

Aka didn't answer right away; she looked down at her own hand. Only after she had opened her palm and closed it again did she speak, slowly.

"A place that's too orderly is sometimes closer to the thing that handles living things than to something alive."

It went quiet inside the hull. Han Jiwoo's hands stopped inside the panel, and Kael looked at Aka, but Aka kept her eyes on what lay beyond the viewport. No one asked her to explain what she meant all the way through.

All the while, Sern was reading the array. His finger, moving down the record panel line by line, stopped on one line, went back up, and stopped on the same line again.

"The outer baseline has changed."

Han Jiwoo turned.

"Meaning what?"

Sern spoke as he paged through the record panel.

"This is not the noise we were reading in our original orbital zone. The intervals in the background interference are different, and so are the gaps the relay net leaves."

When he turned the record panel around to show her, Han Jiwoo pulled her hands out of the open panel and came over to look. The two lines were spaced differently. The upper line was uneven, but the lower one ran on evenly, and when she traced it with her finger, it stayed that way all the way to the end.

Ater asked from the passage.

"On which side does the changed baseline lie?"

"The inner side."

Sern answered.

"The side we are heading into."

Ater nodded and turned his eyes to a loose panel in the passage wall. He tried to push it back into place with his foot, and when it would not hold, he dragged over the crate beside it and stood it up against the panel.

Kael spoke low.

"So are we actually closer, then?"

No one answered. Sern turned the record panel back toward himself, and Seorin, beside Sion, turned toward the viewport. Outside, it was still black.

Luhai, who had been leaning his back against the wall without a word for a long while, clicked his flashlight off and on, then muttered very low.

"Hazran got torn loose, but it's like this ship's been traveling places like this all along."

At that, Han Jiwoo looked back toward the panel. Neither she nor Sern nor Seorin nor Sion argued.

Han Jiwoo picked up the panel cover, but she set it back down without closing it. She left the inside showing.

Sion went to the wall two spans from the panel. It was the spot with the two lines he had scored with his nail, and a metal plate patched over it had lifted half a span. He pried the plate off with his fingertips. It was the plate with the two lines scored into it.

He didn't score a third line. He touched the two lines once with his fingertip and put the plate in his pocket.

Aka looked once more into the condensed spot inside the panel. She went close to look, but she didn't touch it.

"It's still far."

When Sion looked at her, Aka went on.

"But now we're passing the places it isn't."

Sern raised his eyes, very slightly. He lifted his finger from the record panel, looked at Aka, went back to the record panel, and crossed out one line.

Han Jiwoo saw it.

"What'd you cross out?"

"An incorrect assumption."

Sern answered.

"I had written that we cut off the pursuit."

Han Jiwoo looked back into the panel.

Sion slowly drew in a breath in front of the viewport. Even without touching it, he could tell the viewport was cold. Beside him, Seorin put her hand on it, and her palm print stayed, then faded.

"I don't think it's a good place."

Sion said it quietly.

Luhai answered, not even managing a short laugh.

"If it were a good place, it wouldn't have called for the likes of us."

No one on the ship laughed.

Kael switched off his flashlight, and Han Jiwoo sat back down in front of the panel. Sern's finger began moving down the record panel again, and Aka stepped back from the viewport and put her back to the wall.

Ater had not moved from his place by the passage, where he had stood from the start. He was watching not the viewport but the people, the Hazran people. The old man noticed his gaze and bowed his head, but Ater neither bowed his own nor looked away.

The sound of the hull changed.

Han Jiwoo was the first to notice. She pulled her hands out of the panel and put her ear to the wall.

"The sound's changed."

Kael switched his flashlight back on.

"Did it get worse?"

"No."

Han Jiwoo took her ear away.

"It's even. This ship has never run this even."

Even after she stepped back from the wall, she looked at it once more.

Luhai laid his palm on the floor under his feet.

"It really is."

He didn't take his hand away.

"When we lifted off Hazran, the soles of my feet were buzzing."

Kael crouched and put his hand on the floor too, and the two of them stayed like that for a while. The old Hazran man who had been watching them laid his own palm on the floor, and the child copied him. No one spoke.

The planet of machines still wasn't in sight, and beyond the viewport, it was black.

Sion closed his eyes again. That air was there. A little closer than before.

He did not open his eyes.
