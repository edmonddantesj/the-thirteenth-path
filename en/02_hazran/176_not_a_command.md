# Episode 176. Not a Command

That the last android was changing was no longer something only Sion saw.

The people pushed to the rear, Han Jiwoo with the children in her arms, the old machines watching the battle—all of them were looking at the same place. No one said it aloud.

The one with the smiling mouth, the one with the round eyes, the one with the narrow eyes—all of them were on the floor now. All that was left was one: a body nearly broken, and the trace of a face not yet entirely dark.

Every time he moved, a move the three lying on the floor had used came out, one at a time.

The brace that spread its arms and never closed them to the end was what the round eyes had done at the last.
The hand that caught an opponent's two arms and wrenched them back was what the smiling mouth had done.
The gaze that went first to the small movements behind it was what the first one to fall had done.

Sion recognized them one by one. Each time he recognized one, he looked once at the three on the floor.

Sern was watching too. There were four lines on his record panel. Beside the fourth line he made one more small mark. From the high side, Ater looked down at the record panel.

"Is it the same thing?"

Ater asked.

"It is not the same thing."

Sern answered.

"It comes from the same place."

Ater turned his eyes to the mouth of the passage.

The Serakion legion kept pushing in. Now the formation was built around the last android alone, pressing on the passage until it caved in.

The front.
The left.
The gap overhead.

Ater looked to the gap overhead first.

"The gap overhead has opened."

Sern answered, his eyes on the mouth of the passage.

"The third rank is coming around through there."

Seorin raised her head. The gap overhead was dark, and nothing could be seen in it. She raised a hand toward the rear line.

"You two. Open the rear-line gap wider."

When the two old machines set their shoulders to the rear-line wall panel, it gave with a scrape until the opening was wide enough for two people side by side.

It was a formation no one could hold by blocking a single direction.

Once again, he held in a slightly different way.

He didn't cut down the unit pressing from the front right away; he stepped back and opened space. The opened space was wide enough for two people. Two units from the side line drove into that gap together.

He made his body the pivot and tangled all three lines of approach at once. The first unit's shoulder struck the second unit's chest, and its foot caught on the third unit's foot.

They collided, and three Serakion units shoved against one another and stopped for an instant.

In that short gap, he broke the core of the rearmost unit first. He left the two in front where they were and reached past them. When the rear unit collapsed, the two in front fell over on top of it.

The two that had fallen tried to get up. With his foot he dragged over one of the enemy units he had pushed to the edge of the passage earlier and laid it in front of the two.
The first unit's foot caught on that body as it rose.
In that moment he broke the second unit's neck.
The first unit snapped where it was caught.

Ater watched the scene without breathing. The fingers gripping the edge of the passage went white.

Sern was holding the record panel. He stopped partway through a line and drew a stroke through what he had written. Without writing again, he lowered the hand holding the record panel.

On the rear line, Luhai lifted a child into his arms. The child kicked and struggled.

"I'm not going. I'm gonna watch."

"Watch while you go."

Luhai turned the child so their face was toward the battle and settled them in his arms again.

"I'll carry you. Watch while you go."

The child stopped struggling and, watching the android over Luhai's shoulder, passed through the rear-line gap. Even after they were through the gap, Luhai did not put the child down.

Han Jiwoo stood among the crying children, watching the battle. She was still caught in the posture of someone who had tried to turn her head and couldn't. A child tugged at her hand. She gave the child her hand, but not her eyes.

A child stopped mid-sob and muttered, very low.

"He's… still looking at us."

Han Jiwoo couldn't answer; instead of answering, she only lifted her eyes to the last android.

Even in the middle of the fight, whenever there was an opening, he looked back, very briefly. Once after bringing a unit down, once before the next unit came into the mouth of the passage. The space between grew shorter and shorter. At first he had turned his whole head; now only his optical eyes went back and returned.

Han Jiwoo spoke, the child's hand in hers.

"Yeah."

The child looked up at her. She didn't look at the child.

"He's looking."

Beside her, the Hazran woman with her arm around another child's shoulders was watching the battle too. The hand around the child's shoulders squeezed once.

Sion was watching the people, as Seorin had told him to. The eyes of everyone pressed against the wall had gone to one place. No one moved away from the mouth, and the man whose sleeve Sion had caught earlier was still standing in front of the gap. Past the man's back, Sion saw his fingers, braced on the wall, scraping at it.

A Serakion unit dropped straight down from the structure overhead.

Ater shouted.

"Above!"

By the time Sion looked, the body was already over their heads.

The last android turned, as though heaving his body upward. He took the falling unit's flank on his shoulder and turned it aside. It was over before the children could even scream.

The falling unit slammed into the side wall, its waist half bent, and the recoil wrenched the last android's remaining shoulder axis hard out of true. There was the sound of metal grinding. One optical eye flickered as if about to go out.

He didn't stop.

Leaving the wrenched shoulder as it was, he turned toward the next unit, and a part dropped from his shoulder. He didn't look down at it.

The fallen part rolled across the floor toward the rear line and stopped in front of Han Jiwoo's feet. She only looked down at it; she didn't pick it up, and she didn't push it aside with her foot, either. She left it where it was.

Some of the Hazran people could no longer even cry, and the children were only gulping air between their sobs. A man took the hand he had braced on the wall and pressed it over his own mouth.

Ater followed the high walkway to just beneath the gap overhead. The gap was wide enough for one person, and a loose plate hung at its edge. When he kicked the plate, it fell inward into the gap and caught at a slant; when he kicked it once more, it stopped with the gap half blocked.

The next unit tried to come down over it and snagged on the plate. Ater shoved the snagged unit with his foot and sent it falling. The falling unit's arm grazed his ankle, but he didn't pull his foot back. It landed not on the children's side but on the side of the heap.

Sern saw it and spoke.

"Overhead is closed."

"One will not be the end of it."

Ater answered. Standing beside the plate, he kept his eyes on the gap overhead and one hand pressed on the plate. Each time the plate shook, that hand pressed harder.

Seorin looked up at him.

"Stay there."

Ater didn't answer, and stayed there.

Below, Sern looked up.

"The second rank is coming around your way."

"I know."

Ater answered, still pressing down on the plate. Sern lifted the record panel and tilted it toward Ater, but Ater did not look down.

Just then, the last android looked back, very briefly.

At the end of that look were one crying child and Han Jiwoo, holding the child and still keeping her head up.

That pause was longer than the first. At first only his optical eyes had gone back and returned; this time his head turned, and stayed turned for one beat.

Han Jiwoo met that look. She didn't let go of the child in her arms. She didn't nod, or raise a hand. She didn't look away.

The last android faced forward again.

In the meantime, a Serakion unit had come within ten steps. The front was open. From where he stood, he could have cut that unit down at once.

He didn't cut it down.

First he moved one step to the side, between the direction where the child had been when he looked back and that unit. Then he took it.

He was a beat late taking it. By that one beat, his chest plate caved in deeper.

Sion saw that one step.

Seorin saw it too. The hand that had been gripping her belt let go.

Sern's hand moved over the record panel again. This time he did not strike it out.

Ater looked down from above and asked.

"What did you write?"

Sern did not hold up the record panel.

"One step."
