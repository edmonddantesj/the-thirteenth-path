# Episode 185. Serakion's First Hesitation

The change in the circulation hum inside Serakion reached the last android first.

His optical eye flickered, very briefly, and beside him, holding her breath, Han Jiwoo watched that flicker. The old machine took its hand off the wall.

"Is it here?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

The machine nodded without a word, and the two Hazran men's wall panels went up. The arms of the man without a younger brother shook, but he did not lower his.

Right after that, a run of metal sounds came from the far end of the gray route ahead. It was the same sound they had heard in the first battle, but the spacing was different.

"The spacing's different."

Sion said.

"Longer."

"Shorter."

Sern said.

"It has become shorter. More of them are coming."

Sion took his blade in both hands.

Serakion's android legion did not come flooding in at once. Seorin raised her hand, and every axis stopped.

Left.
Right.
Above.
Center.

Breathing, and the sound of wall panels going up. That was all.

The first line stepped into the mouth of the route and paused for a beat, though not enough to see. Their footsteps slipped out of time once, and the feet of the front row and the feet of the row behind landed apart.

Sion heard that sound, and Seorin heard it too. Sern's hand stopped above the record panel.

"They landed apart."

Sion said.

"Last time it was one step."

"I heard."

Seorin said.

"Why?"

"Because of that android."

Sern pointed with his chin toward the last android.

"Two optical eyes in the front row looked over there. They looked, and their feet were late."

"Now."

Seorin said it low.

With that one word, each axis moved.

The team that had gone in by the left route made a short cut in the surface panel at the point set in advance. Behind the panel, the light of the circulation line slowed once. The right-hand recovery axis pried two Serakion units sideways off the main line, and as the two it had pried loose were pushed toward the wall, the line behind them split in two.

The ones covering the upper level twisted a guide rail before the drop line could form. A unit that dropped where the rail was twisted landed on floor two steps off from where it was meant to land.

Ater looked down at that unit. Two steps.
The unit got up and walked back to its place. Two steps.
For those two steps, the next unit could not drop and waited on the rail.
Ater twisted the rail once more.

The first line of the Serakion legion pushed into view.

Faceless surfaces, unwavering speed. The spacing between their shoulders was all the same, and the corridor lights fell on those surfaces exactly alike. The surfaces Han Jiwoo saw had not a single line on them. She looked at the faint lines left on the face beside her, then looked ahead again.

The optical eyes of the two units at the very front turned, almost at once, toward the last android.

And when the pause came again, Han Jiwoo counted it.

One.
Two.

The two stood with their feet on the floor, and the next step did not lift. The line behind bumped their backs and drew off.

Seorin was watching it.

"Push."

"Where to?"

Luhai shouted from the right.

"Where they stopped. The stopped ones can't take it."

The moment the word fell, an old machine on the side line knocked an enemy unit off its center first. The fallen unit landed across the feet of the unit beside it. Ater, twisting the entry angle of the next line coming up behind, kicked a wall panel and cut the width of the route in half. Two tried to come through the narrowed width at once, and their shoulders struck.

The last android did not go out head-on.

"Go."

Han Jiwoo said.

"Only the stopped ones."

He turned from the same axis as Han Jiwoo toward the two units that had stopped at the very front.

One step.
A short turn.
Stop.

When he stood there, the two units' optical eyes turned to follow him. Their bodies did not turn. Optical eyes and bodies facing different ways, they stopped a beat longer.

To Sion, their eyes and their bodies looked like two separate things. In a person, that stance ended in a fall, but they didn't fall; they stood there, wrong.

The last android went into that one beat.
He buckled the front unit's knee and shoved the buckled unit into the one behind.
The two went down in a heap.

His shoulder axis made a sound, but he didn't grab the shoulder; he stepped back once, to his original place. Han Jiwoo looked at the shoulder. The three knots were as they had been.

"You okay?"

He looked ahead, and she took that as an answer and gripped the edge of the wall panel again.

Watching it, Sern spoke almost under his breath.

"Their reactions are splitting."

"What's splitting?"

Seorin asked.

"The ones that looked at that android and the ones that did not. The ones that looked are slower."

"By how much?"

"One beat. Always one beat."

"One beat's enough."

The Serakion legion could not come in like a single wave. Some turned their optical eyes toward the last android, some went only for the target point along the existing command line. As the feet of those that had turned and those that had not landed apart, one line walked in two beats.

"Two beats."

Sern said.

"One line is walking as two. It is the first time."

"And if it's two?"

Seorin asked.

"A space opens. Between front and back."

"Go into the space."

Seorin said.

Through that gap the left team cut one more panel, and the right team pried off one more unit.

Kael shouted from the left.

"Two panels left!"

"Save them!"

Seorin shouted back.

"Use them on the next line!"

When Kael stopped his hands, the old machine took its hand off the panel too. The light of the circulation line stayed slow.

Han Jiwoo held the same axis as the last android.

Not right beside him but from behind cover one step back, one hand on the edge of a wall panel, she looked from him to the enemy units and back. Whenever it was needed, she spoke short and low.

"Left."

He turned left.

"Don't stop now."

He did not stop.

"One more coming."

He took it.

There was no gap between word and movement. The tips of Han Jiwoo's fingers on the edge of the wall panel went white. The man without a younger brother looked at that hand, then at his own hand on his wall panel; it was white too, and he gripped harder.

One of the Serakion legion suddenly broke off the main line.

Not toward the last android. Toward the safe axis at the rear, where the children were. Only that unit's footsteps were different. Fast.

The last android's optical eye turned that way, and his body started to turn.

The right-hand recovery axis got there first. Luhai's team cut the unit off from the side.
The iron bar went into its knee joint.
The unit hit the wall and stopped.

"Nothing gets to the kids!"

Luhai shouted.

"This is as far as it goes!"

As the unit slid down the wall, Luhai pulled the iron bar free and looked at it. It hadn't bent.
Not yet.
He gripped it again.

The last android's body stopped halfway through the turn. His optical eye rested for one beat on the spot where the unit had fallen, and Han Jiwoo saw that beat.

"Aka's with the kids. Eyes front."

Then he came back to face the front.

Almost at the same time, Seorin widened the gap in the middle of the main line.

"This is it."

Sion moved.

He went into his own share of the axis and, just as first planned, brought down a blocked junction. As the metal split, the light of the inner circulation line leaked out through the gap.

Serakion did not scream. Instead a low, polite rerouting chime passed once, announcing in the same voice which path had closed and which had opened in its place.

Before that sound ended, Sion was on the second junction. He set the tip of his blade into the seam and pressed, but his palm slipped. It was the new blade, not yet used to his hand. When he took it in both hands and pressed, the second gap opened.

The rerouting chime started again before it had finished. As the second chime overlapped the first, the overlapping words could not be made out. Two of the same voice covered each other. Sern heard it, but he could not tell what it said, so he could not write it down.

In that moment, for the first time, in the very middle of the front, the last android stood face to face with a single Serakion unit.

Two steps lay between them.

The same body. The same height, the same shoulder width, the same optical eyes.

One had no face. The other still kept the child's faded lines on his face.

The Serakion unit's optical eyes stopped on those lines.

While its feet stayed rooted, Sern counted.

One.
Two.
Three.

Han Jiwoo stopped breathing.

The unit's hand came halfway up toward its own face. It stopped. It did not come down.

The last android's foot lifted first.

It was one beat. In that one beat he closed the two steps. His hand went in between the unit's neck and shoulder.

He stood where he was until the fallen unit's optical eyes went dark, and only after they had did he slowly draw his hand out, while Han Jiwoo watched his back.

He looked at the raised hand of the fallen unit. It had stopped halfway up from the floor. He didn't lower it for the unit; he only looked.
One beat.
He turned away.

The next line of the Serakion legion, standing at the mouth of the route, was out of step.

"They are out of step."

Sern said.

"It began before they halted."

Ater spoke from above.

"They came having seen it. What became of the front line."

"They see that and still come?"

Luhai asked.

"They come."

Ater said.

"They come having seen it, and their feet are out of step. That is what is different."

Han Jiwoo took her hand off the wall panel. The edge of the panel had left a red line across her palm. She looked at the line, then put the hand back in the same place.

Seorin spoke with her eyes ahead.

"Keep going."

Luhai shouted from the right.

"How many lines?!"

"I am counting!"

Sern shouted.

"Don't count, just tell me!"

"I do not know!"

Sern shouted.

"For the first time, I do not know!"

Luhai let out a short laugh and gripped his iron bar again.
