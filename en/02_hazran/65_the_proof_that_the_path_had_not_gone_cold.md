# Episode 65. The Proof That the Path Had Not Gone Cold

After Aka said "There's one more thing to see first," the air beside the hull went quiet again.

The silence felt less like hesitation than like putting things in order.

What they had just seen was already big enough.
There was a hull that hadn't died,
they had heard that the ember was no ornament but the proof that would keep that hull alive,
and they had caught the sense that not just any Etherite would do.

But Aka didn't stop there.

Strangely, that made Sion trust it more.
If she had ended it right there, this too would have felt like another Hazran-style deal. Show something big, say half of it, and tell them to take the rest on faith. But Aka had never held on to people that way. If anything, she acted like someone who knew that if you didn't show it all the way through, it died bigger later.

Aka walked ahead into the narrow passage behind the hull.

Han Jiwoo followed without hesitating, and Sion fell in right behind her, the ember in his hand. Deeper in, the passage was darker. The faint lights of the outer work section barely reached it, and instead, from somewhere inside the walls, a very low vibration went on. Less a machine noise than the trembling of a structure that, even after standing still a long time, had never fully gone out.

For no good reason, Sion felt the vibration was like the ember in his hand.

Both small,
both quiet,
and yet not completely cut off.

At the end of the passage, Aka stopped.

Ahead of her was a small, rounded chamber.

Not a workroom,
not a living space,
not a place for storing hulls.

If anything, it seemed made for watching over something for a long time.
Almost all of the walls were covered, and in the middle stood a single low pedestal. What lay on it was a piece of black metal, neither large nor showy. At first glance it might pass for a fragment that had simply gone cold.

Sion looked at it up close.

It was a little bigger than his palm. One end was a cut face. Not cut, closer to torn, but the torn place was smooth. Tearing by force didn't leave it like that.

The surface was strange. Old metal corrodes or peels, but this did neither. The color had eaten its way inward. It looked less like something had settled on the surface than like the metal itself had become that color.

Beside him, Han Jiwoo looked at it and let out a short breath.

"It didn't take heat."

"Then what?"

"Heat damages the outside first. This went from the inside first."

Han Jiwoo didn't touch it. Instead she circled the pedestal once, changing her angle.

"It didn't go from outside in. It came from inside out."

Sion only half understood what that meant. His back went cold by as much as he understood.

But there was something Sion had noticed before that.

The ember in his hand had changed, very slightly.

It hadn't grown.
It hadn't seethed.

Instead, very quietly, as with the three Etherite fragments earlier, it had grown less uncomfortable.

Sion had no words to explain the difference, so he tried raising his hand and lowering it.

Away from the pedestal, his hand went back to the way it had been. A faint, constant push. Closer, and it eased.

Only after trying it three times did he understand.

The ember had not grown, and had not brightened. Instead it was holding out less.

Until now, Sion had thought it kept straining because it was alive.

It wasn't that. It had been standing against something all along. Only here did it do less of that.

Either there was no longer anything to stand against, or they had come to a place where there was no need to. Sion didn't know which, and either one was different from the few days he had carried it around.

As if it already remembered something in front of it.

"What is this."
Sion asked, low.

Aka didn't answer right away.

Instead she went up close to the black fragment on the pedestal and looked down at it for a long time. Her gaze was less that of someone explaining than of someone checking again on a thing she had kept for a long time.

"A place that went cold,"
she said.

Those words alone didn't make sense to Sion right away.

Han Jiwoo reacted first.

"A place fire passed through?"

Aka gave a short nod.

"Not dead,"
she said.
"It just didn't make it all the way."

At that moment Sion looked down at the ember in his hand without meaning to.

What didn't make it all the way.
Not dead, but never able to open.

It was like the ember they were carrying now, too.
Not a complete door, but proof that the path had not gone cold.

Aka touched the edge of the pedestal, very lightly.

"The path in here didn't all die at once,"
she said.
"There are places left where it started opening and stopped."

The moment he heard that, Sion felt all the things he had heard so far—"what mimics a door," "the path isn't dead," "if only the shape ends up the same, it dies fast"—come together here.

It hadn't vanished completely; here and there along the way, places that had gone cold were left.
Then the work narrowed to one thing. Could the points that had gone cold be joined up again.

"Is this what gets the hull up?"
Sion asked.

Aka shook her head.

"No,"
she said.
"This is proof that it left once."

Those words made the air in the room very different.

The look in Han Jiwoo's eyes changed too.

If until a moment ago they had been eyes reading a structure that could be saved, now they were closer to eyes trying to read what grammar this hull had actually been built on, as more than a mere possibility.

"So,"
she said, low,
"what you're holding isn't a guess. It's a trace that's left."

This time Aka nodded slowly.

"Right,"
she said.
"Everyone wanted to say it was gone, but it was never all the way gone."

Sion thought he knew why those words struck so hard.

Someone had wanted to erase it,
someone had wanted to believe there was no door,
someone had kept only the mimicry and rolled it around like a stake,
but in fact there were places left that had never gone completely cold.

Sion put a hand to the inside of his jacket.

Two words were there. **Hazran**, **Aka**.

Until now they had been a direction. Go and see, go and ask, maybe nothing at all. On the way here, more than once, he had wondered whether it was just anything at all, written down at the very end.

It wasn't.

Someone who knew this was here had written it.

Elia had not written those two words from rumor. She had written them knowing this, or at least knowing where it would be.

At that thought, Sion's throat closed for a moment.

There was no one to ask. If there had been, he wouldn't have come this far.

And Aka had known.
She had known it alone, and that was why she hadn't told just anyone.

Sion asked, very low.

"Why show us now."

This time too, Aka didn't look at Sion right away.

She looked at the ember in his hand first.
Then, only after a look toward Han Jiwoo, did she speak.

"Because you brought it now,"
she said.
"Not something playing dead."

It was a short sentence.
But Sion felt that one line was the most exact answer to why Aka had not let them all the way inside until now.

Hands that only chased rumors,
hands that only went after money,
hands that got excited over a door's mimicry—there was no reason to show this place to them.

But hands that had brought the real thing back alive,
that had recognized a hull that hadn't died as not dead,
that wanted to see whether it could really be connected rather than only mimicking a shape—
to those, and only then, could it be shown.

Han Jiwoo took one more step toward the pedestal and spoke, low.

"Then what's next is how to make this cold place mesh with the hull's grammar."

This time Aka moved the corner of her mouth, very slightly.

"Yeah,"
she said.
"Now you're finally seeing the same thing."

Sion heard that and let out a very small breath.

All the way here, it felt as if he had been running across boards someone else held out.
But now, for the first time, he felt he was standing not on a board someone had made, but in front of the trace of a path that had actually been left.

And that trace was saying it clearly.

It was never completely over.
There had only been hands that made people believe it was.
