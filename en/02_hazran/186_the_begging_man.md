# Episode 186. The Begging Man

As the second battle began to mesh, Seorin's plan moved across every layer and route.

The left axis latched onto the first difference in reaction in the Serakion legion and would not let go. Each time it cut a panel, the light of the circulation line slowed once, and the units' feet slowed with the slowing light.

The upper axis threw the drop line off three times. All three times the falling unit landed somewhere else, and the third one wedged between wall and floor and could not move.

The junction Sion had taken, ever since the second gap opened, was keeping the rerouting chime from ever stopping. Before one chime finished, the next began, and for the first time Serakion's voice was talking over itself. Sern heard it.

"Two have overlapped."

He said.

"When the third comes, three will overlap."

"Then?"

"Then none of it will make sense."

Sion found the third seam.

In the middle of it all, the last android did not break through the front. He went in only toward the stopped units, and came back. Each time he went in, a row of the Serakion legion split once, and each time he came back, his shoulder axis made a sound. The place he came back to was always the same one, where Han Jiwoo could see him. Each time he came back, she spoke.

"Here."

He stood there, and not once did he stand anywhere else.

Han Jiwoo stood on the same axis.

She did not move right alongside him; from behind cover one step back, she listened to the sound his shoulder axis made and the enemy units' footsteps together. Her fingers on the edge of the wall panel had gone white long ago.

When she tossed out a short, low signal, he reacted, very briefly. That one reaction set the first line of Serakion's legion back by a beat each time.

The two Hazran men held their wall panels up behind her. Their arms were shaking, but they did not lower them.

"Switch arms."

Han Jiwoo spoke without looking back.

"I'm all right."

The man without a younger brother answered.

"No, you're not. Switch."

The two shifted the wall panels to their left arms, and the shaking moved over with them.

Ater raised his hand first.

"Stop."

He spoke very low.

"Something is wrong with the sound ahead."

"What is?"

Seorin asked.

"It is a person's sound."

Ater said.

"Among the machines."

Between the metal sounds came the sound of a person's feet slipping in a hurry. Slipping once, hitting the wall, slipping again. After that came a person's breathing, short and ragged.

The two old machines turned their heads that way at once. Sern lifted his eyes from the record panel, and Seorin's steps stopped.

Out of the dark of the upper-level junction on the right, a person stumbled into view, nearly falling.

It was the man who had been rescued a few days earlier.

His clothes were the same ones he had worn a few days ago, leaning against the wall. The tears were the same too, and there were no new ones.

The corridor wall where he came out was smooth. The gap in that clean surface was open just wide enough for one person to crawl out. The edges of the gap were straight. It was not a place that had broken. It was a place that had been opened.

Sern looked at those straight edges, then at the dot on his record panel. Where the man stood was two steps from the dot.

His face had gone white, his breathing was so ragged it seemed about to snap, and his eyes swept over the people and could not stop anywhere. As his knees buckled, he braced one hand on the floor and reached forward with the other.

"Help me…!"

His voice was cracked.

"Not that way…! There's another way, not there… quick…"

The way his outstretched hand pointed was the axis Seorin had judged the most unstable—the spot where they had marked dots on the floor, where Han Jiwoo and the android stood now.

Sion looked at that fingertip, then at the man's face. It was the same face as a few days ago, pressed to the wall, only breathing. Back then no one had looked at that face for long, but now everyone was looking.

One of the Hazran men lowered his wall panel.

"That man…"

The man beside him caught his arm, and the wall panel went back up.

"You know him?"

Han Jiwoo asked without looking back.

"No."

The man said.

"But he's a person."

Han Jiwoo did not answer.

Her body moved first.

One step.

"Wait…"

She said it, and looked down at her own foot; it was out past the cover, but she did not pull it back. The hand that had gripped the wall panel's edge had come away from the wall.

The last android's optical eye came to her foot, out past the cover. Then to the man, then back to the foot. He came half a step out in front of her. Not blocking her; standing in front.

Seorin did not look at the man. She looked at the gap he had come out of and the dark beyond it, then at where each of her teams stood, one by one: the left axis, the upper level, Han Jiwoo and the android, the ground under her own feet. Only then did she look at the man. The man got the shortest look of all.

"Why does the man get the shortest look?"

Luhai asked from far off.

"The man's there. He's not moving. What moves is behind him."

The man spoke again, close to tears.

"Come on, quick…! If you go this way they won't see you…! I saw it from above… I saw it all from above…!"

Far off on the right axis, Luhai gripped his iron bar again. He did not leave his place.

"Don't go."

Luhai shouted it to Han Jiwoo.

"I want to go too. Don't go."

One old machine took a step forward and stopped. The machine beside it had not caught its arm; it had stopped on its own. One of the Hazran men started toward the man and had his arm caught by the man beside him, and the caught arm started to pull free, then didn't.

Sion's foot moved once too, and only after it moved did he notice and draw it back. The hand on his hilt was sweating, and Sern was looking at him.

"Have you drawn your foot back?"

"I have."

"Why did you draw it back?"

"Don't know."

Sion said.

"I wanted to go. But Seorin didn't."

Sern looked at the record panel, but he did not write.

The last android's optical eye flickered, very briefly.

He was looking that way too. His optical eye, which had been on the man's shaking hands, passed over the man and rested on the gap behind him, and rested longer on the dark beyond the gap. It did not come back to the man.

Seeing that, Han Jiwoo looked at the man, then again at where the android's optical eye was turned, but it was dark there. To her eyes, nothing was there.

"What's there?"

She asked him.

He did not answer. His optical eye stayed turned toward the dark. She looked into that dark again, and still there was nothing. Even so, without taking her eyes off where his optical eye pointed, she took hold of the wall panel's edge again.

Sern murmured, low.

"It is too precise."

"What is?"

"Where he pointed."

Sern said.

"The axis we judged the weakest. How does that man know it?"

Seorin did not answer.

Sern was not looking at the record panel. While he looked at the space between the axis the man had pointed to and where they themselves stood, his finger had gone to rest on the dot on the record panel.

Han Jiwoo spoke very low.

"He's asking for help."

She said it looking at the man, not toward Seorin. When she let go of the wall panel's edge she had been gripping, the freed hand went to her side, where the inner pocket was.

Seorin answered, short.

"I know. That's why I'm being more careful."

Seorin's hand was on the hilt at her waist. She did not draw. Han Jiwoo looked at that hand.

The man staggered one more step forward. He took away the hand he had braced on the floor, reached out with both hands, and pushed himself along the floor on his knees. Where he pushed, no mark was left. It was Serakion's floor.

"Please… I can't go alone…"

From the dark behind him came the click of metal locking into place.

It was a sound hard for a person to hear. The two old machines turned their heads that way at once, and the last android's body turned.

His twisted shoulder came forward—not toward the man, but into the space between the gap behind the man and their own side.
He took one step out. His shoulder made a sound.
He stood where he was.

Han Jiwoo looked at his back. She looked at the man's two outstretched hands, then at that back again. Her foot came half a step back toward the cover. Only half a step.

Ater, eyes still on the upper level, raised one finger.
Then two.
The third finger started up and stopped.

Sern folded the record panel shut and tucked it under his arm. Both hands were empty.

Seorin took her hand off the hilt and raised that hand, palm turned back—a hand that said wait.

Everyone looked at that hand: Han Jiwoo, Luhai, the two old machines too. The two Hazran men's wall panels stayed where they were, and no one went out. The man wept, both hands outstretched.

The man's hands were still outstretched. There was no dirt on his trembling fingertips. They were clean. A few days ago, the hands that had leaned on the wall had been caked with dirt.

Han Jiwoo looked at those clean fingertips, then at her own, still marked with oil and the edge of the wall panel. Without lowering her hand, she gripped the edge of the wall panel again. The foot that had come half a step back came no further.
