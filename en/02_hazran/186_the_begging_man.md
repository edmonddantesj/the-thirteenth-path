# Episode 186. The Begging Man

As the second battle began to bite,
Seorin's plan moved across every layer and route.

The left axis latched onto the first difference in reaction in the Serakion legion and would not let go.
Each time it cut a panel, the light of the circulation line slowed once.
The units' feet slowed with the slowing light.
The upper axis threw the drop line off three times.
All three times, the falling unit landed somewhere else.
The third one wedged between wall and floor and could not move.
The junction Sion had taken, ever since the second gap opened,
was keeping the rerouting chime from ever stopping.
Before one rerouting chime finished, the next began.
For the first time, Serakion's voice was talking over itself.
Sern heard it.

"Two have overlapped."

He said.

"When the third comes, three will overlap."

"Then?"

"Then none of it will make sense."

Sion found the third seam.

In the middle of it all,
the last android did not break through the front.
He went in only toward the stopped units, and came back.
Each time he went in, a line of the Serakion legion split once.
Each time he came back, his shoulder axis made a sound.
He always came back to the same spot.
A spot where Han Jiwoo could see him.
Each time he came back, she spoke.

"Here."

He stood there.
Not once did he stand anywhere else.

Han Jiwoo stood on the same axis.

She did not move right alongside him.
From behind cover one step back,
she listened to the sound his shoulder axis made and the enemy units' footsteps together.
Her fingers on the edge of the wall panel had gone white long ago.
When she tossed out a short, low signal,
he reacted, very briefly.
That one reaction set the first line of Serakion's legion back by a beat each time.
The two Hazran men held their wall panels up behind her.
Their arms were shaking.
They did not lower them.

"Switch arms."

Han Jiwoo said.
Without looking back.

"I'm all right."

The man without a younger brother answered.

"No, you're not. Switch."

The two of them shifted their wall panels over.
Left arms.
The shaking moved with them.

Ater raised his hand first.

"Stop."

He spoke very low.

"Something is wrong with the sound ahead."

"What?"

Seorin asked.

"It is a person's sound."

Ater said.

"Among the machines."

Between the metal sounds
came the sound of a person's feet slipping in a hurry.
Slipping once,
hitting the wall,
slipping again.
Then came breathing.
A person's breathing.
Short and ragged.

The two old machines turned their heads that way at once.
Sern lifted his eyes from the record panel.
Seorin's steps stopped.

Out of the dark of the upper-level junction on the right,
a person stumbled into view, nearly falling.

It was the man who had been rescued a few days earlier.

His clothes were the same as then.
The clothes he had worn a few days ago, leaning against the wall.
The torn places were the same too.
There were no new tears.

The corridor wall where he came out was smooth.
The gap in that clean surface
was open just wide enough for one person to crawl out.
The edges of the gap were straight.
It was not a place that had broken.
It was a place that had been opened.
Sern looked at the gap.
Straight edges.
He looked at the dot on his record panel.
Where the man stood.
Two steps from the dot.

His face had gone white,
his breathing was ragged, close to breaking off,
and his eyes swept over the people and could not stop anywhere.
His knees buckled.
He braced one hand on the floor and reached forward with the other.

"Help me…!"

His voice was cracked.

"Not that way…!"
"There's another way, not there… hurry…"

The way his outstretched hand pointed
was the axis Seorin had judged the most unstable.
The spot where she had put a dot on the floor.
The spot where Han Jiwoo and the android stood now.

Sion looked at that fingertip.
Then he looked at the man's face.
It was the same face as a few days ago, pressed to the wall, only breathing.
Back then no one had looked at that face for long.
Now everyone was looking.

One of the Hazran men lowered his wall panel.

"That man…"

The man beside him caught his arm.
The wall panel went back up.

"You know him?"

Han Jiwoo asked.
Without looking back.

"No."

The man said.

"But he's a person."

Han Jiwoo did not answer.

Han Jiwoo's body moved first.
One step.

"Wait…"

She said.
She looked down at her own feet.
Her foot had gone out past the cover.
She did not pull it back.
The hand that had gripped the wall panel's edge had come away from the wall.
The last android's optical eye came to her foot.
The foot outside the cover.
Then to the man.
Then back to the foot.
He came half a step out in front of her.
He did not block her.
He stood in front.

Seorin did not look at the man.
She looked at the gap the man had come out of.
She looked at the dark beyond the gap.
Then, one by one, she looked at where her teams stood.
The left axis.
Above.
Han Jiwoo and the android.
The ground under her own feet.
Only then did she look at the man.
The man got the shortest look of all.

"Why does the man get the shortest look?"

Luhai asked from far off.

"The man stays there."

Seorin said.

"He doesn't move. What moves is behind him."

The man spoke again, close to tears.

"Come on, quick…!
If you go this way they won't see you…!
I saw it from above… I saw it all from above…!"

Luhai gripped his iron bar again.
That was far off, on the right axis.
He did not leave his place.

"Don't go."

Luhai shouted.
At Han Jiwoo.

"I want to go too. Don't go."

One old machine took a step forward and stopped.
The machine beside it did not catch its arm.
That machine stopped on its own.
One of the Hazran men started that way
and was caught by the arm by the man beside him.
The caught arm started to pull free, then didn't.

Sion's foot moved once too.
Only after it moved did he know.
He drew it back.
The hand on his hilt was sweating.
Sern looked at Sion.

"Have you drawn your foot back?"

"I have."

"Why?"

"Don't know."

Sion said.

"I wanted to go. But Seorin didn't."

Sern looked at the record panel.
He did not write.

The last android's optical eye flickered, very briefly.

He was looking that way too.
He saw the man's shaking hands.
Then his optical eye passed over the man.
It rested on the gap behind the man.
It rested longer on the dark beyond the gap.
It did not come back to the man.

Han Jiwoo saw that.
She looked at the man.
She looked again where the android's optical eye was turned.
It was dark.
To her eyes, nothing was there.

"What's there?"

She asked him.

He did not answer.
His optical eye stayed on the dark.
She looked at the dark again.
Nothing.
She kept her eyes on where his optical eye was turned.
She took hold of the wall panel's edge again.

Sern murmured, low.

"It is too precise."

"What is?"

"Where he pointed."

Sern said.

"The axis we judged the weakest. How does that man know it?"

Seorin did not answer.

Sern was not looking at the record panel.
He was looking at the space between the axis the man had pointed to and where they stood.
His finger rested on the dot on the record panel.

Han Jiwoo spoke very low.

"He's asking for help."

She did not look toward Seorin as she spoke.
She was looking at the man.
She let go of the wall panel's edge she had been gripping.
The hand she let go came to rest at her side.
Where her inner pocket was.

Seorin answered, short.

"I know."
"That's why I'm being more careful."

Seorin's hand had gone to her waist.
It was on her hilt.
She did not draw.
Han Jiwoo looked at that hand.

The man staggered one more step forward.
He lifted the hand he had braced with and reached out with both.
He pushed himself along the floor on his knees.
Where he pushed, no mark was left.
It was Serakion's floor.

"Please…"
"I can't go alone…"

From the dark behind him
came the click of metal locking into place.

It was a sound hard for a person to hear.
The two old machines turned their heads that way at once.
The last android's body turned.

His twisted shoulder came forward.
Not toward the man.
Toward the space between the gap behind the man and their own side.
He took one step out.
His shoulder made a sound.
He stood where he was.

Han Jiwoo looked at his back.
She looked at the man's two outstretched hands.
She looked at that back again.
Her foot came half a step back toward the cover.
Only half a step.

Ater, eyes still on the upper level, raised one finger.
Then two.
The third finger started up and stopped.

Sern closed the record panel.
He tucked the closed panel under his arm.
Both hands were empty.

Seorin took her hand off the hilt.
She raised that hand.
Palm turned back.
A hand that said wait.
Everyone looked at that hand.
Han Jiwoo too.
Luhai too.
The two old machines too.
The two Hazran men's wall panels stayed where they were.
No one went out.
The man wept, both hands outstretched.

The man's hands were still outstretched.
His fingertips were shaking.
There was no dirt on them.
They were clean.
A few days ago, the hands that had leaned on the wall had been caked with dirt.
Han Jiwoo looked at those hands.
Clean fingertips.
She looked at her own fingertips.
Grease.
The mark of the wall panel's edge.
She did not lower her hand.
She gripped the edge of the wall panel again.
The foot that had come half a step back came no further.
