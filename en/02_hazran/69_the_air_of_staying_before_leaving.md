# Episode 69. The Air of Staying Before Leaving

Even after Harun's settlement was over, the scrap market did not go straight back to its usual face.

But it had not become an entirely different face either.

Seorin found, strangely, that she did not like that vague gap.

The overturned board had been put right, Zahir had drawn the line again, and Harun had been punished.

So she had expected everyone to start talking again soon, to haggle, to go on about which prices went up today and which went down, and to act like a market again.

But the scrap market did not hurry to pretend nothing had happened.

People moved with lower voices, and the feet going back and forth between the tents were half a beat slower than usual. Someone looked toward the return lines and whispered; someone else ran an eye over the heap of metal plates torn off the edge of the arena and walked on.

They were all going back to living, but their bodies still remembered what had just happened.

Watching it, Kael spoke shortly.

"Still, they're coming back fast."

Ater answered, low.

"Because they have to live."

Luhai muttered right beside them.

"It's kind of cool, though."

Seorin shot him a sidelong look.

"What is?"

"No, just,"
Luhai shrugged.
"Everything got turned upside down, and they still don't look like it's all over."

Sern spoke very low.

"Because if it is all over, there is no price left to keep rolling."

It was dry, and that made it sound all the more like Hazran.

Seorin threw her gaze out past the railing.

The work inside still showed no sign of ending.
There was no telling what Sion, Han Jiwoo and Aka were looking at. But now, not knowing it did not make her uneasy right away. If anything, the stronger sense was that in there they were handling something too important to be touched just now.

Meanwhile the outside was coming back to life on its own.

Someone set an overturned tent upright,
someone stitched a torn heat-shield cloth,
someone passed by carrying water jugs. The wreckage of desert skiffs that had come over from the arena's outskirts was being dragged off, piece by piece, to other sections.

Harun really had started the repairs with his own hands.

Seorin watched it for a long time.

He did not put anyone on it. It was a job he could have put people on, and he did not. He lifted a return-line brace alone, one that normally took three. He tried three times and got it up on the third.

After he got it up, he rested once. While he rested, he did not look at anyone.

That was what Seorin found most galling.

The man was not being punished right now; he was paying the punishment his own way. He did what he was told, did more than he was told, and while he did more he did not let it show.

Seorin knew it was not an apology.

An apology is made to someone else. That, he was doing to himself.

The sight of it seemed, if anything, to shut the scrap market people's mouths even tighter.

Here, hands that did not have to do it lifting the broken things themselves made a bigger statement than an apology in words.

Watching it from far off, Kael spoke.

"He's not running, at least."

Sern answered.

"If he ran, it would amount to admitting he was wrong."

"Then what about not running?"
Luhai asked.

Sern looked toward Harun for a moment before he spoke.

"It means that, by his own way of thinking, he still believes he is right."

Seorin felt that it grated on her somehow, and yet it was not wrong.

Harun was hateful. But he was not flat. That made him a more tiring kind of person.

Zahir was already sitting in his own place again.

His face said that punishing a man in the courtyard a little while ago, and drawing the line of the whole board again, were nothing special here.

Seorin knew now. The man was not indifferent; he was the kind who kept turning order back into place with an indifferent face.

Ater seemed to have felt something similar.

He spoke low.

"There is a reason this place has held out longer than one would expect."

Luhai picked it up with a grin.

"Admitting it now?"

Ater did not reply to that.

Instead, looking down past the railing at the floor of the scrap market, he added to it.

"There is a kind of order that is kept by looking like disorder."

Luhai clicked his tongue.

"He sure makes it sound hard."

Kael summed it up shortly.

"He means there are rules that pretend to be a mess."

At that, Luhai nodded right away.

"That, I get."

Hearing the three of them, Seorin almost laughed through her nose.

Whatever it was, one thing mattered now. The air was no longer that of leaving as if they were running away.

That feeling was bigger than she expected. At first Hazran had looked like a place to drop in briefly and slip out of. Now it was starting to look like a place where, before setting out, you caught your breath, made repairs, filled what you needed, and took one more look at people's faces.

Luhai was the first to put that air into words.

"But we're not leaving right away, are we?"
he said.
"Nobody knows how long whatever's going on in there will take, the arena's wrecked, and it'll take a few days for the market to get going again."

Seorin cut him off at once.

"Since when are you an us?"

Luhai did not even blink.

"No, I mean, that's how it feels,"
he said.
"And it'd be kind of off to leave just me behind now."

Hearing that, Kael gave a short laugh.

"Why?"

"No, just,"
Luhai said, looking off elsewhere for no reason.
"Looks like there's a lot to see."

Seorin cut in again.

"Shamelessness is a talent, huh."

Luhai did not deny it at all.

"Yeah,"
he said.
"This place is too good to see for just a day."

Seorin heard something strange in that.

*This place*, he had said.

The kid was born and raised here. He knew the names of every alley, read the price boards, knew which stall hid what. For a kid like that to say his own home was too good to see for just a day did not add up.

*Too good to waste* is something people say when they are about to leave.

Once she caught that, Seorin looked at Luhai again. He was looking somewhere else, and he was looking somewhere else so hard that it gave him away.

The kid has already decided.

Seorin did not say it out loud. If she brought it up now, he would say no, and once he had said no it would be harder to turn it around later.

Before, Seorin would have snorted at it. This time no retort came right away.

Too good to waste. That was truer than she had thought.

The scrap market was still being repaired, inside Sion and Han Jiwoo were joining something together with Aka, Harun was serving his punishment, and Zahir was keeping Hazran rolling again.

The time that opened up in between was not a blank. It felt like time for the faces you would come to see if you stayed here a while.

Ater spoke very quietly.

"Then while we wait, it would be better to sort out what we need."

Kael answered right away.

"Parts, fuel, water, heat-shield cloth. More, depending on how things look with the hull."

Sern added, his voice low.

"And people as well."

Seorin looked at him.

Sern was as calm as ever.

"Who sees what here, who takes what away, who remembers whom and how,"
he said.
"That will be sorted out too."

"Why sort that out?"

It was Luhai who asked.

"Because if it is not sorted out, people remember it differently later."

That was Sern's answer.

"If people who were together remember it differently, the next time they meet they are people from different places."

Luhai shut his mouth at that.

Seorin looked toward Sern.

He carried the mark of having once, somewhere, come to remember things differently from people he had been with.

She did not ask. Here, not asking seemed to be the courtesy, and Seorin had received that courtesy a few times herself.

His words stayed strangely long.

Seorin looked down past the railing again, without a word.

The arena being repaired, the tents going up again, the market slowly coming back to life. And the people who still had not come out from inside.

It was not time to leave yet.

Seorin took her hand off the railing.

They had come here a few days ago, and it felt longer than a few days. Coming in, they had meant only to get Etherite and leave straight away. No one had broken that plan, yet right now no one was keeping it.

The sun began to slant.

Somewhere came the sound of tent ropes being untied. It came from one place, and soon from another. It was a sound that had not been there until a while ago.

Seorin did not know what that sound was. She had watched this market all day, and that sound was new.

Instead, the air of a place where they would, at least once before leaving, properly stay was settling in little by little.
