# Episode 165. Hands Turned Inward

The air in the hidden level was dry that day.

The Hazran people kept stopping as they folded cloth, as they poured water. There had been no sound, and still they stopped, then went on.

Sion, who had been watching them, stopped too. Far off, toward the gray route, there was a sound.

Footsteps.

Not human feet, and not the old machines' feet either. Each of the old machines' steps sounded different; these were the same.

One step.
One step.
One step.

The same interval. There were many of them, yet the many sounded like a single step.

Two of the Hazran people backed up to the wall first, and the woman who came with two children put both of them behind her. The tired-faced woman dropped her cloth and didn't pick it up, and the man without a younger brother stood up.

At the end of the gray route, they came out.

In front were two machines whose bodies were different from the old machines'. They had two optical eyes and exposed joints, and the gray dust of outside was on them. The first recovery hand looked at the two.

"Outside hands."

It said.

"Outside hands?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"Hands that go out by the gray road. We're recovery; they're outside."

Behind the two outside hands were the others.

Androids.

A single column, and Luhai started counting and gave up. They weren't worn, but neither were they sleek like the guide machines up above. Their joints showed, their casings were thin, and their hands were long. Their smooth faces had no place for eyes, no place for a mouth, and no light. They stood at the same intervals, at the same height.

Han Jiwoo's face set, and Luhai didn't smile. Aka looked that way for a long time without taking her eyes off them.

The old machine that had begun to pull away from its pillar came one step out and stopped, and it made no sound. The optical eyes of the wall-side machine rose to the androids, and their faint light passed once over those smooth faces. The light stopped there and did not come down.

Sion looked at those optical eyes, stopped where they were.

One outside hand stood at the front and spoke, low.

"Serakion's started changing how it deploys inside. Several rows of units it was sending up have stopped, whole. Now that side's turning its androids inward too."

"How do you know?"

Seorin asked.

"Three transports are tied up on the mooring line. No departure marker came up. What was going to be loaded wasn't. It went to another sector. Inward."

A short silence.

The outside hand turned and looked back once at what it had brought.

"So we brought some too."

No one answered right away. On the gap side, the second recovery hand stood on its straightened leg, a hand braced on the wall, and looked at the androids.

Seorin asked first.

"Explain."

The outside hand's optical eyes came to Seorin.

"There's a limit to what our force can do. Inside or out, once that side starts turning more androids loose, people alone can't hold."

The words did not stop.

"Against androids, you need androids. Those aren't something you persuade—you just rewrite their command line and use them. It's not like they've got a will."

Han Jiwoo cut those words off.

"You say that."

She said it low.

"Far too easily."

The outside hand's optical eyes turned to her.

"It's not easy to say. I'm saying it because otherwise we die."

"So you won't die—"

Han Jiwoo looked straight at it as she spoke.

"—you decide there's nothing there."

"Those aren't even people—"

Han Jiwoo cut in again.

"The ones who say there's no will are the first to erase it."

While no one could step in, the old machine's hand came half a span away from the pillar, then went back.

Luhai let out a breath like a curse.

"But if we don't use them, we get pushed back."

Han Jiwoo looked at Luhai, but Luhai was looking at the androids with his hands clenched. Both.

The outside hand gave a small nod.

"Right. We know. We didn't bring them because we like it. But if our lives aren't going to end, we have to turn that side's things back on that side."

Seorin didn't take a side; she looked at the gap between the androids and the outside hand. Three steps. The outside hand stood with its back to the androids, at a distance where it could afford to show its back.

"Control?"

"We've got the baseline pinned down with jamming. They're not the deep-thinking kind. Give them a precise command and they move."

The old machine's optical eyes moved, very slightly—from the outside hand to the androids, then to the floor.

Sern spoke low.

"The judgment that they do not think deeply—who made it?"

The outside hand answered.

"The ones who saw them in the field."

"In the field."

Sern went on, cold.

"Fast movement is commonly mistaken for having no will."

"What did you see?"

The outside hand asked.

"On Hazran."

Sern said.

"When the front rank fell, the next row came at the same angle. We called that empty. I do not yet know whether we were wrong or right."

Ater did not take that up; he was looking at the androids. The distance they stood at was the same, and so were the intervals at which they stopped. Only one, at the end of the column, held its head half a knuckle lower than the others, and it had stayed that way ever since Han Jiwoo cut in.

"It is not an entirely empty array."

His voice was very low.

"What isn't?"

Seorin asked.

"The one at the very end. Its head is different."

Everyone looked at the end, at that half a knuckle. Some could see it, and some couldn't. Luhai couldn't, so he narrowed his eyes and looked again. Aka could, but she didn't nod.

Sion looked at the one at the end, then the one beside it, then the one beside that. They were the same; only the end was different, by half a knuckle. He pressed down on the metal plate in his pocket.

"Is this really all right?"

The woman who came with two children spoke with both of them pressed close behind her, her eyes on the androids.

"Things like those are why we ran. And now you want us standing here with them?"

One of the children behind her looked at the androids through the folds of her clothes. The woman pushed the child's head back, but the child peeked out again.

No one could tell her she was wrong. The man without a younger brother, standing and looking at the androids, moved his lips. It was a counting mouth, and he stopped partway through the count.

Instead of answering, Han Jiwoo walked three steps forward—not all the way up to the androids, only as far as the outside hand's side—and stood there. She didn't run.

"I don't know yet."

She spoke low.

"Whether they're really empty, or whether we just want to believe they are."

A short silence.

She looked at the one at the very end, the head half a knuckle lower, and steadied her breath once.

"But I know one thing. If we start repeating what Serakion used to say, just for different reasons, the ending might not turn out as different as we think."

The outside hand didn't answer; its optical eyes stayed on Han Jiwoo.

Seorin, who had said nothing for a while, looked at the androids, at the Hazran people pressed to the wall, at the old machine with its hand that had come half a span off the pillar, and only then at the outside hand.

"I'm not throwing them out right now. But they don't get folded into our strength either. Movement, response, command execution, chance of breaking away—we check all of it again."

One of the outside hands set its face hard—the other one, the one that had been standing behind.

"There's no time for that."

Seorin didn't so much as blink.

"Then we make time. If we cross the wrong line from here, we won't be going to break Serakion—we'll be assembling another Serakion."

The room went quiet. The two outside hands looked at each other, but neither said anything.

The outside hand in front turned toward the androids and raised a hand, and the androids moved.

One step.
One step.

They went deeper in, into an empty sector, and stood there in a single column. The one at the very end, its head half a knuckle low, went last.

Behind the adults, the Hazran children watched that column, and the child with the screwdriver gripped it hard.

Seorin sat down in front of the plate, picked up a stone, and drew one more square at the edge of the plate, outside the four. It was an empty square, with no stone set in it.

"What's that?"

Luhai asked.

"Don't know yet. So I'm leaving it empty."

When the old machine took its hand off the pillar completely, there was one step toward the sector, and a sound. Then it stopped and went no farther.

That machine's optical eyes were on the column. So were the optical eyes of the wall-side machine, and neither came down.

Sion and Han Jiwoo looked at the two lights.

"What's with them?"

Luhai asked, low.

No one answered.

The yellow lamp flickered, but the column inside the sector did not. The head at the very end stayed half a knuckle low.

Han Jiwoo did not take her eyes off it.
