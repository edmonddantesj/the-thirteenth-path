# Episode 165. Hands Turned Inward

Serakion's upper level had always been closer to the side that handles how a problem looks before handling the problem itself.

So changes always showed late. Or rather, they were arranged to show late.

Transport vessels that would normally have passed through the gray zone and slipped outward were left tied to the mid-level mooring line that day, without departure markers.

On the surface it looked like a simple delay. Parts alignment issues. Departure sequence readjustment. Internal inspection on hold.

The words on the surface were always calm.

But it was not only the transport vessels that had stopped.

The androids that had been slated to load inside were being quietly relocated to different sections.

Hands that should have gone outside were being folded back into the inner staging line.

Some might have seen that as a loss. These were resources already tied to external trade lines, flows that had been passing through multiple hands under concealed identities.

But right now, Serakion was counting control before loss.

Children had vanished. The sorting that never stopped had gone empty a few times. Gaps that should never have opened downward had actually opened.

Serakion had not yet called it a disturbance. Instead, it had begun quietly increasing its internal forces.

---

The air on the old layer's side was drier that day.

No one was speaking loudly, yet people's movements kept breaking more often than the day before.

Sion understood the reason only when he heard the returning footsteps of hands that had been outside.

At first, the footsteps felt wrong.

Not irregular like human feet. Not dragging heavy like the old machines.

Too even. Too aligned.

Those who reacted before Sion were several of the people from Hazran. Two of them stepped back almost by instinct. Someone swallowed their breath. Someone hid a child behind them.

And soon, at the end of the gray corridor, they appeared.

Androids.

Not worn. Not smooth like Serakion's upper guide machines either.

Bodies built for far more practical use. Joints arranged to move far faster. Faces designed to erase emotion far more easily.

There were not many. But not few either.

The number was not the problem.

The problem was that the ones who had brought them in were the hands that had been moving in and out of Serakion.

Jiwoo's expression hardened first. Luhai was not smiling. Aka watched them in silence for a long time.

Several old machines stopped their hands entirely. The reaction was so brief that someone unfamiliar might have missed it. But Sion did not.

That was not simple wariness.

It was a reaction closer to memory.

One outside hand stood in front and said it low.

"Serakion has started changing its internal deployment. Several lines being elevated upward stopped entirely. They are turning androids inward now too."

Brief silence.

He looked behind him at the androids he had brought.

"So we brought some as well."

Even after those words, no one answered right away.

Seorin asked first.

"Explain."

The outside hand continued, face tired.

"Our manpower has limits. Inside or outside, once that side starts turning more androids inward, people alone cannot hold."

He continued without pause, as though the logic had become too familiar.

"Androids need androids. These are not something to persuade. You just rewrite the command line. They do not have will."

In that moment, Jiwoo cut him off.

"Those words." She said it low. "You say them too easily."

The air in the room tightened, barely.

The outside hand fired back immediately.

"I am not saying it easily. I am saying it because we die otherwise."

"To avoid dying," Jiwoo said, looking at him straight, "you are declaring there is nothing there."

He narrowed his brow.

"They are not even people—"

Jiwoo cut in again.

"The side that says there is no will is always the first to erase it."

This time no one could easily interject.

Those words were not aimed only at the androids. They were aimed at everyone inside simultaneously.

Luhai let out a breath that was almost a curse, low.

"But if we do not use them, we get pushed back."

The words were not cowardly. They were so honest they hurt more.

The outside hand nodded small.

"Right. We know. We did not bring them because we wanted to. But if our lives are not going to end, we have to turn their things back on them."

Seorin heard it and did not take a side right away. She looked once at the gap between the androids and the outside hand, and asked low.

"Control?"

"We have the baseline locked with signal jamming. They are not the deep-thinking kind. Give exact commands and they move."

At that last sentence, one old machine shifted its optical eye, very faintly.

Very small. But that small movement looked like a refusal that came before words.

Sern said it low.

"Who made the judgment that they do not think deeply?"

The outside hand answered.

"The side that saw them in the field."

"In the field," Sern continued, cold, "fast movement is commonly mistaken for absence of will."

Ater did not pick up that thread, but his gaze was already elsewhere.

He was looking at the androids' arrangement. The distance they stood at. The intervals of their stops. Who shifted gaze first. Who held the front to the end.

And he said it very low.

"The arrangement is not completely empty."

No one could immediately ask what he meant.

In that moment, Sion had the strange sense that while the androids appeared to simply be standing still, each was silent in a slightly different way.

He could not declare there was a difference. But he could not say there was none either.

That ambiguity was more frightening.

"Is this really all right?"

This time it was not Sion but a middle-aged woman from Hazran who spoke first.

She held a child behind her and looked at the androids.

"We ran because of things like those. And now we are supposed to stand alongside them?"

No one could easily call that wrong.

Jiwoo, instead of answering, walked a few steps forward.

She did not go all the way to the androids. But she did not retreat either.

"I still do not know." She said it low. "Whether those are truly empty, or whether we want to believe they are."

Brief silence.

She steadied her breath once, then continued lower.

"But I do know one thing. If we start repeating the same words Serakion uses, for different reasons, the ending might not be as different as we think."

That was not a conclusion. Not a prohibition. Not a permission.

Seorin said nothing for a while.

Then she spoke in a very practical voice.

"We do not throw them out right now. But we do not integrate them as full forces either. Movement, response, command compliance, defection risk — we re-examine all of it."

One outside hand's face hardened.

"We do not have time for that."

Seorin did not blink.

"Then we make it. If the line gets crossed wrong now, we will not be going to break Serakion. We will be assembling a different Serakion."

After those words, the room went deeply quiet.

No one fully agreed. No one could fully argue back.

And at that same hour, on Serakion's upper side, in a similar silence, androids were being redeployed inward.

Different justifications. Different fears. Different languages.

But more and more, both sides were beginning to hold the same means in their hands.
