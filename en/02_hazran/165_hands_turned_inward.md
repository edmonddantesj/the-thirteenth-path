# Episode 165. Hands Turned Inward

Serakion's upper level had always been closer to the kind of place that dealt first
not with the fact that a problem had arisen
but with how that fact would look.

So change always showed late.
No,
it was arranged so that it would show late.

Several transport ships that on any other day would have slipped out through the gray zone
did not receive their departure markers that day
and stayed tethered where they were, on the middle mooring line.

On the surface it looked like a simple delay.
A parts alignment issue,
a readjustment of departure order,
an internal inspection on hold.

The words written on the surface were always calm.

But the transport ships were not the only thing that had stopped.

The androids that were to have been loaded onto them
were also being moved, quietly, to a different sector than usual.

Hands that should have been going outward
were folded back, this time, into the inner standby line.

Someone would have thought of it as a loss.
They were resources already tied into the outside trade lines,
a flow that had been passing through many hands with its identity hidden.

But Serakion, right now,
was counting control before loss.

Children had disappeared,
the sorting that never stopped had gone empty a few times,
and a gap that should never have opened downward had actually opened.

Serakion did not yet call any of this a disturbance.
Instead,
it quietly began building up its forces on the inside.

---

The air on the old layer was drier that day than usual.

No one was talking loudly,
yet, strangely, people's movements broke off more often than the day before.

Sion only understood why
when he heard the hands that had gone outside coming back.

At first the footsteps felt wrong.

They weren't irregular like human feet,
and they didn't drag heavily like the old machines.

They were too even.
Too much in step.

The first to react, before Sion, were a few of the people from Hazran.
Two of them stepped back almost on instinct.
Someone caught their breath,
someone hid a child behind them before anything else.

And soon,
at the end of the gray route, they came into view.

Androids.

They weren't worn,
and they weren't sleek like the guide machines on Serakion's upper level.

Bodies built far more for practical use.
Joints arranged to move far faster.
Faces designed to make emotion far easier to erase.

There weren't many of them.
But there weren't few, either.

The number wasn't the problem.

The problem was that the ones who had brought them in
were the hands that went in and out of Serakion's outside.

Han Jiwoo's face set first.
Luhai didn't smile either.
Aka watched them for a long time without a word.

A few of the old machines stopped their hands outright.
The reaction was so brief
that anyone who didn't know might have missed it.
But Sion didn't.

That wasn't simple wariness.

It was closer to a reaction born of memory.

One outside hand stood at the front and spoke, low.

"Serakion's started changing its inside deployment."
"Several rows of units it was sending up have stopped, whole."
"Now that side's turning its androids inward too."

A short silence.

It turned around
and looked once at the androids it had brought.

"So we brought some too."

Even after those words fell,
no one answered right away.

Seorin asked first.

"Explain."

The outside hand went on, its face tired.

"There's a limit to what our force can do."
"Inside or out,
once that side starts turning more androids loose,
people alone can't hold."

As if it were logic it had grown far too used to,
it went straight on without the slightest pause.

"Against androids, you need androids."
"Those aren't something you persuade,
you just rewrite their command line and use them."
"It's not like they've got a will."

At that moment,
Han Jiwoo cut it off.

"You say that,"
she said, low,
"far too easily."

The air in the room stiffened, very slightly.

The outside hand shot back at once.

"It's not easy to say."
"I'm saying it because otherwise we die."

"So you don't die,"
Han Jiwoo said, looking straight at it,
"you decide there's nothing there."

It narrowed its brows.

"Those aren't even people,"

Han Jiwoo cut in again.

"The ones who say there's no will
are the first to erase it."

This time, no one could easily step in.

Those words weren't aimed only at the androids.
They were pointed at everyone in there at once.

Luhai let out a low breath that was close to a curse.

"But if we don't use them, we get pushed back."

That wasn't cowardly.
If anything, it hurt more for being so honest.

The outside hand gave a small nod, too.

"Right."
"We know.
We didn't bring them because we like it."
"But if our lives aren't going to end,
we have to turn that side's things back on that side."

Hearing that, Seorin didn't take a side right away.
She just looked once at the gap between the androids and the outside hand,
and asked, low,

"Control?"

"We've got the baseline pinned down with jamming."
"They're not the deep-thinking kind."
"Give them a precise command and they move."

At that last sentence,
one of the old machines shifted its optical eye, very faintly.

It was very small.
But that small movement
looked like a refusal that had come out before any words.

Sern said, low,

"The judgment that they do not think deeply,
who made it?"

The outside hand answered.

"The ones who saw them in the field."

"In the field,"
Sern went on, coldly,
"people tend to mistake moving fast for having no will."

Ater did not pick that up,
but his gaze had already gone elsewhere.

He was looking at how the androids were arrayed.
The distance they stood at,
the intervals at which they stopped,
who shifted its gaze first,
and who kept facing forward to the end.

Then he said, very low,

"It is not an entirely empty array."

No one could ask right away what he meant.

At that moment Sion had the strange sense
that those androids, though they seemed simply to stand still,
were each keeping silent in slightly different ways.

He couldn't be sure there was a difference.
But he couldn't say there wasn't, either.

That uncertainty
was more frightening.

"Is this really all right?"

This time it wasn't Sion
but a middle-aged woman from Hazran who spoke first.

Keeping a child pressed close behind her,
she looked at the androids and said,

"Things like those are why we ran."
"And now you want us standing here with them?"

No one could easily tell her she was wrong.

Instead of answering,
Han Jiwoo walked a little way forward.

She didn't go all the way up to the androids.
But she didn't run, either.

"I don't know yet,"
she said, low.
"Whether they're really empty,
or whether we just want to believe they are."

A short silence.

She steadied her breath once
and went on, lower.

"But I know one thing."
"If we start repeating what Serakion's been saying,
just for different reasons,
the ending might not turn out as different as we think."

It wasn't a conclusion.
Not a word that stopped it,
and not one that allowed it.

For a while, Seorin said nothing.

Then she spoke in a very practical voice.

"I'm not throwing them out right now."
"But they don't get folded into our strength either."
"Movement, response, command execution, chance of breaking away. We check it all again."

One of the outside hands set its face hard.

"There's no time for that."

Seorin didn't so much as blink.

"Then we make time."
"If we cross the wrong line from here,
we won't be going to break Serakion,
we'll be assembling another Serakion."

After that,
the room went very deeply quiet.

No one fully agreed,
and no one could fully refute it either.

And at the same hour,
up on Serakion's upper level, in a similar silence,
androids were being redeployed inward.

Different pretexts,
different fears,
different languages.

But more and more,
both sides were beginning to take the same means in hand.
