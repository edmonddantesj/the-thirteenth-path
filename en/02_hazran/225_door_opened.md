# Episode 225. Door Opened

When the door opened, what Sion felt first was not grandeur.

It was quiet.

The space was closer to a secret meeting point barely preserved deep inside a building disguised as long-abandoned in the city center.

From outside, it would have looked like a records shop closed ages ago, or an unmanaged relay office.

Inside was different.

No unnecessary decoration. Only what was needed remained. Quiet traces that someone had used this place to survive for a long time.

The light was low. Presences were few. The air inside the room was excessively ordered.

And inside, a young man—almost a boy—was standing.

Sion stopped, without meaning to, for just an instant.

Young.

Far younger than expected.

What entered before the youth was the person's stillness.

Not stillness that forcibly suppressed the atmosphere.

The stillness of someone who had endured alone for so long they had learned to settle before speaking.

He was not ornate.

But strangely, every line in the room seemed to organize itself toward him.

Tarin was the first to bow.

"I have brought them."

The boy raised his gaze very slowly.

And looked at Elia first.

That gaze lingered, very briefly.

In that single instant, Sion saw surprise.

Very short. Truly short. The kind only those who noticed could notice.

The surprise vanished in the very next moment.

Understanding entered.

And after that, a certainty strangely quiet remained.

Like someone watching a possibility long known arrive as reality before the eyes—he looked at Elia, then at the nameless man beside her.

Then finally, one by one, slowly shifted the gaze—to Sion and Aka. Sern and Seorin. Kael, Jiwoo, Serakion. And last, Luhai.

Only Ater stood in the shade beside the door. It was not the moment for the heir of Valkar to step forward before the Emperor first.

In that brief gap, Luhai's face had already decided what to do.

Sion felt a bad premonition the instant he saw it.

Sure enough, Luhai strode forward as though to lighten the mood.

"Wow. Closer to my age than I thought?"

Sion thought: we are done.

Seorin's gaze hardened for just an instant. Sern said nothing but his eyes sank very faintly. Aka immediately frowned.

But Tarin, strangely, said nothing.

Instead, he stood quietly—as though deliberately holding back one beat.

Luhai apparently took that as permission.

He stepped closer and lightly tapped the boy's shoulder.

"What is this atmosphere. You look like someone carrying everything alone."

Sion genuinely thought his breath had stopped.

Aka turned immediately.

"Hey, what are you—"

Luhai did not stop.

He looked at the young man's face once more and grinned, as though they were already friends.

"Still, good thing. I was expecting someone terrifying, but you actually look like a person."

This time Kael lowered his head. Seorin bit her lip for just an instant.

Sion could not tell if they were holding back laughter or sighs.

And then the boy smiled—very briefly.

A soundless smile.

Faint. Like someone who had suddenly recalled something forgotten for too long.

He looked at Luhai and said, in a very low and soft voice.

"It would be a problem if I were not a person."

Luhai's eyes sparkled.

"Oh, nice answer."

And then, truly naturally, he leaned as though about to half-drape his arm around the boy.

"Yeah. Let us talk like people now. Everyone here is being way too serious."

The boy did not pull back this time either.

Instead, for just a moment—truly just a moment—as though playing along, the corner of his mouth loosened a little more.

"Is that so."

At that single brief response, Luhai was fully energized.

"See? You are not all stiff either."

Sion, from beside, was seriously thinking someone needs to stop this—yet strangely, no one intervened right away.

Sern was silent. Seorin deliberately turned her head slightly. Jiwoo's hand rose, then stopped. Kael covered a laugh with a throat-clear.

Everyone had already noticed.

Tarin was deliberately not cleaning this up right now.

Only Aka was genuinely angry.

"Luhai. Enough."

Luhai turned, looking truly wronged.

"What? I am lightening the mood."

"Why does it have to be you."

"Everyone was so stiff, I just…"

The young man looked at Aka briefly this time.

And said it with strange composure.

"It is all right. It has been a while."

At those words, even Aka paused one beat.

A while.

That word caught strangely.

Luhai rode the flow completely.

"See? I was not wrong."

Then, looking at the young man again—this time smiling the way you smile at a real friend.

"But you are fun. Instead of sitting here alone setting the mood, come over to our side sometime and relax."

Sion nearly cradled his own head.

At this point, resignation that watching how far it went might be faster than stopping crossed his mind.

The young man did not get angry this time either.

Instead, he looked at Luhai once—as though genuinely intrigued.

Then asked, low.

"Is he always like this."

Jiwoo answered immediately.

"Yes."

Seorin added small.

"Mostly."

Kael, head turned, muttered very low.

"Less so on lucky days."

In that moment, the young man's smile became a little more clear.

Brief, but unmistakably a smile.

And right then, Tarin finally spoke in a very neat voice.

"Luhai."

Luhai turned without a thought.

"Yes?"

Tarin said it without a single crack in composure.

"This is the Emperor of the Empire."

At that single line, Luhai froze solid.

The expression, the shoulders, the hands, the lips that had been moving moments ago—all stopped at once.

Eyes went wide. Mouth opened slightly. Even breathing seemed to pause half a beat late.

Sion saw that expression and, knowing he should not laugh, nearly did.

Luhai, only much later, caught up inside his head to what he had just done.

"Ah…"

And smaller.

"Ah?"

And at the very end, nearly flinching.

"Ahh?"

Jiwoo finally tapped the back of his head once.

"Too late."

Luhai nearly bounced backward.

"S-sorry. No, I just… I mean, I… I really did not know…"

The Emperor was still watching him with a quiet face.

But that quiet was a little softer now than before.

"I know." He said. "And that it was not intentional."

Aka still looked displeased, but did not say more.

Instead, Sion felt: from this one short episode, this person might be a far stranger kind than expected.

And one more thing caught his eye.

While Luhai had been tapping shoulders, joking, breaking the mood—the child's posture had not shifted by a fraction.

Not startled. Not offended. Instead, quietly watching Luhai.

That was not tolerance.

It was closer to the face of someone who, for a very long time, had not had anyone speak to them that way—and was very carefully placing that sensation into memory.

Sion saw it and felt for the first time with certainty: this person's stillness was not born. It was made.

He was a person who held order. Even within that order, he was someone who did not immediately cut people away.

The Emperor's gaze returned to Elia.

And lingered, very briefly. Truly briefly.

"You really came."

Elia answered dryly.

"I had to."

"I suppose so."

Then the Emperor's gaze moved to the nameless man beside.

This time it stayed longer than before.

Inside that brief silence, Sion felt the real question of this room was just now crossing its threshold.

The Emperor said it very quietly.

"It has been a long time."

The man was silent for a moment.

Then straightened his posture.

As though—even if the body did not fully follow—the propriety would not be broken.

He bowed his head, very precisely.

"I have come late. Your Majesty."

Between those two sentences, far too much time that Sion did not yet know had passed.

The Emperor, and the nameless man who had been confined at the deepest level.

The two did not look like strangers meeting for the first time.

At least one of them was treating the Emperor of this moment thoroughly as Emperor.

That was not familiarity. It was the etiquette of someone who understood order.

Tarin stepped back one quiet pace.

The movement of someone who knew the next words in this room belonged to those two before anyone else.

The Emperor said it low.

"I heard from Tarin. What you said. And how those words were severed."

The man's gaze deepened—very slightly, for the first time.

"It reached that far."

"Not all of it." The Emperor answered. "To know all of it, the pieces given to me were too few."

The man asked, very slowly.

"Even so… you were waiting?"

"Yes."

The Emperor answered immediately.

"Because I had come to believe that the inside alone was no longer enough."

The moment those words fell, Sion felt with certainty for the first time.

This Emperor—while sitting at the Empire's center—had known for a long time already that the inside alone could no longer hold.

The Emperor's gaze moved to Sion's group.

"That is why I needed to meet you."

Luhai still had not fully recovered, but this time he kept his mouth shut.

Sion asked very slowly.

"You… knew about us?"

"In fragments." The Emperor said. "In names. In resonance. In the shards of the outside that Tarin brought." He steadied a very short breath. "Serakion. Elia. The remaining grain of the Black Covenant. And all of you."

Aka's gaze sank.

The realization that they had not simply arrived here by chance—that they had already been inside someone's gaze for a long time—was gradually becoming real.

Elia said it low.

"Then do not change the order. If you have been waiting, ask what needs asking first."

The Emperor nodded—very faintly.

And this time, looking only at the nameless man, opened his mouth.

"Do you still believe the Empire can be rebuilt?"

Even Luhai held his breath this time.

Sion felt that sentence was not a simple political question.

This was a confirmation the Emperor was offering to a person who had been buried for too long.

Do you still hold the same answer. Does the reason we can sit facing each other still remain.

The man did not answer right away.

Instead he looked at the Emperor for a long time.

Not looking at the seat called Emperor, but reading the time one person standing in that seat had endured.

And said it very low.

"Rebuilding is not the right word. You must first ask again what it originally was."

The moment that sentence fell, Sion felt his back go faintly cold.

This is the person.

The name was still unknown. But why the Empire could neither kill this person nor release, why it had buried him at the deepest level—that single sentence made it clear enough.

And the Emperor, hearing those words, did not step back at all.

Instead, very quietly, something close to relief—close to certainty—remained in his eyes.

"That is the answer I was waiting to hear."

With that single line, Sion felt it clearly.

Now, truly, the inside and the outside of the Empire were beginning to stand on the same question for the first time.
