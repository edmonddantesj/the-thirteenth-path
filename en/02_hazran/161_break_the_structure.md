# Episode 161. Break the Structure

After the third rescue failed, no one inside the hidden layer raised their voice for a long time.

Everyone was exhausted. Everyone was thinking about what came next. Everyone knew this failure was more than a simple failure.

So when Jiwoo opened her mouth, it sounded like something that had burst too late.

"But."

Her voice was not loud. Yet no one in that room could take the word lightly.

Jiwoo stood without leaning against anything and spoke.

"I understand. Why we cannot extract them all at once. Why we have only been able to bring back a few at a time. Why one wrong move kills everyone together."

The words did not begin like an attack aimed at the recovery hands. They were too precise, and therefore hurt more.

She truly did understand.

Serakion closes fast. The hidden layer has few hands. The old machines cannot hold forever. One wrong move and humans and machines fold together.

She had seen all of it with her eyes. Lived it with her body.

"But." She said it again. "My heart still does not understand."

That sentence split the room.

"Why has this been all you could manage for so long. Why you kept letting them be taken. Why we have to endure toward the next attempt on the strength of a few who came back alive. Why those children always have to look at the side that stays first."

The last sentence sounded almost like self-reproach.

Jiwoo was not blaming the recovery hands or the old machines. She was closer to something more terrible.

She understood why the humans collapsed the way they did. She understood why the old machines could only bring back a few at a time. She understood that those machines were also discarded beings clinging to existence before their lights went out.

Humans made sense. Machines made sense.

But both making sense did not make this reality less terrible. If anything, it was harder to bear because there was no one easy to hate.

"Why has everyone lived this long only in the direction of enduring inside this structure?"

After those words fell, no one answered right away.

Not because there was no answer, but because the answer was not one that could be spoken easily.

After a long while, one old machine said it very low.

"There was a long time when enduring was all that remained."

The voice was not defensive. It was not an excuse.

It was simply the voice of a being that had watched the same fact for too long.

"We were discarded by the order above. Decommissioned. Severed. Pushed to the side that was no longer needed. It was not only the humans."

Jiwoo looked at the machine.

It continued.

"We tried not to go dark in our own way. The humans tried not to be dragged away in theirs. The two were not always possible at the same speed."

Cold words, but brutally honest.

The old machines had not lived as heroes. They too were discarded waste, beings barely continuing life on what function remained before their lights went out.

In the midst of that, they had rescued humans. Hidden them. Retrieved them. Held on to memories.

But that did not mean they had no lives and maintenance of their own.

Holding on before the light goes out. Remaining before memory is fully severed. Keeping even one more connection line alive.

That had been both their life and their purpose.

Another old machine added very low.

"We pitied the humans. We still do. That is why we have done this. But we also had our own lives. We also had to calculate the side that kept our lights on first, every time."

That sentence did not make Jiwoo angrier. It sank her deeper.

Because those words were also right.

This place was not filled only with malice. Here were beings all discarded together, each enduring so their own light would not go out.

Jiwoo understood that. That was why it was more agonizing.

If only the humans were pitiable, the direction would have been simpler. If only the machines were cold, she could have endured by hating them.

But here, humans were pitiable and machines were pitiable too. Both had been discarded. Both had been holding on to each other, little by little.

So this tragedy ran deeper not because anyone was evil, but because the structure itself allowed no other way to live.

Jiwoo clenched her jaw and asked.

"Then do we just keep doing this? A few come back alive, we wait for the next few, lose a few more, endure toward the next?"

This time the first recovery hand could not answer.

Neither could Kael. Nor Ater. Nor the old machines.

Because that had been the reality until now, and it was also true that everyone was already exhausted by it.

Right then, Luhai spoke in a perfectly ordinary tone.

"Then why not just break that structure?"

The room went quiet.

So simple that everyone seemed unable to process it right away.

Luhai, looking as though he did not quite understand why his words were strange, leaned against the wall and said it again.

"If we keep pulling a few out from in there, someone always stays behind. So why not break the thing that makes them stay?"

That had essence before logic.

Everyone until now had only been thinking about where to extract, how many to bring, how not to die, how to buy the next beat.

But Luhai was simply asking why they kept looking for answers only inside that structure.

If Serakion divides humans, reads them, elevates them, returns them, and forces a reality where all you can do is extract a few at a time — what reason was there to keep accepting that structure as a given?

Ater reacted first.

More precisely, he could not hide how quickly that statement aligned inside him.

"Extracting people while leaving the order intact is ultimately a way of enduring while accepting the order." He said it very low.

Kael raised his gaze too.

His eyes were already looking at structure, not paths.

"For it to be possible," he said low, "we would have to sever not the upward transit line but the axis that sustains it."

Jiwoo slowly raised her head too.

Her anger had not yet subsided. She was still pained. She still could not understand with her heart.

But precisely because of that, Luhai's words embedded deeper.

Until now, they had always been on the rescuing side. But perhaps rescuing alone was no longer enough.

Only by shaking the structure itself that kept making people live this way might the words "saving everyone" become possible.

One old machine was silent for a very long time, then spoke.

"That is... something we have been unable to do for a long time."

There was not only defeat in those words. There was a very faint shame, and a tremor — as though seeing that possibility again for the first time.

"Perhaps that is why," the machine continued very low, "since you arrived, we keep looking outward again."

That sentence struck everyone in the room differently.

For the recovery hands: what they had been doing was not wrong, but it might not have been enough.

For Jiwoo: the anger she had carried might not have been wasted emotion but a signal that the direction truly needed to change.

For Kael: not a rescue operation but the starting point of a tactic to bring down the structure itself.

For Ater: dismantling order was the question he could throw at both his own world and Serakion.

And for Luhai: he still did not quite understand why everyone thought so hard about everything, but something had started moving in the right direction.

At the end of that day, every being inside the hidden layer held a differently shaped piece of hope.

There was no plan yet. No path yet. Far too much was still lacking.

But at least, when thinking about what came next, the only answer in their minds was no longer just "how many more can we extract."

Perhaps for the first time, they were beginning to shift — from the side that rescues people, toward ending the structure itself that allowed people no other way to live.
