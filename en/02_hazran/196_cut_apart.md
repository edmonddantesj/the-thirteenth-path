# Episode 196. Cut Apart

As soon as they stepped onto the next level, the ground underfoot changed.

To look at, the floor ran on as one piece, but a few steps in, it was firm under Sion's left foot and gave, very faintly, under his right. He stopped and switched feet, and this time it was the other way round: the left gave and the right was firm.

The people behind him stopped at the same moment.

"What."

Seorin asked.

"The floor's different."

Sion said.

"Under each foot."

Seorin looked down at the floor lines, but no line showed, and no squares either. When she moved one foot to the side, it gave; when she moved it back, it was firm.

"It's splitting us."

She said it low.

Sern bent his knees and put his palm close to the floor, then lifted it; no handprint came up, which was not like the wall.

"Response lines are coming up separately. Speed, weight, heat, stride. Even if we come in together, it makes sure we are not read together."

"How?"

"Because each person steps differently."

Sern said.

"My stride and Kael's stride are different. The other side uses that to count Kael and me separately."

Han Jiwoo looked back. Nine in one line, the line she had thought had come in together, and it was out of step. Sern was half a step inward, Kael on the outside, and the two old machines a little late on their heavy feet. When she looked down at her own feet, they were half a span off from the last android's beside her.

"We've already split."

She said.

"Only a little, so far. This place is built to make that little grow."

Kael ran his eyes over the grooves and fold lines on both sides of the corridor; there were nine grooves, as many as there were people. He counted them from first to last, twice.

"If even one person lags, they'll get cut off."

"Yeah."

Seorin's answer was short.

"This level doesn't block the path. It makes it so we can't move together."

The two machines at the back traded static, one watching the android response line ahead, the other the robot reference line behind.

One of the old machines spoke low.

"If the androids are shaken. People split."

The other took it up.

"Robots close behind. They cut the split side first."

"Shake them and we split. And if you don't?"

Sion asked.

"Don't shake, they just come."

Seorin drew in a short breath. This was not a level for opening gaps; it was a level where they must not come apart. She looked at the nine, one by one.

"Changing formation."

She said.

"Sion in front. Sern right beside him. Han Jiwoo, hold the flow at the back. Kael, the outer fold lines. Ater, watch the inner array lines. Call out which sequence separates people first. Machines, don't shake them yet."

They changed places. Sion went to the front and Sern stood beside him; their shoulders touched, but this time he did not pull away. While Han Jiwoo went to the back of the line, Kael pressed against the outer wall and Ater against the inner wall.

On the inner wall Ater raised his head to look at, lines of flowing light ran from top to bottom, in a sequence.

"Yes. The inner array is still imitating the approval sequence. Its standard for cutting people, too, tries to fix the sequence before the response."

"So what's the sequence?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"Whoever is read first is cut first."

Ater said.

"Not whoever is read last. At home it was the reverse."

While one of the old machines looked toward Ater, its optical eye traveled down the lines on the inner wall.

"Then we get read first."

Seorin looked at that machine.

"And if you get cut."

"We move aside before we are cut."

The old machine answered.

The other took it up.

"We know the beat for moving aside. A beat older than that."

Seorin did not answer right away; she looked at the two machines' heavy feet, nodded once, and looked at the last android.

"You stay at the back to the end. Get read along with everyone, then cross at the very last."

He took the words without a sound and went to the very end, behind Han Jiwoo. Han Jiwoo looked back at him.

"Stay there. I can see you."

She spoke.

He was there.

The light deeper in the corridor spread thin, and the walls on both sides opened. Two androids and two Serakion robots, but in a different formation: the androids in front, spread apart, and the robots behind, holding closing plates.

Sern spoke at once.

"Front: separation. Rear: severing."

"Here we go."

Seorin kept it short.

The two old machines stepped out of the line, forward, ahead of Sion. The floor read their heavy feet first, so it gave only under the two machines; under the other seven, it held.

The face surfaces of the two androids in front turned toward the two machines. The rear robots' closing plate came up as well, toward the two machines, the side that had been read first.

Kael, still against the outer wall, watched the angle of that plate: the fold line, the hinge, the way it would fall.

"It drops forward. One span."

He said it low.

"If you move, move sideways. Not forward."

Han Jiwoo signaled briefly from the back, counting the steps without saying them aloud.

One.
Two.

The seven went in a single line, treading on the two machines' shadows, so that no one went ahead first and no one was left behind. When she matched her feet to Sern's beat, the last android's feet came behind her on the same beat.

On the inner wall Ater looked at, the lines of flowing light had all bunched toward the front.

"It has all gone to the machines. We have not been read yet."

The closing plate began to come down over the two machines' heads.

One of the old machines let out a very low static, not toward the androids but toward its own feet, and the other answered with the same sound.
Both stepped half a step aside at the same moment. The way Kael had said.
The plate came down on floor where no one stood, one span ahead.
The sound rang out long.

The floor began to read again, but the place that had been cut was empty, with nothing to read. Sern's lips moved.

Half.

No one said "now." They were all already moving.

Sion, Seorin, Sern, Han Jiwoo, Kael, Ater, the two old machines. And the last android.
In one line on the verge of breaking, they pushed into the next section.

Kael's foot caught on the edge of the splitting floor and his ankle bent, but he wrenched the caught foot free as he came across. The instant the last foot crossed the seam, the floor beneath it gave, and the last android's foot slid half a span toward the splitting side.

Without looking back, Han Jiwoo reached behind her. What her hand caught was not his arm but the knot, and she gripped it hard and pulled.
He came across. The floor closed behind him.

The two old machines looked back at where the plate had fallen, one span ahead, as Kael had said. One of them dipped its head toward Kael, once, but Kael held his ankle and pretended not to see.

It had been a gap too short for one long breath.

Once they were through, no one could say anything more; they gulped for breath first. While Sern leaned against the wall with the record panel held to his chest, Kael slowly rolled the ankle that had bent, his face unchanged. Sion looked at the palm the wall edge had scraped; the blood had dried.

As the corridor realigned, the misaligned responses were erased, and the beats they had held were erased. Sern counted: two beats.

"It has been erased."

He said.

"Even that we came through in one line."

"We know it."

Han Jiwoo said.

Sern nodded and wrote one line on the record panel.
Nine.

Seorin steadied her breathing and spoke.

"The next one will be worse. It won't just shrink the gaps now; it'll try to make each of us separate."

"It already tried to make us separate."

Sion said.

"This time with our feet. Next time, I don't know. With something other than feet."

When Sion looked farther in, the corridor was widening and the ceiling was rising. Light came from above, and he looked at that light for a long time.

Ater looked at the light too.

"The top is closer now."

He said.

"The light Aka saw."

When Han Jiwoo looked back, the last android was looking at the light too; his optical eye blinked, then came back. She retied his knot, which had not come loose, once more.

"Why?"

Sion asked.

"Just because."

Seorin moved off first, and the rest followed as one line on an unbroken beat. The floor gave differently under each foot, but no one stopped.
