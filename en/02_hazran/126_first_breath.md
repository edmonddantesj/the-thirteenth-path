# Episode 126. First Breath

The moment they emerged from the end of the corridor,
what entered first wasn't light—it was smell.

Old metal.
Dried grease stains.
A smell that hadn't been polished too well,
and because of that, couldn't hide the fact that machines had held on here for a long time.

Jiwoo smelled it
and for the first time since entering Serakion,
felt the tension in her shoulders ease, just slightly.

It was strange.

This place was clearly older,
rougher,
darker than everywhere they'd passed through.

But precisely because of that,
it felt for the first time like a place where human breath could land.

Sion noticed the difference immediately too.

If the Serakion above was cold, ordered air,
this was air that wasn't ordered—but at least held *traces of surviving*.

Not perfect.
And therefore,
closer to the side that lies a little less.

Aka said, very low.

"Less polished here."

Luhai took it right away.

"That's why it's better."

At those words,
several Hazran evacuees reacted with surprising speed.

Someone leaned against a wall and steadied their breathing.
Someone looked around
and wore a slightly less frightened face for the first time.

It wasn't wide,
but it had a strange sense of breathing room compared to the Serakion zones above.

The walls were old.
Everywhere, large device remnants sat embedded and still.
By current standards, there was plenty of what looked like wasted space.

But to the Hazran people,
even those useless-looking empty spots felt strangely sheltering.

Because it looked less like a world that organizes people neatly and puts them in
and more like a space where things had broken once and then held on, each in their own way.

Jiwoo approached one wall
and touched a pipe, corroded smooth, with her fingertip—very lightly.

A faint old warmth lingered there,
something impossible to feel from the smooth machines of Serakion above.

She clenched her hand once, slowly, without realizing.

This time Luhai didn't miss it.

He came closer and asked, low.

"What's with that hand thing you keep doing?"

Jiwoo didn't answer right away.

She kept her hand on the wall for a moment,
remembering her ship left behind in Hazran,
the desert skiffs.

A hull battered by sandstorms.
A cracked helm plate.
Metal forced together.
Things that wouldn't have held without hands.

And she said, very low.

"I was telling it to hold on."

Luhai went quiet for a moment.

Jiwoo withdrew her hand and added.

"I never actually believed it was listening,
but if I didn't do that, I couldn't bring myself to touch."

That was less an explanation
and closer to finally putting an old habit's nature into words.

Luhai looked at her once,
then muttered without managing to smile.

"No wonder things you've touched last a long time."

Jiwoo didn't answer right away.
But after a brief pause, she looked at Luhai.

"Why are you speaking informally to me, anyway?"

Luhai blinked once, stupidly.

"…Auntie?"

Jiwoo gave him a look of disbelief.

"…Call me noona."

After a short silence,
Luhai immediately dipped his waist slightly and took it.

"Yes, boss!"

Jiwoo swallowed a laugh in spite of herself,
and finally, the faintest trace of something loosened at the corner of her mouth.

Aka had been watching quietly,
then walked very slowly toward the side where the old devices sat still.

Since entering this place,
she had been stopping more often than in the Serakion zones above.

Sion came closer and asked, low.

"What do you see?"

Aka, looking at one stopped device, answered.

"Something old."

Sion couldn't quite smile.

Aka continued.

"But it is not just old."
"It was made in a different way from what is above."

Those words were short,
but enough to change the meaning of the entire space.

This might not be simply an abandoned lower zone.
It was more likely a place where the layer from before Serakion became what it is now remained—
from a time far clumsier and far less ordered.

Then,
the machine that had opened the path earlier
shed its Serakion guide face completely for the first time.

Part of the outer surface opened as if releasing,
and beneath the excessively smooth exterior,
a far older, rougher body was revealed.

Not beautiful like the machines in Serakion's upper zones.
But because of that,
for the first time it gave the feeling that *someone had survived and made it here*.

Seorin spoke low.

"Now it finally looks like something you can talk to."

The machine glanced her way briefly.
Then said, very short.

"Is that a compliment, among humans?"

Luhai fired back almost reflexively.

"Right now it is."

In that brief exchange,
even amid the tension, the thinnest breath loosened for the first time.

But it didn't reach the point of reassurance.

Sern was still reading signals near the entrance.
Ater was checking structure and cover.
Kael was confirming to the very end that all the evacuees had actually made it inside.

Everyone knew
that even if the place they'd fled into let them breathe,
it couldn't yet be called safe.

The machine swept the surroundings once,
then spoke low and dry.

"You can hold here for a while."
"Not for long."

Those words
sounded like the most precise sentence to describe this entire place.

The first spot inside Serakion where they could breathe.
But not a spot where they could catch their breath forever.

Sion looked around slowly.

Old pipes.
Stilled devices.
Unpolished metal.
Walls that looked like people could lean against them.

And the things the completed world above had discarded and passed over.

Only then did he think he understood.

The first space inside Serakion that lets people live
was closer not to the place Serakion is proudest of,
but to the place Serakion believes it has already forgotten.
