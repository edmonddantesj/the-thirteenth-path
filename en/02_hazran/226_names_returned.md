# Episode 226. Names Returned

The room was quiet for a while.

The Emperor's last words still remained in the air.

That is the answer I was waiting to hear.

Sion felt that sentence lingering strangely long.

They had rescued someone. Reached a hidden place. Even met the Emperor. Yet this room felt strangely closer to a beginning than to stability.

A place where questions long deferred were finally about to open their mouths.

That was the true grain of this space right now.

The first to break the silence was, as expected, Luhai.

This time, not with the charging energy of moments ago.

Traces of not having fully processed what he had just done still sat on his face.

But Luhai was Luhai.

He raised his hand carefully, and when no one stopped him, opened his mouth a little smaller.

"Um…"

Aka glared immediately.

Luhai hunched his shoulders.

"No, this time I will ask really carefully."

The corner of the Emperor's mouth moved—very faintly.

Seeing that, Luhai strangely gained a little courage.

"I am truly sorry about earlier."

"It is all right."

"No, but that was really very sorry."

This time Kael's mouth moved for just an instant.

The Emperor said it quietly.

"That point seems to have already been conveyed."

Luhai touched the back of his own head briefly.

"Yes. Physically as well."

Aka still looked displeased but did not interrupt further.

Luhai steadied one breath and this time asked truly carefully.

"Then… what is your name?"

Sion found himself looking at Luhai without meaning to.

Not a rude, barging question like before. It sounded like someone wanting, for the first time, to properly know a person.

The Emperor heard and looked at Luhai for just a moment.

That gaze was strangely not on the refusing side.

Keeping some distance, yet not fully pushing away.

"You ask that now?"

Luhai muttered small.

"Hazran people do not get intimidated in places like this…"

Aka cut in right away.

"Quiet."

But the Emperor, seeing that brief commotion, loosened the corner of his mouth even more faintly.

He spoke toward Luhai.

"Erion Serik."

The moment that name fell, Sion strangely felt as though one piece of the room's air had found its place.

Erion.

Now this person stood before them not only as the seat called Emperor, but as one person with a name.

Luhai wore the face of someone rolling the name inside once.

"Erion… Serik."

Then in a voice that was genuinely more careful.

"Understood."

That short answer was Luhai's own kind of courtesy.

The Emperor—no, Erion—wore the face of someone who could not have missed that, and nodded very small.

In that brief moment, Sion saw something strange.

After Erion's gaze left Luhai, it did not fully return to its place.

For just an instant—truly just an instant—it dropped toward his own right hand.

The hand held nothing. It was lightly open, resting quietly on the ordered surface, as an emperor's should.

But Sion felt somehow that Erion was looking at that hand.

Not the mouth that had just spoken his own name, but the hand that had to remain in the same place as though nothing had happened after giving the name away.

That brief gaze vanished soon. Erion was standing with a quiet face again.

But Sion thought, strangely, because of that single instant: this person may not have always been this quiet.

Someone who had learned over a long time to reveal nothing. And because of that, someone who now sometimes looked at even their own hand as something unfamiliar.

Then Sion noticed a very faint movement from the nameless man's side.

He was still holding his posture without collapse. Still maintaining the courtesy of treating the Emperor as Emperor. But in the moment the name was spoken, he looked like someone who had clarified something more sharply.

Tarin turned toward him.

After a very brief silence, Tarin said in a low and neat voice.

"There is no longer reason to conceal names in this room."

Sion held his breath without meaning to.

Elia was silent too. Sern and Seorin. Kael and Aka. This time no one interjected.

Tarin's gaze reached the man.

And finally, that name was placed inside this space—for the first time, again.

"Edmond Aurantius."

In that moment, the air in the room changed.

Luhai did not make a sound like before this time. Instead, his eyes went wide again.

Sion could feel without explanation that the name he was hearing was not merely the name of someone long imprisoned.

The way Tarin spoke that name was already different.

The naming of someone carefully returning what had been erased to its rightful place.

The Emperor's gaze sank very faintly too.

Edmond did not speak immediately after the name was called.

Instead he lowered his eyes very briefly, then raised them.

Even though his own name had been properly spoken for the first time in twenty-five years, he looked like someone who bore the weight quietly first, rather than receiving it emotionally.

He finally said to the Emperor, very precisely.

"I am remiss to have my name corrected so late before Your Majesty."

Sion held his breath for a moment.

Even in the instant of reclaiming his own name, this person was placing the Emperor as Emperor first.

A person who understood order, position, and propriety.

That grain needed no further explanation.

Erion said it very quietly.

"I have no intention of receiving an apology for a name returning."

Edmond's gaze wavered—very faintly.

Less surprise, and closer to accepting that this young Emperor had chosen more precise words than expected.

Luhai now truly wore the face of someone who could not contain curiosity.

But he could not barge in like before.

Instead he glanced at Sion and mouthed almost silently.

Seems like a really huge name?

Sion honestly thought the same.

This time Aka reacted first.

She was watching Edmond. More precisely, she was watching the grain of air that had changed in the room after the name was placed.

Not that one person became clearer when one name returned—but the sensation that one severed question had reconnected inside this space.

That was strangely more vivid to Aka.

The Emperor said it very low.

"What I heard from Tarin was not much. Only fragments. But I know one thing."

Edmond did not answer. Instead he watched the Emperor with the posture of a listener.

"You were not simply a person who survived." Erion continued.

Sern's gaze shifted for just an instant. Seorin did not lower hers either.

The Emperor continued.

"The Empire is accustomed to holding people for a long time. But it does not do so for just anyone. That they could neither kill nor release means there was reason enough."

Edmond was silent for a while.

Then answered, very slowly.

"The Empire has always preferred severing and keeping useful minds over killing them."

Luhai murmured without thinking.

"Wow…"

No one interrupted him this time.

Probably because everyone felt the line was too precise.

The Emperor looked at Edmond straight.

"Will you tell me what you said that caused that to happen?"

Edmond did not answer right away.

Instead he looked slowly across—the Emperor, Tarin, Elia, and Sion's group.

That gaze seemed to be measuring who was qualified to hear this story, and who could bear how much.

He finally said it low.

"To say everything now is still too early."

Luhai made a tiny face of but I want to know, and Aka glared immediately, silencing him again.

Edmond continued.

"But I can say one thing. What I dreamed of was not a stronger Empire."

The Emperor did not stir. He looked, instead, like someone who had been waiting for the next part of that sentence for a long time already.

Edmond's voice was low. Dry. Quiet yet strangely vivid.

"What I dreamed of was not an Empire that holds more, but an Empire that can finally see, without lies, what it has been making others bear in whose name."

Tarin's gaze wavered for just an instant.

Sion felt that sentence sounded less like political ideology and more like one fragment of an original sentence—severed too long ago and only now reconnecting.

Edmond was briefly silent.

Then, looking directly at the Emperor, added in a lower voice.

"If Your Majesty must hear, then I should begin not with what I dreamed, but with what I saw."

Tarin did not stir. Elia did not lower her eyes. Sern and Seorin wore faces that had fully turned toward waiting for the next words.

Edmond's gaze seemed to be returning not to this space, but to some point in a time very long ago.

And Sion felt then.

What had found its place in this room was not only two names.

One question that had been erased was just now beginning to reclaim its own mouth.
