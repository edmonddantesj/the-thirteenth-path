# Episode 192. Perfect Cage

Past the first gate, the corridor changed.

It was quieter and smoother, the light lower; there were no scratches on the walls and no dust on the floor. Even when Sion set his foot down, his own footsteps made no sound.

The second gate could not be seen.

The corridor widened all at once, and there was nowhere to hide. The floor was divided all the way to the end into tight squares two palms wide, and the wall surfaces on either side trembled very faintly, like breath.

When Sion put his hand to the wall it was warm, and when he took his hand away, the wall brightened in the shape of it and then slowly went out.

Sern saw it and spoke very low.

"Sorting floor."

"Not a gate?"

"Not a gate."

Sern said.

"Step in wrong and it does not close on you. You are moved somewhere else. The person, entire."

"Where?"

"Unknown. Up or down. Wherever Serakion decides."

Sion looked down at the square under his feet, not yet lit, and still did not lift his foot.

"Did the kids come through here too?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"They would have."

Sern said.

"This is where the children going up and the children going down would have been split. While they walked."

The square Han Jiwoo looked into was two palms wide, the width of one child's foot, and she didn't step on it.

Ater looked up at the top of the wall; below the ceiling, three lines of flowing light were each running in a different direction.

"The point is not to make a living path."

His voice was low.

"The point is to divide perfectly."

After a moment, he went on.

"A house divides you at the door. This place divides you as you walk."

"Where you go is decided while you are walking. You do not know it yourself."

"Did you know?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"I did not."

Ater said.

"I knew once I stood at the door. Here there is not even a door."

Seorin had been studying the guide lights on the walls and the floor for a long while when the light on the wall came down to the floor, and a square no one had stepped on lit and went out.

"Don't look for a path."

Seorin kept it short.

"Make the cage close too hard."

Sern picked it up at once and drew three lines on the record panel.

"A three-way input is needed. Two humans, one machine, one android response."

"Why three?"

Sion asked.

"There are three lines."

Sern pointed up.

"Make it count all three at once and it will try to close all three. Until it runs short of squares to close."

The last android looked at the three lines on the wall, then at where each of them pointed, one by one.

Up.
Sideways.
Down.

He didn't stand toward any of them.

Seorin cut the axes again.

"Sion, one of the old machines. Left. Ater. Up top. Jiwoo and him, front. Don't go in. Touch only the line that gets read first."

"Which one gets read first?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"The middle."

Kael said.

"Always the middle."

The middle row of floor squares he was looking at was dark, and still a little brighter than the other squares.

"Why the middle?"

Sion asked.

"Because people walk down the middle."

Kael said.

"The second recovery hand told me. Down below. Up here's the same."

When he looked at the middle row, Sion's feet wanted to go there on their own, and he made himself stand to one side.

"You?"

Seorin asked Kael.

"Outside. The closing angle."

Kael said.

"Need to see where the squares start closing from. Can't be from where people are standing."

While Seorin nodded, one of the old machines came and stood beside Sion.

"I will step first."

"Why?"

"Because I am heavy. It reads the heavy first."

"Then you get moved first."

Sion said.

"If I am moved, I will brace from wherever I am moved to. Down or up."

The optical eyes of the machine Sion faced did not waver in the slightest.

"Step with me. Half a beat behind."

The move began.

Sion stepped into a square at an ordinary pace, neither fast nor hiding. The square under his foot lit faintly, and at the second step the square beside it lit; he didn't take the third step, and waited.

Under the heavy feet of the old machine, which came in half a beat late, the squares lit darker, two of them at once, because the machine's feet were large. When the wall read the weight, the light on the wall came down that way.

Up above, Ater set both palms on the wall and took them away. The wall brightened in two hand shapes.
He moved two steps sideways and set them again. Two more.
The line at the top of the wall wavered after those hand shapes, upward, sideways.

The last android stood at the front, in the middle row, right before the first square he had not entered, at the spot just before being read, and Han Jiwoo stood beside him, one step back.

"There. No farther."

He stopped. No one had stepped on it, but the middle square lit.
To count him.

As the sorting floor's light spread thin through the whole corridor, the threads of light under the walls split three ways. The floor lines became three as well, one toward Sion, one toward Ater, one toward the middle, running in different directions.
Serakion was dividing.

Sion saw that the left guide line and the front blocking line were both trying to take the same square. It was the square right beside his foot, and the two lines stopped on that one square, overlapping.

"Now!"

Sern shouted.

The last android moved half a step onto the first square, and his optical eye and the faint lines on his face came over the square.
The square read him.

It lit.
It went out.
It lit again.

It couldn't decide what to count him as.

The sorting floor began to close.

Squares closed on Sion's side, on Ater's side and in the middle all at once, too many of them.
Closing squares struck closing squares, the light piled up, and where it overlapped it went white.

As the square under Sion's foot tried to close, the light climbed to his ankle.
He pulled his foot out.
The next square.
The next square rose too.
The old machine caught his arm and pulled him into the square it stood on, and the light stopped at the machine's ankle. The machine was too heavy; the light couldn't rise any further.

Then one passage in the middle came loose.

It was the passage leading farthest in; the squares of its floor all went out at once, and a floor that read nothing, a dark row, ran all the way to the end.

Kael saw it from the outside and spoke up.

"It's closing from the outside in. The middle's emptying."

Seorin kept her eyes on the dark row as she spoke.

"Going in."

Sion and the old machine stepped onto the dark squares at the same moment, and nothing lit under their feet; it was quiet. Sion looked down for a moment at his own feet on the dark squares, then ran, and nothing lit under his running feet either.

One square.
Two squares.
Three.

He stopped counting partway; the dark row stayed dark all the way to the wall, all the way to the end.

Ater put both palms to the wall and twisted the upper guide line once more, and the line went there. Meanwhile, at the front, Seorin struck the two units following them with the back of her blade and held them half a beat behind, and the squares they stood on, lit, read them as Serakion's own units and closed. The two units were shut inside the squares, in their own side's cage.

Han Jiwoo pulled the last android's arm.

"Now."

He lifted his foot from the first square, and the square went out without having counted him.

His shoulder axis made a sound as he came into the dark row, but the dark floor did not read that sound either. While Han Jiwoo fell in behind him, Kael came in from the outside and Ater came down from above, and the last was Sern.

The wall surfaces trembled very low, and the guide light came.
"Resuming sorting."
It was polite, but faster than before, with less space between the words.

Sion ran to the end of the dark row with that sound in his ears, and when the wall at the end folded open, he went in.

He looked back.

The threads of light on the sorting floor were still split three ways, politely dividing the empty squares, where no one stood, one at a time. The two Serakion units stood unmoving inside their lit squares.
Their own places.

Sern wrote on the record panel.

"Two."

He went on.

"Two of their units, shut in their own squares."

"Want to let them out?"

Kael didn't smile when he said it.

"They cannot be let out."

Sern said.

"Their side will not let them out. Their places have been decided."

"Decided where?"

"Unknown. It is their number."

Sern said.

"A person would go up or down. Those belong to their side, so their side will not know where to send them either."

Kael looked at the two standing motionless inside the lit squares.

"So they just stand there?"

"Until it is decided."

Han Jiwoo looked at the two, at the faceless surfaces inside the lit squares, and didn't look away. The last android looked at them too, and his optical eye rested there for a while.

One beat.
Two.

"Let's go."

Seorin said.

He looked one beat longer and turned away, and his shoulder axis made a sound. The inner corridor didn't give the sound back.
