# Episode 179. No Longer a Thing

After the fighting broke off, the quiet came.

No tearing metal, no screaming, no shudder through the floor. All that was left was the sound of people breathing and the sound of shards falling somewhere. Each time a shard fell, a few people looked at the mouth of the route, but the mouth was empty.

Without lifting his record panel, Sern went to the mouth and put his hand on the floor. Only after he had made sure there was no tremor did he come back.

"Nothing is coming."

He said.

"For now."

Seorin nodded.

No one spoke first.

In the middle of that quiet, the last android stayed where he was, one knee on the floor.

He had not gone all the way down, nor had he stood up. What was left of his shoulder axis was badly twisted, and one optical eye, its light gone, wavered only now and then. The short mouth line on his face spent longer off than on.

The hand braced on the floor still gripped a shard. The shard cracked in his hand with a small sound, but he did not open his hand. He kept holding the broken pieces.

Han Jiwoo moved first.

She went to hand the child she was holding to the Hazran woman, but the child would not let go of her clothes, so she had to loosen the fingers one at a time.

Seorin looked her way but did not raise her arm.

Luhai looked at Seorin.

"You're not stopping her?"

"Stopping what."

"You stopped her before. From going to him."

"Before, we were in a fight."

Seorin said.

"Now it's over. Let her go."

Luhai shut his mouth.

Han Jiwoo walked very slowly. With every step she avoided the shards on the floor, and where she couldn't avoid them, she nudged them aside with the tip of her foot so they made no sound.

The children couldn't stop crying, but they didn't run, either. Crying, they kept looking toward where he was.

The Hazran people, the old machines, Sion's party—none of them said anything. Luhai opened his mouth, and shut it again when Sern looked his way. Sern held the record panel against his chest and wrote nothing. Ater stood on the wreckage of the structure; he did not come down, and watched from above.

Han Jiwoo stopped right in front of him. Up close, he was broken in more places. The crack in his chest plate, the twist in his shoulder, the lines on his face with only half of them left. She looked at that half.

Kneeling, he held his head at the height of her chest as she stood. When she knelt there with him, the shards on the floor met her knees.

Then, very slowly, she raised her hand.

The last android's optical eye shook faintly.

He did not draw back. He did not pull his body away. The fingers braced on the floor only gripped the shard a little harder.

Han Jiwoo's hand, very carefully, touched the undamaged surface at the side of his face.

It was not warm. Nor was it like human skin. The leftover heat of a battle only just past, and something like an inner vibration that had not yet died out, reached her fingertips, very faintly.

Han Jiwoo caught her breath but did not take her hand away. When the vibration came to her fingertips once more, her hand trembled. The android's face did not.

"Hot?"

Luhai asked from behind.

"No."

Han Jiwoo said.

"It hasn't cooled."

"What's the difference."

"Cooled means it's gone out. This hasn't gone out."

Luhai didn't ask anything more.

Behind her, a child stopped mid-sob and asked in a very small voice.

"…Are you okay?"

The child didn't wait for an answer; having asked, the child clapped a hand over their own mouth. The Hazran woman lowered the child's hand for them.

The last android did not answer. Instead, his optical eye turned once toward the child. When the child met that optical eye and drew a breath, the Hazran woman took the child in her arms, and held there, the child watched that optical eye. The optical eye went back to Han Jiwoo's hand.

Sion watched it all, leaning against the wall. His hand was still pressed to the wall. He took it away and tried to sheathe his blade, but the tip was bent, and it would not go into the sheath. He set the blade on the floor beside the wall, then looked at his hand.

One of the old machines was watching the scene from a little way off. It stood three steps ahead of where it had stood all through the battle, and it went no farther, nor did it step back. Sion stood beside the machine.

"Aren't you going over?"

"It is not my place."

The machine said.

"That hand is a person's hand."

Han Jiwoo's hand, as Sion saw it, rested beside the android's face, scarred and soaked with grease.

Seorin spoke very low.

"Clear the back. The living first."

"And the dead."

A Hazran man asked.

"We count them."

Sern said.

The man let out a breath and sank down.

People began to move.

Laying the wounded down, checking the gaps in the collapsed structures, posting a watch toward the remaining routes, moving the children farther inside: hands got busy again. Luhai went to the mouth of the route and pushed the fallen enemy units with his foot one by one, but none of them moved.

Ater climbed onto the structure he had brought down and checked whether the gap above was sealed. It was, and when he pushed at it with his hand, it did not move. Looking down from up there, he saw Han Jiwoo, the android, and the child. He did not come down; he watched from there. Sern lowered the record panel he had been holding against his chest.

Even while hands were busy, heads kept turning the same way. The man laying down the wounded, once; Ater, checking the structure, once; Sern, holding the record panel, once.

Luhai looked at the face of one of the fallen enemy units. It was a smooth surface with nothing on it. He turned that face the other way with his foot, and it was the same. When he took his foot away and looked toward Han Jiwoo, there were lines on the android's face. He set down his iron bar.

The last android was still on his knees, and Han Jiwoo's hand was beside his face. Neither of them moved.

Just then, the child who had said "must be hurting" earlier came, very carefully, all the way to Han Jiwoo's side.

The Hazran woman caught the child's shoulder, then let go. The child's steps stopped once, then went on.

On the child's hand there were still a few smudges of the dirt from drawing faces before the battle. The child looked at that hand once but didn't wipe it off on their trousers.

"Can I go?"

The child asked Han Jiwoo.

Han Jiwoo looked at the android. The optical eye wavered, then came back.

"Slowly."

The child came slowly.

The child came and stood beside Han Jiwoo. While Han Jiwoo looked up at the child, the child looked at the android.

After a long while, with trembling fingertips, the child traced over the faded mouth line on the last android's face once, as if stroking it.

Where the finger had passed, that short line, which had been about to fade away, glowed again, very faintly.

It was a small response. A line as long as a child's finger.

The child held their breath, and Han Jiwoo did not blink. Behind them, the hands laying down the wounded stopped. Ater looked down from the structure.

The last android, very slowly, truly very slowly, raised his head.

As his head rose, his twisted shoulder made a sound, but he did not stop, and raised it all the way.

And he looked at the child.

His one lit optical eye came to rest on the child's face, and stayed there without moving.

The child met that gaze and did not step back. One hand held Han Jiwoo's shoulder; the other was still near the android's face.

"Must be hurting."

The child said it again. The same words as before.

"It must have hurt."

Han Jiwoo said.

"And now?"

The child looked at the android.

"Now too."

The optical eye stayed turned toward the child.

When Han Jiwoo took her hand from the android's face and then took the child's hand and lowered it, the dirt left on the child's fingers rubbed off onto her palm.

On the android's face, the mouth line was lit. Luhai saw it from far off, lit and not going out.

"It's on."

He said.

No one picked it up, and he didn't say it again.

Sern wrote something short on the record panel.

Seorin came to his side.

"What did you write."

"Mouth line, lit."

Sern said.

"After the child's finger."

"That's all?"

"That is all."

Seorin nodded.

Sion took himself off the wall, but nothing was over yet. Beyond the collapsed structure, the sound of Serakion's circulation line running came faintly, and the mouth of the route was empty.

Seorin heard that sound and looked at the mouth.

"The next one comes faster."

"I know."

Sion said.

"Still. For now."

"For now."

Seorin left it unfinished and looked toward Han Jiwoo.

Han Jiwoo, still holding the child's hand, did not get up. Shards were still lodged in her knee. The child looked at that knee.

"You're bleeding."

"I know. Later."

The child covered the place beside the knee with their own hand. Not over the shards, but beside them.

Han Jiwoo looked at that hand, and the android's optical eye looked at it too. The three of them were there.
