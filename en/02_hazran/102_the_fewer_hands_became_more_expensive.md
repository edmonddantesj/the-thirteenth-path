# Episode 102. The Fewer Hands Became More Expensive

Sion was the first to take water to the corridor.

Until now there had been other hands for carrying water. Those hands had gone up with the fifth line a while ago.

"Water."

Sern didn't look back.

"Leave it and go."

"I'll hold it."

"I said leave it and go."

"If I put it down, you can't drink it."

Only then did Sern glance at him, briefly.

Sion was right. Putting it down meant pulling out of position to pick it up, and pulling out left the place empty. Here, right now, the time a place stood empty was not time for a drink of water. It was time for the corridor to open.

"Hold it, then."

"Okay."

Sion carried the bowl up to Sern's shoulder. Sern turned only his head and drank. Two sips, and he stopped.

"Have more."

"Will you be coming again?"

"Yes."

"Then I will leave some."

Sion knew what that answer meant. This man wasn't saving water. He was leaving Sion a reason to come back.

He went to Ater too. Ater took one sip and turned his head away.

"You should not be here."

"I know."

"You know, and yet you are here?"

"There's no one else to carry it."

Ater said nothing more to that.

From where he stood, Sion looked at what he could see.

The width the two of them stood across had grown. They had to cover as far as Harun used to hold. Sern high, Ater low. But both of them looked back now and then. Toward the pile. If the other side tried to break through the pile, that had to be stopped too.

Three places to hold, and two hands.

"How many?"
Sern asked.

Sion didn't follow right away.

"The person underneath. The interval between the knocks."

Sion started to answer and stopped.

The last one he'd heard was twelve. Before that it was eleven, and before that, ten.

"Why are you asking?"

"I ask because I need to know."

"What changes if you know?"

Sern answered that question at once.

"I know how fast I have to be."

Hearing that, Sion looked toward Ater.

Ater spoke without taking his eyes off his angle.

"Do not tell him."

"Why?"

"If he hears it, his hands will speed up."

"Isn't faster good?"

"Here, hands that speed up lose the next beat."

Sern cut him off.

"I am already losing it."

"I know."

"Then let him speak."

For the first time, the two of them looked at each other. One beat. For that one beat, nobody was watching the angles.

That frightened Sion, so he spoke first.

"Twelve."

Sern looked ahead again.

"And before."

"Eleven."

"Before that."

"Ten."

Sern took the three numbers and said nothing. Instead, the next time he went in, he went in half a beat early.

Ater saw it.

"That was early."

"I know."

Then a sound came from the pile.

The people digging from the top had all stopped.

"Get down!"

Someone shouted it. Stones were moving at the bottom of the pile. Nothing was pushing from above. They were being pushed out from inside.

"They are digging too,"
Sern said.

"To get him out?"

"No."

Ater answered for him.

"Right now the fastest way inside is that pile."

Only then did Sion see it.

The twelve were digging the same pile from above, and those things were digging it from below. The direction was opposite; the work was the same. Once it was all cleared, the person underneath would come out, and at the same moment the corridor would open.

Ater put two short rounds into the bottom of it. What had been pushing out stopped.

"I cannot hold it for long."

"Why?"

"Those things do not tire."

Sion looked at the people digging from above. The backs of their hands were all scraped raw. They had been digging all this while.

So the question wasn't who would tire first. It was which side would get through first.

Sion stood holding the water bowl.

For a while after that, neither of them spoke. Ater gave only three words—high, low, behind—and Sern moved every time. The gaps between the three words were shorter than when Sion had come here before.

Sion looked at Ater's arm.

"Your sleeve."

"Yes."

"It's torn."

Ater did not look at his own arm.

"Is it catching?"

"I think it will."

"Then tie it, please."

Sion set the water bowl on the ground and took the end of Ater's sleeve. He folded it twice and tucked it inside. The whole time, Ater never once took his eyes off his angle.

"Done."

"You will not check it?"

"I did."

"Then it is done."

As he let go, Sion understood. Right now this man couldn't see even his own sleeve. He had two eyes to see with, and both were somewhere else.

A long while later, Ater spoke to Sion.

"From now on."

"Yes."

"Tell me as well."

"You told me not to say anything."

"The problem is when only one person knows."

Then, for the first time, Ater added a short explanation.

"If he knows it is twelve and I do not, I do not know why he goes in early. If I do not know, I am late."

"Then why did you stop me before?"

"It would have been better if he had not known,"
Ater said.

"Now that he knows, we both have to know."

Sion caught that the sentence had just turned itself over.

Hiding was one way. Sharing was another. The moment the first was no longer possible, this man went straight to the second.

"I'll tell you the second it hits thirteen."

"Not thirteen."

"What?"

"Tell me every time it goes up by one. Tell me even if it does not."

"Even if it doesn't?"

"Not going up is information too."

"What about the water?"

For a moment Ater did not take that question.

"Next time, bring only one."

"Not two?"

"If you bring two, you will have to stand here twice."

Sion knew what that meant. If he stood here long, he would become one more hand to be counted.

"Then whose do I bring?"

"In turns."

Sion backed out with the water bowl.

On the way out, he stopped once.

"Sern."

"Yes."

"You only had two sips before."

"Yes."

"Drink now."

Sern didn't look back.

"It is not coming yet."

"That's why I'm saying drink now."

Sern waited a moment, then turned his head. Sion raised the bowl. This time he drank three sips.

"That is enough."

"There's some left."

"That is for the other one."

Sion took the water bowl to Ater. Ater didn't drink.

"I drank earlier."

"One sip."

"One sip is enough."

Sion lowered the bowl again. The water left in it was still there.

Each of them was pushing his own share toward the other. They hadn't said a word to each other, and it came out the same.

On his way out he looked back at the corridor once. Two people were standing for three, behind them twelve were digging from the top down, and nobody was looking at anybody.

He went toward the hull.

Han Jiwoo had the back of her hand against the ramp's joint. The same posture as before, but a different face.

"What's wrong?"

"The sound changed."

"Worse?"

"No."

Han Jiwoo lifted the back of her hand and set it down again.

"It's gotten quieter."

"Isn't that good?"

"It's quiet because there are fewer people."

Sion understood.

Twenty went out each time, same as before, but the mass pressing in at once had gotten smaller. That came of cutting the line into threes. For the ship, it had gotten easier.

"Then the ship will last longer."

"Yeah."

"Then why that face?"

Han Jiwoo was quiet a moment before she answered.

"Because the easier it gets for the ship, the finer the people get cut."

"How much longer will the ship last?"

"Don't know."

"You don't know?"

"There's no way to know. Nobody's ever done this with a ship like this."

Strangely, that answer comforted Sion. Nobody knowing also meant it hadn't been decided yet.

"But I know one thing,"
Han Jiwoo said.

"What?"

"If I let go, it definitely won't."

Then she lowered her hand.

"Go load them."

Sion went to the line.

"Three."

Six groups of three. Two at the end.

"Twenty."

The ramp folded. The ship lifted. Quieter than before.

Sion went to the board. The marks had become seven.

Eighty-eight, minus twenty. Sixty-eight.

Four trips.

Sion looked at the number once more.

When he first started counting, he couldn't see the end. Now he could.

It meant that within the four trips left, the person down there would either come out or not—one or the other.

Beside it were two short marks. Sion drew the third.

Then he went to Aka.

"How many?"

"Twelve."

Hearing that, Sion ran toward the corridor.

He'd been told to tell even when it didn't go up.
