# Episode 35. The Hand That Counted Them

In an instant the market went from air that swallowed a name to air where everyone was watching who had run off with the real thing.

The old cargo container went all the way over on its side, and the metal pieces inside sprayed in every direction. Even the people who had been haggling a moment ago stepped back a pace on instinct, and through that gap a small body slid, almost gliding, into the alley farther in. Even the way it ran was practiced. Not running so much as a body moving that already knew where to put its feet so as not to get caught.

The two chasing it were the exact opposite.

One was the merchant who had just been laying out fake Etherite, and the other was a man a size bigger than him and quicker to anger. Neither was just making noise; this time they both had the faces of men who had lost hold of something they truly could not afford to lose.

"Grab him!"
the merchant shouted, his voice cracking.
"That's mine!"

"Yours, my foot,"
someone spat from the side, half a sneer.
"You laid it out without even knowing it was real."

That one remark turned the air of the market's outer layer over even more roughly.

From that short exchange alone, Sion could tell that what the small runner had in its hand was not just something that would fetch money. A real one mixed in with the fakes. No one had checked it openly, but a few had already caught on, and that was why the commotion was growing.

And Kael moved first.

His body was faster than any word.

He did not go for the small runner; instead he turned first toward the bigger man charging in from behind to pile right onto it. He twisted the angle and drove his shoulder in first. For an instant rough breath and curses tangled together; the two of them went down close to the ground together and then bounced apart again.

Sion watched the movement and understood it half a beat late.

That was not the reaction of someone trying to snatch the goods.
It was the reaction of someone putting his own body in the way before somebody got smashed up worse.

Seorin spoke briefly.

"Sion."

That one word was enough.

Sion turned at once toward the alley too. Ater went after him. Even while running, Ater swept his eyes over the metal pieces scattered on the ground and the response in the dust. If what the runner had in hand was real Etherite, at the very least its grain of response might differ from dead iron powder.

The child running away was fast, but not perfect.

The alleys of the market's outer layer were complicated for someone running them for the first time, but it was the kind of complicated that tripped up anyone in too much of a hurry. The child cornered well, as if this had happened a few times already, but was hugging something on the inside of the right hand, and the balance lagged, very slightly, each time. Sion did not miss that small drag.

"Right side's blocked!"
he shouted.

The child cut left at once without even looking back.

That reaction gave its position away even more.

The moment Sion picked up speed, two sheets of filter cloth suddenly dropped in front of him. The child had torn them down on purpose. The trick to block the view was quick. Sion tried to push straight through the cloth and lost half a beat, and right beside him Ater swept the edge of the cloth aside.

"Lower left."
Ater said it through the cloth.

The child cut away again.

This time the child slid down, almost slipping, into a narrow, sunken gap like an old drainage channel. Sion was about to follow it all the way in and stopped for an instant. A person could get through, but anyone big would get stuck at that angle right away.

And before that, another hand shot out.

It was Kael.

Whenever it was that he had shaken off the man behind, he had already gone wide around the line of shadow below and gotten in first on the side where the child would come out. The movement was more practiced than fast. The movement of someone who knew how people who survived on the market floor rode the gaps instead of the paths.

The moment the child straightened up on that side, Kael's hand caught the wrist first.

To be exact, he did not twist the wrist; he killed, once, the direction it was trying to run in.

Without even time to be startled, the child swung the other hand. The tip of the metal piece in that hand caught the sunlight briefly. It wasn't large. But there was clearly a response. A heavy kind of light that plain scrap would never give off.

The moment Kael saw it, the look in his eyes changed very slightly.

Only when Sion saw that face was he certain.

It was real.

At least it was not completely dead.

At that moment, from behind, the merchant and the big man caught up again, pouring out curses. People on the upper level of the alley began to stick their heads out; somewhere far off someone laughed, and someone else called out a price. In no time the outer market of Hazran was turning into air where onlookers, buyers and hands looking to snatch it were all mixed together.

Seorin arrived last, and yet she was the first to take hold of the situation.

"Empty your hand first."
She said it to the child, low.

Breathing hard, the child glared at Kael. It was a face that looked younger than sixteen. Thin, but with eyes that were oddly quick. Not a frightened look; a face measuring, at the same time, how far it could hold out and where it had to give up.

"I didn't steal it."
The child spoke first.
"It landed in my hand first."

"Nice,"
Seorin said, low.
"A Hazran kid."

At that, the child's face screwed up at once.

"I'm not a kid."

Kael, still not letting go of the wrist, asked briefly.

"Name."

The child kept quiet for a moment. It was the silence of someone weighing whether to pick a lie first or look for an angle to bolt again. But the merchant was already running up behind, shouting, and every eye in the market's outer layer had swung this way. It seemed the child had been quick to work out that refusing even a name here might get them tied up worse.

"Luhai."
The child spat it out.

Sion listened once to the way the name sat in the mouth.

Luhai.

It was a name with a touch of green on it. A different grain from the market dust, and yet the kind of name you could see someone needing, a quick-mouthed name like that, to survive long here.

The merchant came almost nose to nose and shouted.

"Let go of that hand. I caught that thief first."

Seorin asked without even turning around.

"Is it yours?"

The merchant couldn't answer right away.

That short pause was enough.

Kael spoke without loosening his grip.

"You mixed it in with the fakes and were only trying to push the price up."

The merchant glared.

"What do you know about it?"

This time Ater spoke.

"Its response differs from dead metal,"
he said, low.
"At the very least, that piece is not from the same batch as the things laid out there."

The moment those words fell, the air around them changed once again.

No one said *real* first, but it was the air of everyone starting to think the same thing. In Hazran, that kind of moment was the most dangerous. The moment when something still belonged to no one, and at the same time everyone started putting a price on it.

Luhai seemed to read that change faster than anyone. Even held in Kael's hand, his eyes kept measuring the side path, the frame overhead, people's hands. He was scared, yes, but he was a kid whose arithmetic ran first even while he was scared.

Sion asked, low.

"Did you grab it knowing it was real?"

Luhai didn't answer right away. Instead he ran his eyes over Sion's face, the gear in Ater's hand, Seorin's eyes and, last, Kael's hand.

"Half,"
he said, flatly.
"No. Two-thirds."

"What kind of answer is that?"
Sion said.

"It was playing dead too hard among the fakes,"
Luhai said, catching his breath.
"Then it's usually one of two things. It's real, or somebody hid it there on purpose."

It sounded like bragging, but it didn't sound like complete nonsense either.

Even after hearing that answer, Kael did not let go. He only eased his grip a little from before. The change was so slight that only Sion could see it, but it was clear. It meant Kael, too, was not seeing this kid as just a petty thief.

Right then, the air outside the alley began, strangely, to settle into order.

It wasn't that the noise had dropped. Words still went back and forth. But the laughter, the curses and the calling of prices were each coming half a beat late.

The merchant who had been chasing the kid was still cursing. When Sion looked, only his mouth was doing it; his eyes were on the far end of the alley.

A moment ago these people had been knocking over containers and yelling over one thief. That was a few minutes ago.

Seorin read the change first.

"Good,"
she said, dropping her voice.
"Now it's reached their ears."

Sion didn't bother asking.

He thought he already knew whose ears.

A real piece of Etherite,
a young hand called Luhai,
outsiders who had said the name Aka out loud,
and a commotion that had broken out in the middle of the market's outer layer.

With all that, this was no longer going to end in the outer layer.

The merchant seemed to feel it too; he couldn't raise his voice as loud as before. The big man stepped back a pace outright, and the people who had been sticking their heads out above the alley began, all too naturally, to pretend they hadn't seen.

Seeing that change, Luhai's face only got worse.

"Ah, damn,"
he muttered, very quietly.

Seorin asked right away.

"Why?"

Instead of answering, Luhai glanced once toward Kael's hand.

"It's too late now,"
he said, low.
"This doesn't end with just getting caught."

The moment he finished, from beyond the shadow at the mouth of the alley, someone began to walk slowly in.

The noise of the market's outer layer had not fully stopped, and yet the way cleared on that side first, a strange kind of order.

Someone was coming.

Not a hand coming to watch the commotion, but a hand coming to count, in person, who had wrongly touched a price.
