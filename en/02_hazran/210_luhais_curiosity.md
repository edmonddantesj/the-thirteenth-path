# Episode 210. Luhai's Curiosity

The decision to go to the Empire was made faster than expected.

Being decided did not mean they could leave immediately, though.

Inside the ship, a direction came first. Then a very short, uncomfortable silence.

Everyone understood where they had to go. But how—no one said right away.

Luhai endured the silence for about two breaths, then opened his mouth first.

"But how do we get to the Empire?"

No one answered right away.

Luhai narrowed his eyes and looked around the ship once.

"Come to think of it, how did we get from Hazran to Serakion last time?"

Seorin moved one eyebrow very faintly. Kael wore the face of someone briefly considering whether the question was worth answering. Jiwoo was pretending not to hear, looking only toward the window.

Luhai was earnest, alone.

"Do we all have to think the same thing at the same time?"

This time even Sion looked his way.

Luhai was already adjusting his posture.

"Okay. On three, everyone thinks about the Empire together."

Aka recoiled immediately.

"Why would we do that."

"You never know. Last time we got there in some weird way too."

That was not entirely wrong, so no one could immediately argue back.

Luhai raised his hand, excited.

"Here we go. One, two, three."

Nothing happened.

Luhai blinked.

"Huh?"

Sern sighed very small, eyes lowered. Ater turned his face away entirely.

Luhai quickly regrouped.

"Then maybe this? Do we have to shout?"

Aka, already tired, asked.

"Shout what."

Luhai said it seriously.

"Empire."

A brief silence.

Then Luhai shouted louder.

"EMPIRE!"

The ship did not respond.

Luhai pouted.

"That is not it either."

He looked at Aka beside him.

"Aka. You know, right? How to make the ship move."

Aka looked at Luhai as though he had lost his mind.

"Why would I know."

"Just a feeling. You seem like you would know this kind of thing."

"I do not."

"Really?"

"Really."

Luhai looked deeply disappointed.

Next he looked at Serakion.

Serakion met Luhai's gaze silently.

Next was Jiwoo.

"Boss."

Jiwoo's expression was already not good.

"What."

"Do you know?"

"Know what."

"The ship. How to get to the Empire."

Jiwoo looked at Luhai for a long moment, then said it very low.

"Come here so I can hit you."

Luhai immediately backed away.

"Um. That will not be necessary."

Sion could not hold back a laugh and briefly lowered his head.

Luhai saw it and looked wronged.

"What? If nobody knows, we should all brainstorm together."

Kael finally spoke.

"Your brainstorming method is the problem."

"It is unusual, sure. But it is rational."

Seorin cut in.

"Not at all."

Luhai thought briefly, then slapped his knee again.

"Ah. Then maybe we all have to jump?"

This time no one responded.

Luhai took the silence as agreement.

"I knew it."

Aka let out a hollow laugh.

"What do you mean, you knew it."

"Everyone get ready. On three, we go 'hup.' One, two, three."

Sern raised his head for the first time.

"Please do not."

Luhai said it with full confidence.

"Too late. One, two, three."

And shouted alone.

"HUP!"

Nothing happened.

This time Sion laughed too, and even the corner of Ater's mouth moved, barely.

Luhai looked genuinely hurt.

"Why did you all think together with me before but not this time?"

Aka asked, incredulous.

"When did we ever do it with you."

"The vibe was there."

"That was your imagination."

Luhai crossed his arms and muttered.

"…Embarrassed? Me?"

Seorin cut in.

"Yes."

"Tch…"

Luhai pouted briefly, then started muttering to himself again.

"Then what do we do… Empire, we are coming to save you, so wait up. Maybe if I say that…"

That was the moment.

From somewhere deep inside the ship, a very low vibration sounded first.

Every gaze stopped simultaneously.

Kael rose immediately. Jiwoo raised her head. Ater reflexively looked toward the wall signals. Sern wore the face of someone trying to read the vibration's cycle first.

Only Luhai stood with wide eyes.

"Huh?"

Beneath the ship's floor, a tremor spread as though long-sleeping lines were slowly coming alive.

Once. And again.

From somewhere in a power line that had lain quiet all this while, the sound of something very faintly reconnecting.

Aka was the first to swallow her breath.

"Wait…?"

Seorin said it short.

"Hold on."

Kael was already moving toward the panel.

"Who touched something?"

"Not me!" Luhai said, almost indignant. "Well, I mean technically what I just said might have been it, but surely it was not actually because of that?"

Jiwoo was already reading the cockpit-side responses.

"Response lines are coming alive."

Ater's gaze wavered for just an instant.

Serakion raised his head too.

Standing silently until now, he was very quietly listening to the stirring that had just begun inside the ship.

Luhai looked at everyone in turn and asked carefully.

"…Was it really me?"

Sion finally could not hold back his laugh.

"Just stay still for a moment."

"But what did I even do?"

Kael murmured low, looking at the panel.

"We need to figure out which word triggered it first."

Luhai still looked dazed.

Just moments ago he had been the person talking alone, jumping alone, shouting "hup" alone. And now the ship was acting as though it understood him.

He straightened his posture for no real reason and cleared his throat, very small.

"…Ahem."

Aka fired immediately.

"Do not get smug."

"I did not."

"You just did."

"Maybe a little."

At that, even Jiwoo laughed.

The ship departed like that.

Which of Luhai's words exactly it had responded to, whether he had truly been the key, or whether something unknown to everyone had simply awakened at that timing—Luhai himself would probably never know.

After some time under way, as the outer approach line drew near, a short station flickered past below the side viewport.

Less a station and more a small approval waiting post attached to the Empire's outer perimeter.

The light was bright. The line was short. People stood quietly.

Sion would normally have passed the scene without notice, but it caught his eye strangely.

The man at the front of the line had produced something wrong.

Whether it was a credential, a permit document, or the sequence itself—from this distance, it was not visible.

What was visible was that he paused for just an instant. And in the next moment, two people standing beside the waiting post removed him from the line with too-familiar hands.

Not rough. No sound. The people around did not turn their heads.

The man did not protest. No—there was no gap in which to protest.

His wrist had not even been gripped hard, yet he simply vanished through the side door, like someone who had already lost his turn.

And the line was pulled one space forward, as though nothing had happened.

Aka looked toward the window first.

"Did you see that?"

No one answered right away.

Sern said it very low.

"That is the Empire."

A short line.

But Sion felt it sounded colder than any explanation he had heard before.

Who did something wrong did not matter. Who vanished would not be remembered long. What mattered was only that the line was tidy again.

The ship moved further inside.

And Sion understood then. The place they were heading toward was not simply the enemy's center—it was a place where, when one person disappears, the order finds its place first.
