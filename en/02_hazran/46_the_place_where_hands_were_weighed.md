# Episode 46. The Place Where Hands Were Weighed

After Zahir's "Get ready" fell, the inner space moved quickly again, but nothing about it was scrambled.

Sion felt that was the most unpleasant thing about Hazran's inside. The outer market was loud and scattered, so at least you could see the gaps there; here, everything moved already in order. Who carried what and where, at which moment which words had to stop, who went ahead and who stayed behind. They all moved like people who had done this many times. Which meant the "board" Zahir had spoken of was most likely no improvised game, but the way this place had always been run.

Harun went out first. Nasim fell in a little after him, and he looked like a man sorting in his head the words to leak outside from the words to keep inside. Nahira stayed by Aka. It wasn't the shape of holding her and pulling her along, but the distance itself was already an inside rule. Keep her close, but not where any hand could reach her carelessly.

Luhai ended up moving without handing over the Etherite. Instead, one of the inside people stuck close to his wrist. Not entrusted, and not wholly kept either. Sion could tell the ambiguity had been made on purpose. Hazran knew better how to let you keep holding a thing while making sure you couldn't run than how to take it from you outright.

Kael, too, was steered off separately, the fragment still held against him. He had been split off openly, but not yet stripped of it entirely. If Aka was right, the two had to be kept far apart. So right now they were drawing Kael's fragment and Luhai's Etherite onto different lines, further and further, even inside the same field of view.

Sion, Seorin and Ater moved last.

It felt less as if the three of them had heard less of the explanation, and more as if they were the ones who would now hear the rules firsthand.

As they came out of the inner passage, the heat grew stronger again.

But it was not the outer market's heat. This was not heat warmed by people crowding together; it was the day's heat, long since soaked into the ground here, the iron plates, the tent ropes, the hanging metal frames, slowly rising back up. Heat that had not cooled though the night was deep. It felt like proof that all of Hazran was still awake.

Nasim spoke lightly beside them.

"Lucky."

That nearly grated on Sion again, but this time Ater asked before Seorin could.

"In what way?"

Nasim smiled.

"Tonight lined up just right,"
he said.
"The rumor from outside, the fragment that kid picked up, the name you lot brought in."

Sion asked quietly.

"So something was going to open tonight anyway."

Nasim answered with his eyes kept ahead.

"Something's always opening in Hazran,"
he said.
"But not all of it becomes a board."

That was a yes.

Tonight had always been a night when eyes gathered from as far as the outside, and Zahir had set them on top of it.

Seorin muttered, very low.

"He didn't fit it to us. He's throwing us into a board that was already there."

"It comes cheaper that way, doesn't it,"
Nasim answered smoothly.

Sion hated how naturally that came out. A place where a few people, one name, one ship were handled as nothing more than prices that sat nicely on a board. But at the same time, maybe that was exactly why they had been able to get this far in so suddenly. If Zahir were a man who built the whole board fresh, it would have taken longer. This was closer to a stage that had already been set, with a few unexpected real things added on top.

As they rounded the end of the passage, the view opened wide.

It was a broad courtyard, and the desert night sky felt as if it had come down to just overhead. Half-collapsed hull walls, metal walkways and pillars driven into the sand made a ring, and between them tents, railings and things like makeshift stands clung in tiers. It was far wider than the outer market and yet more orderly; it should have been louder, and yet the noise was strangely held down. The people gathered here had the faces of those who had come to watch, not to chatter.

The moment Sion saw the place, he missed Han Jiwoo again. With a structure like that, a single sweep would surely have shown her every way in, every way out and every line of danger.

Ater was already reading the heights and how the walkways joined.

Seorin was looking at how the people were placed before she listened to the sound.

Watching the two of them, Sion found a sentence rising in his mind without meaning to.

This isn't a place for watching fights. It's a place where hands are weighed.

Which hand catches the real thing,
which hand lets it slip,
which hand holds out to the end.

Harun stopped up ahead.

At the sight of him, several pairs of eyes around them dropped at once. Less respect than a kind of wariness: let's not make trouble. When Nasim stood beside him, the air around them, by contrast, loosened a little. That man was less a hand that enforced the rules than a mouth that translated them to suit people's tongues.

Luhai slowed without meaning to, then matched his pace again to the watching hand at his side. Kael was being steered along a different line. Not very far, but far enough that they could no longer reach each other by sound alone.

Aka moved with Nahira toward the side nearest the innermost shade.

Just for that moment, Sion thought he might understand a little why Zahir did not readily put Aka up in the middle of the board. That child was closer to a blade than to a symbol. Show her too fast, too openly, and the board itself might not roll the way he wanted.

Then someone stepped up onto a low metal plate in the middle of the courtyard.

Sion had never seen the face, but he knew at once it was a Hazran man. Someone who knew how to draw attention without raising his voice. The bearing of a man who had stood in this spot a long time, calling out prices and names and stakes.

When he opened his mouth, the murmuring around him settled on its own.

"Tonight was supposed to be three lots,"
he said.
"But the night got greedy."

A few people nearby laughed low.

That short reaction alone told Sion enough. It wasn't an off-the-cuff line; it was closer to an opening phrase the people of this floor were used to taking in.

"We've got two hands that came in from outside,"
the man went on,
"one real thing picked up from inside, and a rumor with a name's worth on it has slipped in too."

He openly ran his eyes over Sion's party and over Luhai.

"So tonight even the watching eyes had better pay their price properly."

Sion's brow drew in, very slightly.

The watching eyes pay a price too.
That meant the people gathered here weren't mere onlookers either. This wasn't a place that only watched who won and who lost; it was a place where who could recognize the real thing was staked as well.

Nasim spoke, very low.

"Now you see,"
he added with a smile,
"in Hazran even watching isn't free."

This time Sion didn't answer.

He was already seeing with his own eyes that it was true.

Hands were moving over by the stands.

What changed hands wasn't money. Scraps of metal, bundles of cloth, small capped canisters. What counted as a price in Hazran differed from one person to the next, and so each of them had something to stake.

Someone glanced once toward Sion, then passed something to the person beside them.

Sion knew what that meant.

A price had been put on him.

He didn't know how much. It was a number they knew and he didn't. Here it was always like that. The one being priced is the last to learn the price.

Seorin saw it and spoke very low.

"You went on cheap, I bet."

"How can you tell?"

"Big bets make the hand shake,"
Seorin said.
"That one isn't shaking."

Sion couldn't tell whether that was meant as comfort or not.

Harun spoke without turning around.

"Names get called soon."

"Whose name,"
Sion asked.

"Whoever pays the price of their own hand first,"
Harun answered shortly.

That might mean a person's name, or a thing's name, or both.

Sion looked toward the stands once more.

The one who had put a price on him earlier was looking somewhere else now. Whatever had been in their hand had already gone to the person beside them.

Once a price is put on you, it wanders off on its own. No matter what the one it's on does.

Sion had no way of knowing how far that price had traveled by now.
