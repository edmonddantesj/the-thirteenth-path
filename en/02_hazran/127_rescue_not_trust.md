# Episode 127. Rescue, Not Trust

For a very short while, there was room to catch their breath.

While the Hazran refugees leaned against the walls, the floor and the gaps between the old devices, getting their breath back, other beings came out of the inner passages, one and then another.

They were machines.

The marks of repair showed plainly, and parts that had been replaced were never matched for color, so the colors differed. One machine's left arm was shorter than its right; another had one leg of a different make, so when it walked, that side touched down first.

The way those machines looked at humans was not warm. They stopped and only looked; they did not come close.

When a Hazran child shrank back in fright and hid, an old machine nearby stopped short. It did not come closer and did not try to soothe; from where it stood at a distance, it took one step back. From behind an adult's legs, the child watched the machine. The machine did not move back any further, nor did it come any closer.

Sion looked at the distance between them. Three steps. The machine had set it.

The child came one step out from behind the adult's legs. The machine did not move.
The child came out one step more. The machine moved back half a step.
The child stopped. Between them it was three steps again.

The child looked back, and the adult nodded. The child went no farther, but crouched down on the spot and watched the machine. The machine stayed where it stood and watched the child.

A little later, the machine that had first opened the way spoke low.

"We will move farther in. It is hard to hold out here for long."

Luhai asked right away.

"Whose side are you even on?"

The machine answered without missing a beat.

"The side trying to keep things from coming apart right now."

Luhai opened his mouth and closed it again; meanwhile Seorin, who had heard, looked at the machine, but the machine had already turned away.

Hearing that, Aka lifted her head, just slightly.

"So that's why this place hasn't gone under yet."

The machine looked at Aka but did not answer right away. Its small optical eye blinked once, and then it turned away.

Farther in, they could see partitions pieced together for the time being, worn cloth, supplies gathered in a hurry. Crates were stacked along the wall, and each crate bore not writing but scratched marks. One, two, three.

Sion looked at the marks and pressed his pocket. Meanwhile Sern copied the marks on the crates onto his record panel.

"What are you writing?"

Luhai asked.

"A way of counting."

Sern answered.

"These people scratch lines too."

Luhai looked at the marks on a crate. Three. The next crate, four. He tried folding down his own fingers, but stopped partway.

"What were they counting?"

"I do not know."

Sern answered.

"This is not the time to ask."

Where the humans had stayed, cloth was spread out; where the machines had stayed, tools and disassembled parts lay. Between the two ran a passage wide enough for one person, and nothing lay across it.

Han Jiwoo looked at that passage and then at the tools. Two of them were the same shape as the ones used on Hazran, and one had its handle wrapped in cloth to fit a human hand. A human-hand wrap on a tool the machines used. She did not pick it up, but looked at it for a long time.

Luhai asked from beside her.

"Who uses that?"

"Don't know. Looks like both do."

Han Jiwoo did not take her eyes off the cloth-wrapped handle.

A human woman sitting near one of the walls set her face hard the moment she saw Sion's party; it was the machines behind them that had made it harden.

Her face was tired, with deep shadows under the eyes, and her hair was tied up in cloth. She had a half-stripped part on her knees, and her hand did not leave it.

"New here?"

She asked.

Sion nodded.

The woman ran her eyes over Han Jiwoo and Luhai, and over the Hazran refugees behind them too. Only after her counting eyes had counted them all did she speak, low.

"If you're alive, you're still in the confusing part."

Luhai asked right back.

"Confusing?"

The woman turned over the part on her knees.

"Who wants to use you, who wants to save you, and when those two flip."

Silence.

Hearing that, the old Hazran man pressed his pocket, and the screwdriver was there.

Han Jiwoo looked at the machines beside them. They were not looking at the woman; they were looking toward the entrance.

At that moment, the machine that had first opened the way spoke, very low.

"What she says is not wrong."

Luhai grimaced.

"Wow, that doesn't reassure me one bit."

This time Seorin spoke first.

"Probably wasn't meant to reassure you."

The machine made no reply; only its small optical eye went once toward Seorin and came back.

The woman put the part down and stood, and her knees made a sound. She went over to the Hazran people and crouched in front of a man with cloth wrapped around his leg. She undid the cloth to look, then wound it back.

"Boil water."

She said it over her shoulder to no one in particular, and from farther in came the sound of someone moving.

Kael sat down beside the man, and the woman looked at Kael.

"You brought them?"

"We came together."

The woman nodded, finished winding the cloth and tapped the man's knee twice, and the man nodded. Rising, the woman spoke to Kael.

"When the water comes, the first cup."

"Yes."

"Not you. Him."

Kael looked at the man.

"Yes."

The woman was already in front of the next person.

Ater spoke low to Sern.

"How many exits are there?"

"Three that can be seen."

Sern answered.

"The one we came in by. Two farther in."

"Which are blocked?"

"Unknown. We will not know until someone goes."

Ater looked at the two farther in. One was lit and one was dark, and he looked at the dark one longer.

"I will go and look."

"Not yet."

That was Seorin.

"We hear what they have to say first."

Ater nodded, but did not take his eyes off the dark one.

Then, from an inner passage, a far older machine came walking out, very slowly.

It looked older even than the machine that had first opened the way. Some of its joints were connected with parts of a different make, and the light in one optical eye was uneven. With every step, its knee made a sound.

One step down,
a sound,
one step down,
a sound.

When it appeared, a few of the machines nearby turned their bodies, ever so slightly. No one had told them to, yet the way they stood shifted toward the old machine.

The humans saw the movement too. The tired-faced woman's hand stopped winding cloth, and after a moment it moved again.

The moment Aka saw it, she spoke, low as a breath.

"That one's old."

Han Jiwoo, too, looked at the machine for a long time instead of answering. Below its neck there were three solder marks, where the machine that had opened the way had one. The three were marks laid one over another. Soldered once, soldered again on top of that, and again.

For a while the old machine said nothing, looking in turn at the newly arrived people of Hazran and at Sion's party.

Its optical eye with the uneven light rested on one person and then moved to the next, and how long it rested differed from person to person. It was long in front of the injured man, and long in front of the child too. Short in front of Seorin, then long again in front of Han Jiwoo. On Han Jiwoo's hands.

Han Jiwoo did not hide her hands.

When the old machine's optical eye came to Sion, Sion looked at that light too. It was uneven, like the flickering yellow lamp.

Only after a long while did a low, worn-down voice come out.

"This time… how much have you lost to get here?"

No one answered right away.

The old Hazran man took his back off the wall and opened his mouth, then closed it, spread his fingers and began to count them one by one. Partway through, he lowered his hand.

The tired-faced woman finished winding the cloth, stood, and went to stand beside the old machine. She stood there without saying a word.

The old machine only waited and did not hurry anyone; only its optical eye blinked unevenly.

Luhai drew a breath. Let it out.

"Do we have to count?"

He asked.

"Yes. Now."

Luhai looked at the old woman, and the old woman nodded. Luhai drew one more breath.
