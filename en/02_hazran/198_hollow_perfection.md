# Episode 198. Hollow Perfection

As they set foot on the next level, the air was heavy.

The corridor was smooth, its light orderly, its surfaces clean. When Sion breathed, air drier than the level below caught in his throat, and when he took one more breath, it was the same.

Sern stopped first.

"Response density ahead is different."

"By how much?"

"I cannot count it."

Sern said.

Thin grooves inside the wall surfaces lit up one after another.

One.
Two.
Three.
Four.

Sern counted and stopped at ten, but the grooves ran on down both sides to the end of the corridor, at the same spacing and the same brightness.

Seorin spoke low.

"This was prepared."

"Since when?"

Sion asked.

"Since before we came. Or from the start."

Sern stood in front of one groove and looked inside; it was empty. The groove next to it was empty too, but from the one after that, they were full.

"The first two are empty."

He said.

"Those have gone out. Downward."

Han Jiwoo looked at those two empty grooves, each one android wide, but she did not touch them.

A little farther in, they could see inside the grooves.

Androids.

Not one row. Not two either.
In the deep storage lines and standby lines along both sides of the corridor, tier upon tier inside the alignment grooves, above and below, they stood without moving, no light on their face surfaces. Their arms hung at their sides at the same angle, and their shoulders sat at the same height.

Sion looked at that height, then turned to the last android, one of whose shoulders sat lower; over there, no shoulder sat lower.

Holding his breath, he looked for a face with lines, even one, but there was none.

Smooth surface.
Smooth surface.

He started counting and gave up.

"How many?"

Kael asked.

"I didn't count. Count them and you freeze."

Kael looked at the grooves and counted, only his lips moving, and stopped at twenty. There was another groove after that, and he stopped there.

Han Jiwoo was looking too, and her gaze rested a long time on one face with nothing on it. She looked at the last android's face, at the faint lines a child had drawn, then looked back at that other face.
Nothing.
She put her hand to her inner pocket and pressed, and the fragment with the smiling mouth drawn on it was there; she took her hand away.

The last android looked at that hand, at the inner pocket, and then pointed a finger at the pocket.

"I know."

Han Jiwoo said.

"It's not yours. It's from the one before you."

He drew his finger back.

Kael's voice was very low.

"This is for an offensive."

Sern answered.

"Yes. It is not defense. It is a deployment meant to push us back in one blow, before we climb any higher."

"How many?"

Kael asked.

"I am not counting."

Sern said.

"If we count, we will not go up."

Ater looked at how the grooves were laid out: above, below, the spacing.

"It is the same as the holding area below."

He said.

"Grooves instead of footprint shapes. These instead of people."

"To send them up?"

"To pour them down."

Ater said.

"Only the direction is reversed."

Han Jiwoo looked into the grooves; each was exactly wide enough for one, the same width as the footprint shapes.

Seorin looked at the structure above the corridor.

"There are robots too."

Behind the androids, one tier higher, Serakion robots holding pushing plates and closing plates stood one behind each android in every row. They were not looking at the androids; they held their plates and looked ahead.

"Are they commanding them?"

Sion asked.

"No."

Ater said.

"The commanding was finished above. Those are setting the angle. So that when they pour out, they do not scatter."

Han Jiwoo murmured, very quietly.

"Not people, not weapons either… They just made them good at pouring out."

No one picked it up.

Sern looked at one robot's plate, at the angle of that plate.

"The angle is different from what we saw below."

He said.

"The ones below were angled to block. That one is angled to push."

"Push what?"

"The ones standing in front. Toward us."

Kael looked at the plate.

"It pushes its own?"

"They are not its own. Not by their count."

Sern said.

"They pour. Like water."

The last android looked at the rows one groove at a time, his optical eye moving up, then down. The same body, the same shoulders, the same hands. His hand rose toward his own face, where the lines were, stopped before touching, and came down.

"Don't look."

Han Jiwoo said.

Without turning his head, still looking at the grooves, he brought his hand to his own chest plate, then pointed toward the grooves. The same place. Han Jiwoo looked at that hand.

"I know. Don't look anyway."

He looked.
Han Jiwoo said nothing more and looked at the knot on his shoulder, the one that had not come undone.

"If that's you—"

Sion asked.

"What makes you any different?"

The last android looked at Sion and raised a hand over the lines on his own face, but it stopped before touching. Then it went down to his shoulder, to the knot, and his hand stayed there.

Sion looked at his blade.

The two old machines stood in the middle of the corridor, facing forward. One put its hand to its chest plate, as it had earlier; the other looked at that hand and put its hand to its own chest plate, and then the two took their hands away together.

"What are you doing?"

Kael asked.

"We are counting."

The machine said.

"What is left."

"So what's left?"

The machine did not answer and looked ahead.

Seorin looked at the machine.

"You going to use it?"

The machine looked at her with its optical eyes.

"When the time comes to use it."

"When's the time?"

"We will know. If we say it, it is too late."

Sern heard that, but he did not write it down.

Seorin set the positions, briefly.

"Sion in front. Sern beside him. Kael outside. Ater inside. Jiwoo behind. The machines in the middle. He goes at the very back."

"At the very back he's the last one."

Sion said.

"He has to be last. If he shows up in front, those things count him first. He can't be counted now."

"What happens if they count him?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"You heard it up there."

Seorin said.

"Android collective. He's in that too. By their count."

Han Jiwoo looked at the last android, who was looking at the grooves.

"He isn't."

She said.

"By their count he is. So, the back."

The last android went to the very back, behind Han Jiwoo. Han Jiwoo did not look back; she listened to the footsteps, to the sound of his shoulder axis.
He was there.

Farther down the corridor a metallic sound rang out, low and long. Not just one but layer on layer, it spread along the levels from top to bottom, into the grooves, into the androids' bodies, and the floor thrummed. Sion heard the sound through the soles of his feet.

The light changed very slightly, and a thin light passed over the face surfaces where there had been nothing. They had not come on; they were just short of it.
On every surface, at the same moment.

Sern's hand stopped over the record panel.

Sion looked at those surfaces, at what he had seen at Hazran, the things without expression that would not break. When he gripped his blade, the dried blood on his palm cracked, and he turned the chipped tip inward.

"It's quieter than at Hazran."

He said.

"Quiet is worse."

Seorin said.

"The Hazran ones at least made noise."

Kael looked at his own ankle.

"At Hazran there was someone behind us."

"And now?"

Sion asked.

"There isn't anyone."

Neither of them said more.

Sern spoke very low.

"They are coming."

Seorin looked farther in and swallowed, briefly, without a word. Han Jiwoo looked behind her: the last android was there, his optical eye on what lay ahead, on the grooves, on the ones that were the same as him.

"Stay behind me."

She said.

He stood at her back, and his chest plate touched her back, but Han Jiwoo looked ahead.

At the end of the corridor, the first android tilted forward, very slightly. Its feet were still in the groove; only its shoulders had moved.

Sern looked at those shoulders.

"That is the reference."

He said.

"Everything aligns to that one."

"Then that one."

Sion said.

"Not that one. The one after it. Strike that one and the next becomes the reference. At once."

Sion raised his blade, then lowered it. Following that one, the whole alignment line on both sides swayed in the same direction, almost at once. Storage lines and standby lines, top and bottom, swayed once like a wave and stopped, still tilted.

Sion drew a breath in and did not let it out.

The two old machines took one step forward, and Seorin did not stop them. The two did not look at each other but looked ahead, and this time they did not put their hands to their chest plates.

The android legion's attack began.

The first foot came out of its groove. There was no sound.
The second.
The third.
They sounded like a single step.
