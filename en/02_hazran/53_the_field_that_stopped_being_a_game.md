# Episode 53. The Field That Stopped Being a Game

From the moment Sion took the ember in his hand, the heat-layer ember zone was no longer a race course.

Han Jiwoo wrenched the skiff around and brought it up against Sion, and Sion kicked off the shaking glassy ground, almost sliding, and climbed aboard. The instant his foot barely caught on the rear auxiliary footplate, the skiff leaped straight to the outside.

Behind them, things were already splitting apart.

Several of the lights that had called to people as if real a moment ago burst upward all at once, and the glass layer beneath them spat out crack after crack in a chain. It was not heat twisting naturally; it looked as if a device someone had planted in advance was only now shaking the heat layer in one go. The lights that had pretended to be alive burned up louder still, and the teams that had failed to grab the real ember were fooled by those showy responses one last time.

The dark-red glider lurched hard first.

The rear rider who had grabbed the fake let go of the ember as if shaking it from the hand, but it was already too late. One side of the glider slid on the cracked glass layer, and the pilot at the front, forcing the balance back, lost the return angle. It was a seasoned team, so it did not die outright, but at the very least the composure it had shown a moment ago was broken.

"Don't need to watch behind?"
Sion asked, short.

Han Jiwoo answered at once.

"For now, just look ahead."

The skiff's right hover plate lifted late once more.
The hull lurched, but Han Jiwoo pushed the craft from the inside outward as if putting even that lurch to use as a return line. It was not a showy turn; it was piloting that rode the push of the collapsing terrain in reverse.

With no chance even to look back, Sion clutched the ember in his hand.

It was hot, but not a burning heat.
A heat that knocked at the inside of his palm, low and stubborn, like a living pulse.
It did not flash the way the fakes had, and it did not wrap itself around whoever held it as if to show off.
It just did not go out.

It was real.

That made it heavier.

The glider Kael and Sern rode drove, half a beat late, along the outside of the breaking terrain. Sern was already watching the flow of the collapse before the race's return line. Where was still holding, and where would die next. Kael pressed the craft just as he directed. While others turned first toward the return flag, those two picked the line that would not kill them first. Right now, that was the better call.

"The return line is closing,"
Sern said, low.

"Go around?"

"Not yet. Watch Han Jiwoo's pair get out first."

Kael clicked his tongue briefly.

"At times like this they're always in front."

But it did not sound like a complaint. It was closer to knowing exactly who, on a board like that, would tear a way open and get out first.

Over at the viewing stand, it was already past uproar.

At first the gamblers cheered louder. Like people who believed the more the board broke, the bigger the money would jump. But once the heat-layer collapse began reaching the return lines, the reactions split. Some backed away to look after their own lives before their stakes, and some leaned out over the railing even further. A Hazran-style race had always been a board where you watched a few people die, but now the traces of a hand from outside the race reaching into it were far too clear.

Luhai, hanging off the end of the railing, spoke almost in a shout.

"Those aren't racing hands!"

Ater immediately followed the flow of shadows below.

"I see them,"
he said, low.
"They are being placed too fast."

Seorin was already looking at something else.

At the outer edge of the heat-layer area, on a line recovery hands were never supposed to enter, three shadows were moving separately. One toward the heat devices, one toward the return guide lines, and one toward a narrow bottleneck where the racing hands would be easy to set at each other's throats. People who had not wandered in by chance but had gone in with their roles split in advance.

"That's Harun,"
Seorin said, very low.

Ater looked at her at once.

"Are you certain?"

"Around here, devices and recovery lines don't shake together like that by accident,"
Seorin answered.
"Somebody went in having decided to break the board."

Luhai screwed up his face.

"Are they insane? They're going to strip the race course too?"

Seorin spoke without taking her eyes off it.

"In Hazran, the one who costs the most is the one who trusts the rules. Not the one who breaks them."

The words never reached Sion's ears, but they stayed in the air well enough.

Nasim had not stepped back once until then. If anything, the bigger the uproar grew, the faster he read the faces around him. He could not have failed to know the board was broken. But on his face just now, calculation showed before dismay. A face weighing how to cover this collapse with words, whose responsibility to pass it to, and what, at least, to protect.

Harun, by contrast, was quieter.

Watching the space between the end of the heat layer and the return line, he moved only in very short motions. That silence was strangely more ominous. If Nasim was the hand that arranged the words, Harun looked like someone who already knew what had gone in there and what had to come out.

Between the two of them, Aka spoke.

"It's not a race anymore."

Nasim stopped talking.

It had been said by the youngest one where six adults stood. Nasim opened his mouth to argue, left it open, and let nothing out.

It was not that he had nothing to argue with. It was that what he had just been weighing was how to cover the board, and he had heard that there was no board left to cover.

Only Harun turned his head halfway and looked at Aka.

Having looked, he went back to watching the end of the heat layer. He said nothing, but his was the hand that kept weighing what it had been weighing a moment ago.

Nahira laid a hand on Aka's shoulder. She did not pull her back.

Under that hand Aka kept watching. She no longer followed the racing hands. Hers were eyes that already knew which hand the ember had gone into.

Behind Han Jiwoo, Sion kept his breathing shallow.

Normally, by now, he should have been reading the angle of the return line.
But not now.
The ground kept splitting,
fake lights burst and made a mess of their sight,
hands from outside the rules were shaking the inside.

This was getting closer to an escape than a return.

"See the return line?"
Han Jiwoo asked.

Sion looked ahead.

The red guide line the racing hands were supposed to follow back was still there. But about half of it was alive and about half of it was dead. One stretch still looked like a path, and right beside it the heat collapse had torn the line to pieces. Someone seeing it for the first time would not read the difference and would plow straight in.

"I can see it,"
Sion said.
"But that's not a race line anymore."

Han Jiwoo gave a short laugh.

"Good. Then we go my way."

With those words, the skiff pressed itself lower, not onto the red guide line but onto the half-dead outer ground beyond it. Not a line for returning by the rules, but an escape line only a pilot could choose in the moment the rules collapsed.

Just then the dark-red glider closed on their tail again.

Even though it was the team that had lost the fake ember, it had not fully backed off. It seemed to have judged that in this melee, taking the real one out of someone else's hand might be faster. Like the seasoned team it was, its calculations were quick too.

"It's coming,"
Sion said, short.

Han Jiwoo knew without looking back.

"Hold on."

The words meant don't lose the ember, and they also meant get your body ready from here on.

From the north, Kael and Sern were watching the same dark-red glider.

Sern said, very low,

"That team has switched from racing to seizure."

Kael answered.

"Then it's not a race for us anymore either."

Sern answered short.

"It stopped being one long ago."

The moment he finished, one of the devices at the edge of the heat layer burst harder.

A red glare spread into the sky and swallowed one of the remaining guide lines whole. The light was so huge that screams broke out in the stands too, and a few people fleeing too late tangled with each other and fell below the railing.

The courtyard began to turn completely upside down.

Sion gripped the ember in his hand harder.

What mattered now was not proving this and getting points.
It was carrying it and getting out alive.
And most likely, the mere fact of holding this ember had already become the reason for the next pursuit.

Han Jiwoo spoke through clenched teeth.

"Hold on tight."

The skiff tore out along the outside of the cracked glass layer, nearly scraping it.

Behind them the dark-red glider closed in,
off to the side Kael and Sern's glider was watching for the angle to join them on a wider line,
and up on the viewing stand, people who had realized the rules were broken were tangled together with people still counting their money first.

And Sion knew that no one could call this a race anymore.

The Ember Run was not over.
But it would no longer roll the way Hazran had first promised.

The board had now stopped being a game.
