# Episode 206. Not Abandonment

The repairs were finding their footing.

The machines brought temporary power lines and auxiliary lift axes back to life.
The humans sorted the materials worth saving from the debris to be thrown away.
The androids cut away, one at a time, the traces of alignment left deep inside.

An aristocrat robot came down, briefly passed on word that a way had opened higher still, all the way to the upper-level hangar that had been shut for a long time, and went back.

Aka, the first to lift her head, looked up for a while.

"We have to go now."

No one asked her what she meant.

Sion, Seorin, Sern, Ater, Han Jiwoo, Kael, Luhai, Aka and the last android went up, along with a few of the Hazran survivors and the children. The first and the second stayed below, because of the second's leg, and the first stayed beside it.

The topmost sector was quiet, and it smelled of a place shut for a long time. When they stood before the hangar, what stopped them all was not anything unfamiliar.

A ship.

It was the ship they had boarded when they left Hazran, old and shabby, the ship that had brought them this far.

But it was different. The caked dust on the hull had been cleared away, and the edges of the torn outer skin had been trimmed straight. The wreckage beside the broken ladder had been cleared, and the sharp-edged shards had been gathered neatly to one side. In the maintenance sector, salvageable parts and replacement pieces had been laid out in rows.
In rows too straight.

Sern was the first to speak, low.

"…Someone has worked on this."

Kael walked around the hull, running his hand along the seams of the outer skin, but nothing had been forced apart. Even when he saw a screw half a turn short of tight, he did not tighten it.

"Hands doing it for the first time."

Kael said.

"Careful hands."

"Whose hands?"

Sern asked.

Kael tipped his chin toward the aristocrat robots.

"The long-handed ones."

Luhai narrowed his eyes.

The aristocrat robots standing on either side of the hangar each stepped back quietly and cleared the way. One took half a step and one a full step, so their angles were not the same, and having stepped back, they watched the party and the ship.

Han Jiwoo looked at the scene for a while.

"…They did all this."

At the very front of the aristocrat robots Aka looked at was the robot that had first welcomed the party. The corners of its mouth were raised as they had been then, but it was different from then. The corners came down and went back up, and they would not hold still. Han Jiwoo looked at those corners. Someone had said something when they first arrived.
A blade that only learned how to smile.
The blade was folded.

The robot bowed its head clumsily, too deep. It lifted its head halfway, then bowed again. A greeting, or an apology.

Luhai clicked his tongue softly.

"Now that's really something."

There was almost no sneer in it.

"Where would they have learned to fix things?"

Han Jiwoo said.

"They didn't."

Kael said.

"That's why it's half a turn short."

Han Jiwoo looked at that screw too, but she did not tighten it.

When Sion walked toward the ship, the last android walked beside him. The moment the last android stood before the ship, the air in the hangar trembled faintly, and he stopped and for a long while could not raise his hand, then lifted his fingertips and touched the hull.

Deep inside the hull, threads of light slowly came alive.
One line.
Then another.
A response from somewhere in the ship sounded low through the hangar.

Ater followed that light along the ship's flank for a long time with the face of someone reading, yet for a while he did not put it into words.

"This… is older than I had thought."

Sern asked.

"What do you mean?"

Ater did not take his eyes away.

"I cannot tell you all of it now. But this much is certain: this ship did not remain only in the shape we knew."

Aka's eyes wavered, but she did not bring it out as though it were the answer. She looked from the last android to the ship and back, and let out a breath.

Han Jiwoo spoke quietly.

"So that's why you stopped here like that."

He did not take his hand off the hull, and under his hand the light brightened once.

"Was it like that when you first came here too?"

Sion asked.

He shook his head without taking his hand off the hull.

Aka walked over and stood beside him. After standing like that for a while, she spoke low.

"You should have a name now."

As eyes gathered on him, he turned just slightly toward Aka.

Aka steadied her breath.

"Serakion."

No one breathed.

"Not the name that stayed a prison. A name closer to what it first meant."

He stayed still for a long time: the hand on the hull, and Aka. Then, slowly, he bowed his head so slightly it could hardly be seen. Watching that bow, Han Jiwoo put her hand on the knot.
Touched it. Took it away.

Serakion.

After that, the movements parted. Some of the Hazran survivors decided to take the children and a few androids and go see whether another ship could be brought back to life. To go back to Hazran, the Hazran they could never go back to, and start from the ground up. While the woman who came with two children got ready to leave with three, the man without a younger brother stood on the same side as his brother, and the old Hazran man picked up his plate with its seventeen lines.

There were those who stayed too.
Some of the machines.
Some of the humans.
Some of the androids.
They were the ones who had decided not to leave this ruin abandoned. The wall-side machine, the first and the second, and the tired-faced woman stayed.

The wall-side machine looked at the hangar door.

"We will make it so that ships can set down here. Ships going out. Ships coming in."

Luhai heard it and clicked his tongue.

"Now I've seen it all. Talking about turning a prison into a harbor."

Then he added right away, calmly.

"…Still, it's better that way."

Sion raised his head and, without a word, nodded.

Seorin looked at the ones staying, at the wall-side machine and the tired-faced woman.

"Leave the plate down below."

She said.

"All five stones."

The wall-side machine's optical eye came up, then went down.

Small footsteps came from the edge of the hangar. The child who had first set lines on Serakion's face came over slowly after hesitating, and the adults did not step in. The woman who came with two children raised a hand and lowered it.

The child looked up at him for a while.

"Do you have to go?"

The hangar went quiet. While Aka held her breath and Han Jiwoo lowered her head, Sion could not look away.

Serakion did not move right away; he took his hand off the hull and looked at the child. As he looked toward the faces the children had drawn, toward the hands that had cleared the debris with him, his body swayed very slightly toward the lowered shoulder.

He raised his hand slowly. It stopped near the child's head. It did not touch.
His fingertips trembled.
For the first time.
The child did not cry, only looked at that hand, eyes wide.

Serakion brought those fingertips to his own chest, rested them there, and then pointed far off, toward the ship. There were no words.

The child's eyes followed the hand to the ship, then came back to him. After pressing their lips together for a long while, the child asked, very small.

"Will you come back?"

Serakion did not hesitate.
He nodded, slowly and clearly.
Once.

The child smiled. It had been a face about to cry, but what came out first was a smile.

"Then it's okay."

A drop fell slowly from the corner of Aka's eye, but she did not wipe it away.

Han Jiwoo put her arm around the child's shoulders.

Luhai, looking off into the distance, muttered.

"You'd better come back. If you don't, then I really won't let you off."

A few people laughed quietly.

Serakion looked back at all of them.

Sion.
Seorin.
Sern.
Ater.
Han Jiwoo.
Kael.
Luhai.
Aka.
The children.
The machines.
The humans.
The androids finding their faces again.

One by one, slowly. His optical eye gave each of them a beat.

Beyond the hangar the ruins of the upper levels could be seen, and somewhere below them people were moving.
Machines mending the paths.
Children running.
Androids looking at one another's faces.

Just before departure, Sion looked at Serakion from the foot of the ladder. The child, still standing on the hangar floor, spoke very quietly.

"Go, and come back."

At those short words, Serakion turned his body just slightly toward the child and paused, and his optical eye rested on the child for a beat.

Far below, from those going back to Hazran and those staying to build a base, two sounds rose together, mixed: the sound of bundles being tied and the sound of wall panels being raised.

The aristocrat robots opened the hangar door clumsily; it did not open on the first try but opened on the second. The robot that had first welcomed them stood there to the end. The corners of its mouth went up and came down, and the robot left them as they were.

The ladder.

Han Jiwoo climbed first, and Serakion followed behind her. The sound from his shoulder axis went into the ship. Hearing it from the hangar floor, the child raised a hand.
Did not wave it. Just held it up.

The first sailing was beginning.
