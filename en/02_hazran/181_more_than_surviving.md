# Episode 181. More Than Surviving

The time they were given after the fighting broke off was not long.

It was a pause to catch their breath before Serakion came again, enough to lay the wounded down, block the collapsed routes for the time being, and sort out the next move. Seorin set that pause at two cycles of the circulation line.

The last android was moved farther inside.

It took four to move him. Two old machines held him up under both arms while Han Jiwoo pressed the twisted shoulder in place with her hand. Luhai went in front, kicking the shards on the floor aside to clear a way.

They took him not right to the front line but to a quiet sector a little apart from where the children and the wounded were gathered. A place where he could sit with his back against the wall.

Han Jiwoo stayed by his side.

Even after laying out her tools, she didn't touch him for a long while. She picked up a piece of broken casing and put it down again; she reached for the misaligned axis, then drew back. Before, she would have gone straight in.

When she reached out again, she looked at his optical eye first, before taking hold of the casing.

"Hold on."

She spoke very low.

"This might hurt a lit…"

The words broke off there, and her hands stopped with them. It was the face of someone who had heard her own words. Only after she closed her mouth and took one breath did she take hold of the casing.

A child nearby seemed to have heard it, and looked at the last android with a face about to cry.

Then, very carefully, with a much more familiar motion than a little while ago, the child sat down by his side. The child drew their knees together and rested their hands on them.

"Will it be over soon?"

Han Jiwoo couldn't answer right away. The last android's optical eye flickered, very faintly, toward the child. The child saw it, and so did Han Jiwoo, but neither of them said anything.

Han Jiwoo removed the casing. She set it down on the floor slowly, so that it made no sound.

With the inside exposed, she could see the shoulder axis had slipped halfway out of its seat. She took the axis in both hands. She didn't push it in all at once. A little push, a stop, a look at the optical eye, and she pushed again.

The axis made a sound as it went into its seat. The child hunched their shoulders at the sound, but even then the android did not move.

She took out a cloth with no blood on it and wound it over the axis.

Once around, a knot.
Twice around, a knot.

Her fingers trembled as she tied the third knot. She tied it anyway. Then she tugged each of the three knots in turn.

Sern asked from the entrance of the sector.

"What condition is the shoulder axis in?"

"Half."

Han Jiwoo answered, looking at the axis.

"Half of it turns. Half of it's useless."

"I will record that."

Sern wrote it down. Then he looked once at the android.

Luhai brought a water bowl and set it beside Han Jiwoo, but she didn't wash her hands; with greasy hands, she took hold of the next piece of casing.

"Is that going to work?"

Luhai asked.

"It has to."

Luhai pushed the water bowl toward the children and left.

One by one, the children gathered near him again.

They were all careful. One child stopped three steps away, and another stood behind that child's back. A third came all the way to Han Jiwoo's side and sat. None of them pushed.

No one looked toward where the three had been lying on the floor. One child started to turn that way, then turned back.

One child, with the dust on their hand, very carefully drew the faded short mouth line again. The finger went along the line twice. Another started to trace a slightly rounded corner of an eye beside it with a finger, but stopped when Han Jiwoo caught their wrist.

Han Jiwoo's face was neither smiling nor crying.

"Leave one for later."

Her voice was low.

"We can't touch too much right now."

The child drew their hand back and set it on their own knee.

Another child looked at the android's dark optical eye and asked.

"Is he asleep?"

"Machines don't sleep."

Han Jiwoo answered, looking at the axis.

"Then what's he doing?"

Han Jiwoo's hands stopped. When she looked at the android's lit optical eye, that optical eye was looking at the child.

"…He's listening."

The child closed their mouth. The child beside them closed theirs too, and the one next to that put a finger to their lips. The other children lowered their voices. No one had told them to.

Sion watched the scene from a little way off. The way the children had gathered around the android was the same as the way they had gathered around a wounded Hazran man a few days before.

The old machines didn't come close either, but they stood just inside the entrance of the sector. Seorin asked the machine at the entrance.

"Outside."

"It is still quiet."

The machine answered without taking its eyes off the outside.

One of them kept watching the guard route. Another, when Han Jiwoo groped along the floor for a piece of a part, silently brought over the piece it had been holding and set it down. Han Jiwoo looked up at the machine.

"Thank you."

The machine stopped. Without turning around, it stood for a beat, then went.

She picked up the piece and fitted it into the shoulder axis.

It fit.

When she laid her palm on the place where it fit, a vibration came, steadier than before. While she kept her palm there for a long while, the children watched that hand, and then one child put a palm on the android's leg.

"I can feel it too."

Another child did the same.

"Me too."

The third child didn't touch, only watched. When Han Jiwoo looked at that child, the child shook their head, and Han Jiwoo nodded. She didn't push.

Ater came back and spoke low.

"The flow outside has turned strange. There is not much time."

The hands in the sector stopped for a moment. Only Han Jiwoo's did not. They were tying the last knot that held the axis in place.

Seorin looked at the last android for a moment. Then she looked at the children, and after that at Han Jiwoo.

She said nothing. She crouched and drew lines in the dust on the floor with her finger, lines dividing the layers, the same shape she had drawn before the battle. When she had drawn them all, she stopped her finger at one spot and set a dot there, then looked at the last android once more.

Ater looked down at the lines.

"The third line is different from before."

"It's the same."

Seorin answered without lifting her finger.

"There's one more dot."

Ater looked at the lines again, then at the dot. He said nothing more.

"Time."

Seorin asked Sern.

"Half a cycle of the circulation line."

"Half a cycle."

Seorin repeated it. Her eyes moved faster over the lines in the dust.

Sern looked down at the dot and made a mark at the same spot on his record panel.

A child whispered to the last android, very softly.

"I hope you don't get hurt this time."

The last android's optical eye flickered once more, very faintly.

"…Yeah."

Han Jiwoo said. You couldn't tell from her voice whether she was saying it to the child or to the android.

She stopped the hand that was tying the knot. She didn't look at the child. She didn't look at the android. She looked at her own hand. Then she finished tying the knot.

Sion looked toward the entrance of the sector. The old machine that had been keeping watch had turned where it stood and was looking inward, toward the children and the android. The machine turned back to face outside.

Seorin wiped out the lines in the dust with her palm. She left the dot. She stood up.

"Gather up."

People took their backs off the wall, but the children stayed by the android. Han Jiwoo pressed the shoulder axis one last time, and as she got up she laid a hand on one child's head.

One child didn't get up. The child sat leaning beside the android's knee, eyes half closed. When Han Jiwoo laid the last of the cloth over the child's shoulders, the child leaned their head against the android's leg. The android did not move his leg away.

Han Jiwoo looked at the twisted side of that leg. The side the child leaned on was the sound side. She pulled the edge of the cloth up to just under the child's chin.

Leaning against the wall, the last android looked at Han Jiwoo's hand. Then at the dot Seorin had left, and then at the head of the child leaning on his leg. His optical eye stayed there.
