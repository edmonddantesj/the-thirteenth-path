# Episode 221. The Nameless Man's Question

The upper body that had crossed the wall looked lighter than expected and was actually heavier.

Even with Sion and Aka holding simultaneously, the person's body felt strangely heavy—as though the time of being pressed down for so long was crossing over with it.

Elia did not let go.

Her fingertips were trembling, but she was quietly holding on—like someone who never drops what they have once gripped.

Ater was still holding the wall's balance. Sern was watching the closing rhythm from outside and the shaking grain inside at the same time.

"More."

Sern's voice fell low.

"Pull more now."

Sion pulled harder. Aka matched from the opposite side.

The person's shoulders came through. Then the back. Finally, fully past the wall's gap.

In that moment, a very thin friction sound brushed from somewhere inside the wall.

The closing side.

Sern said immediately.

"Your lordship."

Ater's gaze sank further. He twisted the angle of his fingertips, barely.

One light running along the base of the wall wavered briefly.

Thanks to that, the gap held just a little longer without fully closing.

Sion clenched his teeth and pulled one last time.

And finally, the entire person crossed the wall.

Aka stepped back one pace and swallowed her breath.

Elia closed her eyes very briefly, then opened them.

Only now did Sion see the face properly.

Pale.

Skin that had not seen light for too long. Much of the flesh had fallen away. The body was stiff like someone who had endured the same posture for far too long.

What caught the eye first was not frailty.

On that face, the grain of someone who had refused to collapse for too long remained deeper than any trace of collapse.

The eyes were open.

Not dim.

The kind of eyes that looked deeper precisely because they were excessively quiet.

Even right after fully crossing the wall, the person did not immediately take a large breath.

Instead—very briefly—looked around first.

Sion. Aka. Elia. Ater.

And toward Sern, still holding the outer door.

That short gaze was the gaze of someone who reads only what is needed to assess the situation first.

Finally, the person spoke.

The voice was low. Dry in places, like an instrument long unused. But strangely clear.

"You are… not as late as I had thought."

Sion felt his breath catch at that first line, strangely.

A person confined for so long, offering neither relief at surviving, nor explanation of circumstance, nor a plea for rescue, but first measuring time—that alone showed what kind of person this was.

Elia said it very low.

"Save your words."

The corner of that person's mouth moved—almost invisibly faint.

Less a smile, more the reaction of someone confirming that tone was still Elia's.

Very slowly, the person turned and looked at the wall.

Then asked low.

"How did you open it."

Ater answered.

"Forced it to misalign."

The person's eyes rested on Ater, a little more clearly this time.

Ater did not look away.

For a moment, a silence no one could enter settled.

The person said it very low.

"Valkar."

Ater answered short.

"Yes."

The eyes wavered—very faintly.

Not surprise. Not gladness. Closer to the trembling of confirming that a structure long erased was now reconnecting before the eyes.

"I see."

From outside, Seorin's voice arrived.

"Almost done."

The person heard that and still did not move the body right away.

Instead, looking around the corridor once more as though reading it, asked in a low, dry voice.

"Outside. How far has it rotted."

Sion blinked.

This person was truly asking—before personal condition, rescue method, or who had come—how far the Empire had rotted.

Elia said it almost like a sigh.

"Hear it after we get out."

The person lowered the gaze briefly.

"That is fair."

Aka was still watching from a short distance.

And murmured very small.

"So it was this person…"

The person's gaze went to Aka for the first time.

Strangely, the face looked as though the meaning had been fully understood.

Despite no explanation. Despite not knowing who Aka was.

Very low, toward Aka.

"It seems you heard."

Aka could not answer. Only frowned slightly.

"You were always… so quiet."

The person nodded at that—barely.

"Being quiet means less gets cut."

Elia's gaze wavered.

From that single short sentence, Sion felt he could sense a little of how long these two people had endured—not in the same way, but in similar ways.

Sern's voice fell more decisively this time.

"We have to leave now."

Only then did the person push fully off the wall and try to stand.

The body did not follow right away.

Sion immediately gripped an arm. Aka attached from the other side.

Elia, one hand on the wall, forced herself upright too.

The person did not try to hide the condition. But did not shape it into leaning either.

Simply acknowledged, coldly.

"Walking is fine. Will not be fast."

Elia said almost simultaneously.

"Same here."

In that moment, Sion nearly laughed, strangely.

Both wore faces hurt enough to die, yet both said the exact same thing first.

The laugh did not last.

The metallic sound from outside was much closer now.

The structure was realigning.

Ater withdrew his hand and said it low.

"It is closing."

Sern received right behind.

"This way."

Seorin, Kael, Jiwoo, Luhai, and Serakion were already sorting the exit order.

Luhai was holding his words tight, but from his eyes alone, "who is this person" was already written across his face.

This time he truly did not open his mouth.

Perhaps because Jiwoo might hit him one more time preemptively.

Sion and Aka turned the person toward the corridor, supporting.

Elia attached right beside.

The person looked at Elia—very briefly.

The gaze was short, but strangely, it seemed as though many words had passed.

Why did you come too. So you came anyway. Why did you not leave me. I knew you would not.

Words like those seemed to pass inside that very short gaze.

And then Sion saw the person look back at the inner wall one last time.

Less lingering, more the gaze of someone trying not to erase from memory what still remained there.

Very low—barely loud enough for the self to hear.

"It was not too late, then."

Sion could not tell whether that was about the fact they had come, or the fact Elia was still enduring, or the meaning that before the Empire fully ended, something could still be pulled out.

Sern gestured from ahead.

They truly had to leave now.

Sion's group, holding both Elia and the nameless man together, began turning their bodies back toward the corridor outside.

And in that moment, the Empire still did not know.

That the two questions it had buried deepest and longest had just begun coming out—simultaneously.

---

Very far away, in another place, Kairon Valkar stopped mid-step.

A very brief tremor.

Faint enough that anyone pretending not to notice could have passed it by. But for him, it was a far too familiar grain.

The approval structure had been twisted once. And inside that twist, the habit of a familiar hand remained.

Ater.

Kairon's gaze sank very low.

They reached the heart.

That single line of judgment came down almost without wavering.

Elia was probably already out. Having entered that far, those children would not have turned back empty-handed.

Up to there, Kairon thought, it was not too late.

It could be managed. The wound would be large, but perhaps the entire Empire had not yet tipped past the point of no return.

But the very next thought hardened something inside him, very faintly.

If they were true to themselves, they might not have left the other person nearby behind either.

Kairon did not let himself think the name.

The moment he did, the possibility would become too concrete a reality.

If that person had truly been released—then it was different.

That was not a simple rescue. That was the moment the very structure the Empire had buried deepest began to speak again.

If that happened, the situation would become uncontrollable.

And if it truly came to that, he might eventually find himself in a position where he had to stand against his own son.

Kairon said nothing for a long while.

He did not share that possibility with anyone right now.

Not yet.

At least for now, he could still keep silent—for his son.

So he walked again.

Very slowly. Like a person who had felt nothing.
