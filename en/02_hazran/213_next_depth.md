# Episode 213. Next Depth

That night, the air inside the warehouse refused to lighten.

On the surface, everyone had settled into their spots, steadied their breathing, and turned their bodies toward preparing the next move. But in truth, no one was fully resting.

The words that Elia was alive. That the precise location was still unknown. And the nameless person waiting beyond.

Those three remained quietly in the room.

Sion had given up on sleep and leaned against the end of a narrow corridor inside the warehouse.

Almost no noise leaked from outside. All that could be heard inside was someone turning over once, fabric brushing, and very rarely, someone letting out a long breath.

Strangely, even that quiet could not change the feeling that too much had moved in a single day.

They had left Serakion. Entered the Empire. Met the resonance, the Myojok who had survived the Black Covenant. Caught a thread to Elia. And now heard that someone whose face they did not even know was waiting for them.

It felt like they had gone too far inside, all at once.

"Still awake?"

At the low voice, Sion raised his head.

Sern.

He stood leaning against the opposite wall of the corridor, still wearing the same outer layer from the day.

"You too."

Sern spoke without a trace of a smile.

"This has never been a place where sleep comes easy."

Sion looked at Sern's face briefly.

Throughout the day, Sern had been quiet multiple times. But now that quiet looked older and more tired.

Sion asked first.

"Someone you know."

Sern did not deny it.

"Yes."

"How well?"

Sern lowered his eyes briefly, then raised them.

"Someone I have known since I was young."

Sion heard the words coming from further inside than expected and paused.

The resonance. Sern's old line. Someone inside the Empire. Someone known since childhood.

That combination was not far from meaning most of one person's life had been woven inside the Empire from the very start.

Sion said it low.

"Can they be trusted?"

Again, Sern did not answer easily.

"That is not a good question."

"Why?"

"Because inside here, the people you can trust and the people you cannot do not divide cleanly."

Only then did Sion begin to understand.

This was not a world where good and evil, ally and enemy, traitor and loyalist could be cut apart simply.

There would be people who bind while trying to save. People who break while trying to protect. People who chose the more cowardly side just to endure.

Sern spoke like someone who had known such a world from far too young.

"Then why did they let us in."

Again Sern answered short.

"You are needed."

"Us?"

"Yes. Sion's group. And his lordship, Ater."

Sion narrowed his eyes slightly.

"Ater?"

Sern's expression tightened, very faintly.

"Yes."

"They would not like that."

"There would be no reason to."

A short answer, but Sion could immediately tell too much was omitted inside.

Between Valkar and the Black Covenant, far too much time that Sion did not yet fully know seemed to be piled.

Sion did not press further.

Sern did not seem inclined to unpack it now either.

A moment later, one more set of footsteps sounded from the opposite end of the corridor.

Ater.

He wore the face of someone who had already known the two were talking, and simply stopped at a nearby wall.

No one asked why he had come.

Ater spoke first.

"Only the sleepless have gathered."

Sion smiled brief.

"You too."

Ater did not bother to deny it.

Sern glanced away for a moment, then back.

That small movement alone showed something remained between the two that had not been fully spoken during the day.

Ater asked Sern first.

"Do you truly not know what kind of person they are?"

Sern did not answer for a while.

Sion felt the air between the two shift again from moments before.

This was not an information check. It was closer to a conversation between two people with a long history, measuring how much the other was still hiding.

Sern finally spoke.

"I have a guess."

Ater's gaze sank very low.

"That much, then."

Sern answered only short.

"Yes."

Neither went further.

But from that brief exchange alone, Sion could feel that the one person the old man had mentioned occupied a position far heavier than expected.

Ater adjusted his leaning posture by the smallest degree.

"It will be more dangerous."

Sern received.

"Yes."

"But we have to go in."

"Yes."

Both answers were short, yet too much had passed between.

Sion watched and felt again that the two knew the Empire's interior in genuinely different ways.

Ater knew it through the language of structure, justification, and accountability. Sern knew how people actually wore down inside that structure.

A moment later, Aka's face appeared at the corridor entrance.

"Everyone awake?"

Sion looked her way.

"You too."

Aka approached slowly, without even rubbing her eyes.

"That person is still in pain."

No one attached the name Elia right away.

But this time, everyone was thinking of the same person.

Aka frowned slightly.

"But it is strange. In pain, yet still enduring inside. The side holding them is solid, but the inside is not collapsing either."

Sern's expression hardened again.

Ater asked low.

"Mental side or physical?"

Aka thought briefly.

"Both. But… the heart feels like it is not breaking first."

Sion heard that and closed his eyes, then opened them, for no particular reason.

He could not explain it, but if it was Elia, that sounded exactly right.

Aka continued immediately.

"And one more thing."

Sern and Ater looked her way simultaneously.

Aka spoke slowly, like someone unsure whether her own words were correct.

"Nearby. There is one more person. Very quiet. Not cold, but strangely still."

Sion narrowed his brows.

"Who."

Aka shook her head.

"I do not know. But they seem like the side that is waiting."

Sern said nothing.

Sion felt that silence sounded almost like an answer.

Ater said it very low.

"Truly deep inside."

This time Sern did not deny it.

Right then, a very low vibration entered through the warehouse wall from outside.

Not footsteps. Not a maintenance vehicle.

More uniform, slower, passing across a wider range.

Search patrol.

Sern raised his hand reflexively.

No one moved.

The vibration flowed along the warehouse wall, very slowly, from one side to the other. Longer than a breath in and out.

Then it passed.

Only after it had fully receded did Sion realize he had been holding his breath.

Sern lowered his hand slowly.

"The interval has shortened." He said it very low. "About two cycles faster than during the day."

Ater's gaze sank.

The meaning was clear. The time they could stay here was shrinking.

Sern pushed off the wall.

"We move at daybreak."

A voice that needed no further elaboration.

Sion nodded.

Aka still stood with an uneasy face. Ater had gone quieter. Sern entered the silence again, first.

Sion looked at each of the three in turn.

It felt as though they had gone too quickly, too far inside, to a depth hard to return from.

Strangely, he did not want to pull back now.

Only by coming this far had things finally begun to reach their hands.

Elia. The resonance. And somewhere deep inside, someone whose name had not yet fully surfaced—waiting.

That night, the silence remaining in the warehouse was no longer a desolate silence.

It was dangerous. Quiet. Hard to undo.

But it was clearly a silence tilting toward somewhere.
