# Episode 207. Different Dread

The highest floor of the Empire's Central Approval Bureau was always excessively quiet.

The corridors were wide. The light was evenly arranged. Footsteps flowed only along permitted paths to the end.

In that place, even when something collapsed, the sound did not grow louder first.

It grew quieter first.

At the end of a long report panel, a figure from the upper echelon had been staring at the displayed numbers for a while.

The clerk who had submitted the report had kept his mouth shut for some time, waiting only for permission.

The figure did not speak right away.

Instead, he slowly scrolled through part of the screen.

Severed supply lines. Unresponsive relay axes. The scheduled android force reinforcement—placed on hold. And at the center of all those vacancies, one name, stated explicitly for the first time.

Serakion.

His fingertip paused over that name for just an instant.

The air in the room cooled with that single brief pause alone.

The clerk continued the report without raising his eyes.

"The re-survey of gray zone perimeter supply networks has confirmed that the missing segments were not simple accidents. Anomalies previously treated as dispersed gaps have all surfaced simultaneously following the collapse of a single central node."

The figure still did not pull his gaze from the screen.

"Single central node."

The clerk answered very low.

"Yes. Presumed to be Serakion."

The word "presumed" was not particularly safe in that room.

But stronger language was not permitted by the current atmosphere either.

He said nothing for a long while.

What he was looking at was not the name of a single planet, but something like the shadow of how long that name had been used within the Empire—in ways that could not be seen.

Lower on the screen, more uncomfortable lines sat quietly.

No additional android reinforcement. No follow-up formation response. Replacement deployment authorization line—unconnected.

The clerk reached that point, steadied his breathing very faintly, and spoke.

"As of current confirmation, additional force reinforcements are no longer arriving."

After those words, no one tried to follow immediately.

Everyone in the room knew it was not merely a reinforcement delay.

But no one was the first to voice the precise scale of that discomfort.

The clerk ultimately could not complete the next sentence.

That this fact spreading outward would not be good—everyone already knew without saying.

The figure finally raised his gaze, slowly.

"Leaks?"

"Currently contained."

"Currently."

The voice was low and refined, yet that single word made the clerk's spine stiffen faintly.

"Yes. However, several perimeter munitions lines have noticed the supply variance, making a full seal difficult to guarantee."

He closed his eyes briefly and opened them.

He did not rage. Did not strike the desk. Did not raise his voice.

But precisely because of that, the room grew quieter.

He scrolled the screen again.

This time, not numbers—records closer to battle traces.

Alignment gate malfunction. Recovery axis mutual collision. Sorting chamber central corridor lost. Full silence following final node collapse.

He studied the arrangement for a while, then said it very low.

"This was not done by breaking."

The clerk answered immediately.

"Correct. At least one segment appears to have been opened not by forced breach, but by causing the approval sequence to misalign very briefly."

When those words ended, his fingertip paused again—very faintly.

A truly brief moment.

But that briefness alone cooled the room once more.

This was not the trace an ordinary intruder would leave.

A very small mark. But vivid precisely because of its smallness.

Not someone who had broken through by force, but the trace of someone who understood how the inner sequences stood—and had briefly twisted them open.

The clerk brought up the next file and added carefully.

"In some residual lines, traces resembling approval-bureau-class bypass reading were also identified. Confirmation is difficult. However…"

He could not finish.

Because the reporting side already knew who the figure above was thinking of.

The figure did not say the name first.

Instead he asked very quietly.

"The last confirmed position of the Valkar heir?"

The clerk, as though prepared, immediately brought up the next screen.

"Since departure from the Hazran front, officially unconfirmed. However, when cross-referenced with likely accompanying personnel and movement patterns…"

He paused there briefly.

The upper figure permitted continuation with his gaze alone.

"The possibility of association with Sion Lapis's group is difficult to exclude."

The air in the room thinned a little more.

No one wore a surprised face.

It was closer to the uncomfortable silence when an expected name arrives too precisely.

He said nothing for a long while.

Sion Lapis. Ater Valkar. And Serakion.

Names that should have stayed apart were now on the same line.

He said it very low.

"Tracking?"

"In progress. However, since the collapse of Serakion's center, gray zone routes have blurred again. Right now, there are too many traces to immediately pinpoint coordinates."

He heard the answer and did not issue orders right away.

Instead he looked briefly out the window—at the Empire's center, arranged with too much calm.

That calm had always looked as though it was maintained by its own strength, but in truth it stood on a great deal of concealment and selection.

He turned his gaze back to the clerk.

"All Serakion-related records are to be immediately re-sealed above current classification. Supply variance detection lines are to be dispersed under alternative causes. And…"

He paused briefly.

"Reports concerning Ater Valkar are to be submitted directly to me only, from now on."

The clerk bowed his head instantly.

"Yes."

Only then could several people in the room no longer deny who was receiving this report.

Kairon Valkar.

The center of House Valkar, and Ater Valkar's father.

He had not asked about his son's safety first.

Instead, he was calculating how far to seal this incident his son was entangled in, and from where to begin tracking.

But precisely because of that, the room sank colder.

The fact that a person who saw the entire board before blood was sitting in that seat felt like a kind of warning to the clerks as well.

Kairon said nothing more.

But that single final order was enough for everyone to understand: personal matters and imperial order had now risen onto the same line.

Even after the report ended, the clerks could not move right away.

Until Kairon closed the screen first, no one dared to draw a loud breath.

Only after the screen went dark did the name Serakion vanish from the light.

But vanishing did not make it something that never happened.

If anything, this was just the beginning.

---

On the Alliance side, this information had not yet been opened to everyone.

The commotion existed only on the outer operational lines. The real records had been brought up only inside one small conference room, deeper in.

Very few had been admitted.

Kanus Han. And several Alliance command figures.

Plus one person whose presence made the conference room's air distinctly restless.

He could not stay quiet even while the records were being opened.

"Nice. Folded an entire planet in and kept a straight face the whole time."

But strangely, every time he closed his mouth, the rest of the room began choosing only the words they truly needed.

Kanus Han did not bother to stop the chatter.

She rested only her fingertips on the report and for a while neither asked questions nor organized—simply waiting for the information to settle into place first.

Her gray hair, looking as though it had gone white, did not wear the face of someone confused because there was too much information.

The opposite, in fact.

It was closer to the face of someone who, among pieces that had arrived too late, was finally seeing one empty slot begin to fit.

The general across from her asked very low.

"General Kanus, your assessment?"

Kanus did not answer right away.

Instead she slowly pushed one record panel aside and brought up a map of relay lines outside the gray zone.

One flow that had looked nearly dead was now strangely vivid from a recent point onward.

The restless figure propped his chin and said flatly.

"Did it open? Or is it leaking?"

Kanus answered then, in a very low and conclusive voice.

"It did not open."

Every gaze in the room gathered at once.

Kanus continued without raising her eyes, calmly.

"What was holding it from inside collapsed. The seal. The supply lines. All of it."

The room went briefly quiet.

Even the restless figure did not add anything this time.

Kanus lowered her gaze back to the files and continued.

"And this did not open by accident. Someone turned the inside over."

One general asked very low.

"How many subjects do you estimate could do that?"

Kanus did not answer right away.

The movement trail that vanished after Hazran. The Empire's delayed response. The timing of the android supply gap. And the traces of internal collapse inside Serakion.

She passed through each one as though scanning, then said it very quietly.

"Sion Lapis's group."

Another general objected immediately.

"That is too far a presumption."

Then the figure who had been disrupting the room's air all along murmured, chin still propped, almost smiling.

"No. Not that far."

After that short line, the room's air went strangely quieter.

Kanus showed no reaction to the remark.

Instead she pointed at one line in the data with her fingertip.

"After Hazran, those people vanished too cleanly. Not the trajectory of people running. The trajectory of people who had somewhere they absolutely had to reach."

She pointed at the next line.

"Serakion was shaken from inside. Not the trace of someone knocking from outside. Closer to the trace of someone who understood the internal structure and touched it."

And finally, she looked at the supply gap line that had just opened.

"And now, the result is leaking outward."

No one could follow right away.

Kanus Han finally withdrew her hand from the screen and said it very low.

"Sion's group was there."

The words were not loud like a definitive declaration.

But that was why they were heavier.

Because what that single sentence meant was not simple survival confirmation.

That Serakion had actually existed. That its android supply structure was now shaken. And that Sion's group had now become not mere fugitives or remnant forces, but the center of a new ripple that both the Empire and the Alliance would have to track simultaneously.

The air inside the small conference room settled, little by little.

Outside, the operational lines likely still had their commotion. But at least the people sitting here had begun looking in the same direction.

Kanus did not close the screen again.

Instead she looked at the record where the name Serakion was displayed—for a long time.

The Empire would likely try to bury this. The Alliance would try to read it as opportunity. And between them, Sion's group had once again opened a door that an entire world wanted kept shut.

Then the figure who had been disrupting the room's air tapped the edge of the desk lightly with his fingers.

"Good. Then all we need to do is watch who tries to put on a calm face first."

It sounded deflating by anyone's measure, yet strangely no one in the room dismissed it as idle noise.

Kanus Han, expression unchanged even at that, exhaled very quietly.

"The outside will start moving now too."

That was neither expectation nor fear alone.

It was closer to the voice of someone who knew: when something hidden for too long finally enters the world's sight, no one can move the same way as before.

Far beyond the gray zone, Serakion the planet was probably not yet fully recovered.

But it was already enough.

That single name alone was enough to push both the Empire and the Alliance into the next phase—with no way back.
