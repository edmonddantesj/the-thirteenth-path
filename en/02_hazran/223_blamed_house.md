# Episode 223. Blamed House

That room had never been one that permitted commotion.

A long table. Too neatly spaced chairs. Air where silence divided hierarchy before words. And people who did not truly believe that silence, yet maintained it like courtesy to the end.

Rooms where the Empire's upper nobles gathered were always similar.

But today, inside that similarity, there was a very faint crack.

No one raised their voice, yet everyone was already displaying irritation and unease, sufficiently.

One noble spoke first.

"What exactly does this mean, Lord Valkar."

The tone was courteous. What it contained was not courtesy—closer to organized accusation.

Kairon Valkar did not answer.

He stood as always—quietly. Showing little of his expression, as always.

Another noble continued right away.

"The inner heart has been shaken. Parts of the preservation zone are empty, and the status of two deepest-level subjects is also unconfirmed. And yet House Valkar detected no prior signs?"

Detected no prior signs.

Kairon lowered his eyes very briefly, then raised them.

"My failing."

When those words fell, several nobles' expressions actually worsened.

An apology given too easily makes people more uncomfortable at times like these.

They may have expected a larger excuse, a more specific failure report, or words that diluted responsibility by pulling in other possibilities.

But Kairon gave almost no room for that.

A third noble spoke, low and sharp.

"Lord Valkar. Was it not your house that has held this kind of sensitive authorization and coordination the longest?"

Courteous words.

But the courtesy was not to preserve face—closer to a shell for transferring blame more precisely.

The noble continued.

"If only one simple preservation subject had gone missing, this session would not have been convened so urgently."

The name was not spoken directly.

But everyone knew another person had escaped together.

That name too—not brought to the lips, but already circulating through the room like fact.

So the nobles were precise in the more cowardly way.

Everyone knew who was being discussed, yet spoke without names to the end.

Kairon could not have been unaware of even the direction of that silence.

An elderly noble seated opposite opened his mouth slowly.

"There is sufficient possibility that Myojok remnants who diverged from the Black Covenant shook the inner structure. If they used that gap to extract two preservation subjects, that is not simple infiltration—it means the entire heart perimeter was voided."

Another noble received right away.

"Which is why we are asking. Was Lord Valkar not in the very position that should have received such signs first?"

Kairon still did not waver.

"Correct."

A short affirmative.

No excuse. No grievance. No forced explanation.

That actually made several nobles more uncomfortable.

Because they too were not entirely ignorant of where this situation had grown from.

They had long neglected. Long looked away. Long consumed the heart's silence only in ways advantageous to themselves. Kairon was not the only one.

But no one in this room would be the first to say so aloud.

The Empire had always been this way.

Even fractures they had all made together—blame was laid first on the side that had longest been sealing those fractures.

Kairon was someone who had endured that structure for too long.

He said it very quietly.

"I will re-inspect the entire preservation zone and heart access lines. Including the authorization structure and perimeter connections, I will trace the escape route. If necessary, I will fully readjust the Valkar response lines as well."

One noble exhaled then—a breath barely distinguishable from a sneer.

"Will readjusting now solve anything? They may already be outside."

Kairon did not argue back right away.

He answered in a very neat voice.

"That is why, from now, I will take responsibility including the routes after the escape."

Take responsibility.

Words that many in this room had spoken countless times with their mouths, but rarely carried through to the end with their own bodies.

And so, several nobles briefly closed their mouths.

Something close to sheepishness passed—very brief.

It was truly brief.

Soon another noble spoke again.

"Very well. Then Lord Valkar will handle the aftermath."

That sounded like permission, but was actually a complete transfer.

Was this not your house's charge from the start. Then handle it again this time. The failure, the cleanup, the blood and silence that follow—all yours to bear once more.

Kairon could not have been unaware.

He lowered his head—very faintly.

"Yes. I will ensure it is resolved."

The nobles each withdrew their gazes. Someone ran a finger along the table's edge once. Someone re-aligned the edge of a document.

Their faces were not those of people entirely unaware they shared blame for this situation.

Neither were they people who would voice that blame first.

When the meeting ended and people left one by one, Kairon remained in his seat to the very end.

Only after the last door closed did he exhale—very slowly.

The room was quiet again.

A different quiet from before.

No one's gaze now. No one's words.

And because of that, Kairon could be a little more precise.

The nobles did not yet know.

The heart had not simply been shaken. That door had been opened in a method House Valkar had handled for too long.

Few people could read that trace.

And Kairon was one of those few.

He did not say it aloud.

The moment he did, the possibility would become not suspicion but near-certainty.

Ater.

Kairon's gaze sank very low.

That child had reached the heart. And that child's hand had touched that door.

That fact now held almost no room for doubt.

Elia and one more person.

That both had already gone outside was also approaching the side that could no longer be denied.

Kairon steadied his breathing very slowly.

And clenched his jaw—very slightly. Even if he kept his son's name hidden, he could not also cover over what that hand had opened.

This was no longer the time to prepare only for damage control.

He turned.

Very slowly. Like a person who would not collapse to the end.

But this step was not merely a quiet one.

Kairon Valkar now knew clearly.

He was truly beginning the preparations to stand against his own son.
