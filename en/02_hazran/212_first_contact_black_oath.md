# Episode 212. First Contact: Black Covenant

The inside of the warehouse building was quieter than it appeared from outside.

Once the door closed, the street noise thinned by one layer. What remained was only the kind of dry-breath stillness found in spaces that had not spoken for a long time.

It looked like an ordinary logistics warehouse on the surface, but the internal structure was different.

The spacing between stacked crates was too uniform. The corridors were not wide, yet gaps to slip through immediately remained throughout. Less a place for long stays and more a location arranged for brief visits and quick dispersal.

Kael checked the ceiling and wall junctions first upon entering. Jiwoo looked at the door side and floor load. Seorin calculated entry lines and sight-blocking positions without a word.

Sion, through all of that, watched Sern first.

Sern had been sparing with words the entire way here, but after entering this place, he seemed wrapped in an entirely different kind of silence.

Not the silence of guarding against an unfamiliar place. Closer to the silence that comes from stepping back onto a spot you had been looking away from for too long.

Ater was not pretending not to notice either.

Between the two, some old line that no one had yet explained was already laid.

A moment later, one inner partition was slowly drawn aside.

What emerged first was neither a person bearing weapons nor sharp hostility.

An old Myojok man walked out, very quietly.

His back was not fully bent, but he had the kind of body where fatigue would accumulate first if he stood too long. His clothes were plain, yet carried the kind of tidiness that stays on a person who has not lived carelessly.

The old man looked at Sern before Sion.

That gaze lingered somewhere between gladness and wariness for a long time.

Sern did not immediately bow his head either.

For a moment, both stood without speaking—like people measuring how much the other had changed first.

The old man finally spoke.

"Later than I thought."

Sern answered very low.

"Earlier than I thought." Sion felt that inside that short exchange, a great deal of time that outsiders would never know was already contained.

The old man then looked at Ater, standing beside Sern.

His gaze wavered, very faintly.

Less surprise and more the confirmation of: so you came this far after all.

"Even your lordship is here."

Ater lowered his gaze briefly, then raised it.

"That title is not needed right now."

The old man showed little reaction. Only exhaled, just slightly, like a tired person.

"Unnecessary titles are always the first to remain."

Luhai heard the brief exchange and nearly opened his mouth, but ultimately held back.

Aka said nothing this time either.

This place was on an entirely different level from the playful misunderstandings on the street moments ago.

Here, no one read people by appearance first.

If anything, they knew too much and therefore could not easily choose their words.

Seorin asked first.

"Why did you let us in."

The old man looked at Sion this time.

"Because Sern came."

A short answer.

Those words could mean they were only letting Sern in, or that they were tolerating the rest because of Sern. Both readings were possible.

Kael asked low.

"Do you have information on the Elia side."

The old man looked around once before answering.

A checking gaze. The habit of someone measuring, to the very end, who among those present was allowed to know how much.

"I do."

Sion straightened unconsciously.

The old man continued.

"Not the precise location yet. But I know which division she is bound to."

Ater asked immediately.

"Central?"

The old man's eyes went that way, very briefly.

"You would have read it that way already."

That was both an answer and a test.

Ater asked nothing further.

The old man turned his gaze back to Sion.

"Alive."

No one exhaled right away.

If anything, the word was more uncomfortable.

Alive did not mean safe.

Sion asked first.

"Why did they not kill?"

The old man answered very plainly.

"Because the reason to keep alive is larger than the reason to kill."

Aka clenched her fingertips beside.

"That person is in pain."

The old man looked at Aka straight on for the first time.

A trace of surprise crossed that gaze, very faintly.

"You can see."

Aka did not answer right away.

Instead her face only tightened slightly.

A person who cannot explain how they see what they see only grows more uncomfortable the more they try.

Sern cut in.

"Conditions?"

The old man was silent for a while.

That silence felt less like the pause before presenting a deal, and more like choosing how to voice a request that had been deferred for too long.

He finally spoke.

"Meet Elia. Bring her out alive."

Then paused very briefly.

"After that, you must meet one person."

Luhai could not hold back this time.

"Who?"

The old man did not answer that question right away.

Instead he looked at Sern first.

Sern said nothing.

Ater's gaze sank very faintly too.

But neither asked further.

In that brief pause alone, Sion dimly felt the unnamed person was not merely a contact or a safehouse keeper.

Beyond that, no one had yet spoken aloud.

The old man added one last thing.

"I am not telling you to trust us right now. You do not have to trust us. But that person already knows you are coming."

Aka swallowed very small.

Sion asked low.

"How."

The old man answered very briefly this time.

"Because they have been waiting."

Strangely, no one could press further right now.

Sern finally spoke.

"Elia first."

The old man nodded.

"Yes. That is the right order."

Sion could already sense, even inside that short answer, that one more step in the sequence had opened.

The task of saving Elia. And after that, going to meet someone whose face they did not yet know.

The air inside the Empire looked this quiet on the surface, yet beneath that quiet, something may have been prepared for far longer than anyone realized.

Sion closed his eyes briefly and opened them.

And said it quietly.

"We bring Elia out first."

The old man, at those words, lowered his head very faintly for the first time.

"Let us do that."

In that moment, Sion knew.

They had truly begun entering the inside from which it would be hard to return.
