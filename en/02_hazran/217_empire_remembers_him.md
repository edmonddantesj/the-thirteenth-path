# Episode 217. Empire Remembers Him

Once it responded, that thin line spread very quietly, following something inside the wall.

The door did not open right away.

Instead, the air changed first.

Who had breathed more. Who was one step ahead. Who was standing still. A silence that said it would only move after measuring all of it filled the space before the door.

Ater did not withdraw his hand.

Sion looked at his profile.

Ater did not look like someone trying to accomplish something right now. He looked like someone recalling.

Sequences the body knew before memory. Authorization lines the fingertips remembered before thought. How far to go before being read. Where to stop so it looked like permission. The face of someone who had learned all of that far too long ago.

Sern said it very low.

"Your lordship."

Ater did not answer.

Instead, he shifted his fingertip—very faintly.

The light inside the wall trembled once, almost invisibly small.

Three thin lines surfaced briefly below the smooth panel beside the door, then vanished.

Sion saw it and still could not precisely understand what it was.

But Ater understood.

And so did Sern.

Ater spoke for the first time.

"Tighter than before."

Sern received right away.

"Yes. But the skeleton is the same."

"Same. That is what makes it revolting."

What Ater found revolting was not that the door was difficult.

It seemed to be that the order he had once known was still sorting people in nearly the same way.

The light inside the wall spread this time like a very thin circle.

Ater, without moving a single step, said it low.

"It reads three things. Sequence, presence duration, approval trace."

Kael asked by barely a breath.

"Can you open it?"

Ater, instead of answering, scanned below the wall once more.

"It opens. But not as a normal passage."

Sern added low.

"We have to force it open. And the closing time will move up as well."

Luhai scrunched his face small.

"So it is basically a trap?"

Jiwoo wordlessly gripped Luhai's arm once, pressing down.

Meaning: quiet.

Ater spoke without looking away.

"That is how Empire doors have always been. They are more precise at locking people in than at opening."

Aka murmured very small.

"I hate this."

Ater steadied his breath very shallow, then brought his hand close to the wall.

This time, it truly touched.

The instant it did, the thin lights came alive in a flash.

Along the base of the wall, a brightness almost like a solid line spread, and a sleeping structure woke coldly—as though it had been made to verify its credentials too late.

The door hummed, very small.

It did not open.

Instead, from inside, a low and thin metallic sound flowed out—like something checking alignments one by one.

Ater's gaze wavered for an instant.

Truly an instant.

But Sern did not miss it.

"Your lordship."

A slightly stronger voice this time.

Ater said it low.

"I know."

Sern closed his mouth.

But Sion could feel: opening this door was not a simple technical problem for Ater. It was the act of tracing the center of his own past again with his hands.

How to receive approval. How to stop. How to stand like a person permitted to pass.

The fact that his body remembered all of it was what seemed to harden Ater, little by little.

The door made a gap this time—very faintly.

Barely enough for a single finger, if that.

Sern said immediately.

"Now."

Ater, hand still in place, asked.

"Order?"

"Seorin, Kael. Sion, Aka. Luhai, Jiwoo. Serakion. Last is you and me. I will hold back the closing, and your lordship must keep it open to the very end."

Ater nodded short.

"Go."

Seorin and Kael moved first.

The open gap did not look like a door welcoming people. It looked like a gap that would cut you if you did not pass in time.

Neither stopped once as they slipped through the narrow stretch.

Sion and Aka were next.

Sion gave Aka's shoulder a very short push, sending her in first.

The moment he crossed the door, the inside of his arm went tinglingly cold.

This time it was not simply the feeling of being read.

Something was quietly saying: you should not be here.

Sion pressed his breath down as though clenching his teeth.

Did not stop.

On the other side, Aka said it almost inaudibly.

"Colder."

Luhai and Jiwoo followed, then Serakion.

When Serakion passed through the door, the light wavered just slightly more.

Kael immediately looked back. Sern's gaze shifted too.

But the door did not close.

It was still holding—for now.

Only Sern and Ater remained.

Ater was still standing with his hand in place.

Keeping the door open seemed to rest almost entirely on that one hand.

Sern said it very low.

"You have to come now."

Ater did not answer.

Sion, from the other side of the door, saw his profile.

Ater looked more disgusted by the self that could open this door than by the door itself.

Ater finally pulled his hand away.

Simultaneously, Sern grabbed his arm briefly and pulled him inside.

The instant the two crossed the threshold, the light shuddered once, hard.

The door moved from behind—fast, nearly biting shut.

At the last moment, Sern twisted his body and struck one point inside the wall, short.

Metal sparked. The closing door was half a beat late.

Thanks to that gap, both barely slid inside.

When the door sealed completely, the corridor went quiet again—as though nothing had happened.

But everyone knew.

What just happened was twisting the Empire's inner order once and passing through.

Ater said nothing for a while.

Sern did not speak first either.

Sion asked for the first time, low.

"Are you all right?"

Ater did not answer right away.

Instead he looked back at the closed door once.

In that gaze was anger. Exhaustion. And revulsion toward a structure that remained too similar to what he had known long ago.

"I feel filthy."

So honest that for a moment, no one could follow.

But Sion found those words good, in fact.

They meant Ater still could not accept that order in comfortable language.

Sern said it very low.

"Still, it opened."

Ater finally looked at Sern.

"Yes. That is what makes it worse."

Sern lowered his gaze briefly.

He could not have missed what those words meant.

Aka said it very small then.

"Close."

Every gaze turned forward again.

The end of the corridor was quieter than before.

But inside that quiet, something was clearly getting closer.

Aka rubbed her own arm once.

"Elia is here. Really close."

And a moment later, very quietly added.

"The other person too."

This time no one let those words pass.

Sion looked at the corridor's end.

They had only crossed one door, yet the air was already different.

From here, the option of turning back would grow dimmer and dimmer.

But what came first was the sense of having nearly arrived.

Sern turned his body forward again.

"The next stretch is shorter."

Short words.

Everyone understood immediately.

From here, even the gaps to catch breath were shrinking.

Sion clenched and unclenched his hand once.

Elia was right ahead.

And somewhere deeper inside, someone whose face they still did not know was quietly waiting for them.
