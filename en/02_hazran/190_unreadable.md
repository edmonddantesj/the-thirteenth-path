# Episode 190. Unreadable

The guide lights were polite, but a lane that should have opened opened half a beat late, and a barrier panel that should have closed closed early. It closed so early that it tripped its own legion.

One unit caught its foot on the barrier panel's edge and pitched forward, and the one behind ran into its back. A recovery line came by the shortest path and filled the empty spot. A spot already emptied, one more time.

Seorin kept her eyes on that flow.

"Don't just look ahead."

She said it low.

"Watch where the recovery line trips over its own feet."

Sion looked not at the unit in front of him but behind it. Watching where the line got filled when one fell, and which row came over when a row went empty, he touched the floor with a finger.

Here.
Here.

The third was filled before he could touch it.

"The third one's fast."

He said.

"The third is always fast. So don't watch the third. Watch the fourth."

"Where's the fourth?"

"Next to where the third filled. If the third is fast, the fourth is late. Always."

Sion looked beside the spot the third had filled. It was still empty, and he put his finger there.

The old machines knew that beat. Not a young speed, but a slow beat. When one old machine drew back half a step late on purpose, two Serakion units came in at the same moment to fill that empty square.

In that instant another machine shoved one unit's axis from the flank. As the shoved unit slid along the gray wall, scraping its shoulder, the unit following close behind caught its foot in that spot and staggered once, and the row fell out of line.

Serakion tried to make the straightest row. So the moment that row went off, the whole of it tripped on its own feet.

Luhai drove the iron bar into the staggering unit's knee. The bar bent as the unit buckled, and Luhai looked at the bent iron bar.

"How many more can this take?"

"Two."

Kael said from the left axis.

"On the third, your wrist goes."

Luhai shifted his grip on the iron bar so the bent side faced out.

The old machine looked at the iron bar, then at its own arm.

"Please use my arm."

It said.

"It does not bend."

"You use your own arm."

Luhai said.

"Two more left, he said."

The old machine lowered its arm and stood beside Luhai, half a step ahead of him.

Han Jiwoo was on the same axis as the last android, at the drawn-back distance, beside the wall panel. The arms of the two Hazran men holding up the wall panel shook. She watched what he looked at first and what he reacted to first, then spoke short.

"Not the front. Behind."

He looked behind.

"Third row now."

He stood in front of the third row.

"Stop there and the right opens."

When he stopped, the right opened, and Luhai and the old machine went in there.

The gaps between her words grew shorter, and the gaps between his moves too. As Sion saw it, between Han Jiwoo's mouth and the android's feet there was no gap at all.

The last android was on the edge. Every time he came back his shoulder axis made a sound, and his optical eye lost its light.

A flicker.
It came back.
A flicker.

He didn't touch his optical eye with his hand. Han Jiwoo counted the flickers. Four, five.

"Are you okay?"

He didn't answer; he looked at her once with the optical eye that had come back, then looked ahead again.

Han Jiwoo passed the wall panel to a Hazran man, set both empty hands on his shoulder, over the knot, and pressed. The shoulder came up half a knuckle, and when she took her hands away it sank back down.

"I can't fix it now. When it's over."

He was looking ahead.

Seorin drew a breath.

"Open the inside from here."

Her voice stayed low.

"Before the center reads us, we reach it first."

"Luhai."

Luhai looked back.

"Go down."

"Now?"

"Now. Don't leave the lower level empty. Take those two."

Seorin pointed her chin at the two Hazran men holding the wall panel.

"We're short of hands here."

Luhai said.

"Down there is shorter. The wall panel won't work inside. There isn't even room for three people to stand."

Luhai looked at the wall panel, turned his eyes to the narrow inner corridor, then looked at his bent iron bar.

"Got it."

The two Hazran men set the wall panel down and let their arms hang. Luhai looked at Han Jiwoo, then at the last android.

"His shoulder."

"I know. Go."

When Luhai went, the two Hazran men followed him and left the wall panel behind, and Han Jiwoo looked at the panel still standing where it had been set.

Luhai looked back from the corner.

"Jiwoo."

Han Jiwoo looked at him.

"If his shoulder goes, you come down too. Come back together, the two of you."

"Okay."

"That's a promise."

"I said okay."

As Luhai turned the corner, the uneven human footsteps faded, but Serakion's floor didn't give the sound back.

Each axis moved.

On the left, Kael cut a recovery line by one panel. The cut recovery line pushed into the upper drop line, and the pushed drop line slowed the central circulation line. A drop plate overhead came halfway down and stopped. Trembling, it went neither down nor up.

Sern looked at the record panel.

"Realignment is late. One beat."

"And one beat means?"

"One person gets through. Under the drop plate."

"If the plate falls?"

"If it falls, that person is crushed."

Sern said.

Sion looked at the plate trembling halfway down. He drew a breath and didn't let it out.

He went in on that one beat.

He dropped low under the half-stopped drop plate. The plate trembled above his back.
He was through.
He set the tip of the blade to the side seam of the inner bulkhead and pressed.

The bulkhead opened.

Quietly.

There was no tearing sound, no breaking sound. When the wall folded like a path that was meant to open, the guidance tone came.
"The route has been reassigned."
It announced the path that must never open as an open path, in the same voice.

What lay beyond came into view.

Deeper, and narrower. The walls were smoother and the light lower. The three rows of boundary lights on the floor ran so far that no end was in sight.

While Sion swallowed, the hand on the blade dropped and came back up.

Sern spoke very low.

"Core approach line, opening."

"Have they read us?"

Seorin asked.

Sern looked at the record panel and listened to the guidance tone, then looked at the boundary lights across the corridor; their color didn't change.

"Not yet."

He said.

"They were the side that read first. Now we are the ones who are in first."

Seorin didn't nod. She looked at the open bulkhead, and past it.

"Once we're in, we can't get out."

She said it to no one in particular.

"I know."

Sion said.

"And down below?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"Luhai's there."

Seorin said.

"The first and second recovery hands too. The wall-side machine too."

"The children?"

"Aka's there."

Han Jiwoo looked at the wall panel she had left standing, and left it there.

Ater came down from above without brushing the dust off his shoulder. Sern tucked the record panel under his arm, and Kael came over from the left axis and tossed away the fragment of panel in his hand. The two old machines stood behind.

The last android stood beside Han Jiwoo. His shoulder axis made a sound, and Han Jiwoo heard it.

"The front isn't yours. Behind. After they're shaken."

He turned his head and looked at her, then looked ahead again.

Seorin crossed the bulkhead first. Sion, Sern, Ater, Kael, the two old machines, Han Jiwoo. The last android was last. His shoulder touched the edge of the bulkhead, but he didn't stop.

Behind them the bulkhead closed quietly. The guidance tone came once more and announced the closed path as closed. This time it was right.

In the inner corridor there were only the three rows of boundary lights, and nothing came. Sern opened the record panel again, but there was nothing to write, so he wrote nothing.

"It's not reading us?"

Sion asked.

"It is reading."

Sern said.

"It has not yet decided what we are."

"And when it decides?"

"When it decides, they will come."

Sern said.

"Not now. It does not block what it does not know. It counts first, then blocks."

"How long will that take?"

"I do not know. We are the first to come in here."

Kael rapped on the corridor wall with a knuckle. The wall was thick enough to make no sound, like the walls on the lower level.

"The walls are the same."

He said.

"The walls are the same."

Sern said.

"What does the reading is different."

Sion looked at the end of the corridor, but there was no end. His own shadow wasn't on the three rows of light; the light was coming from below. He lifted his foot and set it down, and the boundary light under his foot didn't change color. Not yet.

The last android stood beside him, lifted his foot and set it down. The boundary light under him changed color once and went back.

"You, it reads."

Sion said.

Sern wrote one line on the record panel.

"That android is the only one they know."

He said.

"Us, they do not know yet."

Han Jiwoo looked at the color that had gone back under his foot. She set her own foot beside it, but the color didn't change. Their two feet were side by side, one read and one not.
