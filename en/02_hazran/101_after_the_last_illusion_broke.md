# Episode 101. After the Last Illusion Broke

Setting up the seventh line, Sion noticed they were short at the back.

Of the clumps he had cut into threes, the third from the back had only two.

"Where'd the other one go?"

Nobody answered. Instead, a few of them looked the same way.

Sion looked there.

It was the collapse.

Three people were pressed against it. They were lifting stones away by hand.

Sion ran.

"Don't."

One of the three answered without even raising his head.

"There's someone under there."

"I know. But if you dig now…"

"I know."

Sion's words stuck.

These people weren't doing it without knowing. They had heard what Zahir said earlier, or even if they hadn't, they would have seen it. If that pile was gone, the enemy came in.

"Then why are you doing it?"

"Our hands are free."

Sion didn't know how to take that answer.

Only then did the man raise his head.

"You can hear it from here."

"Hear what?"

"The knocking from underneath."

Sion knelt and put his ear to the pile.

For a long time there was nothing.

Then it came, once. Small and dull. It came from inside the stone, so it seemed to reach his cheek rather than his ear.

Sion couldn't get up right away.

What he could only hear with a hand on the hull wall, he could hear like this, here. The people pressed against this pile had been hearing it all along.

"You want us to hear this and just stand there?"

the man said.

Sion couldn't look him in the eye.

Nasim came. His voice was so far gone that only half of it came out.

"Get out of there."

The three didn't move.

"I said get out."

"It's better here."

"Get in line."

"If we get in line, it's not our turn."

At that, Nasim stopped.

Only then did Sion understand too. These three were in the third clump from the back. The ship had to lift three more times before their turn. Three times meant it wouldn't come today.

Luhai had come up beside him.

"Twelve,"
he said, low.

"What?"

"The people on that pile."

Sion looked again. He had thought it was three, but it wasn't. There were more behind the pile. Counting the ones sitting and moving small pieces by hand, it was twelve.

"When did it get like this?"

"Kept growing since the ship lifted."

Then Luhai said one more thing.

"All from the back of the line."

"All of them?"

"Not one came from the front."

Luhai pointed at the top of the pile with a finger.

"And at that speed, it won't work."

"What do you mean it won't?"

"I've been watching. One person moves one piece at a time. Twelve on it isn't twelve. There's no room, so only four can get a hand in."

"Then how long will it take?"

Luhai didn't answer. It was the face of someone who had started counting and stopped.

"I tried counting, but I figured I shouldn't, so I quit."

Then Luhai went back toward the line.

He went back and pushed frightened people on the back. While he pushed, he said only three things.

"Look ahead."

"Go now."

"It's okay—and if it's not, go anyway."

That last one stayed with Sion a long time.

It meant you had to go even knowing full well it wasn't okay. This kid must have said it any number of times today.

It took Sion no time at all to understand it.

The ones digging were all people who couldn't board today. The ones who would board stood in line, and the ones who couldn't had come here. Nobody told them to split that way, but they had split on their own.

Zahir came.

The twelve didn't stop their hands. They knew Zahir had come, and still they didn't stop.

"Stop."

No one stopped.

"I said stop."

Only then did a few of them stop. They rested their stopped hands on their knees and looked at Zahir.

"Dig and they come in,"
Zahir said.

"We know."

"If you know, why are you doing it?"

There was no answer.

Then Nasim spoke, beside Zahir.

"Leave them."

Zahir looked at Nasim.

"What did you just say?"

"I said leave them."

"Do you know how many people's price that is?"

"No."

Nasim pressed on his throat once. He had to, to make a sound.

"I'm saying it without knowing."

At that answer, Sion stopped breathing.

This man had set prices all his life. It was the first time Sion had heard him say he was speaking without knowing.

"You're the one who said nothing goes if the sums don't stand up,"
Zahir said.

"That was about the market."

"This is a market too."

"Then I'm done with the market."

Zahir didn't take that up right away.

After a long while, he asked,

"Can those twelve dig through to him today?"

Sion answered.

"He says they can't."

"Who says?"

"The one guarding the corridor. He says only one can fit into that width at a time."

Zahir looked at the pile. He looked for a long time.

Then he said,

"Dig from the top only."

One of the twelve raised his head.

"From the top?"

"Don't touch the corridor side. Top down, only."

"Then it will take three times as long."

"I know."

"Then we won't reach him."

"I know."

"Then why do you tell us to do it?"

For the first time, Zahir was slow to answer.

"If you leave the corridor side alone, they don't get in."

"That we know. Not that."

"There's nothing besides that."

Sion knew that was a lie. If there were no reason, he wouldn't have needed to say dig from the top; he could just have said don't.

"Make them take their hands off where they can hear it,"
Zahir said,
"and next time I put them in line, they won't stand."

Sion looked at Zahir's face from the side.

This man was still watching the board. Letting them dig was placement too.

Zahir said that and turned away. As he turned, he left Sion one more word.

"Watch the line."

Sion stayed where he was.

The twelve dug again. From the top down. It was slower than before. The stones up top were bigger and had nothing to grip, so it took two to move one.

Each time a stone came out, what had been above it slid down into the space little by little. So it didn't shrink by as much as they dug. Lift out ten and it looked like seven had gone, and the other three filled back in. Nobody said it out loud, but they all knew it with their hands.

The powder had all settled, so no dust rose where they dug. Instead, everything that came up from below showed just as it was. Pieces of broken wall, snapped cables, stubs of metal plate that had been a barrier until a while ago. The things Hazran had made by cutting up its own heart, mixed together in no order.

Sion watched for a while, then went over to Nasim.

"Why did you say to leave them?"

Nasim was looking toward the board.

"If they don't dig now, what do they do?"

"Get in line."

"And do what, standing in line?"

"They know they're not boarding today,"
Nasim said.
"Tell them to stand there knowing it, and that kills them faster."

"Then if they dig, they live?"

"No."

Nasim pressed on his throat again.

"If they dig, today goes by differently."

"That's all?"

"That's a lot."

Then Nasim looked at Sion.

"You count too, don't you."

"Yes."

"What changes when you count?"

Sion started to answer and stopped. Not because he had no answer, but because this man had just said that answer for him.

Sion held on to it a long time.

The hand that set prices had, for the first time today, let something be that had no price. And it wasn't because he was soft or weak. This man was still counting something, even now. It just wasn't heads he was counting.

Sion went back to the line.

"Three."

Three went up. The third clump from the back was still two.

Sion didn't fill the gap.

To fill it, he would have to call someone over from the pile, and if he called them, that person would stand in line knowing they couldn't board today.

"Two."

The two went up.

Sion went to the board. The ship marks were still six. It hadn't lifted yet.

Five runs.

Beside them, next to the short mark, he scratched one more. It was the second hour.

Sion went over to Aka.

"What is it now?"

The child counted with her hand still against the wall.

"Twelve."

Before, it had been eleven.

Sion took the number and went over to the pile.

The man from before was still there. The backs of his hands were scraped raw.

"How many does it knock?"
Sion asked.

The man answered while moving stones.

"Twelve."

"You were counting?"

"Everybody counts."

Sion looked at the people on the pile. Their hands were on the stones, and a good few of their mouths were moving a little. They made no sound.

"And if it goes up?"

"Then we dig harder."

"Then you'll reach him even less."

"I know."

Sion started counting how many times he had heard those two words today, and stopped.

Here, everyone knew. They knew, and did it anyway.

Sion stood there and looked at the two numbers together.

There were twelve on the pile. The gap between knocks from below was twelve.

The same number, and not the same at all.

Twelve were counting the twelve.
