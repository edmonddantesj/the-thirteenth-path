# Episode 136. The Hidden Path Breathes

They saw the path actually open only after that evening had fully passed.

It did not open all the way; it took one brief breath.

All day the old machines said nothing; they neither denied what Han Jiwoo and Aka had seen nor showed it to them. Two of them took turns standing in front of the wall. When one machine left, another came, and the spot was never empty.

Sion watched the changeover, but there was no place to put it, so he didn't mark it on the board.

All day Han Jiwoo didn't look at the wall; she sat in front of her parts and never once raised her head. Sion saw that she never once raised her head. That was her way of watching.

Even if the sun had gone fully down, there was no sun on this layer. The yellow lamps went out one by one; someone was turning them off. Meanwhile the noise of the day settled.

The old machine came over toward Sion's group.

This time it called not only Sion but Han Jiwoo, Aka, Sern, and Luhai too, who tagged along to the end without saying a word.

Luhai had left the child with the old woman to come. The child watched him and cried, but Luhai didn't look back. The old woman held the child and put her back to the wall, and the child watched Luhai's back over her shoulder. Luhai looked back once as he turned the corner of the passage, and only when the child stopped crying did he turn away.

The machine neither stopped Luhai nor gave him leave; it turned and spoke briefly.

"Now that you have seen it, you can hardly pretend not to know."

"No, we can't."

Luhai answered.

The machine said nothing more.

Seorin and Ater stayed behind. When Seorin looked at Sion, Sion nodded, and Seorin turned toward the dark exit where Ater was standing. Kael was in front of the cargo bay.

The six of them went toward the blocked wall.

It was the hour when no one moved. In every passage they went through, someone was asleep, and those who weren't asleep had their eyes closed. The closed eyes followed the six sets of footsteps.

The old Hazran man wasn't asleep; he had his plate on his knees. Sixteen lines. All of them scratched in. He watched the six of them pass in front of him and, without a word, turned the plate over.

The machine standing in front of the wall started to step aside, then looked at the old machine. When the old machine nodded, it did step aside, but it didn't go far.

In the daytime it had been an old metal surface, long sealed, but up close there was a sound. Air pressed once, deep inside, and then let go, a sound like breathing.

Han Jiwoo heard it first and pressed her ear flat to the wall.

"That's not a hollow-wall sound."

Her voice was low.

Sern looked into his record panel and found nothing on it. He lowered the record panel and listened.

Instead of answering, the old machine laid its hands on several grooves along the edge of the wall.

Six places. Two at the top, two at the sides, two at the bottom.

It didn't press or open them; it only rested its hands there. Han Jiwoo watched the hands, and then watched the order in which they were placed. Top, bottom, sides. Her eyes were memorizing it.

Sern raised the record panel.

"Do not write it down."

The old machine spoke with its hands still in place.

"I will memorize it."

"Do not do that either."

Sern lowered the record panel and closed his eyes, then opened them. He had already memorized it, but the old machine wasn't watching him; it was looking at the wall.

"What happens if it is memorized?"

Sern asked.

"If the one who memorized it is caught, the path dies."

"I will forget it."

"You cannot."

The old machine said.

"So do not get caught."

Sern didn't answer; he tucked the record panel he was holding under his arm.

One side of the wall shuddered.

It didn't open wide; a thin gap opened, just wide enough to let one person's shoulders through. The edge of the gap was worn. It was the spot Han Jiwoo had touched during the day.

Air leaked out through that narrow gap.

Unlike the air inside the old layer, it was colder, drier. Luhai rubbed his arm over his sleeve.

Aka spoke, low, almost a breath.

"Smells like coming and going."

Han Jiwoo, too, muttered quietly the moment she caught the smell.

"There's outside air on it."

It was not the well-scrubbed smell of Serakion's interior, nor the long-stagnant smell of this layer. It was the smell of metal that had passed through somewhere and come back, and something else sat on top of it.

Han Jiwoo brought her nose close to the gap.

"Oil. New oil. Not from here."

The tip of her nose stayed at the gap.

At that, the old machine moved its optical eyes, but it didn't deny it.

Sion looked inside the gap.

There was almost no light, but nor was it completely dead darkness. Somewhere farther in, a single line kept flicking on and off as if breathing, at uneven intervals. The same intervals as this layer's yellow lamps.

Sern spoke low.

"There is a signal. It is not fixed. It connects briefly and then cuts off."

"Which way."

Sion asked.

"It is coming this way."

Sern answered.

"This side is not sending. It is receiving. From in there, toward here."

Luhai frowned in front of the narrow gap.

"It's real. They keep this hidden like this and go in and out?"

He started to reach into the gap, then stopped. The old machine was watching, so he drew back the hand he had been putting out.

The old machine made no reply to what he had said, and spoke very low.

"It is a path that must close if it is found, and must live if it is not."

"And if you close it, what about whoever's inside?"

Luhai asked.

The old machine didn't answer, and its hands stayed on the wall.

"Have you ever closed it?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"Twice."

The old machine answered.

"Was anyone inside?"

"Once."

"And the second time."

"They were outside."

"So they came back."

"We opened it for them."

The old machine looked at one of the six, the lower one, a single groove in the wall.

"That hand did."

The deep scratches beside that groove had been made from the inside.

Han Jiwoo looked at the shoulder-wide gap, then down at her own shoulders.

Sion felt the cold, dry air from inside the gap again, but he didn't take out the metal plate; he only rested his hand on his pocket.

From the darkness farther in, a single scrape sounded.

It was short. Metal brushing metal.

Everyone stopped at once.

Luhai's body went stiff first, and Sern raised the record panel. While Sion held his breath, Han Jiwoo's hand went to her tool pouch. Aka didn't move.

The old machine kept its hand where it was at the gap and said, very low.

"Do not move."

Its voice was different. Not dry, but drawn tight. The finger resting on the wall pressed hard into the groove once, and the gap began to narrow, then stopped; the machine put no more force into its finger.

Sern looked at the record panel.

"It is getting closer."

He whispered.

"That is not a signal. Those are footsteps."

Aka watched the dark past the gap, then said, so quietly it could barely be heard.

"It's not coming from inside."

Sion looked at her.

Aka went on without moving her eyes.

"It's something coming back."

The old machine's finger came away from the groove, but the gap didn't narrow any further. It lowered both hands from the wall.

The scrape came once more, closer, and then there were footsteps. Not a person's footsteps, and not the sound of a Serakion machine either. One foot was dragging.

Dragging, touching down, dragging.

Hearing that sound, Han Jiwoo took her hand off the tool pouch.

"It's a leg. One leg's damaged."

The old machine looked at Han Jiwoo.

"Which side."

"Left. The dragging's on the left."

The old machine turned its body toward the gap.

"If it is the left, it is the second."

It said it to no one in particular.

"The second is coming back."

Luhai heard that.

"And the first."

The old machine didn't answer. Of its two lowered hands, one finger was trembling, and the trembling didn't stop even while Han Jiwoo watched.

The dragging sound came right up to the inside of the gap and stopped.

In the dark inside the gap, a light came on. One optical eye, an uneven light. It was this layer's light. Light that had been outside and come back.

That light looked at the six beyond the gap, looked at the old machine, and stopped.

The old machine laid its hands on the gap again. Six places.

Top, bottom, sides.

The gap began to widen.
