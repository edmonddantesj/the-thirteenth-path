# Episode 194. Out of Step

Before the third gate, Seorin raised her hand.

Nine sets of footsteps stopped at the same moment.

The corridor was narrower and longer than the two stretches before it. The walls were smooth and the light steady, and along the floor ran a single straight guide line, narrower than a human foot. Sion did not put his foot on the line; he stood beside it.

Nothing had come out yet. Inside the wall, a light had moved this way and stopped, and it waited.

Seorin spoke low.

"The same move won't work."

"What's different?"

Sion asked.

"If he shows first, they bind him first. Up to now they recounted because of him. Now they count him first and start from there."

The last android heard that and looked at the light inside the wall; the light had come to him. When he stepped back half a step, the light followed.

Watching the light spread across the wall, Seorin went on.

"Now they bind first. Push head-on again, and we walk into that calculation."

Sern let out a short breath.

"Then we must change it."

"We have to."

Seorin said it very quietly.

"But what we need right now is to throw that inner habit out of step one more time."

"What habit?"

"Don't know. That's why we're standing here."

Behind them, one of the old machines suddenly raised its head, and the other raised its head the same way, toward below.

Static brushed past. It was a low, short sound mixed into the vibration of the corridor floor, so to human ears it sounded like the wall humming, but the two machines' optical eyes stayed on it.

Han Jiwoo spoke low.

"What is it?"

The machine answered.

"From below. Sending."

"Who?"

"The first. The second too. The three together."

As one of the machines took in the static, its optical eyes blinked to a beat. Sern watched the blinking, but it was a beat he did not know, and he could not write it down.

"What's it saying?"

Seorin asked.

The machine put it into human speech, short and broken up.

"Androids, beat can be shaken. Briefly. Serakion robot class, cannot be stopped. Delay, error, path misreading only. Based on a reject."

"What's a reject?"

"One from the disposal yard. Finished ones may go deeper. That is what came."

Seorin closed her eyes and opened them.

"Ater."

"Yes."

"What the first said during the scouting. The outer-level robots."

Ater answered at once.

"It said they push a line to the end once it is set. That if you twist it, they are slow to recover."

"And the inner ones?"

"That they recount a missed line at once and overturn the sequence. That pushing several responses off at once works better than going head-on."

"Higher up?"

"That they read first and close in first."

Seorin looked at the light waiting, stopped, inside the wall, and at the corridor before her.

"This is the second kind. The kind that recalculates a missed line right away and fills it."

Sion asked briefly.

"Sure?"

"Yeah. So this time we don't shake one; we have to tangle two together. Make the androids and the robots get in the way of each other's recovery."

At the very back, Kael laughed, short, really short.

"Been waiting for that."

He was already scanning the grooves and fold lines along the side of the corridor, the places where the blocking plates would come down. Slowing what closed had been his job since they first laid out the plan, and when he set his toe against one of the side grooves, it went in.

Han Jiwoo reworked the signals with her fingers.

Left.
Stop.
Back.

While the last android watched her fingers, Sern counted the response speed inside the wall, moving only his lips. Sion dropped low, waiting only for the moment the center line went empty. When the last android stood not at the front but half a step back, the light inside the wall followed him back half a step.

Seorin spoke.

"Don't try to force it open wrong from the front. Make it misread the guide line once, and on the next beat, shake the androids. Don't touch the two separately; make them tangle each other's calculations."

The wall surfaces on both sides of the corridor parted thinly.

Two androids came out first.

They had human outlines, neater than before and emptier. The angle of their arms was the same and their stride the same, and their faces were smooth, with no light in them either. Han Jiwoo looked at those faces, then at the faint lines left on the last android's face.
The same body, a different face.

A beat later, two Serakion robots came out.

Pushing plate and closing plate, joints hidden inside: nothing about them tried to look human. Their layered surfaces pushed the light dully away. They had the same shape as the reject seen in the hidden level, only these had not been cut.

Seorin spoke briefly.

"First beat, the guide line. Second, the androids. Last, the center line when it empties."

The two old machines gave off a low static at once, and the first signal ran along the floor as vibration. Just enough to slip into the instant the guide line rechecked its own reference.

The robots were the first to waver.

The left robot did not close the line it had to block right away; it read its reference once more. It neither stopped nor slowed, but with that one rereading, the blocking line meant to cut across the center of the corridor slid half a square to the side.

Half a square.

"Now."

With Seorin's word, the second static settled onto the androids.

The right android's step came half a beat late. When the left android tried to fill the gap, its face surface turned inward, and their corrections got in each other's way. The two that should have meshed in the same instant checked each other first.

One beat.

Sion dropped low and stepped across the emptied center line, the guide line, at a slant.

In.

Kael touched the auxiliary blocking plate coming down on the outside. He didn't stop it, only twisted its closing angle, and the plate halted at a slant; the inner line stayed empty a little longer.

Seorin closed in beside it and grazed the outside of an android's arm with the back of her blade, and the android's arm was pushed half a span. From behind, Sern counted the robot's second rereading.

"It is coming again!"

The robot did not let the first error stand; it overturned the sequence at once and came to fill the missed line. The robot on the opposite side twisted round and began to lower a vertical barrier from top to bottom, deep in the corridor.
Fast.

Han Jiwoo spoke through gritted teeth.

"It's fast."

"That's why we need the second."

Seorin answered.

The static from the two machines behind them crossed and overlapped, one on the barrier's reference line, the other on the androids' line of sight. The thin light running along the robot's surface jumped once, from bottom to top. One android looked at the last android first instead of Sion, and its face surface turned that way.

The alignment could not lock onto one side.
Two targets to block, two lines to close.
The barrier failed to cut the center and came down half a square to the side.
The two androids' face surfaces failed to line up. One on Sion, one on him.

That half square.
That half beat.

This time the last android did not step out first. Behind the gap the wavering had opened, he moved late into the center line Sion and Seorin had emptied, from the side line, at a less bound angle. Up front the two responses were already crossing each other, so Serakion could not make him its reference.

Sern spoke low.

"Now we can cross."

Sion pushed his shoulder into the gap in the barrier first, his collar scraping on the edge, and Seorin followed. Han Jiwoo rolled in along the outer line Kael had kept empty; the moment her shoulder hit the floor, she was up again.

Ater came down from above and followed. Sern came in with the record panel pressed to his chest, and the two old machines came after him. The last android crossed onto the inner line half a step late, and his shoulder axis caught once. Kael was last. When he drew his toe out of the groove, the plate came down behind him.

Metal rang, short and thin.

The beat was coming back into step. While the two androids closed their error faster than at first, the two robots locked the missed line again.

Sion braced one knee on the inner floor and looked back. The barrier that had come down half a square off finally found its place and reached the floor. Beyond it, the two androids stood looking at the spot the last android had slipped out of. Their two face surfaces lined up exactly.

A beat late.

Han Jiwoo looked past the barrier at the two faceless ones, the two looking at the same place. Then she checked the last android's shoulder knot; it was unchanged.

"We're past the second kind."

Seorin said.

"Next is higher up."

"The ones that read first and close in first."

Ater said.

Sern wrote one line on the record panel, then drew a short line beside it and wrote "half a square."

"The signal from below was right."

He spoke.

"The reading from the reject held. The finished ones were the same, up to that point."

"Up to that point."

"Yes. Up to that point."

One of the old machines sent a short static downward, but nothing came back. The machine did not wait; it looked ahead.
