# Episode 200. Empty Throne

The last door opened quietly.

Before Sion could set his blade tip to it, before Seorin could raise her hand, the door parted inward.
Without a sound, with nothing blocking the way, it waited, open.

Sion stood at the threshold with his blade raised, the tip of it hanging in the air.
He lowered the blade.

Seorin went one step in first and stopped.
Sern, Han Jiwoo, Kael, Ater and the last android followed her in and stopped.

No one could speak right away.

It was wider than any level they had passed through, the ceiling high and the pillars straight, and light flowed quietly through it.
On the dustless floor, structural lines were spread at even intervals.
There were no scratches on the walls, no footprints on the floor.

Not nine.
Seven sets of footprints should have been the first ever pressed there. With no dust, none were pressed.

Sern put his hand to the floor and lifted it, but no handprint came up.

"It does not read."

He said.

"Nothing here reads."

"Why?"

"It is a place where nothing to read ever comes in."

Sion went a few steps farther in, and his footsteps rang clearly up to the ceiling and came back.
His own footsteps, back to him.
He stopped. The ringing stopped.

Han Jiwoo spoke low.

"…This is the end?"

Seorin did not answer; she was looking straight ahead, at the highest seat.

The figure was there.

It sat atop a tall, chair-shaped structure.
Its neat clothes had no creases, and its hands rested on its knees at the same height.
Its fingertips were transparent, so that in the light it showed. Its whole body.
Its head was turned this way, exactly toward the door the seven had come in by.

When Sion took a step to the side, the figure's head followed, exactly, with no interval.
He stopped. The head stopped too.

Closer, they could see more.
Its chest did not move; there was no breath, and its eyes did not blink.
The hands on its knees had never once changed place.
The chair-shaped structure had no handprints; the armrests were not worn, and the seat had not sunk.

Ater stood below the figure and looked up for a long time, then spoke upward in the words he had used at home.

"I request approval."

The figure's head turned to him.

"Approved."

There was no interval.

Sern's finger could not fold.

Ater spoke again.

"What have you approved?"

"Approved."

"I have requested nothing."

"Approved."

Ater's hand came down, but he did not take his eyes off the figure.

"This is not an approving authority. Only the proxy body remains."

Sern spoke very low, without raising his record panel.

"Response… shallow."

"How shallow?"

Seorin asked.

"As shallow as a guidance tone."

Sern said.

"The guidance tone on the lower levels. 'The route has been reassigned.' It is the same depth as that."

"So it wasn't that thing talking, it was—"

Han Jiwoo said.

"It was the guidance tone talking."

Sern said.

"A guidance tone shaped like a person."

Han Jiwoo looked at the figure's mouth. It had moved.
"Approved."
Only the mouth.

Kael clenched his teeth.

"This makes no sense."

No one argued.
Kael went right up to the figure, to the foot of the structure, and looked up at the hands on its knees.
He raised his hand and struck the structure with his palm, and the sound rang out.
The figure did not move; its head was turned exactly toward him.
When Kael lowered his palm, it was red.

"What's hitting it going to do?"

Seorin said.

"It's acting like it didn't get hit."

Kael said.

"It doesn't even know it got hit."

"There's nothing in it to know with."

Seorin said.

Kael stepped back from the structure, but he could not turn away.

For a while Han Jiwoo could say nothing; then she spoke like a breath.

"…We came all this way for this?"

No one answered, and the figure stayed where it was.
It was a polite face. Not a face without feeling.
A face with no room for feeling to enter.

Han Jiwoo looked at that face with the same eyes that had looked at the androids' smooth surfaces, and it was different.
Those surfaces had room for lines to be drawn, but this face had none.

"It has a face."

She said.

"So why is there nowhere to draw?"

No one could answer.
The figure's face, as Ater saw it, had everything: eyes, mouth, nose.

"Because everything is there."

He said.

"There is nowhere left empty."

Outside the hall, beyond the door, the aristocrat robots stood halted and did not come in.
One unit's finger, the same finger that had trembled earlier, kept trembling and did not stop.
The other units did not move, having no command.
The thing that should give commands had only said "Approved."

Ater looked at the trembling finger beyond the door.

"What are they waiting for?"

Sern said.

"The next request."

Ater said.

"There is no one left to make the request. They were the ones who used to make it."

"They're not coming."

Sion said.

"No reason to."

Seorin said.

"That thing didn't tell them to come."

"And us coming in?"

Sion asked.

"It didn't tell us not to, either. The door opened, didn't it? That thing opened it. Or nobody did."

Sion looked back at the door; it was still open.

The last android walked out to the very front, below the figure, beside Ater, and looked up.
On his face, the trace of what he had sent earlier still remained as a faint light.
For a while he did not move; then he slowly raised a hand toward the figure's face, not close enough to touch, a knuckle's width short.
It stopped.

Han Jiwoo looked at that hand.
Her own hand had done that, the day she first stopped beside an android's face, a knuckle's width away.
The same distance.

The figure did not respond to the hand. Its head did not turn toward it.
It was not a request.

His hand stayed there.

One beat.
Two.
Three.

There was no warmth. He knew it, and still he did not withdraw his hand.

Four.

Han Jiwoo stood beside him.

"There's nothing there."

She said.

He did not withdraw his hand.

Five.

He reached a little farther and stopped half a knuckle short.
The figure did not respond, but he left his hand there.

Sion watched that hand, then lowered his head. He had no shadow on the floor.
The light came from every side, and there was no room for a shadow.

Seorin opened her mouth a beat later than usual.

"…No one."

The hall gave those short words back from the ceiling.

No one.
No one.

Fainter each time.

While Sern could say nothing at all, Han Jiwoo bit her lip until it bled.
Kael stood below the structure and could not look away.
Ater looked at the figure's transparent fingertips, then at his own. Calluses had set in where he had gripped his blade.

The last android still had his hand raised.

From behind, beyond the door, from below, sounds came up like a late wave.
Feet stopping, hands bracing on walls, the small sound of metal scraping metal.
The sounds of things looking at their own hands came up, each a beat apart.

Han Jiwoo heard those sounds and looked at the figure, but the figure did not hear them.
It was not a request.

"Down there everything's changing."

She said.

"Up here nothing changes."

"There's nothing to change."

Seorin said.

Sern looked at the figure once more.

"Can it be shut off?"

"What?"

"That."

Ater shook his head.

"There will be no place to shut it off. If the line is cut, it will only stop speaking."

"So we leave it like that?"

"We leave it like that."

Seorin said.

"If no one comes up, that thing is nothing. We just tell them not to come up."

"Tell who?"

"Everyone."

Sion drew a slow breath and spoke very low, to himself.

"For this…"

He could not carry it any further. His mouth stayed open, then closed.
No one finished the words for him.

The hall was perfect. The light did not slip out of line, and the floor did not shake.
The figure in its place had its head turned exactly toward Sion.
Because he was the last one who had spoken.

Kael looked at that head.

"It's looking at you because you talked."

"I know."

"And if you don't talk?"

Sion closed his mouth, yet the figure's head stayed turned toward him.
Until the next words came.

The last android slowly lowered his hand and rested it on his own chest plate.
In front of the figure, he did not move.
The light on his face grew a little brighter, but the figure's face stayed the same.
The two faced each other.
Only one of them was looking.
