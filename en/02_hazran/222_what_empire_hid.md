# Episode 222. What Empire Hid

The way back had to be shorter than the way in.

Shorter did not mean easier.

The opposite, in fact.

Going in, the bodies had been light. Only the calculations needed to be right.

Now, holding both Elia and the nameless man, they had to exit backward through the Empire's inner order as it began to close.

Sion held Elia from the left. Aka attached on the opposite side.

The nameless man was passed to Sern and Ater's side.

More precisely, he had to be.

Elia alone was already a heavy load for Sion and Aka.

Even so, Elia refused to fully hand over her weight—holding her center shared to the end, like someone who would not become a person being dragged.

"All right?"

Sion asked very low.

Elia steadied one breath before answering.

"Not all right. But I can move."

Too much like Elia. Sion nearly smiled.

The nameless man's side was different again.

He spoke little. Yet he wore the face of someone who kept reading how this entire corridor was operating.

Sern held one of his arms. Ater supported from the other side to keep the steps from breaking.

It was a strange sight.

The heir of Valkar and the person who had long attended Valkar—together supporting a confined man whose name had not yet been revealed. By any measure, not an ordinary scene.

But right now, there was no time to think beyond that.

Seorin raised her hand from the front.

Everyone stopped immediately.

From a distance, two short, hard metallic sounds. Different in grain from the routine maintenance-line noise.

Kael said it low first.

"It changed."

Sern answered short.

"Yes. They have started looking."

Elia, between Sion and Aka, was regulating her breathing. Walking, but the body was visibly near multiple limits.

Still, she did not walk like someone being dragged.

The nameless man was the same.

Steps slow. Body stiff. Looking as though one bump against the wall would topple him. Yet strangely, the gaze did not waver at all.

Even while walking, he seemed to be reading—wall surfaces, distances between doors, air vibration, spacing between everyone's footsteps.

Luhai muttered very small.

"Both of them are really something…"

This time Jiwoo did not hit him. Probably because everyone was thinking the same thing.

Sern moved again.

"Short this time. We cannot stop."

Seorin and Kael took the front. Serakion held the rear more firmly. Ater glanced at the nameless man once, then returned his gaze to reading the forward structure.

Sion felt, even in that short flow: this was not simple flight.

At the first turn, three white-uniformed maintenance personnel appeared in the distance.

Seorin pivoted immediately. Everyone slid into the shadow along the inner wall.

Elia's breathing wavered as though about to collapse. Sion gripped tighter right away.

The three maintenance workers did not simply pass this time.

One paused roughly mid-corridor.

Sion held his breath.

That brief pause was different from the uniform Imperial movement before.

The other side seemed to have faintly sensed that something was off.

Ater said it almost inaudibly.

"Do not lower your gaze."

Not clear who it was directed at, but everyone understood immediately.

In moments like these, what is more dangerous than a body hiding is a body that shows it is hiding.

A few seconds later, the maintenance workers moved again.

No one detached from the wall until they had fully receded.

Sern said it very low.

"Two segments from here. First, the approval door. Second, the outer auxiliary corridor."

Kael asked short.

"Both still active?"

"Active." Sern answered. "That is the problem."

Elia murmured almost like a breath.

"A living door is the worst kind."

The nameless man's mouth moved, very faintly.

That was probably agreement.

When they reached the approval door, Ater stepped forward again.

Unlike entering, this time the door also seemed to know something.

The light came alive faster. The thin lines on the wall trembled more tightly.

Ater said it low.

"The record remains."

Sern answered.

"Then the record has to be overwritten."

Ater steadied a short breath.

"Getting dirty one more time."

Elia said it very low.

"You already came in."

At those words, Ater only nodded—very short, this time.

He raised his hand and pressed the wall beside the door.

Light came alive. Lines moved. The door did not open right away.

Instead, this time, a silence fell as though the door was counting the number of people first.

Sion felt in that moment: the door was not sensing the nine of them, but first sensing the two people who should not be there.

Sern said it very low.

"Elia and the other one first."

At those words, Luhai's eyes went wide, but again he did not open his mouth.

Sion and Aka held Elia. Ater and Sern held the nameless man.

The door opened—very narrow.

Width for one person at a time, passing quickly.

Elia went first.

In that moment, the light flowing beneath the door distorted once.

Sion knew immediately. This was not a normal passage.

Still, stopping meant the end.

Elia reached the other side. The nameless man followed right after. The moment he crossed the threshold, the light cut out entirely for an instant, then reconnected.

Ater exhaled like a barely suppressed curse.

"Going."

Next, Sion and Aka. Then Luhai and Jiwoo. Then Seorin and Kael. Last, Serakion with Ater and Sern.

The door closed faster than when they had entered.

If Sern had not shoved his body through at the last moment, and Ater had not twisted the nearly closing wall briefly to delay it, someone might have been cut.

After the door sealed, everyone's breathing fell out of sync at once.

That brief silence felt like the longest moment of the entire escape.

Then Sern said.

"Done. Only the outer corridor remains."

The instant those words ended, a low alarm tone sounded from a distance.

Not loud. Not blaring.

Still, everyone could understand what it meant.

The Empire was now beginning to realize that something had gotten out.

Luhai nearly whispered.

"Wow. Is it actually time to run?"

Jiwoo wordlessly pushed Luhai's back once.

Meaning: run.

Sern led. Seorin and Kael cleared the sides. Serakion held the rear.

Sion and Aka held Elia. Ater and Sern held the nameless man. Together they pushed forward through order on the verge of collapse.

The final auxiliary corridor felt longer than when they had entered.

The actual distance was short.

Short, yet in that shortness, too much was crumbling.

Elia stumbled once in the middle. The nameless man had to lean on the wall, very briefly.

Still, neither said the word stop.

In that moment, Elia said it very low instead.

"Almost there."

Sion could not tell who the reassurance was for.

Herself. The nameless man beside. Or everyone.

When the hidden perimeter access point finally came into view, Kael exhaled with relief for nearly the first time.

"It is open."

Sern said.

"It must not close now."

The instant those words ended, the sound of metal sealing in layers rang far behind.

So distant it was clearer for it.

The sound of the Empire belatedly closing vacant spaces.

Sion gathered the last of his strength and held Elia tighter.

Aka gritted her teeth and held alongside.

And everyone, finally, threw their bodies past the access line and into the hidden outer path.

Even after crossing, no one sat down right away.

Like people who had been inside too long—they simply stood, catching breath.

Only then did Sion look back.

The Empire was still there.

Too quiet. Too ordered. Standing as though nothing had happened moments ago.

Elia steadied her breathing very slowly. The nameless man, like someone breathing outside air for the first time after captivity, closed his eyes once and opened them.

And Sern, after what felt like a very long time, said it very low.

"We made it out alive."

It was the moment when the two things the Empire had tried hardest to hold had truly crossed to the outside.
