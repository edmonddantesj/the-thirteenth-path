# Episode 215. Elia Still Remained

In that room, there was almost no difference between morning and night.

The light was always even. The temperature was always even. Even the airflow was even.

Less a room for a living person and more a vessel made to maintain a certain state for a long time.

The walls were excessively smooth. The floor absorbed footsteps. The door was built for the convenience of people on the outside only.

In this place, the sense that a day had passed, or the sense of counting how long one had endured, could only grow duller.

Elia was sitting in the center of that room.

More precisely, "placed sitting" might have been more accurate.

The body was held upright by its own effort, but what sustained that posture was half will and half structure.

There were no chains directly tightening wrists and ankles.

But that was precisely what made it more Imperial.

This was not a place that bound people roughly. It was closer to a place that maintained the state of being unable to escape—for a very long time.

Elia steadied the breathing, slowly.

With every breath in, the air was excessively clean.

Almost no smell.

No blood. No rust. No mold.

A place where pain existed, but the traces pain left behind had been wiped away.

That was worse.

Elia did not close the eyes.

Closing them let time be carved away more easily.

Here, the moment you let your mind go felt like the biggest loss.

The body's condition was already not good.

A subtle tremor—as though a fever might come or might not—had been sitting deep inside the body for a long time. Pain from being touched in the same places multiple times followed late whenever a breath went slightly deeper.

Sensation in the fingertips blurred often. Keeping the legs at the same angle too long brought moments where the body did not feel like one's own.

But the Empire did not let things get worse than that.

Just before collapse, it caught—never too late. After catching, it restored just enough to endure for a long time again.

Neither killing nor releasing.

Elia bit the inside of the lip, very lightly.

Not enough to bleed. Just a habit closer to confirming that inside, the self was still there.

Outside the door, one set of footsteps passed.

Elia did not raise the head.

In this place, it was better not to assign meaning to every footstep. React to each one and your mind gets bound to their rhythm.

Instead, she remembered only very small things.

How the air's grain changed when the door opened. How consistent the intervals between meals were. Whose entrance made the silence more rigid. The difference between the person who asked questions and the person who only confirmed and left.

The Empire knows how to preserve for a long time.

But Elia was not entirely unfamiliar with enduring for a long time either.

Not someone who had been strong from the start.

Not someone accustomed to pain.

But someone who needed to read.

Someone who did not let blurring simply blur. Who did not let severance simply sever. Who traced remnant grains to the end and ultimately turned them into meaning.

So still, little by little, reading continued.

The faint vibration along the wall surface. Cycles that seemed almost nonexistent, yet occasionally overlapped at the same interval—resonance from outer lines. Where this room was attached. How deep it was placed. Where it interlocked with other structures.

It did not read like a precise blueprint.

But even a prison was ultimately structure.

And structure, no matter how smoothly hidden, leaves repeating grain.

Elia steadied the breathing, very slowly.

To keep the fingertips from trembling, it was better not to send thoughts too far.

Right now, more important than where this was—was the fact that the self had not yet collapsed.

There was no certainty that anyone would come.

But strangely, the sense that everything was completely over did not come either.

Like a document held captive for too long, with the last line still unclosed—somewhere, a margin that might still continue seemed to remain.

Elia did not force a name onto that sensation.

The moment a name was given, it would easily become either hope or hallucination.

Both had to be guarded against right now.

Instead, she simply endured.

Not letting go of the mind. Counting breaths. Adjusting by the smallest angles so the body would not break.

After a span of time impossible to measure, Elia moved an eyebrow—very faintly.

Something was different.

The door had not opened. No footsteps. No large shift in the air.

But from far away, very faintly, a grain different from this place seemed to brush past.

At first it seemed like imagination.

Endure too long and you start grasping for things that are not there.

But this time was different.

It was not one.

Unfamiliar grains—from very far, from a distance that had not yet reached—were overlapping their way in, faintly.

Elia did not stop breathing.

Instead, very slowly—truly slowly—raised the eyes.

A change too faint to explain.

In that moment, for the first time, Elia was listening to something other than the silence inside this room.

From far away. From a side that had not yet arrived. Several grains that had been known for a long time were brushing past—with a weakness that seemed ready to vanish.

She did not let herself be certain of it right away.

Not giving certainty was what allowed enduring longer.

But the corner of the mouth loosened—just barely. Truly, just enough not to be noticed.

A faint smile.

So faint that anyone watching would have thought the breathing had merely changed for a moment.

Even so, Elia knew.

It was not over yet.
