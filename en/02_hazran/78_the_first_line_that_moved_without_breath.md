# Episode 78. The First Line That Moved Without Breath

"Visible."

Sern was the one who caught it.

At that one word Sion looked outside. There was nothing.

The heat layer had not fully woken yet, and the air above the outer waterway was hazy. Stare at a hazy patch long enough and you see things that aren't there. Sion had learned that yesterday.

"Where?"

"The third ridge from the left."

Sion looked there. There was a single black line.

It was so thin that at first he took it for a shadow. It was wavering a little above the heat, and at this hour everything wavered.

Sion was about to stop looking, and didn't.

The way it wavered was wrong.

Everything above the heat layer gets pushed together. Rocks get pushed, tents get pushed, people get pushed. But that black line got pushed and then came back to its place. It came back exactly as far as it had been pushed.

Sion watched it three times. All three were the same.

"That's not people."

Sion said it.

Sern glanced his way for a moment. Sion knew those were checking eyes.

"Why do you see it that way?"

"Walk on sand long enough and your stride slips. Once it slips, it doesn't line up again."

Even as he said it, Sion remembered where he had learned it. It was what Harun had said a few days ago, looking at the footprints of people who had come back from outside.

"That slips and then lines up."

Sern gave a short nod and looked outside again.

Ater spoke, very low.

"So it is."

Sion thought he knew why those three words were so cold.

Until yesterday, there had been something coming. Prices were off, paths were pressed down, signals were too even. All of it was a sign that something was on its way. A sign was still only a sign, so it could still be nothing.

Now it couldn't.

Sion looked again. It was not a single black line.

There was something layered behind it. At first he thought it was two layers, but as he watched it became three, and when he thought it was three, what lay behind was still caught in the heat layer.

Sion sent his eyes farther to find the end. It didn't come. It stayed black all the way to where the heat wavered.

At that distance he couldn't make out how many there were, but he could already see how they came.

When the front was pushed, the back filled in at the same interval. Nowhere did it stop. When a gap opened inside the line, it was filled exactly as much as it had opened.

Yesterday Sion had watched the city change its shape all day. Turning the angles of the stalls, rehanging scraps of cloth, setting water barrels against the walls—every person in Hazran had been on it.

Over there, no one was on it. The shape had been that shape from the start.

Aka spoke very low, looking outside.

"It has no breath."

At that, Sion looked again at what he had just seen.

When people walk in a line, the line stretches out and then pulls tight, again and again. When the front tires, the back sags after it; when the front gets scared, the back slows with it. That had none of it.

It was because there was no one to match breath with. Inside that line, no one looked to see who was tired.

Harun turned around at the edge of the outer line.

"Perimeter line, first disruption."

At the order, the thin metal plates set up outside Hazran in advance all tilted at once. The heat-guide plates buried under the sand switched on, and the view across the outer reaches twisted. The frame wreckage torn out of the stadium had not been laid down in the shape of a barrier. It had been laid down staggered, to break any straight line.

Sion knew it was not the shape of something that blocks. It was the shape of something that makes the walk longer.

"Don't make them stop,"
Harun said, low.
"Make them turn."

Two desert skiffs slid low beneath the perimeter line.

They were the boats he had watched during the race. Sion recognized even the scrape along the flank of the lead one. It was the boat that had grazed the wall and come out of it on the final stretch that day.

Back then they had kicked up sand to show off their speed. They had kicked it up big on purpose so the spectators could see it well, and Sion had thought it a waste.

Now they did the same thing for a different reason. The two boats went in at spread angles, swept up sand and metal dust in one go, and before what they had swept up could settle, they slipped straight back into the shadows inside.

The way out was already there. It was the path Harun had set yesterday.

In the first line, one android turned its angle slightly.

Sion stopped breathing. He thought it had worked.

And right behind it, two units filled the emptied angle.

The one that had turned simply got pushed back, still turned. No one waited for it.

It took Sion a while to understand what he had seen.

With people, the whole line would shake there. When the person beside you shakes, you shake too, and seeing each other shake, you shake more. Over there, the shaking did not spread. The one that shook was replaced.

Sern spoke, low.

"The front line is a line to be thrown away."

Ater answered at once.

"I would expect so."

The two remarks were so calm that Sion's back went stiff instead.

They were not calm because they didn't know. These two knew more precisely than anyone else how that thing ran. Knowing that much, there was nothing to be surprised by, and with nothing to be surprised by, they also knew it would keep coming this way to the end.

One of the metal plates on the perimeter line split in half with a short, sharp sound.

It was a distance not yet even reached.

"Cutting type,"
Ater said.

Sern added to it.

"The kind that pins and then cuts."

As Sion took that in, he felt his hands go cold.

Pinning and then cutting meant catching and then cutting. It was an order: first make anything that could run unable to move, then deal with it.

Harun heard the analysis and still didn't look back.

"Good."

Sion looked his way.

"What's good about it?"

"Cutting takes time."

Harun said it looking outside.

"Pin and cut, that's one at a time. They don't walk in between."

"So?"

"So we stand up more for them to cut."

At that, Sion looked at the perimeter line again. Thin metal plates stood in a row. A moment ago he had thought they were a barrier.

They were not a barrier. They had been set up to make the other side spend time. It was the first time he had stood looking at things set up to be broken.

"So we make them walk more."

Sion knew those were the same words Zahir had said yesterday. Let them in and make them walk a long way. Here you could make them lose without killing them.

But the other side didn't seem to lose anything by walking.

Han Jiwoo was standing beside him.

Sion hadn't seen when she came. Someone who was supposed to be sticking to the hull had climbed all the way up here and was looking outside.

Her face was set hard. Sion knew it wasn't set hard from fear.

"That's well made."

Sion thought he had misheard.

"What?"

"I said that's well made."

Han Jiwoo said it without turning her eyes.

"To hold an angle like that above the heat layer, they must have rebuilt the ankles alone over and over. You don't get that in one go."

Hearing that, Sion looked outside again. The black line slipped and came back exactly as before.

Until a moment ago it had only been frightening; now he could also see that someone had sat with it a long time to make it work.

"Then why are you angry?"

Han Jiwoo looked toward Sion only then.

"They'd have been at it a long time, right?"

"Hold on to it that long, and you'd know what you were making."

Han Jiwoo didn't carry on from there. Instead she unfolded her arms and opened her palm once, then closed it. It was what a hand that used to hold parts did when it had nothing to hold.

Seeing that hand, Sion understood.

The one this person was angry at was not that line. It was the hands that had sat a long time refining that line until it came out like that.

Sion looked at the black line again.

The counting habit came out. Yesterday he had counted to nine. Price, folding, pressing down, the heat layer, the way out, the boats that die late, the price that rises first, the hands that take no responsibility, and the fact that the sun comes up.

The tenth had just appeared.

That they throw away the front.

Sion counted it and stayed there a long while before he understood.

The first nine were worth knowing; they had uses. Know the price and you know when to move; know the way out and you can get out; when the sun comes up, the heat layer thickens.

Knowing the tenth made nothing smaller.

However precisely this side knew that the other side threw away its front, the other side would always have a front to throw away.

The black line was walking toward Hazran.
