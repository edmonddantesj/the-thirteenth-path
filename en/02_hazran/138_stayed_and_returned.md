# Episode 138. Hands That Stayed, Hands That Returned

When the human who had been carried in was laid on the floor, the threshold emptied.

Every eye went to that person's body.

They were breathing, but shallowly. Each time the ribs heaved, something somewhere followed late, and it was the left side. The right rose, and only a beat later did the left.

Han Jiwoo knelt with her hands resting on the chest. Two of the inside machines brought cloth, water, and treatment supplies in something like a small metal box. One machine opened the box, but there was little in it.

Luhai ran back with a canister of water and set it beside Han Jiwoo, started to step away, then came back and took hold of the injured person's legs. No one had told him to.

The machine that had come back first spoke very briefly.

"No painkillers first. The breathing goes fainter."

Han Jiwoo shot a sidelong look that way, but her hands didn't stop. She put what she had been about to take out of the box back into it.

"Tell me more exactly where the bleeding is."

The machine answered without a beat's pause.

"Two, lower left. One, back of the shoulder. Ribs, good chance of a crack on the inside."

Han Jiwoo tore the clothes further; at the lower left there were two, and at the back of the shoulder there was one. Without looking at the machine again, she pressed the cloth down.

The old machine handing over supplies from the inside faltered, and the cloth in its hand stopped in midair. A beat later, it handed it over.

Two kinds of hands were on one body. The inside machine's hands folded cloth and passed it over, and the returned machine's hands pointed out where the wounds were. Han Jiwoo's hands moved between them. Taking, pressing, taking.

Sion looked at those three hands in turn. His own hands were empty. He lifted the water canister and moved it to where Han Jiwoo could reach it.

When Kael came over carrying a box, Han Jiwoo spoke without looking.

"Legs up."

While Kael slid the box under the injured person's feet, Luhai lifted the legs and then set them down on top of it. A little color came back into the injured person's face.

Kael had nothing more to do, but he stayed where he was, standing beside them. Ater came and stood next to him, and the two of them stood at the injured person's feet. It was a place for blocking. Nothing had come, and they were blocking it.

The injured person let out a low groan.

At the sound, the second machine lowered itself. Unable to fold the leg that had been dragging, it kept it stretched out and bent only its body, sliding its own hand under the injured person's arm so the wrist would not touch the floor. The body was heavy, but the hand holding it up didn't shake.

Sion watched that hand. The leg was split open, and the hand didn't shake.

Aka watched it and spoke very low.

"You've handled plenty."

The machine that had come back first answered shortly.

"Otherwise they die."

"How many."

"I do not count."

Aka nodded. It was the same answer as before.

Luhai, still holding the injured person's legs, watched the scene for a while and then muttered under his breath.

"Why's it all so different?"

The tired-faced woman who had been watching Sion's party since the day before took it up instead. She had been on her way back with heated water, and she set the bowl beside Han Jiwoo as she spoke.

"We learned how to fall apart late, in here, and they learned how not to die, out there."

Luhai looked at the woman.

"Is that different?"

"It is. Not dying is fast, and falling apart late is slow."

A little steam rose from the bowl.

She said nothing more and stepped back. From where she stood back, she watched.

Han Jiwoo tore the injured person's clothes further to check the wounds and swallowed a curse, low.

"You ran out there carrying them like this?"

The second machine spoke again. Its voice was lower than the first one's, and more worn.

"Lost them once on the way. Caught them again."

Han Jiwoo's hands stopped. A beat later, they moved again.

"Where'd you lose them."

"Stairs."

"Where'd they fall."

"On top of me."

The second machine looked at its own leg, at the open joint.

Han Jiwoo looked at that leg too, and then at the injured person's ribs. The left.

"So that's why it's the left."

"That is why it is the left."

Han Jiwoo peeled away the blood-soaked cloth and put a fresh one in its place.

Seorin, who had been watching from the threshold, asked the first machine without coming in.

"Where'd that one come from."

"Up top."

"Up top where."

"Later."

"You've got a lot of laters."

"Because right now that person comes first."

Seorin nodded. Even so, she didn't step back from the threshold.

When a child tried to come out from deeper in the passage, the old woman caught the child by the shoulder.

"Don't look."

The child looked from under the old woman's arm. The old woman didn't shield the child any further.

"Are they dying?"

The child asked.

"Not yet. They're not dying yet."

The child heard that "yet" and, from under the old woman's arm, kept watching Han Jiwoo's hands move.

Han Jiwoo saw those eyes.

"Water."

She said it to the child.

The child slipped out from under the old woman's arm, brought the canister over with both hands, and set it beside Han Jiwoo, and the old woman didn't hold the child back. When the child came back and ducked under the old woman's arm again, the child's hands were wet.

The old machine tidying the supplies inside spoke very low.

"How far did you go this time?"

Before it answered, the first machine looked at Han Jiwoo's hands pressing the cloth, and then at the old machine.

"Now."

"Now."

The old machine answered.

"We need the number lost to decide what comes next."

A short silence.

The first machine's optical eyes went cold. The light that had been uneven turned even.

"You've always had next decided."

It kept its voice low.

"We go out, and you stay."

The air froze thin.

The old machine didn't answer; it took out one more cloth and pushed it toward Han Jiwoo. The first machine said nothing more either and pointed to the back of the injured person's shoulder, and Han Jiwoo pressed there.

Both of them were looking at the injured person, and neither looked at the other.

Aka looked from one to the other and spoke quietly.

"You can't do without each other, but you really don't trust what the other one says."

The first machine looked at Aka and made a very short sound, like a laugh.

"Quick to see, aren't you."

"Some things I'm slow at."

"Like what."

"Talking."

The machine moved its optical eyes to Han Jiwoo's hands.

Han Jiwoo didn't stop her hands through any of it. She pressed on the wounds to slow the flow, watched the breathing around the ribs, took the supplies the inside machine handed her, and pressed where the outside machine told her. Her right hand and her left hand did different work.

Beside her, Sern was watching his coat soak through with blood. He didn't pull it out, and he didn't write it on his record panel; he only watched.

Sion had been holding the water canister, and he poured whenever Han Jiwoo held out her hands. Three times.

The injured person's breathing changed. The left followed the right. What had come a beat late came half a beat late.

Han Jiwoo let out a short breath, but she didn't take her hands away.

The first machine's shoulders dropped very slightly. It turned at once to look at the gap, which was only the worn spot on a closed wall now, and it looked at that spot for a while.

The second machine kept holding up the injured person's wrist and didn't let go. Its own leg was still stretched out.

The old machine stood beside the second machine's leg and looked down.

"That must be fixed as well."

"This one first."

"Those hands are handling this one."

The old machine pointed at Han Jiwoo.

The second machine turned its head to look at Han Jiwoo, but Han Jiwoo was looking at the injured person.

"Next."

Han Jiwoo spoke without looking.

"I'll do that leg next."

The second machine nodded. The hand holding up the wrist stayed where it was.

The tired-faced woman came back again with more heated water. She set it beside Han Jiwoo, and then she set some beside the first machine too. The machine didn't use water, and the woman knew that when she set it down. The machine looked at the bowl.

"Thank you."

"You don't even drink."

"Still."

The woman stepped back again.

Luhai let go of the injured person's legs and looked at his hands. There was blood on them, but he didn't wipe it off; he looked at Sern's blood-soaked coat, then took off his own coat and laid it over the injured person's legs.

The threshold was closed. In front of it, people and machines sat mixed together with one person in their midst.

The injured person opened their eyes. They were looking at the yellow lamp on the ceiling, and the lamp blinked. Their eyes closed and opened along with it.
