# Episode 82. The Ones Who Knew How to Break Them

The next morning, the third and the fourth came into the same corridor.

The front one stepped on the fallen body and climbed up, and the one behind backed it up at the same angle. Blocking one side did not open the side next to it. The next layer came in at the very spot that had been blocked.

Harun clenched his teeth.

"From the front, they won't cut."

Sern answered.

"Yes."

Sern had already lowered himself as far as the middle of the corridor. He wasn't watching the shots. He was watching where they bent.

"Not the knees or the neck."

"What about those?"
Sion asked.

"They discard those."

Sern spoke without taking his eyes off them.

"The priority coupling axis must be cut."

A shooter beside them asked in a hurry.

"Where is that?"

"Inside the lower right rib line."

The shooter's face went stiff.

Sion found it strange too. On a human body, that spot was covered by bone. Being told to shoot where bone covered it made no sense.

Ater took it up.

"It is an imitation of a human rib cage."

Sion looked toward Ater.

"They build it as if it protects something, and then they put the actual pivot axis inside it."

Sion had to hear it twice.

Once he understood, everything that had felt wrong since yesterday fell into place at once.

Yesterday, the worst thing about seeing one up close had been that it was human-sized. It had shoulders and arms and knees, so his eyes kept reading it as a person.

That hadn't happened by mistake.

Make a thing look like a person, and people hit it the way they hit a person. They aim for the head, the neck, the knees. The other side had built them so they could give up all three.

That was why they had been made to resemble people.

Sion's stomach turned over.

He had thought they were given a human shape to frighten. It wasn't that. With a human shape, you knew in advance where a human would strike. Leave the places they already knew empty, and it broke that much less.

The resemblance wasn't a threat. It was a calculation.

"Then what about the people who hit it there?"

Sion asked before he knew he was asking.

Ater looked at Sion for the first time.

"Someone must have aimed under the ribs yesterday, right?"

"There would have been someone."

"And if they hit it?"

"Then that person hit it and still did not know."

Ater looked outside again.

"If you do not know what made it stop, you cannot use it next time."

Sion held on to that answer for a long while.

Somewhere, someone would already have hit it. They might have hit it and died, or hit it and never known what it was. If no one who knew was beside them, whatever they had found out became as if it had never been found.

"Shoot there."

Harun cut it short.

Behind the screen, two shooters pulled at the same time.

The first shot missed. The thing twisted its body, very briefly.

The second hit. The inner right side of the black body caved in, and for the first time its walk broke off strangely. A foot started forward and stopped halfway.

Sern went into that gap.

He didn't go straight in. He skimmed low past the toppled sacks, came around on the side where its line of sight bent, and drew the short cutter upward from below. He cut the spot that had just caved in a second time.

The black thing stopped.

It hadn't fallen. It had stopped where it stood. That was worse than falling.

For a moment the corridor was quiet.

Sion looked at the stopped thing.

Its lower right side had caved in. Thin plates lay several layers deep around the dent, and the way they overlapped really was the shape of ribs.

Whoever had made it had known that shape when they made it.

Sion watched the faces of the Hazran people change.

They were faces that knew it could be cut. Since yesterday, no one had worn that face.

The shooter who had just fired turned toward the person beside him.

"Lower right."

"Where?"

"Inside the ribs. Inside the part shaped like bone."

The person beside him asked once more, then got it, nodded, and passed the same words to the next person over.

Sion heard the words travel down the corridor. By the time they had crossed about three people, they had gotten shorter.

"Inside the bone."

Now the city knew it.

Ater didn't let that look stay for long.

"The same spot will not work twice."

"That doesn't match what you said yesterday…"

"Yesterday was one day,"

Ater said.

"Now is now."

Before he had finished, the two at the back changed their angle.

Until then they had eaten straight into the narrow corridor. This time they went in to cut down the left screen first.

"You must not give up the left."
Ater raised his voice.
"If that opens, there is a straight line through to the next corridor."

"Hold the left!"

Harun's hand turned, and two people who had run up shoved the metal screen in further. Pushing wasn't enough, so one of them set a shoulder against it and pushed.

From behind, Kael tossed over a thin coupling plate. It was one he had picked out for the ship. Sion recognized which pile it had come from.

The plate was forced into the gap. It wasn't the right size, so it jammed in with one edge sticking out.

A cutting line flew in and split the metal plate.

It stopped halfway. The forced-in plate threw the angle off, and the line didn't go all the way through.

Sion looked at that off angle. If the plate had been fitted right, it would have split clean.

Ater didn't miss that one beat.

"Sern."

Just a name.

Sion didn't know how that became an order, but Sern had already slid into the shadow on the far side.

This time he didn't go for the front one.

He dropped low as if to take on the front one. The front one saw him drop and lowered its angle. Sern flowed in under the lowered angle and slipped right past it.

Sion understood late that he hadn't slipped past at all.

Sern was after the second one. He had shown himself to the front one to make it duck, and when the front one ducked, the space behind it opened for a moment.

He went in under the second one, drove the cutter deep into the inside of its rear-leg coupling line, and slid out to the side.

The second one's leg buckled to one side.

The third, coming up behind, caught for the first time. It tried to climb over where the one ahead had sunk, couldn't find the angle, and stopped once.

Sion held his breath at that one stop. Since yesterday, those things had never stopped.

"Everyone push, now!"

Three shooters fired at once and two short blast plates went off one after the other. The two fallen ones half-blocked the corridor, and metal dust and shreds of torn tent tangled over them.

For the first time, the wave was held for a beat.

Luhai muttered, with a face that could barely believe it.

"It works."

Nasim swallowed a low breath.

"We have to make it work."

Nasim didn't smile. He was the one who had said that when you smile, you can't see the price.

Sion saw what lay between those two lines.

One of them had seen it work for the first time today. The other saw it work and still counted the price first.

Sion counted too. Holding one corridor for a beat had taken two blast plates, five cutting rounds, and one coupling plate meant for the ship.

There were six corridors here.

Sion looked at the end of the corridor.

The two that had just stopped and the one that had sunk were there. No one from this city knew where to hit those things to make them stop.

Two people who knew were here now.

Sion looked at the two of them.

Sern was wiping what had stuck to his cutter on the hem of his coat. Ater was already looking at the angle of the next corridor.

Knowing the inside of the rib line meant having opened one before. Knowing it was an imitation meant having watched the imitating from close by.

There was no need to ask where.

Sion wanted to ask, but he didn't.

If he asked, those hands would stop for a moment, and if those hands stopped now, the corridor would open.

And there was one more thing. If he asked and heard the answer, Sion would see the two of them differently. He could not see them differently right now.

Sion knew what he had just put off. He would ask later, and even if he didn't like the answer then, he couldn't hand back what had been gained now.

This city already knew the inside of the bone.

He remembered what Seorin had said yesterday, watching Harun. She had asked why he knew it so well.

Sion turned those words over again.

What Harun knew was what had been done to Hazran. What those two knew was what the other side did.

Harun had stayed here and was paying that price, and those two weren't even from here, yet they had come and were using it.

Sion couldn't decide which was harder to bear.

There was no time to decide, either. At the end of the corridor, the next one was coming in.
