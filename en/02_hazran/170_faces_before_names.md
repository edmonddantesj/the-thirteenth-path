# Episode 170. Faces Before Names

The sector where the androids stood was still quiet, but it wasn't the same quiet as a few days before.

Now some of the people passing in front of it went by unable to turn their heads away. Their steps quickened, but their eyes stayed inside the sector, and only after they had passed the entrance did their heads turn forward again.

The children were like that. At first the children had been the ones who hid farthest away, but over a few days the places they hid crept a little closer. First it was behind the corner of the passage, then behind a pillar, and today, the sector's entrance.

That day too, two children hovered at a distance by the sector's entrance. One stood almost hidden behind Han Jiwoo, and the other had come out half a step ahead of that one.

Neither of them said anything, but their eyes kept going to the androids.

Without turning around, Han Jiwoo spoke, very low.

"Scary, huh."

One of the children gave a small nod.

"Yeah."

"Me too."

Han Jiwoo said.

The child looked up at her.

"Really?"

"Really. That's why I'm watching."

A moment later, the other child asked, even more quietly.

"But… why do I keep looking over there?"

Han Jiwoo couldn't answer right away and looked down at her hands. Every knuckle had a scar from working on machines. She closed that hand once and opened it, and only after a long while did she speak.

"Things we don't fully know yet keep making people look back."

The children looked as if they hadn't understood all of it, but still, they came a little closer. The one who had been hiding still held the hem of Han Jiwoo's clothes, and the one in front was holding nothing.

Seorin watched it from a distance but didn't stop it. She stayed leaning on the wall, arms still folded. This time Luhai didn't move to hold anyone back first either; he opened his mouth once and shut it.

"Shouldn't we stop them?"

Luhai asked Seorin quietly.

"Stop them and then what? They haven't done a thing for days. All I've seen is the kid getting closer."

"We don't know what happens when the kid gets close."

"That's why I'm watching."

Luhai folded his arms like Seorin and didn't unfold them.

One of the old machines was watching the scene quietly from the wall. When one of the outside hands came to stand beside the entrance with its hands lowered, Han Jiwoo looked at it.

"No orders today?"

"None today. We have decided to watch."

The child who had come further forward, after hesitating a long while, brushed the dry dust off their hands onto their trousers. They brushed once more, and only then, very slowly, began to walk. Not all the way up to one of the androids, but to a spot a little short of it.

Ten steps took three breaths.

Everyone watched the movement, but no one interrupted. Luhai's foot went half a step forward and came back when Aka caught his arm.

"If it falls."

His voice was very low.

"It won't fall. It's been standing. For days."

The child's hand still had soil dust on it from feeling along a crack in the wall a moment before. Even after the brushing, it stayed between the fingers.

The child looked back once. At Han Jiwoo, at Seorin, at Luhai. No one spoke.

So the child turned forward again and, very carefully, raised a hand.

The hand touched the smooth surface of the android's face. The instant it touched, the child's shoulders flinched once, but the hand stayed.

Everyone stopped breathing.

On that surface, with a soil-covered finger, the child drew one round eye. Then one more on the other side. The second eye was a little smaller than the first. After a short pause, this time, a short mouth.

Too simple a face. Like a child's drawing, clumsy, a face that would soon rub away.

No one laughed. Luhai lowered the hand he had been raising to his mouth.

When the child took the hand away, the soil dust stayed faintly on the surface of the face. The child glanced at their finger.

The android didn't move. It made no sound.

A few seconds later, somewhere beneath the smooth surface, a very faint light passed once.

Han Jiwoo was the first to stop breathing. Sern raised his eyes, and Ater pushed off the wall.

The light seemed about to fade, then rose again in very thin lines on the spot the child had just drawn.

Two eyes.
One short mouth.

Not in soil this time, but in very faint digital lines from inside the surface. Just as in the soil, the second eye was a little smaller than the first.

The child froze in place. The other child, behind, gripped the hem of Han Jiwoo's clothes tight.

Luhai spoke, almost a breath.

"You saw that."

No one answered. Sern's hand began to move over the record panel; he wrote one line and stopped. He looked at the line he had written and didn't write again.

The android was still silent.

A little later, very slowly, it tilted its head to the side. And then back the other way. Where it paused once, still tilted, was toward the side where its own face showed in a metal plate.

The child murmured, very softly.

"It got a face."

"You made it."

Luhai said.

"No. I drew it. It got the face itself."

Luhai shut his mouth.

Han Jiwoo stepped forward. Her hand rose first, toward the android's face, right up beside the lines the child had drawn. It stopped one finger joint short and stayed there a long while, not touching, before it came down.

"Why don't you touch it?"

The child asked.

"Because it's yours. You drew it. It's not for my hand yet."

The child looked at their finger, then back at the android.

One of the old machines came forward, very slowly. This time it didn't stop. Just enough that the android and the child would not get too close, it stood exactly one step to the side.

Then for a while it looked at that face. Its optical eye wavered very slightly, looked at the child once, and went back to the face.

"Is it a face you know?"

Sion asked.

"I do not know. There are only three lines, and yet I know. Which ones are the eyes."

Sion looked at the two eyes, the one mouth, those three lines. He knew too.

Sern spoke, almost a breath.

"I will record it."

"Writing what?"

Seorin asked.

"Three lines. Followed."

Sern said.

"I will write nothing more than that. Not yet."

Ater added, very low.

"It is not a surface response. It followed from inside."

Luhai still looked like he couldn't believe it.

"What the kid drew… that thing's copying it?"

"Maybe it isn't copying."

Han Jiwoo spoke low. Her lowered hand was still half closed.

"Maybe it's the first thing it's ever taken in."

"What do you mean, taking in?"

Luhai asked.

Han Jiwoo opened her scarred hand.

"A machine not pushing away the hand that's fixing it. Some machines push it away. This one didn't. Just now."

Luhai looked at the android's hand; it stayed against its side and didn't move.

After that, Seorin walked over slowly. She came off the wall, unfolded her arms, and came all the way to the child's side.

She looked at the child first, then at the android that now had a face. After looking at the three lines of the face one by one, she spoke very quietly.

"We can't look at them the way we did before."

No one argued. Luhai started to say something and stopped, and Ater didn't go back to the wall.

The child asked again, very carefully.

"Then… is it on our side now?"

Seorin didn't answer. Sern looked down at the record panel, and the old machine looked at the child.

Han Jiwoo looked at the android's new face, looked at the child, and spoke, very low.

"I don't know yet."

"But at least, I don't think it's empty."

The child looked up at Han Jiwoo.

"And if it is empty?"

"If it were empty, it wouldn't follow. It followed just now, didn't it. Your lines."

The child looked at the android's face, at the place they had drawn. The light was there. When the child raised a hand and gave a small wave, the android's head tilted half a knuckle that way. The child drew in a breath.

When the child looked at their finger again, almost no soil was left on it. On the android's face, the three lines stayed lit.

That night, up above, another circulation line changed. Serakion was still getting faster.

Down in the sector, the three lines drawn on the android's face stayed lit all night. The Hazran man standing night watch looked that way twice, and both times they were lit. The third time, he didn't go.

The wall-side machine stood there all night, its optical eye resting on those three lines. The man looked at that optical eye, then went back to his place.

The child couldn't fall asleep for a long while. Lying beside Han Jiwoo, the child spread fingers that had no soil left on them and smelled them, and they smelled of soil.

The child slept with that hand closed.
