# Episode 145. Holding and Opening

Serakion was a world that closed faster than it looked from outside.

Kael was the first to start reading it.

The deeper the scouting team dug in, the more Kael's eyes followed the structure instead of the people. Not who passed by, but where things folded; not which door was blocked, but which point slowed first.

He looked at the thin observation slits, the joint lines hidden up near the ceiling, the wear on the floor and the heat warping on the wall panels. He would stay on one spot for a long time, then move on to the next.

The second recovery hand saw it and asked, low.

"What are you looking at?"

Kael answered without taking his eyes off it.

"The order it closes in."

A short silence.

The leg the second looked down at was a leg that knew the order things closed in.

Kael didn't touch anything with his hands, but traced the forks and folds with his eyes as he spoke.

"It doesn't block the middle here. It folds the point that notices last, and folds it first. Then people go farther in, still thinking it's open."

The first recovery hand let out a short breath.

"Right."

Its voice was low.

"That's why you always end up running one more time."

"And if you run one more time?"

Kael asked.

"You stop in front of the one that is closed. And behind you, it is already closed."

Its optical eye followed one of the joint lines in the ceiling.

Kael nodded, looked at one more joint line in the ceiling, and folded a finger down.

Han Jiwoo was crouched beside the second. While they were stopped, she opened the joint, gave the screwdriver two turns, and closed it.

"Go."

The second walked, and the leg dragged less.

A little farther in, they saw a bulkhead built to fold once and open again.

On the surface it was a decorative wall with a pattern, but inside, a thin lattice and joint axes were set in. Kael stopped in front of it for a long time and looked at the lines between the pattern; the lines cut through the pattern.

"This isn't for stopping troops."

He said it low.

"It's for cutting the flow."

Ater asked from beside him.

"Is there a difference?"

Kael answered briefly.

"A big one. Stopping troops means you mean to fight. Cutting the flow means you mean to scatter them."

Ater looked at the bulkhead's pattern and its lines.

"So it does not block."

He said.

"It doesn't block."

Kael answered.

"It cuts."

The joint axis of the bulkhead, as Han Jiwoo saw it, had fresh oil on it. She dabbed some on a finger and smelled it.

"Gets used a lot."

"It is used often."

The second answered.

"Every time we come."

Han Jiwoo wiped her finger on her trousers.

After two machines passed down the corridor beyond the bulkhead, the bulkhead folded without a sound; the pattern broke, and it became a wall. Kael counted.
One.
Two.
Three.
When the bulkhead opened again, the pattern joined up, and it became a corridor.

"Three. That's how long it stays shut."

"Always three?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"I do not know. This time, three."

The second said.

"Last time it was two."

Kael held up three fingers, folded one down to two, then put up three again.

"When it was two, were you inside?"

Han Jiwoo asked the second.

"Outside."

"And inside?"

"Three. One came out."

The open bulkhead Han Jiwoo saw had its pattern joined up again, but behind it, two had been shut in. She didn't look away.

"The one who came out?"

"They are on the lower level."

"Who?"

When the second didn't answer, Han Jiwoo called up the faces on the lower level one by one, then stopped.

The first recovery hand spoke, very low.

"That's why we always watch two things. One is the way out for the people we bring. The other is the holding place, held to the end."

Kael looked that way for a long time, then asked.

"Who stands in the holding place?"

"Depends."

"Do they come back?"

The first paused a beat before answering.

"Depends."

Kael looked at the bulkhead again, then at the width of his own shoulders. His were the eyes of someone counting how many could get past if he stood in front of that bulkhead.

Watching Kael's back, the second spoke.

"You are broad. Broad enough for the holding place."

Kael answered without turning around.

"That's why I'd stand there."

The first looked at Kael's shoulders, then at the width of the bulkhead.

"With those shoulders, two more get through."

"Three. If they're kids."

While the first said nothing more, the second looked at its own shoulders. They were narrow.

A little later, they could see the outer route of the mid-level holding area where the candidates being sent up were kept.

They didn't go in, only watched through an observation slit.

It was more refined than the zones before, its light soft and its sound low. Places to stop were marked on the floor in footprint shapes, and the people standing on them didn't move even when a machine passed.

Kael watched the footprint shapes as he spoke.

"This is no place to fight."

Han Jiwoo looked at him.

Without taking his eyes off it, Kael went on.

"It's shut too prettily. When people get scared, they end up herded farther in."

He pointed to the inner part of the sector, where the light was softer still.

"That side looks easier. So that's where you run. That side's where it shuts."

To Han Jiwoo, too, that side looked easier, and she turned her eyes away.

"How do you know?"

"The floor. There's no wear on the floor of the easy side. Nobody makes it that far."

Han Jiwoo looked at the floor; the footprint shapes grew sharper the farther in they went. No one had worn them down.

"Then where do you run?"

"You don't. The moment you run, you go where they've picked for you. You have to walk, and turn off."

Han Jiwoo looked at the footprint shapes again and measured the width she would walk.

When one person lifted a foot half a step off the footprint shape, a machine came and stopped in front of that foot. Only after the person put the foot back inside the shape did the machine leave. There was a foot on the footprint shape. Exactly.

"Why'd that person lift it?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"Don't know."

Kael answered.

"Must've wanted to."

The leg the second looked down at was a leg that wanted to lift. Han Jiwoo crouched and took out the screwdriver, for the third time.

Kael studied the floor routes, the reflections on the walls, the positions of the hidden closing axes a while longer, then spoke low.

"To get people out, you don't fight here—you've got to steer them off one point earlier."

The second recovery hand asked, very briefly.

"Why?"

Kael answered with a jerk of his chin at the floor.

"Get spotted here, and it shuts faster than a person can run."

The second looked at its own leg, and the first looked at the second's leg. Neither said anything.

Ater looked at the closing axes where Kael had looked, but he could not see them. Only after looking a long while did they show, inside the pattern on the wall.

"Are there three?"

"Four."

Kael answered.

"One more in the ceiling."

Ater looked at the ceiling. He saw it.

Han Jiwoo muttered, very low.

"So it's not about pulling people out, it's about getting them out before it folds."

Kael gave a short nod.

"Before it folds."

He repeated it.

"Which means you have to know when it folds. Before they do."

"Who knows that?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

Ater looked at the four closing axes again.

"Do all four close together?"

"No."

Kael answered.

"Ceiling first. Walls after. The walls can't close until the ceiling does."

"Then the ceiling."

"Slow the ceiling and the walls can't close."

The ceiling Ater looked up at was high.

"Who can reach it?"

"Me."

Kael went on, looking at his own arm.

"With the shoulders I had to turn twice to get in here."

He looked at the second.

"Someone who knows the way."

The second nodded.

"That is me."

"On that leg?"

"On that leg."

Han Jiwoo pressed a hand on her tool pouch.

The first recovery hand looked ahead and spoke, very low.

"The waiting line for going up is about to move."

Three pairs of eyes went that way.

Deep in the sector a light guide line came on, and the people standing on the footprint shapes raised their heads at the same moment. Two machines stood in front of the inner door. It was not a door but a place where the wall opened, and no seam showed.

While Han Jiwoo held her breath, Ater put a hand to the wall. Kael looked at the four closing axes again. One in the ceiling and three in the walls: four fingers.

The second shifted its leg so it would make no sound, but it still made a very small one. The first didn't look back.

As the light guide line stretched inward, the people lifted their feet off the footprint shapes at the same moment. One step.

Kael drew a breath. He didn't let it out.
