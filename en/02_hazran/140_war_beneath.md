# Episode 140. War Beneath

After the recovery hands came back, the board in the old layer changed.

One of the old machines took down the board hanging on the wall, laid it on the floor, and began drawing more lines and dots on it with the tip of a screwdriver. Han Jiwoo looked at that screwdriver; it was the same shape as hers.

At first it looked like a route marking. As the lines multiplied, connections formed between the dots, and some marks it drew and then rubbed faint with its palm. On purpose.

On the board Sion looked down at, there was more than one dot. One for this layer, one beside it, one far off, one joined only by a line.

The old machine didn't explain. The first recovery hand spoke very low.

"Did you think this place was all that was left?"

Luhai frowned.

"That's how it's looked so far."

The machine made a short sound, something like a breath.

"Everyone sees it that way at first."

Its voice was low.

"You don't make it this far if one exposure finishes you."

The old machine added to that, touching the board. One dot, one dot, one dot.

"Not one place, but pieces joined back together each time they were cut."

It rubbed one dot with its palm, and the dot blurred.

"This is where it was cut."

It didn't erase it; it left it blurred.

Sion looked at the blurred dot; on the board there were four blurred dots and five clear ones.

Sern took out his record panel and began copying the board on the floor exactly as it was. The old machine watched his hand but didn't stop him, and Sern drew the blurred dots blurred, too.

The second recovery hand spoke low, its leg stretched out. The leg Han Jiwoo had fixed still dragged.

"We're not the only ones who go outside. Some bring people back, some only pass signals along, and some hold only the paths that keep playing shut to the end."

Sern reacted at once.

"The outposts are not fixed."

The old machine answered.

"Once fixed, they die."

Sern stopped the hand that was drawing dots on his record panel and left only the dots, with nothing beside them: no names, no locations, nothing.

"How do you know where it has been cut?"

Sern asked.

"When they do not come back."

The old machine answered.

"How long must they not come back before it counts as cut?"

"We do not set a length. Set one, and on that day you give up."

Sern didn't write it down; he looked at the four blurred dots on the board.

Ater asked the first.

"How many are in the legion?"

"We have never counted. There has been no time to count."

"Only the ones you can see."

"What can be seen is never all of it."

Aka looked for a long time at the spaces between the dots on the board.

"It's not one thing, it's torn up."

The first recovery hand looked at her.

"Torn up and still alive, that's what it is."

"Don't the torn places hurt?"

Aka asked.

The machine waited a beat before it answered.

"The places that hurt are the torn places."

Aka asked nothing more and looked again at those four blurred dots.

The old Hazran man set his own plate, with its sixteen scratched lines, beside that board. The old machine looked at the plate and then only at the blurred dots on its own board, and said nothing. The board and the plate lay side by side on the floor.

The first recovery hand ran briefly through what it had seen on the way back.

"Gray routes, two of three blocked. Human sectors below the upper level, divided finer. Android legion, not guarding but herding. Humans who could not escape, under better-ordered management."

Four phrases, and Sern wrote four lines.

"They don't care about throwing things away."

Its voice dropped.

"But they care about things scattering."

"Who does?"

Seorin asked.

"Up there."

Seorin looked at the Hazran people: eight of them, the scattered kind. She gripped her hilt once and let it go.

"Then we'd better move before they come looking."

"That is why we are going out."

The first answered.

"When?"

"When the leg is mended."

Seorin looked at the second's leg, the one Han Jiwoo had opened twice.

"And us?"

"You are scattered too. So they will come looking."

Its optical eyes rested on the blurred dots on the board on the floor.

The old machine picked up the board and hung it back on the wall where it had been, and the blurred dots hung on the wall.

The old Hazran man also picked up his plate and stood holding it, but there was nowhere to hang it. The old machine touched the wall beside its own board with its hand, and the man stood his plate there.

The two hung side by side on the wall. One of dots, one of lines. Aka looked at the two of them.

"They're the same thing."

No one said they weren't. When the old man touched one line on his plate with a finger, the old machine touched one blurred dot. Then both took their hands away.

---

Serakion's upper level was quiet.

On the metal floor that reflected like marble, at the center of the hall, light wavered once, thinly.

The wavering took on the outline of a person, and its face was smooth. Where the eyes would be, and where the mouth would be, there was light. It did not move.

Below it, the aristocrat machines stood, each with its own ornaments, each at its own interval. The intervals were all the same, and none of them moved first.

The figure of light spoke first.

"The sorting deviation of the externally introduced population had already been reported. The fluctuations over the past several days have been sufficiently observed. We now pass the matter to the adjustment stage."

Light flowed once across the face of one aristocrat machine. On the others, nothing flowed.

One aristocrat machine spoke in a low, courteous voice.

"The loss of some non-conforming entities is within manageable levels."

Another machine followed.

"The problem is form rather than number. Collective departure lowers the adaptive stability of adjacent zones."

The figure of light did not waver.

"Agreed. Record this matter not as a loss, but as an externalization of sorting."

The hand of the recording machine moved. It was the only hand moving in the hall.

Another aristocrat machine spoke, courteous.

"Follow-up measures for the unrecovered human entities are being coordinated. Reassigning the non-productive group to stable protection zones, the high emotional-response group to hospitality support zones, and the adaptation-delayed group to buffer correction zones is judged to be the most efficient plan."

Three zones. The recording hand moved three times.

The figure of light was silent for a moment. The light thinned once more, then came back.

"Do not handle fear roughly."

The figure spoke.

"Make it look like stability."

A short silence.

"Do not leave loss unattended. Have it interpreted as gratitude."

Several aristocrat machines bowed their heads to the same depth, for the same length of time.

One aristocrat machine spoke.

"To what level will you permit the re-inspection of the old sealed lower zones?"

The figure of light did not answer at once. In the hall, even the recording hand stopped.

"Within limits that do not harm the surface order."

At last it spoke.

"Clear away the remnants of the past quietly. There is no need for display."

The recording hand moved again and wrote one line.

Another aristocrat machine asked.

"Under which item is 'externalization of sorting' recorded?"

"Under stability."

The figure of light answered.

"Leave the loss item blank."

The recording hand struck out one line and wrote it somewhere else.

An aristocrat machine asked.

"And the scope of the clearing?"

"As far as never reaches the surface."

No machine asked anything more.

The face of light did not waver to the very end.

"Serakion remains stable."

Its voice was low and complete.

"Therefore, recover them stably."

The light went out, and the center of the hall was empty. The aristocrat machines turned in the same order and dispersed at the same intervals. No mark was left on the metal floor.

Only the recording machine stayed behind in the hall to close the record, and the cover of the closed record bore no marking. It looked once at the empty hall and at its own reflection on the floor. Then it turned away, and its steps made no sound.

---

On the wall of the old layer hung the board with four blurred dots and five clear ones, and beside it, the plate of sixteen lines.

Under the flickering yellow lamp stood the old machine, and the first recovery hand stood in front of the closed gap. Neither of them looked at the board or the plate; one was looking up, and one at the wall.
