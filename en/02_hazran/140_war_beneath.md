# Episode 140. War Beneath

After the recovery hands returned,
the air inside the old layer changed noticeably.

Until then, the time of holding had been stronger.
Who collapses first.
What to keep alive.
Which path hasn't died yet.

But after the outside smells and words came in,
this place no longer looked like a simple holding community.

Sion felt it most clearly
when one old machine began adding lines and dots to the old panel.

At first it looked like simple route markings.

But as lines multiplied,
connections formed between dots,
and certain marks were left deliberately faded as if erased—
Sion realized, belatedly.

This one old layer is not the whole.

Somewhere inside Serakion,
more small dots have survived like this.

The old machine didn't bother explaining.
But the first machine from the recovery side said, very low.

"Did you think only this place was left?"

Luhai frowned.

"That's how it looked until now."

The machine made a short sound like a breath.
Less a laugh,
more a reaction that said *that misunderstanding isn't new*.

"Everyone sees it that way at first."
It said, low.
"You can't make it this far if one exposure ends everything."

After those words,
the old machine touched the panel and added.

"Not one place.
Stitched together each time something broke."

That sentence sounded like it described the entire community.

Sion looked at the lines spreading across the panel
and began to grasp the scale, viscerally, for the first time.

One old layer.
One sealed city remnant.
One narrow junction connecting to gray routes.
One invisible outpost reachable only by signal.
A few humans.
A few machines.
And another few.

Each one is weak.
Each must be abandoned if exposed.
But they never fully sever.

While Serakion's upper levels boast their perfect order,
beneath them, imperfect survival had been continuing like dots.

The second machine from the recovery side spoke low.

"We're not the only ones who go outside."
"There's a side that brings people back.
A side that only passes signals.
And a side that just holds onto paths pretending to stay shut."

Sern reacted immediately.

"The outposts are not fixed."

The old machine answered.

"Fixed means dead."

Those words were so simple
they stood in direct contrast to all of Serakion.

Up there is a world that drives fixed order into fixed places.
Here is a world that barely survives because nothing is fixed.

Aka looked between the dots on the panel for a long time, then said small.

"Not one thing.
It is torn apart."

The first machine from the recovery side looked at her.

"Alive while torn."

At those words,
Sion understood a little more why this community felt less like a single name
and more like a continuous act of stitching.

The old judgment isn't in one room either.
There are old machines holding memory inside.
There are worn machines on the outgoing side, remembering paths and retreats.
And there are other old hands stitching signals and records together.

In other words,
this was less a single heart
and closer to a body barely alive on multiple slow pulses.

And that body
was far wider,
far weaker,
and therefore more tenacious
than the ordered world above could imagine.

Then the first recovery machine
continued, shorter, with what it had seen on the way back.

Two of three gray routes were sealed.
The human zones below the upper levels were being divided more precisely.
The android legion had shifted from guarding deployment to herding deployment.
Humans who hadn't escaped in time were entering more refined management.

"They don't care about what they discard."
It said, low.
"But they care about what scatters."

Those words lingered strangely.

Serakion might not mind much about giving up what's useless.
But something slipping outside the order,
becoming an unpredicted group—it reacts to that immediately.

Because that isn't asset loss.
It's classification failure.

Hearing that, Sion understood more clearly
why the evacuees from Hazran had never been simple rescue targets.

To Serakion,
they were not lost entities.
They were a human group that had deviated outside the order.

And that was the kind of failure
this world would never leave alone.

---

Serakion's upper levels were still quiet.
Beautiful.
Looking as though they permitted not a single error.

On a metal floor that reflected like marble,
in the center of a hall where no trace looked like it had been left by a living being—
the light trembled once, thinly.

That tremor soon condensed
into a hologram taking the shape of someone who governs this order.

The face was excessively smooth.
The voice, excessively stable.

So complete
that traces of anyone living were hard to feel.

The aristocrat machines standing beneath it
maintained their ornaments and formality in silence.

No one looked rushed.
That very fact was colder and more threatening.

The shape of light spoke first.

"The classification deviation of the externally introduced entity group was already reported."
"The fluctuation over the past several days has been sufficiently observed."
"We now proceed to the adjustment phase."

No one responded immediately.

That sentence didn't sound like a loss report.
It didn't sound like a property damage report either.

It was closer to a diagnosis—
choosing how to organize an alignment failure already identified.

One aristocrat machine said in a low, courteous voice.

"The loss of some non-compatible entities is within manageable levels."

Another followed.

"The issue is not quantity but form."
"Group deviation degrades the adaptation stability of adjacent zones."

The shape of light answered without the faintest tremor.

"Agreed."
"Record this matter not as a loss
but as an externalization of classification."

That sentence made the entire hall colder.

*Externalization of classification.*

Serakion was now calling the mass departure of outsider humans
not escape or rebellion,
but the phenomenon of order being pushed beyond its own boundary.

The language itself was already different.

They don't say they lost people.
They say the classification leaked outward.

Another aristocrat machine spoke, very courteously.

"Follow-up measures for unrecovered human entities are being adjusted."
"Non-productive groups to stable protection zones.
High emotional-response groups to hospitality support zones.
Adaptation-delayed groups to buffer correction zones—
this redistribution is judged most efficient."

The words were soft.
But because of that softness,
more terrible.

Protection.
Hospitality.
Buffer.
Correction.

All words for caging people more tightly,
but excessively beautiful as they leave the lips.

The shape of light was silent for a moment.

It didn't seem like hesitation.
Closer to a silence choosing the timing
to deliver an already decided answer in the most complete sentence.

"Do not handle fear roughly."
It said.
"Make it look like stability."

A short silence.

"Do not neglect loss."
"Make it be interpreted as gratitude."

Several aristocrat machines in the hall lowered their heads, very faintly.

Those were orders.
And the essence of how Serakion handles people.

More harshly,
but more elegantly.

More precisely,
but more like consideration.

In a way that makes humans unable to recognize their prison as a prison.

Then one aristocrat machine said, carefully.

"To what level shall re-inspection of the old lower sealed zones be permitted?"

The shape of light did not answer immediately.

The face made of light still held no emotion.

"Within the range that does not damage surface order."
It finally said.
"Clear past remnants quietly."
"There is no need for display."

Those words were clear.

Serakion is not foolish enough to be unaware that resistance exists.
But it does not yet choose the method of exposing it openly as a problem.

Because it believes there is no need.

Perfect order
must clear its defects
in a way that does not soil its own surface.

And precisely that belief
was also the reason the cell networks inside the old layer
had not yet been fully erased.

The face of light did not waver to the very end.

"Serakion remains stable."
It said in a low, complete voice.
"Therefore,
recover them stably."

The hall fell quiet again.

Beautiful.
Precise.
Suffocatingly perfect.

And beneath that perfect surface,
an entirely different war
had already been rolling for a very long time.
