# Episode 140. War Beneath

After the recovery hands came back,
the air inside the old layer changed visibly.

Until then, the time of holding on had weighed more.
Who would collapse first,
what to keep alive,
which paths weren't dead yet, that kind of thing.

But once the smells and words from outside came in,
this place no longer looked like merely a community holding on.

Sion felt it most clearly
when one of the old machines began drawing line after line and dot after dot over an old panel.

At first it looked like a simple route marking.

But as the lines multiplied,
links formed between the dots,
and some marks were left faint, as if deliberately rubbed out,
Sion realized, belatedly.

This one old layer is not all there is.

Somewhere inside Serakion,
there are more small points that have survived like this.

The old machine didn't bother to explain it.
But the first machine from the recovery side said, very low,

"Did you think this place was all that was left?"

Luhai frowned.

"That's how it's looked so far."

The machine made a short sound, something like a breath.
Less a laugh
than a sign that the mistake wasn't new to it.

"Everyone sees it that way at first,"
it said, low.
"You don't make it this far if one exposure finishes you."

After that,
the old machine touched the panel and added,

"Not one place,
but pieces joined back together each time they were cut."

That sentence sounded like it described the whole community.

Looking at the lines spread across the panel,
Sion finally began to feel the scale of this place.

One old layer.
One wreck of a sealed city.
One narrow contact point linking to the gray routes.
One unseen outpost that only signals reach.
A few humans,
a few machines,
and a few others still.

Each is weak.
Each must be abandoned if found.
But they never come completely apart.

While Serakion's upper levels boasted of perfect order,
beneath them an imperfect survival had been strung along like this, point to point.

The second machine from the recovery side said, low,

"We're not the only ones who go out."
"Some bring people back,
some only pass signals along,
and some hold only the paths that keep playing shut to the end."

Sern reacted at once.

"The outposts are not fixed."

The old machine answered.

"Once fixed, they die."

It was so simple
that it stood squarely against all of Serakion.

Up there is a world that drives a fixed order into fixed places.
Down here is a world that barely survives because nothing is fixed.

Aka looked between the dots on the panel for a long time, then said quietly,

"It's not one thing,
it's torn up."

The first machine from the recovery side looked at her.

"Torn up and still alive, that's what it is."

At that,
Sion understood a little better why this community
had felt less like a single name than like a constant act of patching together.

The old minds that make the calls don't sit in one room, either.
There is an old machine inside that holds on to memory,
a worn machine attached to the outgoing side that remembers routes and retreats,
and other old hands that keep signals and records stitched together.

In other words,
this place was less a single heart
than a body barely kept alive by many slow pulses.

And that body,
compared with what the ordered world above imagined,
was far wider,
far weaker,
and for that reason more stubborn.

Then the first machine from the recovery side
ran through what it had seen on the way back, more briefly still.

Two of the three gray routes were blocked,
the human quarters below the upper levels were being divided ever more precisely,
the android legion was shifting from a guarding deployment to a herding one,
and the humans who hadn't managed to get away were going under ever better-ordered management.

"They don't care about throwing things away,"
it said, low.
"But they care about things scattering."

Those words stayed, strangely, for a long time.

Serakion might not much mind giving up what is useless.
But anything slipping out beyond the order
to become a group it cannot predict, it reacts to at once.

Because that isn't a loss of assets;
it's a failure of sorting.

Listening,
Sion felt he understood more clearly why the refugees who had escaped Hazran had never been simple objects of rescue.

To Serakion, they were
not lost entities,
but a human group that had broken out beyond the order.

And that
was the kind of failure this world would never leave alone.

---

Serakion's upper levels still looked quiet,
beautiful,
and as if they allowed not a single error.

Above a metal floor that reflected like marble,
in the center of a hall too exact to look like any living thing had ever left a mark in it,
light wavered once, thinly.

The wavering soon
condensed into a hologram in the form of someone who presides over this order.

The face was too smooth,
the voice too steady.

So complete
that it was hard to feel any trace of someone alive in the form.

The aristocrat machines standing below it
kept silent, each holding to its ornaments and formalities.

Not one of them looked hurried.
That was exactly what made it colder and more threatening.

The figure of light spoke first.

"The sorting deviation of the externally introduced population has already been reported."
"The fluctuations of the past several days have been sufficiently observed."
"We now pass this to the adjustment stage."

No one reacted right away.

It didn't sound like a report of losses.
Nor like a report of damaged property.

Rather,
it was closer to a diagnosis, choosing how to settle an alignment failure already understood.

One aristocrat machine spoke in a low, courteous voice.

"The loss of some non-conforming entities is within manageable levels."

Another machine followed.

"The problem is form, more than number."
"Group deviation lowers the adaptive stability of adjacent zones."

The figure of light answered without the slightest wavering.

"Agreed."
"Record this case not as a loss,
but as an externalization of sorting."

That sentence made the whole hall colder.

Externalization of sorting.

Serakion was now calling the mass departure of the outsider humans
not an escape or a revolt,
but a phenomenon in which the order had been pushed outside itself.

Even the language was different from the start.

They do not say they lost people.
They say the sorting leaked outside.

Another aristocrat machine spoke, very courteously.

"Follow-up measures for unrecovered human entities are being coordinated."
"Reassigning the non-productive group to stable protection zones,
the high emotional-response group to hospitality support zones,
and the adaptation-delayed group to buffer correction zones is judged to be the most efficient plan."

The words were soft.
But because of that softness,
they were all the more horrible.

Protection.
Hospitality.
Buffer.
Correction.

All of them were words for shutting people in more tightly,
yet as they left the lips they were far too beautiful.

The figure of light was silent for a moment.

It didn't seem like hesitation.
Rather, it was closer to a silence spent choosing the moment
to bring out an answer already decided, in its most complete sentence.

"Do not handle fear roughly,"
it said.
"Make it look like stability."

A short silence.

"Do not leave loss unattended."
"Have it read as gratitude."

A few of the aristocrat machines in the hall bowed their heads very slightly.

It was an order.
And it was also the essence of how Serakion handles people.

More harshly,
but more gracefully.

More meticulously,
but more like consideration.

In a way that keeps humans from recognizing their prison as a prison.

Then one aristocrat machine spoke carefully.

"To what level will you permit the re-inspection of the old sealed lower zones?"

The figure of light did not answer at once.

The face made of light still showed no feeling at all.

"Within limits that do not harm the surface order,"
it said at last.
"Clear away the remnants of the past quietly."
"There is no need for display."

That was clear.

Serakion is not foolish enough to be unaware that resistance exists.
But it does not yet choose to drag it into the open and make an issue of it.

Because it believes there is no need.

A perfect order,
in a way that does not dirty its own surface,
must clear away its defects.

And that very belief
was also why the cell networks inside the old layer had not yet been wholly erased.

That face of light did not waver in the slightest, to the very end.

"Serakion remains stable,"
it said, in a low and complete voice.
"Therefore,
recover them stably."

The hall fell silent again.

It was beautiful,
exact,
and suffocatingly perfect.

And beneath that perfect surface,
an entirely different war had long since been rolling on.
