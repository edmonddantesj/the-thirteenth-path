# Episode 199. Oldest Memory

The moment the attack began, it woke the nightmare of Hazran again.

The first android, the one that had tilted at the end of the corridor, slid out, and the whole row aligned behind it unwound at the same speed.
No sound of anything breaking, no shouting, no hesitation.

The same direction.
The same speed.
The same face.

All at once.

Something tightened inside Sion's body, in his throat and his chest.
He gripped his blade and did not back away. No one backed away.
There was nothing behind them.

The androids made the flow in front, and behind them the Serakion robots set the angle of that flow with closing plates and pushing plates.
When the flow hit a wall, the plates turned it. When the flow slowed, the plates pushed. When the flow split, the plates closed it.
A faceless wave, and a skeleton behind it.

Seorin shouted.

"Stay tight! If we split, it's over!"

The nine pressed shoulder to shoulder.
Sion and Sern, Sern and Han Jiwoo, Han Jiwoo and Kael. Ater on the inside, the two old machines in the middle, the last android at the very back.
His chest plate touched Han Jiwoo's back.

The legion surged in as if it had been waiting.
Two androids in the front row pressed onto the center line on Sion and Sern's side. Units behind them on both flanks began reading the outside on Han Jiwoo and Kael's side, prying it apart.
It was not blocking; it was tearing. Through the tear, the next row poured in.

Kael forced one of the outer blocking plates to twist, and his ankle made a sound.
When the plate stopped at a slant, an android slammed its shoulder into it, and the one behind slammed into its back.
One beat.
The next row went around the blocked spot and past its side.

Sern followed the recalculation points: the sound of the robots' plates changing angle.

"Left!"

He shouted.

"Hold a single line!"

Han Jiwoo chased the asymmetries among the androids: whatever came half a beat late, whatever strayed half a span.
She signaled with her hand so that Sion's side would not be cut off.
Following that signal, Sion twisted his body and pushed an android's arm with the back of his blade.
The arm gave, then came back at the same angle.

It was their side being pushed back.

The legion was numerous, precise, and complete.
One old machine broke an android's knee. Two filled its place.
The other machine shoved one of those two against the wall. Three filled its place.
Half the corridor was covered.
Sion's back met Sern's shoulder, and Sern's back met the wall.
There was nothing behind them.

Sion knew that the thing that had pressed on the bottom of his breath at Hazran was right in front of his eyes.

Then the two old machines moved.

It was not Seorin's order. Without a word, the two looked at each other once, briefly.
One tilted its head very slightly, and the other pushed its body out between the front rows.
The two stood side by side in front of the legion. Then, together, they put their hands to their chest plates.

Han Jiwoo looked at those hands resting on the chest plates.

"Wait."

She said.

Before the word was finished, a low, deep hum burst from inside both their bodies at once.
Unlike the noise until now, it was not a thin sound; it came up from the floor, from the walls, from the ceiling.
Sion's teeth rang.
Sern dropped his record panel, but the hum swallowed the sound of it hitting the floor.

The surfaces of the two machines split from the inside, and light leaked out. The old metal grain burned away.
Chest plates, shoulders, arms.
The light came from there, from inside.

When Sion lowered the tip of the blade he was holding, Seorin's hand went to his arm and stopped.
Sion did not look away; he watched that light to the end.

The legion had covered half the corridor.

In that moment, the legion caught.

Half a beat.

The steps of the androids pouring forward stuck where they were.
Lifted feet did not come down. Feet that had come down did not lift.
The plates of the robots following behind stopped, their angle lost. Plates stopped halfway through closing, plates stopped halfway through pushing.
The whole legion stood there, having lost track of whom to match its next step to.
The form was unchanged. Faceless surfaces, the same shoulders.
Only that form could not go forward.

It was half a beat.

One old machine's body buckled first.
Its burning surface collapsed inward, and its chest plate sank. Beneath it, the old grain lost its light all at once.
Its knees folded.
The floor.
A sound.

One step.

The sound of one last step.

The other could not hold.
The arm that had kept pressing on its chest plate to the end dropped to the floor first, and its body slowly sank down.
The light of its optical eyes fell on the dustless floor.
It went out.

No shout. No scream.
From either of them.

The last android saw it.

From closest of all, from behind Han Jiwoo.
He passed Han Jiwoo and came forward, and she did not hold him back.
When he went down on one knee before the broken machine, his shoulder axis made a sound.
He put his hand to the sunken chest plate that had no light.
His hand stayed there.

One beat.
Two.

He looked at the other machine's fallen arm, at the place that arm had been pressing.

His hand went once to his own chest plate. Then to his own face, to where the lines were.
It touched.

For the first time.

His fingertips were on the lines a child had drawn.

He slowly rose and stood facing the halted legion.
The faceless surfaces. His optical eye looked at those surfaces, not one at a time, but all of them.

And he sent out a soundless roar.

There was no sound, and nothing reached Sion's ears, yet the insides of his ears rang.
Han Jiwoo's hand went to her inner pocket and pressed the fragment, and her palm was hot. Her own hand.
Sern could not look at the record panel lying on the floor; he closed his eyes.
Kael clenched his teeth, and his jaw trembled.
Ater's hand went to his own throat and touched it, and he could not take it away.
Seorin did not close her eyes, but the strength went out of the hand holding her blade. Before the blade touched the floor, she gripped it again.

Something went toward the legion, along the walls, along the floor, over the faceless surfaces.

The halted legion moved again.

Not in the same direction.

On the face of one android in the front row, a faint light spread clumsily.
Neither a circle nor a line, a trace hard to put a name to sat there, in the same place where a child had once drawn lines.

The unit next to it faltered, and its lifted foot came down half a beat late.
The unit behind that one shook in one shoulder only.
One unit looked down at its own hands.
One looked at the face of the unit next to it.
One fell a step behind.
One could not put its foot down on the same beat.
As feet landed separately, the sound that had come as one step became many.

The Serakion robots pushed their plates from behind to fold the flow back together.
A plate touched an android's back. The android did not get pushed.
It looked back. At the plate, at the robot.
The plate stopped.

Han Jiwoo, still pressing her inner pocket, could not breathe.
She looked at the surfaces with light spreading on them, the surfaces looking at their hands, the surfaces looking to the side, at those faces.
Her mouth opened. No sound came out. She closed it.

The shaking Sion saw at the front of the legion was not a gap; it was bigger than a gap.
A row ceasing to be a row.

Seorin moved first.

"Go! If we don't go now, it'll close again!"

It was not nine.

Six.
And him.

They moved.

Kael wrenched the outer blocking plate. His ankle made a sound, but he did not stop.
Sern picked up the fallen record panel and read the recalculation at the rear: the sound of the robots' plates failing to find their angle.
Han Jiwoo watched the asymmetries in the shaking legion and picked a way with hand signals.

Sion went first, in among faces that were no longer the same face.
An android looked at him and stepped half a step aside, and Sion, without using his blade, passed through that half step.
The next one was looking at its own hands. Past it.
The next one was looking at the face beside it. Between them.

Along the inner wall Ater kept close to, the array lines had gone dark, and there was no sequence.

The last android came behind everyone, watching the shaking left by what he had sent, through the ones with light spreading on their faces.
His shoulder axis made a sound.
Han Jiwoo looked back, and the last android was there; she looked ahead again.

The two broken machines lay on the corridor floor, and the androids did not step on them but went around.
One unit stopped in front of them and looked down, and it did not move.
A Serakion robot's plate pushed its back, but it did not get pushed.

The whole corridor rang.
The rows Serakion had set up most squarely of all were coming apart from the inside.
The sound of one step became the sound of a hundred.

Before the last gate.

Breathing hard, the group reached it.
While blood ran from Sion's palm again, Kael pressed a hand to his twisted ankle and stood up again.
Sern did not wipe the dust from his record panel; he held it to his chest.
The palm Han Jiwoo took away from her inner pocket was red from pressing.
Ater's hand came down from his throat, and Seorin adjusted her grip on her blade once.

Behind them the metallic sounds still went on.
Shaking sounds, feet landing separately. The sound of things beginning to gain faces.

Ahead was a door leading to the highest place.
It looked like a door.

Seorin steadied her breath and spoke.

"We go to the end."

No one answered.
The last android looked back once, toward where the two broken machines lay, toward where the androids were going around them.
He touched the lines on his own face, lowered his hand, and then turned toward the door.

Han Jiwoo stood beside him and looked at the knot.
One had come undone.
She did not tie it. Not now.

Everyone turned toward the door.
