# Episode 128. Keepers of Memory

After the old machine appeared,
the surrounding air grew noticeably quiet.

No one commanded the silence.
But the beings that had been in this space for a long time
seemed accustomed to not adding sound before that machine spoke.

Sion saw it and understood immediately.

That is not simply an old machine.
Within this community,
it is closer to the side that holds time itself on behalf of others.

The old machine watched the newly arrived people for a while,
then moved its gaze slowly.

The wounded.
The elderly.
Children.
And those still trying to stay standing.

That gaze held none of the reading seen in the Serakion above.
But it wasn't an empty gaze either.

Rather, from having seen too many cases for too long,
it was closer to a gaze that doesn't name things easily.

The machine spoke again.

"Medical parts are not plentiful."
"But treatment needed now comes first."

With those words,
several other machines deeper inside moved at once.

Startlingly quiet.
No one raised their voice,
yet the space began to reorganize very quickly.

But it was different from the arrangement in the Serakion above.

This wasn't classification to separate people.
This was emergency organizing to keep anyone from dying or breaking right now.

Wounded with wounded.
Children and elderly to a warmer space a little deeper in.
Those able to walk directed to help carry supplies and brace others.

Functions were divided.
But value wasn't split.

Sion felt that difference right away.

Even though it looked like the same sorting,
the Serakion above had tried to fix people into its own order.
Here, they were watching who might go out first
and holding the urgent ones.

Jiwoo seemed to notice too,
watching the moving machines' hands for a long time.

The machines above had hands trained beautiful to the fingertips.
But this side was different.

Rough.
Familiarity before efficiency.
Some had fingertip angles slightly off because the parts didn't match.

But strangely,
that made them look more careful when touching people.

When a child saw treatment cloth and grew afraid,
one old machine stopped its hand and very slowly lowered to knee height.

It didn't offer soothing words.
Instead, it let the child see its hand first.

A scratched metal hand.
Worn joints.
Connectors whose color had faded from long use.

The child who saw that stopped crying.

Aka watched the scene and murmured, small.

"This side doesn't hide."

Whether the old machine heard was unclear.
But a moment later,
the machine that had first opened the path said, very low.

"Hiding makes them more afraid."

Luhai snorted small, hearing that.

"Strange."
"Less intact and less scary."

This time Jiwoo answered.

"Because they're not pretending to be intact."

After that single line,
a brief silence passed.

And in that short silence,
Sion felt one more sensation clarifying the divide between above and below in Serakion.

Above is a world that pretends to be complete.
Here is a world that doesn't hide being broken.

That difference,
right now, was saving people.

A moment later,
the old machine turned its gaze back toward Sion's group.

"This is not a place for holding long."
"It is closer to a place that remains
to pass on memory
and make the next failure smaller."

Sion repeated those words to himself.

*Pass on memory.*

Aka reacted first.

"To whom?"

The old machine's optical eye trembled, very faintly.

"To whichever side has not yet broken."

That answer was strangely cold.
But inside it were both the resignation and the will
that only a being who has held on for a long time can carry.

Jiwoo looked at that machine longer without realizing.

This wasn't a living weapon or a management machine.
Rather,
it was closer to a being that had endured beyond its own function
to remember the names, failures, and paths that keep vanishing.

Sern asked, low.

"Do you run this place?"

The old machine shook its head at once.

"We do not run it."
"We maintain it."

A short silence.

The difference between those two words
sounded like the entirety of this community.

Running means order.
Maintaining means slowing collapse.

And this place
was clearly the latter.

Two humans came out from deeper inside carrying a supply crate.
Two other machines wordlessly took shares of the weight.

The motion was natural.
But it looked less like practiced cooperation
and more like an agreement barely reached after long discomfort.

Watching that, Sion felt for the first time
that this place was not simply where those who hide had gathered.

This is not a place for combat alone.
This is a structure inside a world that keeps collapsing,
where memory, purpose, and the record of failures
are passed on, very slowly.

People and machines,
never fully trusting each other,
yet holding on to something that must not, in the end, be severed.

The old machine looked at Sion's group one last time and spoke.

"Questions come later."
"First, the number of what was lost must be counted."

Those words were ruthlessly calm.
But no one could argue.

After entering Serakion,
they learned for the first time.

At the center of this old layer
were not those who shout about changing the world,
but those who remain first—
trying not to forget how much of the world has already broken.
