# Episode 209. Sign Becomes Direction

The silence that had remained inside the ship did not break easily.

Even after Aka said everything she had seen, no one could follow right away.

Until moments ago it had been the silence of people who had left something behind. Now, mixed in, was the sense that they might have to enter somewhere again.

Sion looked at Aka first.

She was still unable to easily pull her gaze from the newly risen light.

The face of someone who could not precisely explain what she had just felt.

Seorin spoke first.

"Can you see it again?"

Aka could not nod right away.

"A little. But it is not because it is close. I just… think it reached me."

"Deliberately?"

Aka shook her head.

"No. That side was there first. I just… got caught on it."

Ater was not looking at the newly risen light. He was looking at Aka.

"If you feel it again, tell us. This time, cut it into smaller pieces."

Aka did not immediately understand what Ater meant. Sern did.

He glanced at Ater briefly, then added very low.

"Breathing. Temperature. Movement. And if a stop comes, what came right before."

Aka finally nodded small.

Sion only then, hearing that brief exchange, realized how far Ater and Sern had seen the same picture.

Both knew the Empire.

Ater from the side of knowing the structure. Sern from the side of knowing the actual operation.

And what Aka had just described carried exactly the kind of scent that made both stiffen at the same time.

Kael unfolded his arms and asked.

"Explain."

Ater seemed to be choosing his words briefly.

As always, he did not make definitive statements until certainty had gathered to the very end.

"If it were a standard detention structure, it would not be felt that way."

Kael narrowed his brows slightly.

"That way meaning."

"Meaning it is being managed on the side of keeping alive."

A brief silence passed.

Ater continued without lowering his gaze.

"The Empire has places that simply cage. But it also has structures that neither kill nor release—holding a subject in a needed state for a long time. Monitoring condition over movement. Preventing collapse before escape. What Aka described is closer to that."

Sern added very low.

"Touch it wrong, and the disappearance side is fast too."

Aka's expression tightened slightly.

"Disappearance?"

Sern did not answer right away.

Instead he lowered his gaze to the floor once, then raised it.

"It does not mean vanishing without a trace. Records, physical condition, access lines—all are organized first. It is a structure where the searching side is late."

Sion clenched and unclenched his fingers for no particular reason.

The name Elia had not been confirmed from anyone's mouth yet.

But that name kept remaining somewhere inside the ship.

Seorin touched it first.

"So. Both of you are thinking the same thing?"

Neither Ater nor Sern answered right away.

Ater spoke first.

"The probability of the Empire is high."

Sern followed as though receiving.

"High probability of a central-division classification."

Kael asked slowly.

"Including Elia?"

No one answered that question hastily.

That brief hesitation was more vivid than any answer.

Sion kept his gaze on the two lights and said it very low.

"I do not know. But…"

He stopped there.

He did not want to speak like certainty when there was none.

But the fact that he could not escape that name was also true.

Seorin glanced at Sion briefly.

In that short gaze alone, she already wore the face of someone who knew who Sion was thinking of.

She did not go so far as to say it for him.

The one who broke the silence first was Sern.

"There is a way in."

This time, everyone truly looked at him.

Sern straightened from his leaning posture against the wall.

The moment he spoke that line, his face showed he knew: this was no longer a matter of sensation or estimation, but had crossed into something they could actually move on.

Kael asked immediately.

"Into the Empire?"

"Not straight to the center. But there are lines that reach the perimeter."

"Empire-style authorization lines?"

Sern shook his head.

"No. Going through those gets read immediately."

Ater continued as though picking up.

"Official lines will not work. There is a high chance that side does not yet fully know what we touched. All the more reason not to enter through official channels."

Seorin sorted it short.

"So you are saying the Empire is more likely running covert tracking than a full-scale search right now."

"Yes."

Ater cut it short.

"The Serakion problem is too large. Rather than shaking things publicly, they are more likely to try burying it first."

Kael heard that and let out a breath like a sigh.

"Good. So here's where we are: we might have to go into the Empire, they can't come looking for us openly yet, and there's a way in, just not a normal one."

"Not a normal way." Sern said again. "Not a good way either."

Luhai cut in for the first time then.

"Wow, that is really reassuring. Not good and not normal."

Aka fired back immediately.

"Stop it."

Luhai laughed, incredulous.

"Why are you mad at me? I was lightening the mood."

"It did not lighten."

Jiwoo, eyes open, looked at the two of them once, then spoke low.

"The ship?"

Kael looked her way immediately.

"I can hide it. But not for long. Get too close to the Empire's outer lines and they'll read the hull records sooner or later."

"Then we need to disembark before that."

"Yeah."

Jiwoo asked nothing more. Just nodded once.

The face of someone whose calculation was already done.

Seorin asked with arms crossed.

"That way in—who manages it?"

Sern was quiet for a moment.

That silence was closer to weighing whether to hide or to speak.

Sion watched and felt that Sern was not simply pulling out one route, but preparing to expose an old line from deep inside himself for the first time.

Sern finally said.

"My people."

The playfulness left Luhai's face for a moment.

Aka did not interject this time either.

Sion asked very slowly.

"Can they be trusted?"

Sern did not answer that question right away.

Instead he looked at Sion straight on.

"I do not mean you should trust me completely. However. To get in, they are the only way."

Sion looked at Sern's face a little longer.

He could tell this person was not saying this because he had no fear.

The opposite.

It was the face of someone who knew the path too well, and therefore could only speak this way.

Ater said it very low.

"Let us take that path."

Sern heard and shifted his gaze very faintly.

He did not look surprised. But the fact that those words had come from Ater's mouth before his own had clearly landed with weight.

Sion looked between the two.

The heir of Valkar. And the person who had attended him.

Both were not receiving the words "return to the Empire" with exactly the same weight.

But right now, neither was stepping back.

Sion looked at the two lights again.

Strangely, they were quieter than before.

That quiet was closer not to hesitation, but to the quiet of people who had already begun to tilt in a direction.

He steadied his breath, brief.

"All right."

No one interrupted.

"We take that path."

It was not a grand declaration.

But after that single line fell, the air inside the ship changed once more.

This was no longer a vague premonition. It was a path that had actually begun to move.

Sern said nothing more.

Instead he stepped one pace forward.

And said it very low.

"Then we enter from the perimeter."

After a very brief pause, he spoke about time for the first time.

"But it has to be fast. Once the supply gap from the Serakion side starts being felt outside, the Empire will raise perimeter surveillance levels. Lines that are still open now will close after that."

They had to enter before the window closed.

That single fact turned the direction from a decision into a departure.

After those words, it felt as though no one could return to where they had been before.
