# Episode 34. The Name People Swallowed

Going into Hazran did not mean walking straight at the market they could see in the distance.

It was closer to the opposite.

Walk straight in and they would reek of outsiders. As soon as they left the hull's shadow, the four of them slowed down. Fitting their bodies to this planet's wind and the shadows of the wrecked hulls mattered more than walking fast.

Seorin walked at the front. Sion kept half a step behind her, and Ater looked less at the open view than at the traces left underfoot and the resonance clinging to metal surfaces. Kael, at the very back, was reading not so much who was following them as who had been watching them first.

With every step, dry powder scraped thinly under their boots. Inside the shadows of the hulks it was bearable, but step out into the sun once and the heat came straight up through the cloth.

After a long stretch of walking without a word, Sion asked, low.

"Is it always this quiet?"

Kael answered shortly.

"It's not quiet."

He said it without raising his head.

"They're watching us first."

The moment he finished, Sion felt it too.

The spaces between the hulks he had thought were empty were in fact so quiet that no one in them showed a sign of being there. In the gaps of half-collapsed decks, in the shadows of torn-open cargo holds, under every blackened hull plate, there were eyes. They just didn't show themselves openly; they were eyes measuring who had come in from where and what they had brought.

Seorin barely moved her lips.

"Eyes forward."

Sion kept his head where it was. At a moment like this, a face that said *I know you're there* was the more dangerous one.

A little farther in, something that could be called a path appeared. At first it was only footprints pressed over and over between the shadows of the hulks; then wheel ruts overlapped them, and a little later there was even a short stretch paved with iron plates. It was not a proper road. But it was a line made by people coming and going in the same direction again and again.

Ater spoke, looking at the ground.

"Hauling traces."

"What do you think went through here?"
Sion asked.

"Something heavy."
Ater answered shortly.
"And often."

Kael added.

"People too."

Sion did not let that slide past. It meant that parts, water, fuel and the price of people all sat on the same line here.

What appeared first at the end of the path was less a market than the outer layer of one.

Scraps of torn awning hung clumsily between metal frames, and water cans and fuel cans, hastily lashed to the side of an old cargo container, were gathered close to the ground. Someone was grinding scrap into small plates; someone was hanging filter cloth out to dry. On the surface it all looked makeshift, and that was exactly why it looked like a place that had lasted longer than it seemed.

The first to speak to them was a child.

A child who looked under ten stopped halfway through carrying a bucket of metal shavings and stared straight at the four of them. However hard they had tried to hide that they were outsiders, it had not been fully wiped away. The child ran a look over Sion's face, Seorin's clothes, the gear in Ater's hand, and finally the inside of Kael's coat, one after another, then muttered very quietly.

"More of them."

Sion did not pretend he hadn't heard.

"More of what?"
he asked.

The child didn't answer. Instead the child turned around and looked toward a tent farther in. The next moment, a woman who had been there pulled the child back by the arm.

"Don't look."
She said it low and fast.
"Stare at outsiders and get tangled up with them, and it's your price that gets cut first."

She said it to the child, but her eyes brushed briefly over Seorin.

Seorin showed no reaction at all. Without breaking stride, she passed the woman and the child as naturally as anything. Sion did not break the rhythm either. In a place like this, the overcharging would probably start the moment you stopped to ask a question.

A little farther in, the smell changed all at once.

Between the smells of sand and iron filings, the smell of long-boiled oil, human sweat and damp filter cloth began to mix in. Only then could they feel the market getting close. It was still the edge, but from here on it was a district where not only goods but voices turned into money.

Kael spoke very low.

"From there on, even one word comes with a price."

"I like it even less when you're the one saying it,"
Sion muttered.

Instead of answering, Kael only moved the corner of his mouth, very briefly.

That brief change, oddly, made Sion more uneasy. This man was not finding the place strange. If anything, he was reading it with great familiarity.

Seorin spoke with her eyes on the way ahead.

"Information first."

Sion took it up at once.

"Etherite and Aka?"

"Both,"
Seorin said.
"But Etherite is the urgent one. If the ship can't lift off, finding the name won't matter."

Ater filled the space between their words, low.

"It would be better to split the questions. The moment one mouth asks about both, the price is likely to jump or the two will be tied together."

Seorin looked at Ater once.

"Where did you learn that?"

"That was the kind of work it was."

Seorin didn't ask twice.

She settled it briefly.

"Fine. I'll touch the Etherite first. Sion, you just watch how they react to the name."

Sion nodded.

"And Ater?"

"If anything wrong comes out between the two, cut it off,"
Seorin answered.

Kael, without asking anything more, was already running his eyes over the booths around them and the people's hands. Who touched the goods directly, who only talked and then stepped back, who was not the owner here but acted like one. They were the eyes of a man for whom a market was nothing new.

The first one they snagged on was a man who looked like some kind of water seller.

He smiled the moment he saw the four of them coming. It was not a glad smile; it was the smile of someone who had already finished working out, in his head, how much he could get.

"New faces,"
he said.
"You look thirsty. Buy water first. In Hazran, the one in a hurry pays the most."

Seorin stopped.

"Have you seen Etherite?"

The man's smile shook, very slightly.

"Plenty,"
he answered smoothly.
"There's powder, there's crumbs, there's some that's half dead. If the price is right, there's nothing I can't get."

Seorin did not move straight on to the next question. Instead she looked into the man's eyes for a moment and spoke low.

"Real ones too?"

This time it was the man who fell silent first.

The smile stayed, but his eyes changed a little. It was the look of someone who had read that this was not a market novice repeating any name they'd picked up, but a hand that at least knew what it was asking for.

"You're looking for the real thing?"
he asked back.
"Then you'll have to put up something besides money."

Kael tossed a word in from the side.

"That's a rip-off."

The man's ears did not miss it.

"You there, in black,"
he said, smiling.
"Name me one thing in Hazran that isn't a rip-off."

Sion, in that gap, was looking somewhere else.

While the talk of Etherite went back and forth, the people around them split two ways. One kind simply caught the smell of a price. The other, the moment they heard the word, pretended they hadn't heard it at all and drifted farther off. It was the reaction you got when a name belonged to something truly precious, or to something that was trouble to get tangled up with.

So Sion asked, in a deliberately offhand voice.

"Then let me ask about a name too."

Seorin's eyes came his way very briefly and moved on. She didn't stop him. Sion went on.

"Aka."

This time the man's face stopped more clearly.

It was only an instant, but it was clear. Before he could put on a face that said he didn't know, a look that said he did passed over it first.

And faster than that was a child's voice that burst out from the side alley.

"Aka of the Red Ember?"

The very next moment, another hand clamped over the child's mouth.

"Quiet."

It was a low, rough voice.

People's eyes gathered on one spot for a moment, then scattered as if nothing had happened. Everyone pretended not to have heard, too quickly. That was the surer reaction.

In that short silence, Sion knew.

It was real.

At least the name was no phantom.
And here, it was a name you did not say carelessly.

Seorin spoke first.

"Nice,"
she said, low.
"Now you can't tell us you don't know."

The water seller hung his forced smile back on.

"So you know one name. What does that change?"
he said.
"In Hazran, price comes before a name."

"That's your side's problem."
Seorin cut him off.
"All we need is to find where that price gets set."

The man smiled instead of answering. But this smile had none of the lightness from before. It was a face where, ahead of the fun of fleecing outsiders, came the discomfort of knowing that touching too deep a name for no reason could shake his own price too.

Ater lowered his voice.

"He is avoiding the name."

"And he wants to sell the Etherite,"
Sion muttered.

Kael spoke briefly.

"Could be both are under the same hand."

Sion heard that and did not take it up right away. What he needed to confirm now was not that, but where this market started watching its step.

Just then, a short shout burst out a little outside the heart of the market.

"Hey, let go of that."

Before they could even turn their heads, the clang of metal on metal went off again and again. The sound of an old cargo container toppling, a voice spitting out a curse as if swallowing it, and then the stir of onlookers pulling back half a beat late.

From that short commotion alone, Sion could tell.

The air of the market's outer layer had changed once again.

From air that swallowed a name to air where everyone was watching who had wrongly touched the real thing.

Seorin's eyes had already read the same thing.

"Good,"
she said, without smiling.
"Now it really starts."
