# Episode 39. The Breath She Did Not Show

The way Harun led them had a different grain from both the outer-layer market and the inner workshop.

The deeper they went, the fewer people there were, and the clearer each section's silence became. If in the outer layer it was eyes pretending not to see that came first, in here it was a discipline of not looking at all. The hands that passed weren't pretending not to see them; they moved like people who had trained into their bodies that they really must not look. That was more suffocating.

Nasim still walked in front with a smiling face, but he had grown quieter. Harun had not looked back once from start to finish. Instead his back alone carried the kind of pressure that settled how far you were to follow.

From about halfway, Luhai had shut his mouth altogether. Less from fear than like a kid who had learned too fast that in here eyes outlive mouths. Still, he hadn't turned meek. Several times Sion saw Luhai's gaze skim quickly over the markings on the walls, the locked drums, the hands of the people coming and going. The kid wasn't hiding; he was memorizing, deeper.

Ater read the structures they passed only very briefly and moved on. In the market and the workshop he had been the one looking at traces, but in here he seemed, if anything, to avoid seeing too much. Like someone who knew that some places notice you first just for reading them.

Sion tried not to touch the paper inside his jacket. **Hazran**, **Aka**. Those two words no longer felt like a direction; they felt like a measure of how far inside he had come. He had come to find one name, and had been pulled first into the prices and hands and discipline surrounding it.

And in between those short silences, Seorin slowed her step, just for a moment.

To anyone else it might have looked like she was only resetting her footing. But Sion knew at once. Seorin hadn't stopped; she had caught her breath once so that she wouldn't stop. The fingers of her right hand curled and opened for the briefest moment, and the line of her jaw tightened, barely, then eased again.

It was the kind of tiredness two sentences were enough for.

The breath of someone who wished nobody had seen, and had already decided she would not break anyway.

Sion said nothing on purpose. At moments like that, pretending not to see suited Seorin better than comfort.

Seorin matched the pace again, as if nothing at all had happened.

And she spoke very low.

"The smell changes up ahead."

Hearing that, Kael lifted his head a little.

"Water,"
he said shortly.
"And medicine."

Up front, Nasim tossed back in a voice with a laugh in it.

"Good noses on you."

Only then did Sion catch it too.

Beneath the smells of metal and oil, heat and dust, another grain began to mix in. The smell of water kept warm for a long time, the faint smell of disinfecting herbs, and the dry smell of a space where the cloth was changed often. Not a workshop or a warehouse: it was the smell of a space that kept someone there for a long time.

When the passage turned one last time, the inner section came into view.

It was a closed-off area, screened by tall hulk walls and layer upon layer of awnings. It wasn't exactly grand. In true Hazran fashion it was still rough and practical, with cloth that showed its stitching and metal plates patched on in plain sight. But there was something clearly different from the outer layer and the inner workshop. Here they weren't only holding out; they were hiding something, protecting it, looking after it.

Sion saw it at first glance.

This was not a place that only kept goods tied down.

Harun stopped.

They stood before an entrance that was hard to call a door, but was guarded like one. On either side, instead of open guards, stood people who were pretending to work and had stopped their hands. Eyes that pretended not to look but were in fact recording everyone who came in and went out.

Nasim looked back.

"Good,"
he said gently.
"From here on, your face costs more than your mouth."

Luhai muttered under his breath.

"Then you're already broke."

Kael jabbed the kid in the ribs with his elbow at once.

"Quiet."

This time, though, even Nasim looked like he couldn't hold back a laugh. That didn't mean he let them loosen up any further. He was someone used to scattering the tension just a little like that, and then tightening it even more.

Harun sent a very brief signal inward.

The cloth inside was drawn back, and what came out first was not a person but a gaze.

It was different from the eyes of the workshop people. The calculating and the wariness were the same, but before that, these eyes were closer to *checking*. Eyes that looked first at what the newcomers had brought and what they reacted to, rather than at who they were.

The moment that gaze landed on him, Sion forced his hand to stop before it went to his jacket.

That was what those eyes were watching. Not what he had brought, but where his hand went.

**Aka**

One name was finally drawing closer to something real.

In the shadow of the inner awning stood a woman.

She wasn't old, but she had the face of someone who had held out a long time inside this section. Clothes that put use before decoration, eyes that looked first at who was hurt, and yet lips that measured first who would cross the line. Sion knew it by instinct. This was not just a hand that waited on people. This was a hand that kept the people inside in order and set how far anyone could go.

It was Nahira.

Nasim spoke first.

"Got a bit noisy outside,"
he said, smiling.
"But thanks to that, a few interesting things came in with us."

Nahira didn't react to that right away. Instead she swept Sion's group and Luhai in a single look, and finally let her gaze rest for a moment on the Etherite fragment in Luhai's hand. Then on the inside of Kael's coat, and along the line of Sion's jacket. She didn't look too openly, and that was why hers were the eyes of someone who had seen more.

"All of them, in here?"
she asked, low.

Harun answered briefly.

"All of them."

Nahira let out a very short breath. Not from tiredness; it was closer to resignation, the breath of someone who had known that, at this rate, it would end up coming all the way inside.

"Fine,"
she said.
"Then at least they won't make any more noise outside."

Hearing that, Seorin spoke up, very low.

"Is it less noisy inside?"

For the first time, Nahira properly looked at Seorin.

"On the surface,"
she answered.

It was a short answer, but Sion felt that one phrase stay with him strangely long. It was a voice that admitted far too easily that the inside was quieter than the outside, and that this did not mean it was peaceful.

Nasim added lightly.

"Etherite, one name, and one kid who reads ledgers,"
he said.
"Pretty thick crowd of guests today, huh?"

Nahira's gaze sank, very slightly.

"Don't say that name carelessly in here either,"
she said, low.

Luhai muttered.

"That's all anybody says."

This time nobody answered him.

That short silence was all the sharper for it. It meant that even in here Aka was not just a person's name. The moment he heard it, Sion felt the weight shift further toward one side: Aka was not something that only drifted around the outer layer as a rumor, but someone being directly looked after on the inside.

Nahira stepped aside.

"Come in,"
she said.
"But once you're in, things won't roll along in the order you ask them anymore."

To Sion, that sounded almost like a warning.

They weren't going in to ask questions. They were going in to be sorted into the order inside.

Harun went in first, and Nasim followed. Luhai tried to hold his ground for a moment, but when Kael gave his hand the smallest tug, he finally moved his feet. Ater quietly took in the inner structure once more, and Seorin, as if nothing had happened, was the first to cross the line.

Sion followed them inside.

Past the threshold, the sound changed. In the outer layer the loudest thing had been prices being called out; here there was none of that. There were voices, but no numbers could be heard.

Sion thought about it as he walked. Here, prices weren't set out loud.

If they weren't set out loud, anyone who hadn't heard would never know.

Somewhere in there was Aka.
