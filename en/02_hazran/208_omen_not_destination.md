# Episode 208. Omen, Not Destination

After fully leaving Serakion's planet, the inside of the ship was too quiet.

This vessel had never been a loud place to begin with.

But the quiet now was different from the silence that had always been there.

Only a short while ago, this interior had been full of the people brought from Hazran.

In every narrow corridor, there had been the sense of bodies passing each other. Somewhere, a child's crying would break out. Low voices calling for someone. The sounds of people who could not sleep turning over. The breath of barely surviving people confirming each other's survival—filling the ship without pause, day and night.

All that presence felt as though it had drained out at once.

The Hazran people had returned toward Hazran. The beings who would stay in Serakion had stayed. Inside the ship, only those who would travel the next path together remained.

Seorin. Sern. Ater. Kael. Jiwoo. Luhai. Aka. And Serakion.

The ship was not entirely empty.

Yet it looked wider than before.

Because the places where too many people had once barely lain, leaned, and shared breath—those were now left as open space.

The cargo hold was empty. The corridors held footsteps too long. Each time someone steadied their breathing, even that small friction sounded more distinct than before.

Sion could not easily break that stillness.

More than relief at having survived, the sense that this time they had truly left something behind when they departed was larger.

It had been that way leaving Hazran. It had been that way leaving Serakion.

There were things barely held and carried out. And things that had to be left in that place.

The two kept mixing, and Sion could not easily sort whether this ship carried people who had come back alive, or people who had left too much behind.

No one spoke first.

Seorin wore the face of someone who had already scanned the cockpit record lines once and returned. Kael had checked the entry-point anchors and outer wall responses before barely releasing his tension. Sern leaned against the wall like someone who could not fully let go of any part of his body. Ater moved his gaze as though unconsciously calculating something each time the air inside the ship shifted.

Serakion stood in a quiet place inside the corridor.

Even after receiving his name, he still did not speak.

Instead, he seemed closer to the side quietly enduring the stillness flowing through this ship—more patiently than anyone.

Jiwoo had her eyes closed, head leaning briefly. Aka sat with knees drawn up, looking somewhere on the floor without a word.

That was when it happened.

Deep inside the ship, beside the small light that had always remained quietly in its place, a very faint tremor rose first.

At first, no one reacted right away.

The light was not strong. So faint, in fact, that it felt as though the air changed before the eyes noticed.

The sensation of one very thin breath seeping into a corner of dead space.

Aka raised her head first.

Sion looked that way too.

Beside that light—which had stayed quietly as though it remembered human warmth for a long time—above an empty spot, one thin line was slowly surfacing.

Seorin said it low.

"Another one forming."

Again, Aka did not answer.

She was already unable to look away from the newly appearing trace.

The new light was a different grain from the stillness that had been sitting inside the ship until moments ago.

Cold. Courteous. And carrying the feel of something that had mimicked perfect order for too long, only to finally have the hesitation remaining inside it exposed.

It resembled the strangely neat air they had seen at Serakion's upper level to the very end—yet was simultaneously more trembling, more alive.

Looking at it long enough, there was a sensation that arrived before explanation.

A grain that seemed to have hesitated for too long. A grain that had stopped but ultimately moved again. A trace closer to choice than to command.

That light did not touch the small trace that had originally remained there.

Instead, from a very close distance, each was faintly catching the other's light.

One held the residual warmth of hot earth and a life returned late. The other, even within the memory of cold, deep metal, held traces of hesitation and choice that had never been fully erased.

They were entirely different grains.

But inside the same ship, they did not push each other away.

That strangely changed even the air inside the vessel.

Until moments ago, the ship had felt like a space where you should breathe small—having left too much behind.

But after those two lights rose side by side, the stillness became something entirely different.

It did not feel hollow.

Quiet, yes—but now inside that quiet, a grain had appeared as though the things they had passed through were riding along.

Calling it rich might be too much. But at least it no longer felt like dead space.

Sion watched the scene for a long time.

What had remained on this ship from passing through Hazran, and what had newly attached from passing through Serakion—the sense of them finally being placed on the same path was strangely vivid.

It felt as though the things they had passed through were choosing the next path.

Sern watched both from a more distant seat, for a long while.

One looked like breath that had endured after losing too many names. The other looked like proof that even something not human could ultimately tilt toward the human side.

Jiwoo raised her head and watched the two lights for a long time, then wordlessly clenched and unclenched her fingertips once.

Ater had first been watching the reactions more than the lights themselves.

But at some point, he too could not look away.

In a place that had divided people and machines to the end through alignment and approval, classification and recovery, all in the wrong order—the fact that what remained at the very end was this kind of trembling was not light at all.

And then Serakion moved.

Standing silently until now, he shifted his gaze very slowly toward where the two lights floated.

He did not approach.

Instead, from a slight distance, he watched them both for a long time.

Serakion inside the ship, and the thing that had followed from Serakion.

Two that brought the same name to mind, placed in one space—yet strangely, it was clearer rather than confusing.

The grain of a world left behind, and the being who had passed through that world and was now departing together—both visible at the same time, from different positions.

Serakion said nothing.

But Aka, nearby, did not miss his fingertips trembling very faintly, then stopping.

Then Aka's expression changed suddenly.

Not the face of someone watching light. The face of someone who had suddenly, without warning, seen somewhere very far and unreachable.

Sion called low.

"Aka."

Aka could not answer right away.

She took one step toward the newly risen light, then stopped there.

"Someone is there."

Seorin asked short.

"Who."

Aka closed her eyes and opened them.

"I cannot see well. But… they are trapped."

The air inside the ship shifted, just slightly.

Aka continued, feeling her own words as though tracing them.

"Cannot move. Cold. And… the breathing has not stopped, but it feels like it keeps being permitted."

Those few short words alone changed Ater's gaze.

Sern's shoulders stiffened, barely.

Sion noticed both had caught on the same thing at the same moment.

Ater did not ask right away.

Instead he quietly linked, as though passing through once—the newly risen light, Aka's fingertips, and the words that had just spilled.

Trapped. Cannot move. Cold. Breathing being permitted.

Ater said it very low.

"The Empire."

No one said a name right away.

But strangely, Sion was thinking of one.

Elia.

The person who read severed sequences before surfaces. Who read the grain of wounds before names. Who spoke short and dry, yet never let truly dangerous things pass lightly.

The moment Sion thought of that name, he felt his breath catch for no clear reason.

Whether it was truly Elia could not yet be known.

But strangely, no other name came as easily.

The first to speak was Aka.

"I do not know who. But they have not broken yet."

Sion did not answer.

He looked at the two lights again.

One felt like the breath left behind by a place already passed through. The other felt like the hesitation a world they had just departed had ultimately handed over.

Neither looked like simple remains.

They looked like things asking, right here, right now, about the next path.

Where should we go now.

Who will we not leave behind this time.

No one continued right away.

But the stillness that had been flowing through the ship was already something different from moments before.

This path, it seemed, would no longer remain simply a matter of choosing the next destination.
