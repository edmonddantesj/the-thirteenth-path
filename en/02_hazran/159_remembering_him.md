# Episode 159. Remembering Him

While preparing the third rescue, the air inside the hidden layer tightened steadily.

Everyone knew now. If they went back in, this time they would be going in toward that child.

The problem was that the sense of that child being more than just the one they failed to bring was growing sharper.

Jiwoo could not explain it at first.

She only thought the child stayed in her eyes strangely. The habit of checking sideways to the end. The eyes that never missed who slipped out. The way the body twisted then swallowed it again.

But as time passed, that familiarity moved from sensation closer to memory.

Among the children she had seen in Hazran, there had been ones like that.

Children who, even terrified, looked at other people first to the end. Children who, right before running, checked the speed of the person beside them before their own feet. Children who scanned for the side retreating before they cried.

That was less a habit Serakion had made, and more resembled the kind of reflex that develops in children who have survived collapsing places for too long.

Jiwoo was sitting against the wall when she said it very low.

"That child is from the Hazran side."

Ater raised his head. Kael shifted his gaze.

Jiwoo did not continue immediately.

Because the moment she said it aloud, that sensation would stop being a guess and become a memory that truly needed to be retrieved.

But she said it.

"That child — there is a grain I saw in Hazran."

Ater was silent for a while. Then he said it very low.

"I felt something similar."

What he recalled was not the face itself. It was the way of not looking up. The gaze that watched others first even while enduring. And the movement that tried to follow so briefly, then swallowed itself at the end.

When Hazran collapsed, children could never move on their fear alone. There were always children who looked first — who was running, who stayed, who could not follow.

That was a survival habit, and also the expression of children forced to watch others before themselves too early.

Kael heard both of them but did not reach a conclusion right away.

He was the type who matched verifiable behavior against memory, not emotion.

"We need to see more." He said it low. "Looking similar does not make them the same."

Jiwoo did not argue. Because he was right.

Similar habits could develop inside Serakion too. Fear can produce similar expressions. Confinement can leave similar habits behind.

But even so, neither of them could simply pass that child over.

And that very day, one of the three children brought back in the first rescue let a few more words slip in a nearly inaudible voice.

"That child..."

Jiwoo raised her head immediately.

The child's lips were drying. The voice was still small. But this time it did not stop at "one more" like before.

"...was always the one who stayed."

The air in the room thinned.

Jiwoo lowered herself further.

"What did you see?"

The child looked around once with frightened eyes. Then spoke again, very small, almost like breathing.

"Kept... looking back. Not at the side being taken away. At the side that stayed..."

That was closer to a trace held by the child's senses than precise information.

But that was why it felt more real.

That child had recognized it too. The direction the small child inside the line had been watching was not simply up or ahead — it was always the side that remained, and behind.

Ater asked low.

"Is there a possibility they remember Hazran?"

No one could answer right away.

Because that single possibility could completely change the weight of the next rescue.

If that child was truly holding on to memory from the Hazran side, this retrieval was not just another rescue of one more person.

It could be going back to belatedly reclaim a piece of time they had thought lost and passed over long ago.

Though no one had explicitly spoken those words yet, Jiwoo was already sensing it dimly.

If they saw that child again, someone would eventually be unable to just pass by. Not simply because the child was from Hazran, but because they might be touching something within the losses already traveled through.

Only after hearing that conversation did Kael redesign the rescue.

The objective was no longer simple retrieval.

"This time we build around that child." He said it low. "Even if we extract others alongside, the axis is that child."

The first recovery hand asked.

"Why."

Kael answered short.

"That child has already seen the outside once. There is a high chance they know the outside is coming to see them again. Then their side, our side, and the remaining children will all move differently."

Those words sounded like both rescue calculation and a declaration of shifting the emotional center.

It was not about extracting one child. It meant the entire memory surrounding that child was beginning to move.

Jiwoo slowly clenched and unclenched her hand. Then said it low.

"Let us go back."

This time the words did not sound like impulse.

Everyone was already looking at the same face, thinking the same possibility, and had already accepted the same fear.

Ater closed his eyes and opened them, saying it very short.

"This time we bring them back."

That was less a vow and closer to a judgment that there was no more time to delay.

At the end of that night, what solidified inside the hidden layer was not just the rescue plan.

It was memory.

Who that child was had not been fully confirmed yet. But everyone's memory had already tilted toward being unable to simply pass that child by.

And that tilt was turning the next retrieval from a simple repetition into truly going back in one last time.
