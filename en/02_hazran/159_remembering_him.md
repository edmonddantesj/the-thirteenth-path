# Episode 159. How Memory Recognizes

While they prepared for the third rescue, no one in the depths of the hidden level made a loud sound.

The second recovery hand walked with a hand on the wall, its straightened leg dragging once with every step and making a sound. It went to the end of the wall and came back, then went to the end of the wall again.

"Stop it."

Han Jiwoo said.

"I have to make it quieter."

"It won't get quieter. It's the pin."

The second went to the end of the wall once more, but the dragging sound was the same. It didn't sit; it stood with its back to the wall.

The first recovery hand stood in front of Han Jiwoo and held out its hand, and Han Jiwoo took it by the wrist and pulled. The first came along. Too easily.

"A kid doesn't come like that. Pull against me."

Her fingers tightened on the wrist.

The first pulled against her, and when Han Jiwoo pulled again, the wrist slipped in her hand. She moved her grip above the wrist, to where the bone was, and pulled again, and it didn't slip.

"Here. Kids have thin wrists. Grab the bone."

The first looked at its own wrist, then touched the spot with its other hand.

"This is the first time I have been the hand that pulls them out."

"I know."

"I have always stood at the back. I did not use my hands."

"Use them now."

The first held out its hand again, and when Han Jiwoo took it and pulled, it came along. Less easily.

Kael was sitting in front of the lines on the floor, where two lines and the short notch were drawn. He made a dot beside the notch, and beside that dot he drew one more line, shorter.

"What's this?"

Luhai asked.

"That kid."

Kael said.

"This is the adult next to that kid. The shaking hand."

"Thought you said we don't bring adults."

"We don't. But if that adult slows down, the kid comes out."

His fingertip touched the line beside the dot, and Luhai looked at the lines and the dot.

In front of the wall, Ater was pacing out the other side's walk: one step, a stop, then the step again. Luhai's feet, passing in front of him, didn't stop.

"You do not stop anymore, I see."

Ater said.

"'Cause I've seen it."

Luhai answered.

"See it three times and you don't stop."

Ater stopped and looked at his own feet.

"Is three times enough?"

"For me."

Luhai said.

"Don't know about the kids."

Han Jiwoo went over to the three Hazran children. Aka was beside them, and the child holding the screwdriver still hadn't let go of Aka's sleeve. Han Jiwoo crouched in front of that child.

"Let me ask you something."

The child looked at her.

"At the foot of the ramp. What did you see?"

The child didn't answer right away, and the hand on the screwdriver tightened. Aka put a hand on the child's back.

"Behind."

The child said.

"What was behind?"

"People who weren't coming."

Han Jiwoo held her breath, while the child said nothing more and drew the screwdriver in against their arm.

Han Jiwoo didn't get up; she stayed there a long while. Aka looked at her but said nothing.

Han Jiwoo came back to the wall, sat down, and spoke very low.

"That's Hazran."

Ater raised his head, and Kael's gaze moved too.

Han Jiwoo didn't go on at once; she looked at her own hand, closed it, and opened it.

"That kid has a habit I saw in Hazran."

Ater, who had been silent for some time, came off the wall.

"I felt something similar."

"Which part?"

"Not looking up."

Ater said.

"The children at the foot of the ramp did not look up either. They looked to the back of the column, at who could not climb. And the way they start to follow and then put the foot back down. Children who count first who cannot come do that."

"Serakion kids are like that too."

Kael said.

Ater looked at him.

"Serakion children do not turn their feet first."

Ater said.

"A child who turns and then puts the foot back is a child who has tried turning before."

Kael didn't take it up right away; he looked at the dot beside the lines on the floor.

"We need to see more."

His voice stayed low.

"Looking alike isn't the same as being the same."

Han Jiwoo didn't argue, and put her back to the wall.

"I know. Look anyway."

"I'll look."

Kael said.

"We look, and we go."

Sion was sitting beside the old Hazran man. There were sixteen lines on the plate, and when Sion looked at the back, there was one.

"From thirty-six, eight. Wasn't it?"

Sion asked.

"It was."

"How many of them were children?"

The old man looked at the plate.

"Didn't count them separately."

He said.

"Counted them all together. A child's a person too."

"Still."

"Lots. More than now."

His fingernail swept once along the edge of the plate.

The woman who came with two children heard that and shifted one child in her arms.

"A kid who looks to the side first?"

She asked.

Han Jiwoo looked her way.

"There were lots of kids like that. Back there."

The woman said.

"Mine's like that too. Even now."

She looked at the child in her arms, and the child was looking to the side. Not at Han Jiwoo, but beside Han Jiwoo.

Han Jiwoo couldn't say anything.

Just then one of the children brought back in the first rescue spoke: the child who looks down, the one who had stood under the yellow lamp. It was the first time.

"That kid…"

Han Jiwoo looked up, but the child was looking down. Their lips were dry, and their voice was small.

"…was on the side that stays."

Luhai set the bowl down, and Sern looked up from the record panel.

Han Jiwoo got down in front of the child, close to the floor, down to where the child was looking.

"What did you see?"

From down there the child looked around once. There were feet, Han Jiwoo's feet and Luhai's feet. Then the child spoke, very small.

"Kept… looking back. Not at the side being taken. At the side that stays…"

When the child said nothing more and looked down, Han Jiwoo set her own hand beside the child's foot, not touching it. The child looked at the hand.

Ater asked quietly.

"What is the chance that child remembers Hazran?"

No one could answer right away.

The old machine came away from the pillar and took a step forward.

"Of the children who came here, none spoke of Hazran."

It said.

"Many did not speak at all."

The first took it up.

"The children who went up have never come down."

"And the ones before they go up?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"They are in the holding area."

The second spoke from the wall.

"That kid's there."

No one said anything more.

Kael got up from in front of the lines, then, without rubbing out the dot and the lines with his foot, crouched beside them and drew them anew, the dot in the middle and the lines around it.

"This time we build it around that kid."

He said it low.

"Even if we pull others out with them, the axis is that kid."

The first recovery hand asked.

"Why."

Kael answered briefly.

"That kid's already seen the outside once, and this time there's a good chance they'll know the outside is coming back to see them. Then the other side, us, the kids still in there, everyone's going to move differently."

"What if the other side knows first?"

The first said.

"They will clear that child away first."

"So we're fast. Next line."

Sern looked at the record panel.

"The signal is unchanged."

He said.

"It has neither increased nor decreased. This is the third day."

"And three days means?"

"I do not know. This is the first time."

Seorin looked at her own dot on the floor and rubbed at it with her foot, erasing only half and leaving the rest.

"With luck, one."

She said.

"This time it's not with luck."

The child who looks down was watching Han Jiwoo close her hand very slowly and open it again. She left her hand where it was and spoke low.

"Let's go back in."

No one said no, and the second came off the wall and took one step.

"I will go as well."

"You're the gap."

Kael said.

"You don't come inside. You catch them at the gap."

The second looked at its straightened leg and nodded.

Ater closed his eyes, opened them, and spoke very briefly.

"This time, we bring the child back."

Han Jiwoo looked at him, but Ater was looking at the wall, at the closed gap.

Sion picked up the board. He drew the small notch beside the third dot a little longer, but he didn't make the dot.

The old Hazran man turned the plate he had stood up and looked. One line on the blank side. He stood the plate back up.

As the yellow lamp flickered, the child who looks down looked at Han Jiwoo's hand on the floor. The child's foot moved half a hand's width toward it, but didn't touch.

Han Jiwoo didn't take her hand away.
