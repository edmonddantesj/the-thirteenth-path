# Episode 159. How Memory Recognizes

While they prepared for the third rescue,
the air inside the hidden layer drew tighter and tighter.

Everyone knew it now.
If they went back in,
this time they would be going in for that child.

The problem was this:
the sense that the child was more than "the one we couldn't bring out this time"
kept growing sharper.

Han Jiwoo couldn't explain it at first.

She only thought the child stayed in her eye, strangely.
The habit of looking to the side first, right to the end,
eyes that never missed who was slipping out,
the way the body turned and then swallowed the movement back.

But as a little time passed,
that familiarity came closer to memory than to a feeling.

Among the children she had seen in Hazran, there had been ones like that.

A kid who, however scared, looked at other people first, right to the end,
a kid who, just before running, checked the pace of the person beside them before their own feet,
a kid who, before crying, looked for the way to fall back.

It looked less like a habit Serakion had made
and more like the kind of habit that grows in children who have survived a long time in places already falling apart.

Han Jiwoo had been sitting against the wall,
and she spoke very low.

"That's Hazran."

Ater raised his head.
Kael's gaze moved too.

Han Jiwoo didn't go on right away.

The moment she said it aloud,
the feeling seemed likely to stop being a guess and become a memory that truly had to be recovered.

But in the end she said it.

"That kid
has a grain I saw in Hazran."

Ater was silent for a while.
Then he spoke, very low.

"I felt something similar."

What he recalled was not the face itself.
It was more the way of not looking up,
the gaze that looked to others first even while holding on,
and the movement that tried, very briefly, to follow and then swallowed itself in the end.

When Hazran was collapsing,
the children could never move on their own fear alone.
Who was running first,
who was staying,
who couldn't keep up—some children always ended up looking at that first.

That was a survival habit,
and the face of children who had learned too early to look after others first.

Kael heard them both out and still didn't jump to a conclusion.

He didn't go by feeling;
he was the kind who matched verifiable behavior against memory.

"We need to see more,"
he said, low.
"Looking alike isn't the same as being the same."

Han Jiwoo didn't argue right away.
She couldn't; he was right.

Similar habits could form inside Serakion too.
Fear can make similar faces,
and confinement can leave similar habits behind.

And yet,
neither of them could just let that child go by.

And that very day,
one of the three children brought out in the first rescue
let slip one more thing, in a voice almost too faint to hear.

"That kid…"

Han Jiwoo looked up at once.

The child's lips were dry,
and the voice was still small.
But this time it didn't stop at "one more," the way it had before.

"…was on the side that stays."

The air in the room went thin.

Han Jiwoo lowered herself further.

"What did you see?"

The child glanced around once with frightened eyes.
Then, very small, almost a breath, the child went on.

"Kept… looking back."
"Not the side getting taken,
the side that stays…"

It was less precise information
than a trace the child's senses were holding on to.

But that was exactly why it was more real.

This child had seen it too.
That the direction the small child in the line had been watching
was not simply up or ahead,
but always toward the side that stayed, toward the back.

Ater asked, low.

"What is the chance that child remembers Hazran?"

No one could answer right away.

That one possibility, after all,
could change the whole weight of the next rescue.

If that child really was holding on to a memory of Hazran,
this retrieval would not just be a rescue that brought back one more.

There was some part of the time behind them, a part they had walked past believing it already lost,
and this could turn into going back, belatedly, to take hold of it again.

Though no one had yet said it outright,
Han Jiwoo already sensed it, faintly.

When they saw that child again,
someone would end up unable to simply walk past.
Not just because the child was from Hazran,
but because the child might be tied to somewhere in the losses they had already come through.

Only after hearing that conversation
did Kael change the rescue design again.

Now the goal was no longer simple retrieval.

"This time we build it around that kid,"
he said, low.
"Even if we pull others out with them,
the axis is that kid."

The first recovery hand asked.

"Why."

Kael answered briefly.

"That kid's already seen the outside once,
and there's a good chance they know the outside is coming back for them this time."
"Then their side,
us,
the kids still in there,
everyone's going to move differently."

It sounded like a rescue calculation,
and also like a declaration that the center of feeling had shifted.

It was no longer about getting one child out;
it meant the whole memory around that child was beginning to move.

Han Jiwoo slowly closed her hand and opened it.
Then she said, low,

"Let's go back in."

This time it didn't sound like an impulse.

They were all looking at the same face by now,
thinking about the same possibility,
and had already accepted the same fear.

Ater closed his eyes, opened them, and said very briefly,

"This time, we bring the child back."

It was less an oath
than a verdict that it could not be put off any longer.

At the end of that night,
what had hardened last inside the hidden layer was not only the rescue plan.

It was memory.

Who that child was had not yet been fully confirmed.
But everyone's memory now
had already tilted toward being unable to pass that child by.

And that very tilt
was turning the next retrieval from a simple repetition
into truly going back in, one last time.
