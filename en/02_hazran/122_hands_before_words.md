# Episode 122. Reading Hands First

The holding area was wide.

The walls were silver, the floor was silver, and seats rose along the walls at even intervals. Sern counted sixteen of them, but Han Jiwoo didn't sit.

Of the machines acting as guides, three stood just inside the door and the other three stayed outside. The door was open, but no one tried going out through it.

The doorframe Ater examined had no handle, inside or outside. When he put his hand to the frame, there was no seam either.

"There is nothing to close it with."

He spoke to Sern.

"It is not open."

Sern answered.

"It has no need to be closed."

Kael went up to the door and set his foot on the threshold, and the three machines outside turned their faces at the same moment, so Kael drew his foot back.

"First time I've been somewhere open that I can't get out of."

He said it, but no one laughed.

The machines stayed polite all the while.

The questions, ahead of names or origins, went to the ship first. The way the hull was joined, the touch of its emergency reinforcements, the habits of how its parts were fitted together.

"The joint surfaces of your vessel show numerous non-standard manual corrections."

The lead machine spoke very softly.

"How interesting."

Han Jiwoo's back went stiff, and while she looked at the machine, light flowed in its face.

Han Jiwoo answered shortly.

"We put those on to stay alive."

The lead machine tilted its head very slightly.

"And the results remain in your hands, I see."

Sion looked straight at Han Jiwoo's hands, but she had already put them in her pockets. Seorin saw that too, and so did Luhai.

"Don't talk about my hands."

Han Jiwoo said.

The machine kept its head tilted.

"If you were displeased, we will record it."

"Don't record it."

"We will record that it is not to be recorded."

Han Jiwoo shut her mouth, and beside her, Luhai started to say something before Seorin caught his arm.

The machine went on asking.

"May we ask the source of the materials you used for the joins?"

"Picked them up."

"Picked them up — from where?"

"Places that fell down."

The light in the machine's face stopped.

One beat.

It flowed again.

"Places that fell down."

The machine repeated it.

"There are no places that have fallen down in this sector."

"Looks that way."

Han Jiwoo kept her eyes on the machine's face.

Watching the machines, Aka spoke very low.

"They like touching."

Sion looked at her.

Still watching them, Aka added.

"No. They believe touching is knowing."

Sern heard that and wrote it on the record panel, and Ater looked at the record panel.

"You record that as well?"

"Yes."

Sern answered.

"What they record, I record as well."

The Hazran people sat in the seats along the wall, and the seats fit their bodies. The old man ran a hand over his seat, but there was no dust, so he laid his hands on his knees. The child didn't sit, and stood between a grown-up's knees.

Luhai walked along the walls. One lap. Two laps. Each time he passed the door, he watched the machines outside turn their faces, and on the third lap, Seorin spoke.

"Sit."

"I can't."

"Then stand. Don't circle."

Luhai stopped and put his back to the wall, but the wall wasn't cold, so he pulled away and then leaned back again.

People passed beyond the open door.

This time they were closer than the ones before. Four young humans went by straightening one another's ornaments and collars. When one straightened another's collar, the one whose collar had been straightened did the next one's. Four in a line.

One of them looked in through the door and smiled.

"You're from outside, aren't you?"

He asked.

"Everyone feels a little lost at first, but soon you find the place that suits you."

Luhai's expression hardened.

"The place. And who decides that?"

The young human only smiled and gave no answer. While the one beside him straightened his collar, the four went past.

Han Jiwoo looked at the metal tags hanging at those humans' throats. All four were in the same spot, and the shapes differed a little, but the size was the same.

Without meaning to, she clenched her hand inside her pocket, and the pocket cloth pulled tight. Luhai saw it but didn't ask; he looked at Han Jiwoo once, then out the door.

Sern kept reading the structure and the paths of movement. He was counting the people who passed outside the door, and with each one he counted, he drew a line on his record panel.

"This is strange."

He spoke low.

Ater asked at once.

"What is?"

Sern answered without even turning his eyes.

"People's paths are too clean. There is almost no accidental overlap."

"What's overlap?"

Kael asked.

"It means meeting someone and stepping aside."

Sern answered.

"Thirty-two have passed so far. Not one has stepped aside."

When Kael looked out the door, two people were coming toward each other. Before they brushed, each moved half a step to the side. At the same moment. They didn't brush.

Seorin spoke very briefly.

"Here they set people out like scenery."

In the silver wall Han Jiwoo was looking at, they were reflected. Dirt-stained clothes, wrapped cloth, a tool pouch. Behind their reflections, the people outside the door passed by. Clean clothes, tags in the same spot. She watched both reflected in the one wall, then turned her eyes from it.

The lead machine came half a step toward Han Jiwoo.

"We would like to ask a few things about your methods of correction."

"No."

"If you were displeased…"

"Don't record it."

The machine stopped, light flowed once in its face, and it drew back half a step.

"We will defer."

As Han Jiwoo watched, the machine came no closer. Her hand loosened inside her pocket, then clenched again.

Aka spoke very low.

"Jiwoo."

Han Jiwoo turned around.

Aka had been watching the machines for a long while; then she spoke in a truly small voice.

"They've never fixed anything."

A short silence.

The machine's hand that Han Jiwoo looked at had five fingers, and the joints sat in the same places as a person's. There were no scratches and no grease, and not one worn spot.

She took her hands out of her pockets and looked at them. A scar on every knuckle, grease under the nails, a burn from a few days ago on the tip of her right index finger.

"So they haven't."

She said.

Aka said nothing more.

Luhai looked at Han Jiwoo's hands and then at his own, and Kael looked too. When the old Hazran man turned over the hands on his knees, there were calluses on the palms.

Sion didn't look at his own hands; he laid one over his pocket. The two lines of the metal plate were there.

The lead machine turned toward the open door.

"We will return as soon as the stability check is finished."

"What counts as finished?"

Seorin asked.

The machine didn't look back.

"It means confirming whether you entities fit this place."

With the door still open, three machines went out and joined the three outside to make six, and the six stood outside the door. Facing in.

Han Jiwoo didn't take a seat by the wall; she sat on the floor, unbuckled her tool pouch, and set it in front of her. She took out the screwdrivers one by one, laid them out on the floor, and wiped them. There was nothing on them to wipe, but she wiped them anyway.

The child came out from between the grown-up's knees and sat beside Han Jiwoo, looking at one of the screwdrivers without touching it.

When Han Jiwoo put it into the child's hand, the child held it.

"Like this."

Han Jiwoo moved the child's fingers one at a time into the grip. Thumb on top, the rest below.

"When you turn it, use your arm, not your wrist."

The child turned it in the air, but turned it with the wrist.

"Arm."

When the child turned it with the arm, Han Jiwoo nodded.

The old man, who had been watching, closed his hand and opened it.

"I used to know how to do that too. Back on Hazran."

Han Jiwoo looked at the man, then took out one more screwdriver and pushed it toward him. He didn't pick it up right away; he looked at it for a long while before he did, and his grip was right. Thumb on top, the rest below.

Han Jiwoo saw it and still didn't say anything. The man held the screwdriver up in the air and turned it with his arm.

The six machines outside the door were watching.
