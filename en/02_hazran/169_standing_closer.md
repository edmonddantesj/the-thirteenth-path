# Episode 169. Standing Closer

After that day, the space in front of the android sector was never left empty.

In the morning, one of the old machines; by day, Han Jiwoo; in the afternoon, Sion; at night, one of the outside hands. No one had arranged it, yet whenever one got up, another was already there.

The Hazran people still went around along the walls, and near the sector the children grew quieter. The child holding the screwdriver stood behind a pillar rather than behind an adult, and peered out. Aka stood beside the child but didn't stop them.

That day Han Jiwoo sat just inside the sector's entrance: five steps in instead of ten, two steps behind where the old machine and the wall-side machine had stood. When the little child followed her in, she didn't send the child back but sat them down beside her.

Without speaking to them or touching them, she simply sat and watched the column: the head at the very end, half a knuckle lower; the middle; the innermost. And the ones that had never moved once.

Luhai, standing at the sector's entrance, didn't come in; after standing there a long while, he asked.

"What's looking at them like that going to change?"

Han Jiwoo didn't answer for a while.

"I don't know either."

Her voice was very low.

"But the things I used to fix every day, I looked at like this at first, too."

Luhai's brows drew together as he asked.

"You mean you'll look at them the way you'd fix a machine?"

Han Jiwoo shook her head as she answered.

"No. I mean that before I fix anything, I see where it's still alive first."

"What does alive even mean? For a machine."

"Where it's running. Even a stopped machine has one place still running. You find that first. You open it up from there."

"And if nothing's running?"

"There's no machine where nothing runs. You just can't see it."

Luhai looked down at his own hands, then at the androids again. He couldn't see anything running. He took one step inside the entrance, stopped, and stayed standing there.

Then one of the androids moved.

Not a head, but a fingertip of the middle one. One long finger came half a knuckle away from its side, then went back against it.

Away.
Back.

It looked like a tremor, but it wasn't one. The intervals were the same, so Han Jiwoo counted them.

Three.
Three.
Three.

She didn't stiffen; she drew a slow breath. The little child looked at that finger and moved a finger of their own along with it.

"You saw that?"

Luhai asked, low.

"Yeah."

"Are we calling that a reflex too?"

Han Jiwoo didn't answer right away. When she looked at the outside hand by the entrance, it was watching the finger too. The optical eye of the old machine at the pillar had gone there as well. When she looked back at the android, the finger had stopped.

"I don't know. But I don't want to keep calling it a reflex first."

Luhai didn't ask anything more; he took one more step in and stood behind Han Jiwoo.

Ater, at the far wall of the sector with Sern, was watching the distance between the machines and the column. Yesterday the old machine at the pillar had been at the pillar; today it stood three steps out from it. The wall-side machine had been at the wall yesterday; today it was beside the entrance. The other two, which used to pass in front of the entrance, were not passing today; they stood.

"The old machines have come closer."

Ater spoke sooner than he had the day before.

Sern asked briefly.

"Has their wariness dropped?"

Ater shook his head, very slightly.

"No. Something other than wariness has grown larger."

"Like what?"

Luhai asked from inside the sector.

Ater was silent for a while, then looked at the wall-side machine beside the entrance. Its optical eye had gone to the column and did not come down.

"It is the wish to make sure."

He spoke low.

"Make sure of what?"

"I do not know. They likely do not know either."

Ater said.

"It is not the face of something confirming what it knows. It is the face of something confirming what it does not."

Sern didn't write it on the record panel; he only watched.

The wall-side machine took one step in from beside the entrance and stopped next to Luhai, behind Han Jiwoo, without raising a hand. Han Jiwoo didn't look back, but Luhai did. When the optical eye passed him and went on to the column, Luhai moved half a step aside for it.

"Is it all right to stand closer?"

Sion asked from outside the entrance.

The wall-side machine didn't answer; it took one more step in and stopped beside Han Jiwoo. Han Jiwoo raised her head, but the optical eye wasn't looking at her; it was watching the column. When she looked at the column again, the two of them were looking at the same place.

Sion was outside the entrance, on the Hazran people's side. The man without a younger brother stood next to him, only watching the entrance, not going in. The tired-faced woman, passing with a cloth in her hands, stopped, looked at the entrance once, and went on.

The woman who came with two children stood three steps out from the wall, one more than yesterday. The child behind her saw the child behind the pillar and tried to go too, but she held on and didn't let go, so the child looked out from there.

Then, from the gray route, came a short scrape of metal, once with every step. It was the second recovery hand on its way down from above, with Kael following behind. The second came in on its straight leg, bracing against the wall, and though its body had no breath, its shoulders rose and fell.

"The shift lines up top have changed."

It spoke low.

"The columns of people are being reorganized again."

Seorin asked at once, from beside the plate.

"Faster?"

"Yes. This time they are taking less care to hide it."

"Meaning what?"

"There is the sound of walls closing. There was not before. Now you can hear it. They close them so that it can be heard."

Kael caught his breath with his shoulder against the entrance post.

"There are more people in the overlap."

Kael said.

"The footprint shapes are in two rows. It used to be one."

"So the people are the wall."

Han Jiwoo said it from inside the sector, without turning around.

"The people are the wall."

Kael answered.

"Walls do not move."

The second said.

"People move. That is why they build the wall out of people. If they move, they block each other."

The first recovery hand looked at the second's straight leg but said nothing.

Seorin closed her eyes for a moment, then opened them.

"Then we decide too."

Her voice was low.

Everyone looked at her, and inside the sector Han Jiwoo turned only her head.

"We're not deciding yet whether to move with the androids. But we are deciding how the people who watch them are placed."

Luhai narrowed his eyes and asked.

"What's that supposed to mean?"

"It means we decide first where each of us stands so we don't fall apart."

As she spoke, Seorin pressed dots around the empty square with the stone, one at a time.

"Han Jiwoo keeps watching."

A dot.

"The old machines watch the reactions up close."

A dot.

"Sern watches whether it repeats."

A dot.

"Ater watches distance and arrangement."

A dot. She paused a moment and, the stone still in her hand, looked at Sion outside the entrance beside the Hazran people.

"You watch the people."

Sion raised his head.

"The Hazran people, the people in here, and the ones who'll break first from watching it."

Seorin said, low.

"From now on, watch that too."

Sion nodded without a word and looked at the man without a younger brother beside him. While the man watched the entrance, Sion stayed where he was, beside him.

Seorin pressed one more dot outside the square, on the entrance side.

"And me?"

Luhai asked.

"You, right there. Behind Han Jiwoo. Don't leave that spot empty."

Luhai looked at his own feet, inside the sector, behind Han Jiwoo, beside the wall-side machine, and didn't move them.

When the second went back toward the gap, bracing on the wall, the first stood beside it, and the two outside hands stood beside the entrance with their hands lowered.

The androids stood in a single column. The middle one's finger moved again.

Three.
Three.
Three.

The little child copied it with a finger of their own.

Three.
Three.

Han Jiwoo saw the child's hand moving along with it and didn't stop the child.

When the child behind the pillar came half a step out from it, Aka put a hand on the child's shoulder. Not pushing, not pulling; she let it rest there. The child stood there, ten steps from the entrance. Not peering out. Watching.

The yellow lamp flickered, but inside the sector nothing flickered. One finger moved once every three. The wall-side machine's optical eye had gone there, and Han Jiwoo's eyes were on it.

The child's finger moved in time with it.
