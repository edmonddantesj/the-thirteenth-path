# Episode 169. Standing Closer

After that day,
the section where the androids stood was no longer left completely empty.

Not everyone went near it, still.
The Hazran people still went around it,
and the children grew quieter near it.

But there was one thing different from the days before.

Now someone, without fail,
stayed near it a little longer.

One of the old machines,
Han Jiwoo,
Sion,
or one of the outside hands.

Their reasons varied,
and none of them were sure.
But it was no longer left completely empty.

Sion felt it was a small change, but a clear one.

Staying afraid, and still not leaving the place beside them entirely empty.
That was a harder choice than it seemed.

That day Han Jiwoo sat near the androids for a long while.

She wasn't deliberately talking to them,
or touching them.

She just stayed in the same space for a long time.

Luhai, standing some way off, finally asked.

"What's looking at them like that going to change?"

Han Jiwoo didn't answer for a while.

Then she spoke, very low.

"I don't know either."
"But the things I used to fix every day,
I looked at like this at first, too."

Luhai narrowed his brows.

"You mean you'll look at them the way you'd fix a machine?"

Han Jiwoo shook her head.

"No."
"Before fixing anything,
I mean to see where it's still alive first."

Those words stayed in Sion's ears a long time too.

Alive.

He didn't know yet whether that word could be used here like that.
But Han Jiwoo, at least,
was already not seeing what was inside them as simple breakdown or simple function.

Then one of the androids,
not its head this time but the tip of a finger, moved very faintly.

It was almost like a tremor.
But it had too much of a beat to be a tremor.

Han Jiwoo didn't stiffen.
She didn't hide her surprise either.
She only drew in a breath, very slowly.

"You saw that?"
Luhai asked, low.

"Yeah."

"Are we calling that a reflex too?"

Han Jiwoo didn't answer right away.

Instead she glanced once toward the outside hand standing at a distance,
then at the old machines,
then back at the androids.

"I don't know,"
she said.
"But I don't want to keep calling it a reflex first."

It was a small change,
but Sion felt those words crossing a far bigger line than it seemed.

Because it was no longer just "let's not write it off as nothing";
her mind had actually begun, little by little, to lean toward "there might be something there."

That day too, Ater was watching the distance between the old machines and the androids.

But this time he spoke sooner than the day before.

"The old machines have come closer."

Sern asked briefly.

"Has their wariness dropped?"

Ater shook his head, very slightly.

"It has not."
"Something other than wariness has grown larger."

"Like what?"
Luhai asked.

Ater was silent for a while.

Then he said, low,

"It is the wish to make sure."

The words made everyone strangely quiet.

Fear drives people away.
But some fear
makes you look again in the end.

Sion felt that the old machines were standing right inside that second kind of fear.

Before seeing whether those androids were enemies or not,
they seemed to want first to make sure whether the androids were truly, completely cut off from them.

Then the short scrape of metal sounded from the outer passage.

The second recovery hand came in and spoke low.

"The shift lines up top have changed."
"The lines of people are being reorganized again."

Seorin asked at once.

"Faster?"

"Yes,"
the second recovery hand answered.
"This time they are making less effort to hide it."

It was what everyone had been expecting,
yet hearing it said out loud made it far heavier.

Serakion now seemed,
while hiding the changes within,
to be preparing as well not to hide them if it had to.

In other words, that side was moving more and more toward believing it could not be wrong.

This side, by contrast,
was barely deciding its next step from the admission that it could be wrong.

A slow,
frustrating,
dangerous difference.

But strangely, Sion felt
that this was exactly why this side had not completely collapsed yet.

Seorin closed her eyes for a moment, then opened them.

"All right,"
she said, low.
"Then we decide too."

Everyone looked at her.

"We don't decide yet whether to move with the androids."
"But we do decide where the people watching them stand."

Luhai narrowed his eyes.

"What's that supposed to mean?"

"It means we decide first where each of us stands so we don't fall apart,"
Seorin said.
"Han Jiwoo keeps watching.
The old machines watch how the ones up close react.
Sern watches for repetition.
Ater watches distance and arrangement."

She paused for a moment.
Then she looked at Sion.

"You watch the people."

Sion quietly raised his head.

"The Hazran people,
the people in here,
and the ones who'll break first from watching it,"
Seorin said, low.
"From now on, watch that too."

Sion nodded without a word.

It was less an operation yet
than an arrangement to keep the operation from breaking.

But maybe that was exactly what was needed now.

Breaking Serakion would come, in the end, as a later explosion,
but deciding before that who would stand where and refuse to let go of what to the end
might already be the start of the fight.

And in the middle of that start,
the androids still stood quietly.

But now that quiet
no longer looked only like emptiness, as it had the day before.
