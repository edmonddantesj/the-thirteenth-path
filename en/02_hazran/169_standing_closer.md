# Episode 169. Standing Closer

After that day, the section where the androids stood was no longer left fully empty.

Everyone still did not go close. The Hazran people still detoured. The children grew quieter near it.

But one thing was different from the days before.

Now someone always remained nearby, a little longer.

An old machine. Jiwoo. Sion. Or an outside hand.

Each had different purposes, and none had certainty. But the space was no longer left completely vacant.

Sion felt that was a small but definite change.

Being afraid yet not fully emptying the space beside something. That was a harder choice than it seemed.

Jiwoo sat near the androids for a long time that day.

She was not deliberately speaking to them. She was not touching them.

She simply stayed in the same space for a long while.

Luhai stood at a distance and finally asked.

"Does watching like that change anything?"

Jiwoo did not answer for a while.

Then said it very low.

"I do not know either. But the things I used to fix — I looked at them like this at first too."

Luhai narrowed his brow.

"You mean you are going to look at them like fixing a machine?"

Jiwoo shook her head.

"No. Before fixing, I mean looking first to see what is still alive."

Those words stayed in Sion's ears for a long time.

Still alive.

Whether that word could be used here like this — she did not yet know. But at least Jiwoo was no longer looking at what was inside as simple malfunction or simple function.

Then one android moved not its head but a fingertip, very faintly.

It was almost like a tremor. But for a tremor, it had a beat.

Jiwoo did not stiffen. She did not suppress her startled reaction. She simply drew a very slow breath.

"Did you see?" Luhai asked low.

"Yes."

"Are we calling this one reflex too?"

Jiwoo did not answer right away.

Instead she looked once at the outside hands far away, then at the old machines, then back at the androids.

"I do not know." She said. "But I keep not wanting to call it reflex first."

It was a small change, but Sion felt that statement was crossing a far larger line than it seemed.

It was no longer simply "let us not push toward nothing." A tilt had begun, little by little, toward "there might be something."

Ater had been watching the distance between the old machines and the androids that day too.

But this time he spoke before the day before.

"The old machines have gotten closer."

Sern asked short.

"Has their guard lowered?"

Ater shook his head, very slightly.

"No. Something other than guard has gotten bigger."

"What." Luhai asked.

Ater was silent for a while.

Then said it low.

"The need to verify."

That word strangely made everyone quiet.

Fear drives people away. But certain kinds of fear make people look back in the end.

Sion felt the old machines were standing inside exactly the latter kind of fear right now.

Before determining whether the androids were enemies or not, they looked like they wanted first to verify whether these beings were truly, completely severed from them.

Then a short metallic scraping sounded from the outer corridor.

The second recovery hand came in and said it low.

"The upper shift line has changed. The human lines are being reorganized too."

Seorin asked immediately.

"Faster?"

"Yes." The second recovery hand answered. "This time they are not even bothering to hide it as much."

Everyone had expected that, yet hearing it out loud made it far heavier.

Serakion right now seemed to be simultaneously concealing internal changes and preparing to stop concealing when needed.

Meaning that side was increasingly moving in the direction of believing it was not wrong.

This side, by contrast, was barely deciding the next step from the position of admitting it might be wrong.

Slow. Frustrating. A dangerous gap.

But Sion strangely felt that was precisely why this side had not fully collapsed yet.

Seorin closed her eyes briefly, then opened them.

"Good." She said it low. "Then let us decide too."

Everyone looked at her.

"Whether we move with the androids or not is still undecided. But we decide the placement of people watching them."

Luhai narrowed his eyes.

"What does that mean."

"It means deciding where people stand so things do not collapse." Seorin said. "Jiwoo keeps watching. The old machines observe the nearby responses. Sern watches for repetition. Ater watches distance and arrangement."

She paused briefly. Then looked at Sion.

"You watch the people."

Sion quietly raised his head.

"The Hazran people. The people inside. And the people who might collapse before the rest while looking at those things." Seorin said it low. "From now on, watch that too."

Sion nodded without a word.

It was less a battle plan and more a placement to keep the battle plan from falling apart.

But perhaps that was exactly what was needed right now.

Breaking Serakion would eventually be an explosion later. But before that, deciding who would stand where and what they would not lose sight of to the very end — that might already be the beginning of the fight.

And at the center of that beginning, the androids still stood quietly.

But now, that quiet no longer looked only empty, the way it had the day before.
