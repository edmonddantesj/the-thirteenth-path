# Episode 193. Fight Differently

Just past the second gate, the party stopped.

Sion put his back to the wall and drew a long breath. While Sern set the record panel on his knees, Ater looked at the scrape on his ankle and Kael rolled his shoulder. When Han Jiwoo checked the last android's knot, it was unchanged, and the two old machines did not sit but stood looking farther down the corridor.

The corridor light wavered once, came back and fell into alignment, as if nothing had happened.

"How long?"

Seorin asked.

"Enough for one rest."

Sern said.

Han Jiwoo sat down beside the last android with her back to the wall, but he didn't sit.

"Sit."

He sat. Folding his knees took time, and his shoulder axis made a sound meanwhile, but she didn't count the sound. Sion looked at the two of them and laid his blade across his knees.

No one rested long.

---

The hidden level, behind the narrow maintenance line.

Two android wrecks lay side by side, the ones that had pushed in along the gray route a little earlier. The first recovery hand had broken one; the second had pinned the other against the wall with its straightened leg, and Luhai's bent iron bar had gone into that one's knee. The two that had gone down that way didn't move now.

The angle of the arms, the line of the neck, the human shape was all still there. One wreck's hand was stretched toward the wall, a hand that had reached that way as it fell.
The side where the children had been.

A little apart lay something else.

It was neither an android nor human-shaped; it was harder, and more closed. Where its arms should have been it had not arms but a pushing plate and a closing plate, and its lower half had been cut away. The old machines had dragged it in from the disposal yard at the edge of the hidden level, the kind of place Serakion kept apart on every level.
Something thrown away before it was finished.

The child with the screwdriver, the little child and the child who looks down kept their distance. They had been told not to come close, but they didn't turn away.

One of the children, the one with the screwdriver, asked very quietly.

"Were they… hurting too?"

Luhai opened his mouth and closed it. "You'll cut your hand" would have ended it, but he didn't say that. He looked at the wrecks, at the one whose knee he had just broken and the hand stretched toward the wall, and let out a low breath.

"Don't go near them."

His voice wasn't rough. The child nodded and still looked at the android's face line, but there was nothing on the smooth surface. The child looked down at their own hand, which had no soil on it, and rubbed it on their trousers.

Aka stood a little apart without saying anything. The severed line of the neck, the torn shoulder, the fingers half buried in dust: she kept her eyes on them a long while, and didn't try to look away.

Luhai came up beside her.

"What's wrong?"

It was a long while before Aka took her eyes away.

"I don't know."

Luhai bent his knees and looked into the cut face of the Serakion robot; unlike an android's, its lines went deep.

One layer.
Two layers.
Three layers.

Each layer had a cover, and when he put a finger in, it stopped at the second knuckle.

Behind him the two recovery hands gave off a low static. The first sat beside the android wrecks, the second beside the robot's cut face with its straightened leg stretched out. The wall-side machine stood behind them, its optical eyes resting on the face surface of one of the android wrecks.

The first spoke, briefly.

"The androids, we can shake. Briefly."

The second pointed at the cut face.

"This is different. Deeper. It does not break the same way."

Still looking down at the cut face, Luhai asked.

"How far can you take it?"

The first answered.

"Androids, the beat. One instant."

The second spoke with a hand on the cut face.

"That one does not stop. Delay. Error. Misreading the path."

"That's all?"

"That is how a reject is."

The second said.

"A finished one goes deeper."

Luhai looked from the android wrecks to the cut face and back. They had come in by the same route on the same day, but they hadn't broken in the same place.

The wall-side machine spoke for the first time.

"Androids waver by nature."

Luhai looked around.

"That one was made from the start not to waver. That one is Serakion's. As for androids."

While the words paused, its optical eyes were on the wreck's face surface.

"What about androids?"

"We do not know."

The wall-side machine said.

"We know they are not Serakion's."

Aka heard that and looked at the wreck again.

"How do you know?"

"We do not know."

The wall-side machine answered.

"When we were made, that shape already existed. That is all we know."

Aka looked at the wreck's hand stretched toward the wall, then at her own hand.

At the end of the communication line in back, a signal from inside wavered once, and the first took it; it was a short signal.

"Before the next gate. Alignment resumed. Response to known variable strengthened."

"What's the known variable?"

Luhai asked.

"Him."

The first said.

"Their side knows that android. If he is seen, they will bind him first."

Luhai spoke low.

"Same trick's done, then."

The first answered at once.

"Done."

The second went on.

"This time our machines shake them. Two android units. Two Serakion machine units. Not a stop. A misalignment."

The first and the second gave off a low interference tone at the same moment, and the sensor face of an android wreck trembled, faintly, once. The robot's cut face was dull, but somewhere inside its layers something meshed wrong, once, small. It sounded like a door long locked going wrong once from the inside.

The difference was clear: Luhai heard it, and the children heard it too. The child with the screwdriver came a step closer, then stopped when Luhai raised a hand.

The first looked at Luhai.

"Sending it. Inside."

Luhai checked himself.

"Everything inside gets read. Send a signal and they'll catch it first."

"We will not send it by that route."

The second said.

The first and the second looked at each other and gave off static: low, not long, a sound Luhai had never heard. The wall-side machine turned its optical eyes toward the sound and joined in.
Three.

"What's that?"

Luhai asked.

"What we used before Serakion."

The first said.

"Their side cannot hear this. It is from before they were made."

The static was short.
Androids: beat can be shaken. Briefly.
Serakion robots: cannot be stopped. Delay, error, path misreading only.
Based on rejects. Finished units may be harder.
That was all.

"That's all?"

"That is all."

The second said.

"It was read from a reject. The real thing can only be known by going to see it."

"Better than nothing?"

"It is better."

When the static cut off, the first kept it short.

"Reached."

Luhai nodded and looked once more at the wrecks, at the hand stretched toward the wall. He didn't push that hand aside with his foot.

"Them. The ones inside."

"They went."

The first said.

"Up to the third gate. All of them together."

"His shoulder?"

"That did not come through."

Luhai looked down at the bent iron bar.

"Let's clear away what needs clearing."

He said it low.

"But don't just dump them."

No one argued. While the first took one android wreck by the shoulder, the second braced a hand on the wall and rose on its straightened leg. Luhai took the other by the legs and lifted.
It was heavy.
The wall-side machine went over to the robot's cut face, but only looked at it and didn't lift it.

While the children stood where they were, the child with the screwdriver looked at an empty hand with no soil on it. The child touched a finger to the dust in a crack in the wall; the dust stuck, and the child closed that hand. Luhai saw it but said nothing.

Only then, very slowly, did Aka take her eyes away. Where the wrecks had been cleared, a human shape was left in the dust, arms and neck and even the hand stretched toward the wall, and she went around the marks without stepping on them.

The old Hazran man was holding his plate, one line scratched on its back. He looked at the android wrecks and at the plate, but didn't scratch a line.

The yellow lamp flickered, and the hidden level's breathing was held down again. The first stood at the mouth of the gray route, the second beside it with a hand on the wall, the wall-side machine behind. Luhai shifted his grip on the bent iron bar, turned toward the children and counted.

Three.
Four, with the small child.

He didn't count the four again.

The child who looks down came up beside him and looked at Luhai's hand on the bent iron bar.

"It's bent."

The child's eyes stayed on the floor.

"Yeah, it's bent. I still use it."

The child looked at the iron bar, said nothing more, and stayed standing beside him.
