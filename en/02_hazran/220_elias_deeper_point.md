# Episode 220. Elia's Deeper Point

The inner wall Elia had pointed to looked at first like simply a wall.

Excessively smooth. Not a single unnecessary gap. A surface cleaned so that neither light nor shadow remained.

But looking long enough, the fact that there was too much nothing was what revealed it was not an ordinary wall.

The inside of the Empire had always been this way.

What it wanted to hide was never concealed in black. Instead, it was cleaned so smooth that it looked as though nothing had ever been there from the start.

Ater approached the wall.

Before raising his hand, he first traced the lines with his gaze.

Elia said it low.

"Different from my side. That one is not about holding for a long time. It is closer to making you forget anything remains at all."

Sion felt a chill down his spine at those words.

Neither killing nor releasing, holding for a long time—that alone had been Imperial enough. But a deeper inside that turned existence itself into background.

Aka watched the wall and frowned slightly.

"I hate it. The breathing here is thin."

Sern's voice brushed low from behind.

"We cannot go much longer."

He still stood like someone watching both the outer door and this side simultaneously. Beyond the door, Seorin and Kael wore faces that knew time could not be stretched further.

Elia said it very brief.

"Quickly."

Ater asked nothing more.

He placed his hand near the wall. But this time the structure did not respond right away like before.

Instead, the time where nothing happened stretched slightly longer.

Those few seconds felt strangely long to Sion.

Beneath Ater's fingertips, one very faint line passed. No light showed; only the air along that line changed slightly.

Ater's gaze sank low.

"This is not a door."

Sern asked immediately.

"Then?"

"A preservation boundary. Less about opening and closing—more about maintaining different states on each side."

Elia received almost right away.

"That is why it will not open more. But once it misaligns, the inside can be shaken too."

Ater was quiet for a while.

He looked like someone reading far more of the invisible structure than the visible.

Sion glanced at Elia. The color had worsened, but the eyes seemed slightly more vivid.

Elia wore the face of someone who knew: from here, this was not about extracting herself, but truly about extracting something else.

Ater said it low.

"Sern. This is not an Imperial authorization line. It is closer to a preservation balance."

Sern nodded very short.

"Then the inside must not be shaken first. The surface has to be misaligned."

Ater looked at Sern for the first time—very faintly.

The two continued in shortened breaths, like people who had seen the same picture almost simultaneously.

"Three."
"Three."

"Left side first."
"No, the center is late."

Ater's gaze returned to the wall. And he said it very low.

"I see."

Then Aka suddenly swallowed her breath.

"The person inside… is awake."

Elia's gaze wavered faintly.

"Always is."

Sion asked before he could stop himself.

"Who?"

Elia did not answer right away.

Instead, looking at the deeper wall, said it very low.

"No time to bring up names right now."

From outside, a clearer signal this time.

Seorin.

"Thirty."

Just a number.

Meaning that much time remained.

This time, the sound of Luhai swallowing came from just beyond the door.

The room for jokes had been gone for a long while.

Ater's fingertips touched three points on the wall almost simultaneously.

The first, too briefly. The second, very faintly longer. The third, released the instant it touched.

From inside the smooth wall, one very low resonance returned.

Nothing like the sound of a door opening.

More like the sound of breath that had been locked for too long being forced to change direction.

Part of the wall misaligned—just enough for one hand to enter.

What seeped through the gap first was not light.

Cold air.

Aka said it almost in a grimace.

"I hate this."

Elia shallowed her breathing too.

"Yes. That is the inside."

Sion looked at the gap.

Too narrow. It did not look like a structure built for extracting a person at all.

Ater said it low.

"The inside has to help."

Elia raised her head immediately.

"I will."

Sion attached right away.

"In your condition—"

"I can."

Elia, supported by Sion and Aka, approached the wall very slowly.

Steps were slow. The body was clearly in pain.

But she never fully surrendered her center. Not a person collapsing, but a person dividing her remaining strength precisely to keep from collapsing.

Standing near the wall, Elia closed her eyes briefly, opened them, and very carefully slipped her fingertips into the gap.

In that instant, a faint response came from inside.

Sion did not see it, but he saw Elia's expression change—just slightly.

A face of recognition.

The face of someone confirming, for the first time by touch, a being that had endured nameless for too long.

Elia said it almost like breathing.

"I can hear."

Aka attached right away.

"Hear what."

"Still not gone dark."

The moment those words ended, from inside, the sense of something moving very slowly toward the gap.

Sion held his breath.

It felt like one person rising toward the surface from water that had pressed them down for too long—arriving too late.

And finally, from the darkness inside the gap, one hand appeared first.

Thin. Pale. A hand that had not seen light for a long time.

But the fingertips were strangely neat.

Elia took hold of that wrist.

In that instant, Elia's face contorted—very briefly.

Whether from pain, or from the shock of actually touching something endured for too long—Sion could not tell.

But she did not let go.

"Sion."

A low voice.

"Together."

At that single word, Sion asked nothing more.

He pressed close to the wall and gripped the arm coming from inside, together.

It was not light.

More than weight—it felt strangely heavy, as though the time of long captivity was loaded directly onto it.

Aka attached from the opposite side immediately.

The moment her hand touched, Aka knew right away.

The quiet other person who had been close all along yet remained nameless to the end—this was them.

When all three pulled together, the person inside was drawn toward the gap, little by little.

The face was not yet visible.

But the fact that this person truly existed was already changing the air inside the corridor.

From outside, Sern's voice sharpened for the first time.

"It is closing now."

Ater immediately re-caught the wall's balance. Outside the door, Seorin and Kael guarded the opposite corridor simultaneously. Jiwoo twisted her body to be ready to break any closing segment. Luhai, mouth shut now, held his position. Serakion, wordless as always, had stepped one pace forward.

And Sion understood then.

What they were pulling out right now was not simply one more prisoner.

It was one more question the Empire had been burying for far too long.

The gap would not widen further.

But the person inside was crossing over—little by little, very little by little.

Elia did not clench her jaw.

She simply did not let go, to the very end.

And murmured, almost inaudibly.

"This time… it must not be cut short."

Sion did not fully understand the meaning.

But for some reason, it sounded like the most important words of this entire rescue.

And the next moment, the upper body of the person inside finally began crossing over the wall.
