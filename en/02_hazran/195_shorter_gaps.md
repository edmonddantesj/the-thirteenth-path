# Episode 195. Shorter Gaps

After they crossed the third gate, the corridor changed again.

It was narrower, so two people standing side by side touched shoulders; when Sion's and Sern's shoulders touched, Sern stepped back half a step. The corridor was smoother, and quieter.

Sion was about to take a step when a line of light ahead, one no one had touched yet, folded, then straightened. The corridor was still again, but it was the stillness of something that had already finished its next response. The walls had no flaw, and the light inside the ceiling was lower than before, and more even.

Sion watched that light and did not set his foot down; he drew the raised foot back.

"It moved first."

He said.

"Before us."

Seorin's face said she had seen the same thing.

"It's shorter from here on."

Seorin said.

Watching the lines running along the base of the wall, Sern spoke low.

"It has already reduced the error from a moment ago. On this level, even if the same disturbance gets in again, it will hold for a shorter time."

"How much."

"Half a beat. Before, it was one beat."

Kael rubbed the dust from the corner of his mouth with the back of his hand.

"Great. Now we get to breathe even less."

No one laughed, not even Kael.

Han Jiwoo turned to the two machines behind them and asked.

"Will it work this time too?"

One of the machines answered briefly.

"It works. Short."

The other added on.

"Shorter. It closes the gap fast."

"How fast."

"Before we put out the second."

Sern took that up.

"Then the only way is to cross before the second correction comes in."

Seorin nodded at once.

"Don't make a path this time. Twist it only up to just before a path forms. Don't wait for the moment it opens; push it toward where it has no choice but to open."

"What's the difference."

Sion asked.

"If you go after you see it open, you're late. Your body has to be there before it opens. When it opens, you're already inside."

Her chin pointed briefly toward the end of the corridor.

At the end of the corridor, where Sion looked, there was no door and no wall. The corridor narrowed and the ceiling came down, so that it went on like a throat; when he looked down at his own shoulders, there was a span to the wall on either side.

Sern spoke first.

"The corridor itself is the alignment axis. The farther in we go, the more the guide lines and blocking lines overlap and move section by section."

"There's no door?"

"The corridor is the door."

Sern said.

"It does not block in one place. It keeps blocking the whole way we walk. Briefly. Many times."

Kael ran the tip of his foot along the outer side of the corridor; there were many grooves there, one per step.

"Once one closes, the next square picks it up right away, huh."

"Yeah."

Seorin's answer was short.

"So this time, shaking just one square won't be enough."

The low ceiling Ater looked up at had no lines.

"Nothing will come from above."

He said.

"Everything comes from the sides. There is nowhere for me to watch."

"Then watch the back."

Seorin said.

"What closes behind us."

Ater went to the back and stood beside the two old machines.

At the back, the two machines traded low static; to Han Jiwoo's ears it was a sound cut shorter than before, repeating at the same length.

One of the old machines spoke low.

"Android first. Robot right behind. Path does not stay long."

Seorin raised her head.

"Good. If we shake the androids' beat, the robots will move right away to fill that gap. We tangle that up together with the second shake."

Sern went on.

"Then what opens is not the center line, but the junction between sections."

"Where's the junction?"

"Between one section and the next. Just before the next alignment axis connects."

Where Sern pointed on the corridor floor, the guide line broke and picked up again, one finger wide.

Sion asked briefly.

"That gap—does it open wide enough for one person?"

Kael answered first.

"Enough for one. The question is whether we get everyone behind through too."

Han Jiwoo caught her breath. Nine.
She looked back at the last android, the two old machines and Ater, then looked ahead.

Who first.
Who last.
Who would hold the closing line.

She did not say it out loud, and Seorin did not hold on to that question for long.

"We all go this time too. But we have to be faster."

The light on both sides of the corridor split thinly.

Two androids appeared first, and two Serakion robots followed, nearly overlapping them. The spacing was so short that between the back of the unit in front and the shoulder plate of the unit behind there was less than a span.

Sern spoke at once.

"The formation has been revised. They have reduced the spacing."

"I saw."

Seorin said briefly.

"So they'll tangle faster."

"Why."

Sion asked.

"When they're close, they read each other first. Far apart, each reads on its own. Close, they read the one beside them, then read."

The two machines' first static skimmed the floor.

One android's step wavered by half a beat, and the robot a span behind it moved to fill that line; the two read each other first. The space between sections emptied, and one finger's width became a shoulder's width.

Sion went in. He pushed his body through before he saw it open, and the wall edges on both sides pressed his back and chest until the breath went out of him.
He slipped through.
The junction closed behind his back; it took less than a beat. The far side was the same corridor, same width, same light.

The junction was still there, in front of the eight left.

"One opening is one person."

Sern said.

Seorin stood her blade upright.

"Then one at a time."

The static went out again. An android wavered, and a robot filled in.
Seorin twisted her shoulders and went into the gap.
It closed.

Static again.
Sern. The record panel scraped the wall.
It closed.

Again.
Ater.
It closed.

From the far side, Sion grabbed each one coming through by the arm and pulled.

The opening was getting narrower. The two machines took turns sending, one resting while the other sent, and Han Jiwoo, standing beside them, felt the heat rising off the resting one on the back of her hand.

One old machine's shoulder caught as it stepped in, and it twisted its body and went through.

Four were left: Kael, the other old machine, the last android, and Han Jiwoo.

Then the junction moved before the static and tried to close before it was even shaken. It was the second correction, coming in before the second could be sent, just as the machine had said.

Kael hooked his foot into the auxiliary groove on the outer side, up to the ankle, and the closing auxiliary blocking line lost its angle and was pushed outward.
Half a span.

Han Jiwoo put her shoulder to the edge of the inner light line. Her shoulder was pressed down, and the light stopped on it. She clenched her teeth.

"Go."

The remaining old machine twisted twice and went in.
The last android went in, and his shoulder on the side with the knot hit the wall.
Han Jiwoo took one bracing hand away and pushed that shoulder, and the light came down half a knuckle.
He was through.

The instant Kael's foot came out of the groove, the light grazed his collar and the end of his arm.
He was through.

Han Jiwoo was last. When she pulled her shoulder free, the light line came down where it had been, all the way to the floor.

A little later and it would have been cut. Han Jiwoo looked down at her shoulder; the cloth had been pressed into a mark. She smoothed the mark with her hand.
It did not smooth out.

One of the old machines braced a hand on the wall and started to send static once more, then stopped; nothing came.

"How many times."

Seorin asked.

"We opened it five times."

Sern answered.

"The sixth, they closed first."

Seorin nodded and lowered her blade.

Farther down the corridor, where Sion looked, there was no end, only walls narrowing and a ceiling lowering. He shifted his grip on the blade so the chipped edge turned inward. When he looked at his palm, it wasn't sweat but a little blood from where the wall's edge had scraped it, and he wiped that hand on his trousers.

Han Jiwoo checked the last android's knot; it had not come loose. Beside the knot was a scrape from the wall, and she touched the scrape, then took her hand away.

"Go."

She said.

Seorin moved off first, and the nine followed her in one line down the narrow corridor. Whoever's shoulders touched the walls turned sideways; the two old machines turned twice, and the last android turned once, toward his lowered shoulder.

The gaps were getting shorter, and this time Sern did not write on the record panel. There was no gap left to write in, so he counted as he walked.
