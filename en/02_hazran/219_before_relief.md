# Episode 219. Before Relief

Elia did not speak right away.

Sion found that unsurprising.

In this place, bringing out gladness first would not have been like Elia.

Instead, Elia was reading—Sion, Aka, and Ater who had just entered—one by one, very briefly.

Who came first. How hurt each was. Who was in a state to speak. And when this space would close again.

That gaze paused last on Ater—very briefly.

So short Sion thought he might have imagined it.

But there was clearly a micro-pause that was closer to recognition.

Elia spoke first.

The voice was low. Dry in places. But not broken.

"How many."

Sion understood a beat late.

"What?"

Elia said it again in the same tone.

"People who entered. How many."

Sion answered immediately.

"Inside, me, Aka, Ater. Outside, Sern, Seorin, Kael, Jiwoo, Luhai, Serakion."

Elia gave a very small nod.

That single brief nod felt strangely more like calculation than relief.

"Good."

Then asked immediately.

"Did you see the closing cycle?"

Sion was momentarily speechless.

Very Elia, he thought. But it also hurt, a little.

The person they had searched for so long was right here, and the first thing asked was not about personal safety or the outside situation—but this structure's closing cycle.

Yet that also felt like proof Elia had not fully broken.

Ater answered instead.

"Two. The door we crossed closes on approval variance. This side watches the inner state more."

Elia's eyes rested on Ater a little more clearly this time.

"Valkar."

Ater answered very low.

"Yes."

From that short exchange alone, Sion could tell the two did not see each other as complete strangers.

But no one pressed further right now.

Elia spoke again.

"Then we cannot stay long."

Aka stepped closer.

"Does it hurt a lot?"

Elia's gaze shifted faintly at the question.

The way Elia looked at Aka was different from looking at the others.

Closer to the gaze of recognizing, without explanation, someone who holds an inexplicable sense.

"It does hurt."

Aka bit her lip slightly.

"But you have not collapsed."

The corner of Elia's mouth loosened again—just barely.

"That is what I have been here for."

Sion felt his breath catch for a moment.

Too much like Elia. And inside that single line, too much time of enduring all the way here.

Near the outer door, a very small metallic sound rang once.

It was Sern.

From where he still held the door, he seemed to be signalling that time was short.

Elia heard the sound and immediately shifted.

"I can walk. But not fast."

Sion asked right away.

"If we support you?"

"Possible. But if I stop once, I get recaptured."

Ater stepped closer.

"Is there a side that can directly lock this place?"

Elia closed and opened the eyes briefly.

"Yes. But that is from outside. The inside maintains for a long time—it does not release."

Ater and Sion looked toward the door simultaneously.

Why Sern was still holding became clearer.

Elia steadied a breath once.

Even inside that short breath, pain visibly followed.

But the words did not stop.

"When you entered—who opened?"

Ater answered.

"I did."

Elia looked directly at him this time.

Sion felt that gaze was far more vivid than before.

"Right. So that is how you got in."

Ater wore a face that understood the meaning, to some degree.

But his expression was not good either.

Elia saw that and added very low.

"Feels filthy, right."

Sion blinked.

Almost the same grain as what Ater had said outside.

Ater exhaled very short.

"Yes."

Elia did not say more there.

But from that brief sympathy alone, it was visible that both knew too well how the Empire's inner order stays inside a person.

From the outer corridor, a thinner and shorter signal brushed past.

Could have been Seorin's side. Could have meant Sern could not hold much longer.

Elia heard it and did not delay further.

"There is one more person."

Sion raised his eyes immediately.

"What?"

Elia cast a very brief glance toward the far inner side—opposite from the door.

"Cannot be left behind."

No explanation.

Did not say who. Did not say why it mattered.

But strangely, Sion could not ask back.

Because the fact that Elia was saying this right now already felt like sufficient reason.

Aka looked that way too. Her expression shifted, very faintly.

"Right."

Sion looked at Aka.

Aka nodded.

"Someone is there. Close."

Ater's gaze sank too.

His face showed he now had a rough idea of who might be there.

But no one said the name.

Elia spoke again.

"Taking only me is not enough. Leaving that person here—too much of the reason for coming gets cut."

Sion understood then.

Elia was not making a request right now.

It was closer to a judgment.

This person must not be left behind. Otherwise, this rescue becomes only half a success.

From outside, Sern's voice brushed past very low this time.

"Now."

Sion asked nothing more.

He just nodded.

"Understood."

At that single word, Elia's gaze wavered very briefly.

Not quite relief.

Closer to confirming that one line held to the very end had not been severed.

Ater asked immediately.

"Where."

Elia moved the head very faintly, indicating the far end of the inner wall.

At a glance, it looked like just a smooth wall.

But now, looking closely, the air's grain in that section was different.

Pressed deeper. Sealed longer. A structure that seemed to want to make even one person's breath look like it had never existed.

Aka murmured very small.

"I hate this…"

Elia looked toward the wall and said it low.

"Longer than me."

Sion's hand tightened unconsciously.

Longer than Elia.

He did not know precisely how long that meant.

But he could sense that the length was not merely suffering—it meant how long the Empire had been burying a certain question.

Ater moved first.

Not toward the door. Toward the deeper inside that Elia had indicated.

This time he did not hesitate.

Sion went straight to Elia's side. Aka was already at the opposite.

Elia, as the two drew close, said it very low.

"Just a little further. But be careful. That side is… more Empire."

Sion heard that and felt his breathing grow strangely shallow.

He had thought they were already deep enough inside the Empire. But Elia was saying there was a deeper inside still.

Then, from very far away, one truly faint presence brushed past again.

Aka looked that direction immediately.

This time Elia looked the same way.

No one said a name. No one explained.

But from that single brief pause alone, Sion knew.

Someone was truly there, deeper in.

And Elia had no intention of leaving them behind.

From outside, one more low metallic sound rang—as though the door could no longer hold much longer.

Time was nearly out.

But strangely, from this moment on, this rescue was no longer about Elia alone.

Sion said nothing more.

He just nodded at Elia—very short.

Elia, in return, closed and opened the eyes—truly faintly.

As though that was enough.

And Sion's group began moving again—toward the deeper inside that Elia had pointed to, toward one more person still without a name.
