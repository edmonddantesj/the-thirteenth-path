# Episode 218. Imperial Silence

After crossing the second door, even the corridor seemed to be saving its sounds further.

The floor grew smoother. The walls paler. The lighting obsessed more with uniformity than brightness.

Not a space designed to keep anyone from being startled upon arriving—but a space designed so that no one would arrive here at all.

And yet, Sion's group was clearly inside.

Aka stopped first.

"Here."

A very small voice.

But everyone stopped immediately.

Sern raised his hand and sorted the spacing from the rear first. Kael looked at the turning point on the opposite side of the corridor. Seorin wore the face of someone calculating who would look where first when the door opened.

Ater was studying both the wall and the door frame ahead.

This door was different from the one before.

Less the kind that reads approval sequence, and more like a door that resented the very possibility of the inside being disturbed.

Only upon reaching here did the words "Imperial detention structure" begin to take real shape.

Neither killing nor releasing. Holding in a needed state for a long time.

What guarded the inside was not grand violence but an excessively ordered quiet.

Ater said it very low.

"This one must not be forced."

Sern asked immediately.

"Why is that?"

"The closing method will change. The entire inside could lock at once."

Sion did not precisely understand, but he could immediately tell one thing.

If they touched it wrong, before they even reached Elia, this entire zone could seal itself deeper.

Aka watched the door with a furrowed brow.

"Too quiet."

Luhai asked by lip movement alone.

"Is that bad?"

Aka neither nodded nor shook her head.

"It is quiet, but… not just quiet. It feels like it is constantly held still."

Sern's expression hardened.

"Correct. This is the side that keeps the inside in the same state for a long time."

Sion looked at the door.

The door did not move. But strangely, it felt less like a door and more like a lid.

Not blocking something—but covering something so it could not change.

Sern moved forward very slowly.

"This one, I open."

Ater did not argue right away.

Instead he scanned beside and above the door once more, then said it low.

"The method?"

"The inner grain has to be matched first. Not forcing it open—making it look like it was supposed to open."

Ater nodded short at that.

This time the two did not clash.

Here, what mattered was not who knew better but who could open it with fewer mistakes. Both wore faces that understood that.

Sern approached the door and did not place his hand right away.

Instead, from a very close distance, he steadied his breath once and simply looked at the undecorated surface beside the door.

Sion could not understand what he was doing.

A moment later, beneath the surface where truly nothing had been, one shadow passed—so faint it was almost invisible.

A very small grain. Less light and closer to the trace where light had passed.

Sern said it low.

"Did you see it?"

Ater answered very small.

"Yes."

"It will not come twice."

Sern's fingertip rose.

The instant it touched, the door did not open.

Instead, from somewhere inside, one very distant and small resonance returned.

Like dropping a stone into deep water and the bottom answering only much later.

Sern did not remove his hand.

"Now."

The base of the door parted—by just the degree that a breath might open.

Kael reacted immediately.

"Too narrow."

"This is how much it gives." Sern said. "Widen it and the inside knows first."

Sion could not immediately understand how a person was supposed to fit through that gap.

But Sern was already deciding the order.

"Aka first. Then Sion. Then you, my lord."

Sion raised his head at the naming.

Ater also looked at Sern for an instant.

Sern added low.

"The person who reads the inside needs to already be in."

Ater did not argue this time.

Aka was already lowering her body.

Approaching the small gap, she was far quieter than usual.

Sion asked very low.

"All right?"

Aka answered with her eyes on the door.

"I hate it. But it is close."

Sern applied just a fraction more pressure to his fingertip. The gap widened by half a palm.

"Now."

Aka twisted her body and slipped inside first.

The gap was narrow, yet strangely she vanished with almost no resistance.

Sion's turn came right after.

The moment he crossed the threshold, the temperature changed.

Cold was not enough to describe it.

Not merely the absence of warmth—air designed from the start so that human body heat could not linger long.

Sion instinctively reduced his breathing as he entered.

The air itself seemed to dislike deep breaths.

Aka was standing just ahead.

She had stopped a few steps from the door, unable to move, looking only forward.

Sion followed her gaze.

At the corridor's end. Between very smooth walls. A structure that looked less like a room and more like a regulated preservation section.

And inside it, a person.

Elia.

Recognizable immediately, even from a distance.

The body was thinner than before. The posture was rigid like someone who had endured the same angle for too long. The complexion was pale.

But strangely, what showed first was not frailty.

What showed first was that the collapse had not yet happened.

Elia's head was raised, just slightly.

Whether by chance or because something had already been felt—Sion could not tell.

But the eyes were open.

Eyes not yet fully dimmed.

Sion swallowed his breath.

Aka said it almost inaudibly.

"It is her."

In that moment, a very faint sound came from behind.

Ater entering.

And soon after, truly briefly, Elia's gaze shifted.

Whether it grazed Sion in the distance, or Ater who had just entered, or read both at once—it was impossible to tell precisely.

But the change was clearly there.

So small that someone who did not know would have missed it.

Sion could tell from that single faint movement alone: Elia was not simply being held. Elia was the side that read the surroundings and endured, to the very end.

Outside, Sern was still holding the door.

Kael, Seorin, Jiwoo, Luhai, and Serakion would be standing in silence, waiting for their turn.

But in this moment, Sion felt all those presences recede at once.

Elia was here.

Truly.

Someone they had searched for so long. Someone they had feared might be too late to find. That person was still remaining in this cold, excessively ordered inside.

And then, the corner of Elia's mouth moved—just barely. It was close to a smile.

The moment Sion saw it, he felt his heart drop once, heavily.

Elia had not yet spoken.

But it was already the face of someone who knew.

You came.

The words had not been said aloud. Yet Sion, strangely, read them exactly that way.
