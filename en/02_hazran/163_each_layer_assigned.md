# Episode 163. Each Layer Assigned

The plate lay where it had been, its five stones, its four squares, even the line around its edge just as they were.

Seorin picked up a stone again and drew a new line across the four squares. Layers.

"Good. Now let's assign roles not by 'who's good at what,' but by 'who can break which layer.'"

The second recovery hand pushed off the wall, took a step out and stood beside the plate, and the first stood next to it. As the old machine came a step out from the pillar, the optical eyes of the wall-side machine lit the plate with a weak light.

This time Ater came over from the exit and stood in front of the plate without sitting, and Sern set the record panel beside the plate.

Han Jiwoo was sitting with the little child. The child saw the stones on the plate and reached out, then drew the hand back when Han Jiwoo caught it.

The first to lay out a layer was Kael, who had taken the stone into his grip.

"I take the closing axis."

He spoke low.

"The speed at which the upper level re-reads the lower and seals it—I slow that down."

With the tip of the stone, he dug three contact points deep into the plate.

"The upward movement line."

One.

"The auxiliary seal line."

Two.

"The overlap where the living line feeds back into the closing."

Three. When he joined the three with lines, a triangle formed.

"These three are locked together. Touch only one and it holds. But if we slow the speed they realign at, the top can fail to read the bottom."

"You'll touch all three?"

Seorin asked.

"Can't touch all three. Not enough hands. We have to find the spot where we touch one and the other two slow down with it."

"Where."

"Don't know yet. Probably the overlap. Where the people are."

One corner of the triangle, the one the second looked at, was the spot it had named yesterday.

Seorin drew a short line beside the triangle, as if taking notes, and summed up.

"Kael doesn't cut paths, he wrecks the speed at which the closing finds its beat again."

Kael gave a short nod and set the stone down.

Ater was next; he spoke without picking up a stone.

"The upper level splits along face."

Seorin looked at him.

"What matters is less who holds up the top than who appears to hold it up. If so, the real lines of authority, too, are very likely resting largely on the protocol that keeps that face intact."

He paused briefly and looked at his own square on the plate, empty but for a single stone.

"What I will watch is the distance between upper-level protocol and the machines that are treated as precious. Who never lays a hand on anything directly, who looks like the one making the decision, and who handles only the form in which that decision is passed down."

"The ones that handed things over with their fingertips."

Han Jiwoo said.

"Yes."

Ater answered.

"What is handed over by the fingertips, and what is received by the fingertips. Who stands between the two."

"How do you see that? You can't get up there."

"It can be seen without going up."

Ater said.

"Who bows first is easier to see from below."

He put a hand to his neck, then took it away.

Sern pushed the record panel close to the plate and laid a colder layer on top.

"Approval and records run deeper than the surface."

Sern said.

"If protocol is the visible order, approval is closer to the order that makes the flow go on record as normal."

He picked up a stone and drew lines on the plate in several layers, one laid over another.

"Where people go, which hands rise upward, which movements come to look natural."

"For that to hold for long, there must be a form that justifies it on record."

Seorin kept it short.

"So you're watching how this regime keeps its own conduct on the books as normal."

Sern nodded as he answered.

"Yes. And such a structure always has places emptier than its surface shows."

"What's an empty place?"

Luhai asked from beside the children.

"A place where there is approval, but nothing that approved it."

Sern said.

"I said the signal shows up above first. I will see how far up that above goes. There should be an end."

"And if there isn't?"

"If there is none, that is the empty place."

Luhai closed his mouth, and Sern set the stone down.

This time Seorin looked toward the old machines.

"And you?"

The first recovery hand answered.

"We keep the inside running. So people are not crushed, so the signal is never cut entirely, we will hold, so the ones surviving inside can keep pace with the structure coming down."

The second added.

"Recovery too."

"Recovery too."

The first took it up.

Seorin summed it up, short.

"Good. The old machines inside aren't the ones tearing it down, they're the axis that braces it so the people aren't crushed when it falls."

The old machine looked at the floor below the plate, where its lines were, and the light of the wall-side machine went down that far as well.

Then eyes turned to Han Jiwoo.

Han Jiwoo lowered her eyes for a moment to the child's hand, then raised them.

"I'll watch the time on the people's side."

She looked at the stone in the middle of the plate, her stone.

"Who's still holding on to their own beat, who breaks first, who looks to others before themselves, and whose wavering takes other people down with them."

While she paused for just a moment, the child who looks down was over there looking at the floor, and the child with the screwdriver was turning it around with one arm. The little child was holding her hand.

"Shake the structure, and the people shake with it."

"If we lose track of who's still on the people's side when that happens, we'll lose people before we ever break the regime."

"The adult with the shaking hands."

Kael said.

"That adult."

Han Jiwoo took it up.

"People like that, first."

When he had heard that, Sion stood on the line around the edge of the plate and spoke.

"I'm the connecting line."

Seorin gave a short laugh.

"That's too abstract."

Sion didn't laugh; he touched the line around the edge with the tip of his foot.

"The people who stay inside, the people who run outside, and the people whose spirit will break first, somewhere in between."

He looked at the man without a younger brother and the tired-faced woman over among the Hazran people, then at the second beside the plate.

"I'll hold those three so they don't come apart."

Seorin laughed no more, and with the stone she cut the line around the edge once more, deeper.

Luhai blinked at that.

"Then what about me?"

Seorin looked at him and gave a short laugh.

"When everyone's overthinking it, you're the one who says the heart of it first."

Luhai pouted, and the corner of the second's mouth lifted, very slightly. The first's did not; Han Jiwoo's lifted briefly.

"That's a role?"

Luhai asked.

"If you hadn't done it yesterday, there'd be no plate."

Luhai looked at the child on his knee.

"And Aka?"

Seorin asked.

Aka was sitting beside the children, looking not at the plate but at the wall on the gap side.

"Me, I watch."

Aka said.

"Watch what?"

"Don't know yet. Something strange. When we go up."

Seorin lifted the stone but did not draw a place for Aka on the plate.

"Then watch. If you see something, say so."

Aka nodded, though she didn't take her eyes off the wall.

Last, Seorin drew three big lines on the plate, long, across the four squares.

"We've got three goals, too. One, we keep the human recovery line going."

The first and the second looked at that line.

"Two, we slow Serakion's beat of reading and sealing."

Kael looked at that line.

"Three, we shake the upper level's face and the approval structure at the same time, and tangle the very way this order keeps its own face."

Ater and Sern looked at the same line.

Seorin set the stone beside the plate and wiped the dust off her palms onto her trousers.

"When do we go up?"

The second asked.

"When Sern finds the end of the signal. Before that, Kael, look at the overlap again. Ater, go with him and watch the top."

"And me?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"You watch that adult. That kid, too."

As Han Jiwoo nodded, the man without a younger brother spoke from among the Hazran people, toward the plate.

"What would you have us do?"

Seorin looked at him and answered.

"Count. The way you are now. Who is here and who is not."

The man unlaced his fingers and nodded, and did not lace them again.

The old Hazran man turned his plate around, and one line showed on the blank side. He set a fingernail beneath that line, then lifted it away without scratching.

When the yellow lamp flickered, the lines on the plate cast shadows. The triangle, the overlapping lines, the three big lines, the edge. The little child saw those shadows and reached out, but this time Han Jiwoo didn't catch the hand.

The child's finger touched the stone in the middle. The stone didn't move.
