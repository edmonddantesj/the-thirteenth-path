# Episode 216. Intruders

The first door was more ordinary than expected.

For something said to lead toward the Empire's center, it was too quiet, too clean, and looked like nothing was happening.

That was very much the Empire.

Sion's group slipped into the corridor almost soundlessly, matching the pace Sern cut from the front.

Footsteps were small. Breathing was smaller.

The walls were smooth. The floor had almost no dust. The lighting was maintained at a middle value—neither bright nor dark.

That middle value made people more tense, strangely.

Too bright and you could see where to hide. Too dark and you could at least trust in less-visible gaps.

But here, the brightness permitted neither.

Ater raised his hand first.

Everyone stopped immediately.

The wall ahead appeared to bear no markings.

But Ater stepped one pace closer and, without touching the wall, said it very low.

"It reads here once."

Kael asked, nearly by lip movement alone.

"Reads what."

"The sequence of passage."

Sern added right away.

"It is weak right now. But it records."

Sion heard that and understood a little more why the Empire was frightening.

Not visible alarms or traps. A method of quietly accumulating the order of passage and the rhythm of pauses.

Right now it looked like nothing was happening. But looking back later, too much would already have been left behind.

Ater followed an invisible line along one side of the wall, moving his gaze slowly.

"In pairs. Short intervals. Pass without stopping."

Seorin sorted immediately.

"First, me and Kael. Second, Sion and Aka. Third, Ater and Sern."

Luhai protested almost soundlessly.

"What about me?"

Seorin said without looking.

"You and Jiwoo, rear."

Luhai opened his mouth in disbelief, then remembered now was not the time to chatter and barely closed it.

His expression was so comically aggrieved that Sion nearly laughed, then caught himself.

The first passage ended without incident.

Seorin and Kael passed. A moment later, Sion and Aka's turn came.

Sion briefly took Aka's wrist once, released it, and stepped forward.

Crossing the corridor was truly only a few steps.

But within that short stretch, a very thin chill brushed across his back.

Not the feeling of a machine directly scanning the body.

More the feeling that the wall was quietly memorizing—at what speed he passed, at which point he held his breath, where his steps wavered faintly.

Sion did not stop.

Stopping felt like it would leave more behind.

When he reached the other side, Aka exhaled very small.

"I hate this."

Sion asked low.

"What."

"Here. It feels like someone is always watching."

After Sern and Ater passed, no one argued with that.

Luhai and Jiwoo crossed last.

Luhai wore the face of someone searching for a moment to joke until the very end, but this time truly could not manage a single word.

That face alone showed how different the air was inside.

After crossing the first line, the corridor structure itself changed.

It still looked like a maintenance area on the surface, but the spacing between walls narrowed gradually. Corners grew rounder. The distance between doors shortened, yet the line of sight extended further.

Fewer places to hide. Only readable paths remaining.

Ater said it very low.

"Inside."

Sern was no longer walking looking only forward.

Now his entire body was held half a step to the side—ready to change direction at any moment.

Sion saw that posture and felt again: Sern did not merely remember this place. He knew it with his body.

Once, a metallic sound brushed past from a distance.

Everyone stopped at the same time.

Serakion stood at the very back, wordless as always.

But strangely, that presence was the most reassuring right now.

If danger came, the first to move would probably be him.

But what everyone hoped was that Serakion would not have to move at all.

Sern gestured.

Left.

Everyone pressed against the inner wall immediately.

A few seconds later, through a gap in a door far ahead, two people in near-white Imperial maintenance uniforms passed.

They did not speak.

Their steps were uniform. Their gazes did not waver. Even the speed of passing was too perfectly matched.

From that brief scene alone, Sion felt he understood why the Empire's order was suffocating.

That was not trained movement. It was closer to movement that had become daily life.

A place where people live reducing their own rhythm to that extent.

Even after the two had fully receded, no one pulled away from the wall right away.

Ater spoke first, very small.

"From here, we must be even quieter."

Luhai asked by lip movement alone.

"We have been plenty quiet already?"

Jiwoo immediately tapped the back of his head once.

Light enough to make almost no sound.

Luhai looked aggrieved but this time could not even protest.

Sern moved forward again.

"If we reach the next door, we can break briefly."

Sion did not ask how brief that "briefly" was.

Aka kept looking ahead with her brow narrowed.

"Getting closer."

Sion turned his head slightly.

"Elia?"

Aka nodded small.

"Yes. And…"

She trailed off there.

"The other side too."

Sern's shoulders stiffened, very faintly.

Ater did not pretend not to notice.

But right now, no one pressed further about the second presence.

Ahead, another door appeared in the wall.

This one was less ordinary than the first.

The door itself was closed, but the air before it was too neatly arranged.

Anyone could tell this was not a door made for simply passing through.

Sern stopped.

Ater stepped one pace forward.

The two looked at the door and its surroundings together, wordless for a moment.

That silence strangely gave the sense of them reading the same side for the first time.

Ater finally said it low.

"This one reads the approval sequence."

Sern received.

"And if it is wrong, it closes immediately."

Sion looked at the door. It sat still, yet strangely felt alive.

As though, even in this very moment, it was quietly measuring who stood before it and with what qualification.

Ater steadied his breathing, very shallow.

"I will open it."

Sern said immediately.

"Even if it opens, it will not last long."

"I know."

"Your lordship."

Ater glanced at Sern then—very briefly.

Sern said nothing more.

Inside that short address, concern and warning and old habit were all contained.

Sion watched the two and felt his hand tighten without meaning to.

Now it truly felt as though one wrong door would send them sliding into an inside too deep to return from.

Aka said it very small.

"It is beyond there."

No one answered.

That single line focused everything to a single point.

Ater's hand rose toward the unmarked wall beside the door.

Just before the fingertips touched, one light—pale enough to seem bloodless—woke quietly beneath.

The door had not yet opened.

But Sion knew.

From here on, this was a stretch where they could not be wrong even once.
