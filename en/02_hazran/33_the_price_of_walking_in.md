# Episode 33. The Price of Walking In

The silence right after the crash landing didn't last long.

Sern pressed his forehead to the slit and looked outside for a long time.

"There is a hulk out there, about half buried."

"So?"
Han Jiwoo asked from inside the engine panel.

"We are smaller than that."

The sounds coming from inside the panel stopped.

"How long does it take?"

"I do not know,"
Sern said.
"First we have to see which way the wind blows."

Hearing that, Sion looked toward the slit. Outside was bright, and nothing was happening. Nothing on their tail, nothing shooting, but here, if you stayed still, you got buried.

Sion stood leaning against the hull wall, still doing nothing yet.

**Hazran**
**Aka**

The two words still had no explanation. But now they felt less like a direction than like homework. All that was certain was that it was somewhere on this planet; who, or what, or why Elia had left that name, they still didn't know. And yet, strangely, that was what stayed as the clearer goal. One name wasn't enough, but it was the kind of line you couldn't throw away.

Han Jiwoo, her upper body jammed almost all the way into the panel, muttered.

"Just lovely. It's broken real pretty this time."

Seorin came right back.

"Can you save it or not?"

Han Jiwoo didn't answer for a long time; only after something inside came loose with a dull snap did she back out. Between her fingertips was a scrap of blackened response line.

"I can save it,"
she said.
"But it's never leaving as is. One axis went right up to the edge of dying, and two auxiliary lift plates are lagging on response too. If we can't get proper Etherite, it becomes a coffin right here."

A short silence.

Sion asked.

"How much do you need?"

"Not a lot,"
Han Jiwoo answered at once.
"One piece you can hold in your hand. But scraped-together dust won't do. It has to be a single chunk."

She tossed the scrap of response line from her fingertips to the floor.

"And one that still has some reaction left. Put in a dead chunk and she just falls one more time."

Ater spoke low.

"Can you judge it?"

Han Jiwoo let out a breath that was almost a snort.

"I get a rough feel if I look. But in a place like Hazran, there'll be more guys selling fakes than not."

Sern added quietly.

"In other words, we must find not only the goods but also a hand that will not cheat us."

Sion heard that line fit against what he was carrying.

A hand that would not cheat.
Someone who could tell the real from the fake.
The one name Elia had left.

Aka.

Seorin looked at Sion.

"They overlap."

Sion gave a short nod too.

"They do."

Hearing that, Kael took his eyes off the outside of the slit for the first time.

"Is there a particular place that sells Etherite?"

Han Jiwoo rolled her shoulders back once.

"Someplace sells it, sure. The question is whether the real thing's there."

Sern swept his eyes over the outside panel and spoke.

"The nearest heat-source trace is to the northeast. Not a large settlement, but traces of mobile trade overlap there."

Ater went on.

"There are also tracks leading out of the hulk cluster. Not natural wind erosion. Closer to a route traveled again and again."

"Smells like a market,"
Han Jiwoo said.

Seorin didn't draw a conclusion right away. Instead she looked for a moment at the sand and the shadows of the frames outside the slit, and asked.

"The pursuit ships?"

Sern answered, short.

"Their sight was cut right after we passed the heat layer. But it is hard to say they have lost us completely."

"Of course,"
Seorin cut in.
"We fell alive, and they saw it."

Kael added, flatly.

"They won't be able to come straight down. But they'll try to find our trail."

Sion looked outside the slit again. The shadows of half-buried hulks, the sandy wind sweeping between them, metal shells still holding heat. Hazran looked less like a destination than a place that could make the survivors last longer, or rot faster.

Seorin summed it up, short.

"Then we move."

"Now?"
Han Jiwoo asked back.

"Now,"
Seorin cut in.
"If we sit here tearing the whole ship open to check her state, the trail just gets stronger. We take only what we need and find the market first."

Han Jiwoo clicked her tongue as if swallowing a complaint, but didn't argue. The truth was, she knew it better than anyone. She would want to get her hands on the engine heat, the broken axis, the hot outer wall before any of it cooled, but before that they had to get the thing they needed to survive.

Ater asked quietly.

"Is it all right to leave the ship empty?"

This time Kael spoke first.

"Not completely."

A short silence.

Seorin looked at him.

Holding the fragment closer inside his coat, Kael spoke.

"This place looks abandoned, but it's the kind of place where abandoned things get stripped first."

Han Jiwoo gave a small laugh.

"You're saying the same thing I am."

Seorin looked at each of them once, then divided them up.

"Sion and I go. Han Jiwoo and Sern stay."

"I will go as well,"
Ater said.
"If there is approval resonance left in the market, I will see it first."

Seorin gave a short nod.

"I'm not staying,"
Kael said.

Only then did Seorin turn toward Kael.

"Then leave that here."

Kael's hand went halfway inside his coat and stopped.

"Why."

"You know what kind of place we're going to,"
Seorin said.
"They put a price on everything they can see there. Once they price it, the price sticks, and once a thing has a price on it, who takes it is already decided."

Kael didn't answer.

"Leave it here and Han Jiwoo guards it."

"If she can,"
Kael said.

Han Jiwoo started to say something and didn't. The one who knew the ship's state best couldn't argue with that.

A short silence.

"Then bring it,"
Seorin said.
"But know one thing before you go."

"What."

"Leave it here and it might get stripped. Carry it and it gets a price. Either way you lose it. Just at a different speed."

Kael didn't take that up for a long while.

Then he pushed the fragment once more inside his coat. Deeper than before.

"I'm taking it."

Sion looked at that hand. Until a moment ago it had been a hand holding something close; now it was a hand hiding it.

Han Jiwoo clicked her tongue softly.

"Just come back with something in your hands."

Sion took the paper from inside his jacket and unfolded it once more. **Hazran**, **Aka**. The sandy wind seeped faintly inside and stirred the edge of the paper.

Kael watched it, then asked.

"That name. It's a person, right?"

Sion answered, short.

"Don't know yet."

Ater added, low.

"But for now, it is better to move on the assumption that it is a person."

Kael didn't ask anything more. Only the look in his eyes changed, very slightly. How a person's face changes when the smell of a fragment attaches itself to a single name—in that brief moment, Sion had the feeling he had already seen it a few times before.

Getting ready didn't take long.

The first thing Seorin picked up wasn't a weapon. It was water.

Sion found that strange and looked at her.

"People don't die here from getting shot,"
Seorin said, slinging two canisters to split between them.
"They die from not being able to walk."

Then she handed one to Sion. When Sion took it, it was heavier than he expected.

"This is heavy."

"It's when it gets light that you have a problem."

Han Jiwoo muttered, pulling out temporary patches for the hull's outer wall.

"Go on. I'll be here keeping this corpse from dying."

Sern was already picking out the side outside the slit that had no footprints.

"We can walk as far as the northeast route. But it is better to ride the shadows of the frames than the open sand."

Seorin nodded.

"We follow the shadows."

When the hatch opened all the way, Hazran's heat surged inside.

It was nothing like the heat they had felt inside the ship. Not machine heat, but heat like the planet itself breathing out a breath it had been warming for a long time. Sand and burned metal, old oil, the smell of dried-out wind, all rose at once.

Sion stopped for a bare moment as he set his first foot outside.

The sand underfoot was less soft than like ash mixed with metal dust. In the distance, half-buried hulks rose from the desert like the ribs of dead beasts, and between them ran faint traces of human hands coming and going again and again. So much had been thrown away here that it seemed nobody would leave even a person lying around for long.

Kael was the first to step down.

"It smells alive."

Han Jiwoo shot back at once from inside the hull.

"I don't like the sound of that one bit."

Seorin didn't answer and looked ahead.

"Let's go."

With that one word, the four of them started toward the shadows of the hulks.

Behind them were a ship not yet completely dead, and the two staying behind to hold on to it.
Ahead were Etherite, an Aka who was only a name, and a market where no one knew who would try to put a price on them first.

Hazran did not welcome them.

As he walked, Sion glanced once toward Kael. The inside of his coat was flatter than before. Pushing it deeper had come out as showing less on the outside.

No one had named a price yet, and they had already paid once.
