# Episode 202. Emptying the Ruins

The way down was not like the way up.

On the same stairs and through the same corridors, they walked where they had run on the way up and passed the corners where they had hidden.
They walked down the middle instead of keeping to the walls, and still nothing came.

After they left the hall, no one spoke first for a long while.
The empty space between Kael and Han Jiwoo stayed empty even as they walked.
Han Jiwoo didn't move half a step into it, and neither did Kael.

Seorin was the first to move. Not fast, one step at a time, as if checking.

"We clean it up."

The short words sounded more tired than the start of a battle.
Sern didn't write them down. He looked at the wall.

"Upper-level links are still active. They are not fully dead. Command lines persist."

Inside the wall a single streak of light ran slowly from top to bottom. It stopped, then ran again.
It was light with nowhere to go.

Ater picked it up.

"The remaining approval lines must be cut first. The proxy body is empty, but the form still remains below."

"Everything that thing said it approved."

Sion said.

"Yes. Up there it ended with 'Approved,' but down here those words stay on as lines. The lines run on their own."

Kael pushed open a fold seam in the wall with his foot, and inside, a streak of light jumped briefly and went out.
He looked at the spot for a while.

"There'll be stuff like this on every level."

"Yeah."

Seorin answered.

"We have to get rid of them one at a time."

"How many levels?"

"As many as we came up."

As Kael pulled his foot out of the fold seam, his ankle twisted.

Han Jiwoo looked down over the railing and saw three androids standing below, not on the same beat.
One was looking down at its own hands.
One leaned against the wall, slowly turning its head.
The other had a hand on its own face. Clumsy light had spread across that face, and the hand had stopped above the light.

Han Jiwoo spoke very low.

"Now… even knowing what to do must be a first for them."

The last android looked at the three. He looked at his own hand, and at the one with its hand on its face.
He didn't put a hand to his own face. He had done that once already.

"It was a first for you too, wasn't it?"

He looked down at his own hand. He closed it, opened it, and with that hand touched the lines on his face.

Another section down the stairs, the plates of two fallen Serakion robots lay on the floor, and a few androids stood stopped beside them.
At that distance, before, a command would have come first, but no one moved first.
When a robot's plate shuddered once on the floor, one android looked at it but didn't step on it.

Seorin stopped.

"Sern. Remaining responses."

Sern put his hand to the wall and took it away, his eyes following the structural lines.

"Faint. The two robots' restoration lines are cut. No direct commands on the android side either. Only a residual beat remains."

"What's a residual beat?"

Sion asked.

"It is what keeps running after everything has stopped. Like a clock."

Sern said.

"There are no commands, but the beat remains, so the body tries to stand to that beat."

"How long does it last?"

"I do not know. Even a clock stops eventually."

Sern said.

"Cut it, and it stops at once."

Sion looked at the androids standing still. They were bodies standing to what was left of a beat.

When Ater looked at a thin reading line inside the wall, numbers were flowing along it in light.

"The sorting line is still binding the units to their old numbers. If we do not cut it, it will keep trying to pull them back into the old arrangement."

"Numbers, not names."

Han Jiwoo said.

"Numbers."

Ater said.

"The children would have been on that line too."

Han Jiwoo looked at the numbers flowing along the line, but she couldn't read them.
When she put her hand on the line, it was cold.

Seorin nodded at once.

"Kael, rip the panel. Sion, take the left. Han Jiwoo watches the androids. Ater, call the sequence lines. Sern, the timing of the cut."

They all spread out. It was not a battle, but it was more careful than cleaning up after one.
Everyone's footsteps were quiet.

Kael worked his fingers into a seam in the wall casing and pulled, and the casing came open.
Inside was a thin alignment plate. Lines were layered over lines, and light flowed slowly across them.

Sern spoke.

"Now. Second row first."

Ater went on.

"Left side, third approval line. That is the original-number binding. Cut that line first and the arrangement below comes loose."

Sion gripped the structure on the inner left and twisted. The metal let out a short cry, and three streaks of light shook.
When Kael pushed his hand in deeper, up to the elbow, and tore out one alignment plate, the light went out.
One beat that had been running through the whole wall broke off.

A dull snap.

It made a small sound.

Below the railing, one android swayed, very slightly.
Then it looked down at its own feet, slowly.
It lifted a foot.
It set it down.
Not on the beat, but where it chose to set it.

Han Jiwoo went slowly toward that android, at a pace that didn't push, and at a pace that didn't leave it alone either.
She stopped three steps from the android, then raised her hand, palm up.

The android raised its hand a beat late, but they didn't touch.
Three steps. That distance.
The android's hand wavered, unsure whether to go down or up. Han Jiwoo's hand did not waver.
The android's hand stayed where it was.

"That's enough. That far is enough. For today."

The android slowly lowered its hand.
It lowered it on its own.

Sion watched it and let out a long breath. He looked at his blade, but there was no use for it.
When he sheathed it his hand was empty, and he put that empty hand against the wall.

One more level down, there was much the same.
Wreckage of robots whose commands had been cut, androids that didn't know where to put their hands.
On every level, approval lines, alignment plates, numbers, restoration axes, one at a time.
Kael tore them out.
Sion twisted.
Sern counted.
Ater called them.
Han Jiwoo watched.

A little farther down, two newly woken androids stood with a sorting panel in the wall between them.
One's hands shook, and the other was slow.
The two were pulling at the panel at different angles, one upward, one sideways.
The panel went crooked and creaked but didn't come out.
The two didn't let go. Forcing it, clumsily.

Kael saw it but didn't go to help, and Han Jiwoo didn't go either.

The last android watched the two for a while. Then, following the angle they were pulling at, he pressed his hand down on an old anchoring axis at the base of the wall, in a spot the two hadn't seen.
The whole stuck panel slid down at a slant all at once, and the hands of both came down with it.
The two looked at him briefly. Thanks, maybe, or a check.
He looked back at the two, but he gave no command, and they did not obey either.
The panel lay on the floor, and the three of them looked at it.

Watching that, Ater spoke very low.

"…At last, they move without approval."

"You too."

Han Jiwoo said.

She was looking at Ater.

"You move without approval too. Ever since Hazran."

Ater didn't answer, and looked at the dead approval line in the wall instead.
He put his hand on the line, then took it away.

"Ever since Hazran, that is true."

He said.

"Before that, there was approval."

Han Jiwoo said no more.

Seorin spoke without breaking stride.

"We keep going. There are still a lot of levels left."

"How many are left down there?"

Sion asked.

"Don't count. Count while you cut."

Sern marked the record panel: one level, three lines, two plates.
Beside them he did not write the number of androids.

Sion looked at the way leading down. The way they had run on the way up, they now walked.
One wall panel, one number.
Something standing beside something that had stopped. Something standing beside something touching its own face for the first time.
Work that size.

He spoke very low.

"Let's go."

It was short. Seorin went down first, and the rest followed in a column.
At the very end, the two newly woken androids fell in, a beat behind, each to a different beat.
When Han Jiwoo looked back, the two were there.
Even without her slowing down, the two kept following.
