# Episode 47. The Name Called First

After the man standing in the middle of the courtyard swallowed his last words, the air around them sank once more.

People who had been laughing low and muttering a moment ago went quiet strangely fast. The fingers drumming on the railing of the stands stopped too. The man beside Sion stopped halfway through capping his water canister and just held it.

Harun stood at the edge of the courtyard without folding his arms, and Nasim had stepped back a pace. Both of them stood where the whole courtyard could be taken in at a glance.

Sion pressed the paper inside his jacket once, then took his hand away.

**Hazran**
**Aka**

The two words were still there.

The man at the center opened his mouth again.

"The first is simple,"
he said.
"We see whether a hand knows the real thing."

A few people around them drew a low breath.

Three shallow stands draped in black cloth came up onto the central metal plate.

Quiet hands had moved so that no one could say who had set them there, or when. Under each cloth there seemed to be an object of about the same size. The shapes looked alike, but the weight of them felt subtly different. Harun glanced once toward those hands. The hands stepped back half a pace each, in time with his look.

Sion found himself looking for Aka.

Aka was still standing near the innermost shade. Nahira was blocking the space a step in front of her, but Aka's eyes had already reached the three stands at the center. One at a time. In order. They did not stay long on any of them.

The man at the center spoke.

"One of the three is real,"
he said.
"One is an imitation of where the real thing used to be, and one is trash that can't take a price at all."

Luhai swallowed a very small breath that was close to a curse. His hand went toward his pocket, then stopped when he saw the watching hand beside him come down with it.

Nasim laughed very low.

"Pretty way to start,"
he muttered.

Hearing that, Seorin only rolled her eyes once.

"The only pretty thing here is the way you talk,"
she said, low.

The man at the center paid no attention.

"First name,"
he said clearly.

At that moment, oddly, Sion's breathing went shallow before anything else.

"Luhai."

Luhai swallowed a curse almost by reflex.
Kael took half a step forward, then stopped when he saw the watching hand beside him move with him.

A low laugh spread around once, then quickly settled. No one looked surprised. The kid who had picked up the real fragment from inside first, the kid who messed with the ledgers. In a few places in the stands, hands moved at once.

Luhai glanced once at the watching hand beside him, then looked back at the center.

"What if I say no?"
he asked bluntly.

The man at the center answered without even smiling.

"Then your price for tonight ends right there."

Luhai clicked his tongue.

"Real lousy luck."

"You're still going up, though,"
Kael said, low.

Luhai heard that and shot him one irritated look. This time, instead of arguing, he only let out a breath that was close to a snort.

He walked out to the center slowly.

Sion saw that the walk was less light than he had expected. It was twelve steps to the center, and on the way Luhai rubbed his palm on his trousers once.

The man at the center asked him.

"Which hand does the looking?"

Luhai answered irritably.

"My hand. Whose else would it be?"

Low laughter rose around them again.

The man at the center nodded.

"Good. Then pick."

The cloths had not been lifted yet.

Beside Sion, Seorin spoke very low.

"Watch his hand."

"Watch what?"

"He'll pull it back once before he chooses. He's the kind whose hand goes out first when he's sure."

Luhai stood before the three stands and did not move for a while. Sion noticed that the kid's eyes were on the edges of the cloths, the shadows under the stands and the marks left when things were set down, more than on the stands themselves. It was not the eye of someone simply choosing an object. It was the eye of a kid who had stolen and run, and had spent a long time watching where people hid the real thing.

Then Luhai crouched. He looked at the sand around the legs of the stands. Around the first stand, the sand had settled evenly. It had been set down just now and no one had touched it since. Around the third there was no sand at all. Someone had swept it away. Only around the second was one side disturbed. A hand had passed there that set it down and then set it down again.

When Luhai stood up, he did not brush the sand off his knees.

In front of the first stand, Luhai's hand stopped for just a moment.

His fingertips pulled back just before they touched the cloth. Exactly as Seorin had said.
Sion looked at Seorin. Seorin was watching only Luhai's hand.

He didn't pick it.

The second.

This time his fingertips almost brushed the cloth, then pulled back.

In front of the third, Luhai held his breath for real for the first time.

It wasn't the breath of certainty. It was the breath of someone reading how the three lied, each in its own way.

Harun didn't move.

Only Nasim was smiling, very faintly. Up in the stands, the bets moved back and forth between the second and the third. The same canister changed hands twice.

Luhai finally opened his mouth.

"This one."

He pointed at the second stand.

"Why,"
the man at the center asked.

Luhai narrowed his eyes.

"The first one fakes being real way too obviously,"
he said.
"The third's the other way round, hidden way too sloppy. The real thing doesn't roll like that on this floor."

The man at the center pressed further.

"So, the second?"

"The second,"
Luhai said very low,
"acts like it's hiding, but it couldn't keep what it is covered all the way."

Sion found himself evening out a shallow breath.

The man at the center slowly drew back the cloth over the second stand.

Inside lay a single irregular piece of metal. At a glance it could pass for an old part; looked at as something a little more precious, it could pass for an old joining piece.

The smell came first as the cloth came off. The smell of old iron. Freshly cut iron doesn't smell like that. Sion started to recall where he had smelled it, then let it go.

The air around them stirred, very slightly.

The man at the center lifted the piece once to show everyone.

Then he spoke, very low.

"Half right."

Luhai's face went stiff.

Laughter, disappointment and interest all mixed into the air at once.

The man at the center set the piece back down.
The first and third stands were carried off inside with their cloths still on. No one lifted those cloths. Someone sitting beside the board drew one short line in a ledger.

Ater looked toward the ledger.

"Does that go in the ledger?"

"It does."

Nasim smiled.

"A price has to be written down to be a price."


"This is an imitation,"
the man at the center said.
"But it has a price. It held on to the place where the real thing used to be for a long time."

Nasim let out a low breath.

"Half is expensive,"
he said.
"Here, the one who gets half right lives longer than the one who gets it all right."

In the stands, one of the people who had bet on the second took back the canister they had handed over. The one who had taken it gave back only half.

Sion felt those words run cold down the inside of his back.

Then he looked toward Aka.

Aka still said nothing, but it was different from before.

She wasn't looking at the stands. She was looking at Luhai.

Sion didn't know what kind of look that was. He only knew it was the first time on this board that the kid had looked toward a person.

Nahira noticed and moved half a step closer.

Aka didn't move. She kept watching until Luhai came down.

An imitation, but it has a price.

Luhai chewed his lip once.

On Luhai's way down from the center, Kael came half a step out to meet him. The watching hand did not stop that half step.

"Half's good enough."

"Good enough for what?"

Luhai said it.

"You caught the imitation for real."

It was the face of someone angrier because it wasn't a complete failure.

The man at the center spoke one last time.

"Good. Then the next one gets harder."

The sound of prices being called rose from the stands again. Louder than before. Luhai's name came through in it twice. Once with laughter, once without.

Luhai didn't look up at the sound. Instead he rubbed his palm on his trousers, twice. It was the hand that had gone for the stand.

"Wonder what comes out next."

Sion had nothing to answer with. Luhai didn't seem to be asking because he was waiting for an answer either.
