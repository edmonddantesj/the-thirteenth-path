# Episode 224. The Waiting One's Door

The inside of the first safe point was quiet.

The metallic sounds and steady friction of the maintenance lines from outside did not follow here.

That did not mean it was a reassuring quiet.

Everyone knew.

This quiet was a brief gap for catching breath—not the calm after the end.

Elia sat with her back against the wall. Eyes open. Breathing somewhat steadied. But fatigue still sat deep on the face.

Still, her way of speaking was gradually returning toward the original Elia.

That was strangely reassuring to Sion.

Opposite, the nameless man was leaning against a low metal crate.

His condition was clearly poor. Yet the feeling of falling apart was barely there.

It was the face of someone who had deferred collapse for too long, still choosing to endure quietly instead of falling.

And the one staring at that person the longest was, of course, Luhai.

Luhai held back at first.

He truly tried.

That was never going to last.

He finally opened his mouth, very carefully.

"Um."

Jiwoo's hand rose reflexively.

Luhai immediately hunched his shoulders.

"No, no, I will not be loud. But I am really curious."

Elia said without even turning.

"You are always curious."

"That is true," Luhai muttered, looking wronged. "But this time everyone is curious."

Sion could not deny it.

Aka was silent too, but clearly thinking the same.

Sern did not take his eyes off the man. Seorin was watching for movement outside. Luhai leaned forward again.

"So… who are you?"

The man set down his water and looked at Luhai briefly.

A very short gaze.

From that brief look alone, Luhai found himself adjusting his posture without meaning to.

The man said it low.

"Right now, a person who made it out alive."

Luhai blinked several times.

"I know that part."

"That should be enough."

The corner of Jiwoo's mouth moved just slightly. Not quite a smirk—closer to a well blocked reaction.

Luhai did not give up.

"No, I mean… how long were you in there?"

"Long."

"I can see that."

"Then you saw."

Luhai opened his mouth, then closed it again.

Seorin lowered her head and laughed—very brief.

Kael too let out a breath like a throat-clear.

Luhai, looking wronged, changed direction.

"Then the escape. Were you waiting for us, or was it really just coincidence?"

The man did not answer right away this time.

Instead he glanced at Elia, very briefly.

Elia said it dryly.

"He will not answer that question."

"Why."

"Because you are the most likely to talk about it once we are outside."

"When have I ever."

"Always."

Luhai looked aggrieved, but no one took his side this time.

Instead, what Sion felt first was that Elia's way of speaking was returning to its original form, little by little.

Short. Dry. Never explaining unnecessarily. Piercing people with precision.

Meanwhile, Aka had been silent.

She did not ask questions like Luhai. Instead, she kept sensing the same thing inside the man's brief answers.

This person was keeping only the lines needed right now and folding away everything else—his name, his story.

And that was not simply wariness.

It was the method of someone who measures what to say and how far before explaining the self.

That sensation was strangely not unfamiliar.

Elia exhaled very low.

"Stop tormenting him. We need to move too."

Sern scanned the air once as though checking a clock and said short.

"Correct. We cannot stay long."

Seorin rose immediately.

"Next location."

Luhai still wore a face of lingering regret, but this time stood without argument.

"I will ask again later."

The man answered very low.

"If we are alive."

Luhai paused at that.

It did not sound like a joke. But it was not meant to intimidate either.

Simply the most accurate answer for this situation.

So Luhai, rarely—truly rarely—closed his mouth right away.

After a brief silence, the man looked at Sion.

For the first time.

When answering Luhai, he had been defensive. Looking at Elia, there was a familiar distance. But looking at Sion—neither of those.

It was an assessing gaze.

The eyes of someone trying, in a short time, to read why this person had come this far and what they intended to bear next.

And asked, very low.

"How many times did you change on the way here."

Sion considered the meaning briefly.

"Routes?"

"Not routes." The man said. "Judgments."

Sern's gaze shifted, very faintly.

Sion did not answer. But from that one question alone, he could tell this person was not simply someone who had been locked up and waited.

Not asking about routes but about judgments. That was the question of someone who already knew, from the inside, what kind of path this was.

The next movement was more cautious than the first escape.

They had left the heart, yes. But this was not yet what could be called outside the Empire.

Hidden connection lines. Abandoned auxiliary corridors. Unused maintenance shadows. Segments where authorization lines thinned. They moved in short cuts, everyone choosing only those.

Throughout, no one made loud sounds. No one spoke more than necessary.

Strangely, Luhai's face showed he was still curious even inside all that silence.

Once, he pressed close to Sion's side and whispered very small.

"Sion. Who do you think he actually is?"

Sion answered low.

"If you do not know, just go not knowing for a while."

"Are you not curious?"

Sion thought briefly, then said.

"I am. But right now, what catches me first is why everyone is being this careful because of that person."

Luhai closed his mouth at that.

Because he was feeling it too.

The second safe point they reached looked on the surface like a long-empty transit auxiliary hub. But the inside was different.

There were traces of tidying. On the dust unique to a long-abandoned space, recent footprints had been deliberately scuffed.

Seorin knocked twice, paused, then knocked once more.

Inside, a very short stirring moved.

The door opened, and the moment Sion saw who stood inside, he recognized him immediately.

Tarin.

Tarin, upon opening the door, read the entire situation first—as always.

Who was hurt and how much. Who was standing. Who was leaning. And who had been brought.

That gaze, when it reached Elia, paused very briefly.

And the next instant—when it moved to the nameless man—Tarin's eyes clearly wavered.

Truly brief.

Anyone but Sion might have missed it.

A flinch. Just once.

Tarin immediately composed the expression again.

Then looked at Elia once more.

Tarin said it in a very low, neat voice.

"Please come in."

Luhai saw that reaction and went wide-eyed.

Clearly startled just now, yet why not ask? Why not even ask the name and just say come in? The face of total incomprehension.

But Sern, seeing that response, only lowered his head—very faintly.

Seorin too wore the face of someone who read that Tarin had already understood enough.

The inner space was far more organized than the exterior.

Not a place for long stays, but sufficient for briefly hiding and preparing the next move.

While everyone entered, Tarin asked not a single unnecessary question.

Only confirmed what was needed, short.

"Pursuit?"

Sern answered.

"Following. Not here yet."

"Mobile personnel?"

"All mobile." Seorin said. "Just adjust for the two slow ones."

Tarin's gaze went again to Elia and the nameless man.

This time, no wavering like the first.

The eyes of someone who had already accepted.

A very short nod.

"Understood."

Luhai looked like he could not bear it anymore, but would not dare open his mouth in front of Tarin.

Instead he tilted his head slightly toward Sion and asked by nearly lip movement alone.

What is going on?

Sion moved only his shoulders, small.

I do not know either.

But truthfully, Sion was starting to sense it now.

Tarin had been startled seeing that man. After seeing Elia, the reason for the surprise was immediately understood.

That might mean Elia and that person were connected through something in the same direction.

Even if he did not yet know what.

A moment later, Tarin spoke again.

"They are waiting."

Luhai simply took it as: there must be someone very important inside.

Aka's gaze sank slightly too. Sion also felt his heart drop once, heavy.

But strangely, the nameless man's expression barely changed.

He looked more like someone who had already known that possibility—only lowering his gaze, very faintly.

Tarin added.

"From here, I will guide you personally."

Sern stepped back quietly. Seorin and Kael began re-sorting the next movement lines in their heads.

Elia pushed off the wall and rose—very slowly.

Her body still looked heavy, but her eyes were more vivid than before.

And she said it short.

"Let us go."

The tone was now nearly the original Elia.

Sion and Aka attached on both sides. Sern and Tarin quietly settled positions beside the nameless man.

Luhai, following, still wore the face of someone dying of curiosity.

By now even he knew.

That here, bringing out his own curiosity first was truly not the right order.

After a short transit, they stopped before a door—deeper inside, bearing no markings.

The door was neither ornate nor imposing.

So neat, in fact, that it made you tense more easily.

Tarin paused before it briefly.

And lowered the gaze very short, toward the inside.

The gesture was thoroughly formal.

But Sion could feel, inside that brief pause, both quiet preparation and reverence toward whoever was beyond.

Tarin said it low.

"We have arrived."

With that single line, everyone there held their breath a little without meaning to.

One door remaining. Once it opened, the person who had been waiting inside and the questions just pulled from the heart would be placed in the same space for the first time.

And at that threshold, Sion felt with vivid clarity the premonition that from here, truly, no one could return to where they had been before.
