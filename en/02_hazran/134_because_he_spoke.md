# Episode 134. Because He Spoke

When morning came, Sion first checked what he had marked during the night.

He opened the board, and five dots showed. Two people, two machines, and Kael.

The young man who had leaned against the wall still didn't look well, but the person who had sat beside him all night was still there. Neither of them was asleep, and neither of them was talking. The man's hand had left the floor beside him and lay on his knee.

The woman who had nearly collapsed by the children had caught a little sleep, however short, and was up again, folding cloth. Her hands opened and closed and didn't shake. The tired-faced woman sat beside her, with one of the children on her lap.

The machine that had faltered while sorting signals had one optical eye opened up and another machine's hand on its knee. It was maintenance, and even while being serviced, it paged through a screen with its other hand.

The machine whose joint had slipped stood in front of Han Jiwoo. Han Jiwoo was crouched, opening up the knee whose axis had been half a knuckle out.

Kael was asleep, leaning against the wall. It was the spot where Sion had stood the night shift, and Sion had just handed that spot over to Sern before coming back.

It wasn't all right, not completely. But nothing had broken worse overnight.

Sion folded the board again and tucked it under his arm. The fatigue settled into his shoulders, but he didn't roll them.

No one was first to say thank you. This was not a place where words like that came easily.

But the way people looked at him had changed. Eyes that hadn't followed him yesterday followed Sion as he passed. One person nodded, very slightly, but Sion didn't nod back and walked on.

While the morning sorting was in full swing, the tired-faced woman came over.

She stood beside Sion and held out her hand for the board, and Sion gave it to her. She touched the five dots marked in the night one by one with a finger.

"Two of them didn't go as far under."

Sion looked at her.

Without meeting his eyes, the woman went on.

"If you hadn't spoken up last night, by morning one of them would've really burst, and the other would've shut down completely."

"Which ones?"

The five dots on the board were as they had been.

"You don't need to know that."

The woman handed the board back.

"They didn't burst."

She looked at Sion. For the first time she met his eyes.

"Tonight, the two of us watch."

"With whom?"

"With me."

"Who watches me?"

The woman was silent for a moment.

"I do. And you watch me."

Sion nodded. The woman said nothing more.

Only after a long while did Sion ask, low.

"The machines too?"

The woman gave a short nod.

"When a person breaks, it shows. That side gets caught later."

She jerked her chin toward the knee Han Jiwoo was opening.

"That one would've snapped mid-step today if you hadn't said anything."

Sion looked at the knee. Han Jiwoo's hands were pushing the axis back in, not all at once but a little push, then a stop, over and over.

"If it snaps mid-step."

"It can't get up. Not that body."

"Then."

"Then it stays right there. Until another machine moves it."

"And when they move it."

"It becomes parts."

The woman spoke without looking at Sion.

Sion looked at Han Jiwoo's hands. The axis went all the way in.

The woman left without another word.

That morning, too, Aka was watching the blocked passage.

When Sion looked her way, two machines nearby were opening up part of the wall Aka had pointed at, the place with less rust. One machine traced the seam with a fingertip while the other watched from behind it.

Until yesterday they had only looked, but today they were touching it.

Aka saw it too and spoke, very quietly.

"Now they listen."

Sion looked at her. Aka's shoulders had come down, and she didn't take her eyes off the wall.

"They listen late."

She added.

"Always."

Sion didn't answer.

One machine stopped its hand at the end of the seam and looked at the other machine, and the two of them looked at the spot together.

When Han Jiwoo closed the knee, the machine straightened its leg.

One step. No sound. Two steps. None.

The machine stopped and looked down at its own knee, then at Han Jiwoo.

"Try walking."

Han Jiwoo said.

The machine walked five steps across the floor and came back. No sound.

"More."

The machine walked ten steps and came back. As it turned, the knee caught once.

"Saw that. That one's for tomorrow."

Han Jiwoo's finger tapped the spot on the knee that had caught.

The machine nodded. It was a nod that had understood the word tomorrow, and after watching that nod, Han Jiwoo picked up a tool and put it in her pocket.

An old machine saw this and spoke, very briefly.

"You have pushed back the point of scrapping."

Han Jiwoo wiped her forehead with the back of her hand and answered.

"That's what the job is."

Right after she said it, her hand stopped, the back of it still against her forehead.

The point of scrapping.

She lowered her hand and put the tools in her pocket one by one, but the hand doing it was slower than usual.

The machine she had fixed was still standing in front of her, and Han Jiwoo looked up.

"Go."

The machine turned and went without a sound. Han Jiwoo watched its back all the way.

Luhai had worn a tired face since morning.

One of the children who had run for the passage yesterday was stuck to his leg, and when Luhai shook his leg, the child didn't come off. Meanwhile another child came and clung to his other leg.

Luhai stood there with two children hanging off him.

"I'm not cut out for this."

A human nearby answered offhandedly. It was the old woman.

"People who are cut out for it never say that."

Luhai looked at the old woman, but the old woman didn't look at Luhai; she went on folding cloth as she had been.

The child on his leg looked up.

"Are you here today too?"

"I'm here."

Luhai answered.

"Tomorrow too?"

Luhai couldn't answer right away. The old woman's hands stopped on the cloth.

"Ask about tomorrow tomorrow."

Luhai said.

The child hugged his leg tighter.

Seorin watched the scene and, without meaning to, gave a quiet snort, then shut her mouth.

Kael woke, pushed off the wall, got up, and went over to the cargo bay that looked ready to cave in, the spot where he had stood yesterday. Watching his back, Sion opened the board and looked at Kael's dot. He didn't erase it.

While Sern layered fresh changes onto the signal flow he had sorted all night, the machine that had opened the way marked dots beside him. Their dots landed in the same place. Sern saw that, and so did the machine.

"It is the same place."

Sern said.

"The same place."

The machine answered.

"Why is that?"

"Because we saw the same thing."

When Sern marked the next dot below it, the machine marked one too. It was the same place.

A little later, the old machine called Sion over beside the pillar.

It looked more worn out than it had in the night, and one of its optical eyes was dimmer than yesterday. Its standing body leaned slightly toward the pillar, but it didn't touch the pillar.

"From today, do not watch only the people."

Sion looked at it.

The old machine went on, evenly.

"You must watch for breakdown on the machine side as well. Here, if you let even one of the two collapse, in the end both collapse together."

Sion opened the board and showed it the two dots he had scratched last night where the machines were.

"I marked them yesterday."

The old machine's optical eye rested on those dots for a long time.

"Who told you to mark them?"

"No one."

"Then that board is yours now."

The old machine turned, moved away from the pillar, and took one step.

Sion heard the sound. The knee. The same place as on the machine Han Jiwoo had fixed. He opened the board and looked for the old machine's place, but there was no dot. There had never been dots where the machines were.

He set his fingernail there, and after a long while, he scratched.

He folded the board and looked toward Han Jiwoo. Han Jiwoo was sitting down in front of the next part.

The machine she had fixed had come back and stood before her, and the part in its hand was a new one, the same spec as the ones Han Jiwoo had set in the side pile. The machine set it down in front of Han Jiwoo, then, without a word, turned around and left without a sound.

Han Jiwoo picked up the part, turned it over in her hand, and set it on the front pile.

Sion walked that way.
