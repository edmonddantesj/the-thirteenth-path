# Episode 197. Visible Emptiness

At the end of the widening corridor, the ceiling was open.

It was a grate with gaps a finger wide, and it was bright above. Light passed through the grate and drew a pattern on the floor, and the nine stood outside the pattern without stepping on it, pressed close to the wall. Sern raised a hand and put a finger to his lips.

Sound came down from above.

It was a voice, low and even, coming over the wall. The walls on the levels below had swallowed sound, but the walls here sent down sound that did not mind being heard.

"Lower level, third alignment gate: error logged. Middle level, blocking axis: connection delay occurred. Inner section, second line: reference restoration failed, realignment completed."

Listening to the report, not fast and not slow, every line the same length, Sern folded his fingers down.

One.
Two.
Three.

The same length.

Sion crouched beneath the grate and raised his head to look up. He saw a high ceiling and pillars, and thin lines of light rose and fell between the pillars. In every groove of the walls, thin metal ornaments were folded layer on layer, but there was no dust, no scratch, no footprint.

Inside the hall, robots stood in alignment. Light lay thinly over their smooth, seamless bodies like ornament, and they had no pushing plates and no closing plates, which made them unlike the robots below. Instead they had hands, long hands, the kind that had grown longer the higher one went.

Ater crouched beside Sion and looked up at those hands.

"They are the aristocrat series."

He spoke very low.

"The ones that handed things over with their fingertips."

One robot spoke smoothly.

"Response deviation in the middle-level deployment is recurring. Although the correction command was applied immediately, the same kind of misalignment has recurred."

Another unit took it up at once, with no pause between.

"It is not the same kind. It is a similar pattern. The input axis differs slightly each time."

A third unit went on.

"Therefore, rather than a repetition of lower-level failure, it is more reasonable to regard this as a learned intruder deliberately inducing a different error at each level."

Sern counted the three voices, and there was no gap between one speech and the next. He drew three lines on the record panel, close together.

"They don't argue."

Kael whispered.

"Three of them saying different things, and they don't argue."

"They are not saying different things."

Ater said.

"Three of them are splitting one statement between them."

Ater's fingertips tightened until the nails of the hand braced on the wall went white, and Han Jiwoo, beside him, looked at that hand.

At the far end of the hall, in a high place, there was a figure.

It sat on a structure shaped like a high chair, unmoving. Its neat clothes had no creases, and its two hands rested on its knees at the same height. Sion looked at those hands; they were transparent to the fingertips.
When the light caught them, they showed. The whole body did.

The light Aka had seen was there, behind the figure, not moving, not blinking. Each time the robots in the hall passed through the middle, their heads turned half a knuckle toward it and came back.

"That's it."

Sion said.

"What Aka saw."

No one answered.

One aristocrat robot bowed its head, not deeply, at the same angle.

"Your Excellency the President. Regarding the recurring misalignment at the middle level and below, we request attribution judgment and approval for a general redeployment."

Silence.

In the short silence, Sern began to count.

One.

"Approved."

It ended at one, and Sern's finger stopped.

"Lower-level response interval: shorten by 0.12. Middle-level blocking priorities: rearrange. Upper-level prediction axis: open in advance. Shift the standby series of every android collective to third-stage alignment."

The voice that came down was polite, every sentence the same length. No breath. No place to breathe.

The last android's optical eye wavered.
"Every android collective."
It was at those words. His body went half a step forward, under the grate, into the pattern of light.

He stopped when Han Jiwoo grabbed his arm hard; his foot was at the edge of the light pattern. Han Jiwoo pulled, and the last android came back, but his optical eye was still on what was above.

"Not you."

Han Jiwoo spoke very low.

"Those words aren't yours."

He did not answer, only lowered his optical eye to her hand, the hand holding his arm. She did not let go of that arm.

Sern looked at Ater and whispered.

"The answer came before the question finished."

"Yes."

Ater said.

"There is no place where it counts. Where it thinks."

"Then what is it?"

Sion asked.

Ater did not answer right away; he looked at the figure above, at the hands on its knees.

"At home, I saw documents."

He said.

"Documents whose sentences were perfect, with no one to answer for them. Only a place for a signature. That voice sounds like those."

"Documents talk?"

"They do not."

Ater said.

"That is why it is strange."

One aristocrat robot raised its head very slightly, half a knuckle more than the others.

"The entire android collective? The current upper-level corrections alone are sufficient to contain the lower-level errors."

The figure's head turned precisely toward that robot.

"Containability and sustainability are not identical. Only an order that can be sustained is order."

There was no gap, so Sern could not fold a finger.

Another aristocrat robot spoke slowly.

"The lower-level intruders are not a simple breakthrough. They are reading responses, learning corrections, and exploiting the differences between tiers."

"That is recognized."

At once.

"Therefore the android collectives of all tiers are converted immediately. The aristocrat series prioritizes the preservation of upper-level order. Lower-level losses are permitted."

One long finger of the aristocrat robot standing nearest trembled.
Half a knuckle.
It stopped.
Sion saw it, and so did Ater.

"Lower-level losses permitted…"

The unit repeated it, very low.

Another unit covered it at once.

"Upper-level order preservation takes priority. Understood."

The finger did not tremble again; it settled against the side of its knee.

"Where's the lower level?"

Kael whispered.

"Where we came up from."

Seorin said.

"And below that."

Han Jiwoo looked at Seorin. Below. The hidden level. The children. She gripped the last android's arm harder.

Deep structural lines beneath the hall floor lit up all at once, the light through the grate changed, and the pattern on the floor brightened in front of the nine's feet.

Then sound came up from below, from far off: not one thing but, on every level, countless pieces of metal moving at once. While the floor hummed up through the soles of Sion's feet, Sern's record panel trembled, and the two old machines looked at each other, their optical eyes meeting once.

"It's the androids."

Han Jiwoo said.

"All of them."

"They are waking all of them."

Sern said.

"Third-stage alignment. It means standby is over."

One aristocrat robot bowed its head again.

"Commencing preparation for the final offensive."

"Commence. All tiers must converge into a single order."

The hall stayed as it was; the light did not slip, the floor did not shake. While the report lines flowed on, the aristocrat robots dispersed with the same stride, each to its place. The one whose finger had trembled too.

The figure stayed where it was, and neither the hands on its knees nor the light behind it moved.

After a long while, Sion took his eyes off the grate and put his back to the wall.

"Nobody walks around up there."

He said.

"There's not a single mark on the floor."

"Robots don't leave marks."

Kael said.

"Not people either. There are no human marks. As if there had never been any."

No one answered.

Seorin looked at the grate once, then looked ahead down the corridor, at the widened corridor and the way up.

"You heard."

She said.

"All of them are coming."

"Up?"

Sion asked.

"Up. If we go down, they'll all be standing on the way to the kids."

Only then did Han Jiwoo let go of the last android's arm. He looked up at the light above the grate, then down at the floor where the sound was coming up from, and his optical eye blinked, then came back.

"You okay?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

He looked at her, looked ahead, and walked. His shoulder axis made a sound, but the hall above the grate did not hear it. Up there, no one looked down.

As he walked, Sern looked at the record panel, with its three lines drawn close together and the fingers he had not once managed to fold. He wrote beside them.
No gap.

One of the old machines put a hand to its own chest plate once, then took it away. The other saw it but said nothing, and the two walked side by side at the very back.
