# Episode 99. The Moment Before Something Finally Breaks

Next to the short mark on the board, Sion scratched a fifth notch.

That made an hour.

This time he hadn't let it slip. Marked on the board, it didn't slip. Whether that was good or bad, he didn't know.

Sion ran to the corridor.

On the way he passed the space the fifth line had left. A space twenty people had gone from didn't show. A hundred and eight were still there.

Six runs.

Sion turned the number over as he ran. Six runs and all hundred and eight would be out. For that, the corridor had to hold six more times.

He looked as he went. The right-hand corridor wasn't what it had been.

Sern was pressed up above the fallen black thing. Ater was watching the angle below it. Where the two of them stood was where Harun had stood alone earlier.

Harun was three steps farther in.

It was a narrower place. One person wide. The width Sern had measured with his hands before Kael went.

Stand there and nobody behind you can help. It was one wide, so only one person fit. Sion understood only now that the same held for the side doing the blocking.

"Harun."

"You came?"

Sion's feet stopped at that answer.

Until now, whenever Sion came, this man had asked what it was without turning around. He had never once said *you came*.

"It's been an hour."

"Untie it."

"What?"

"I said untie it."

Sion's hands wouldn't rise. Both times before, Harun had said not yet. Both times it had taken a fight to get it untied.

"Why untie it right away this time?"

"Hurry."

Sion put his hands on the knot.

It came loose more easily than before. The cloth was wet, and it slid. It wouldn't go like that when he tied it; it only went easily coming undone.

"Keep holding on."

"To what?"

"The wreckage. You can't let go of it, right?"

Harun took hold of the wreckage again with his right hand. He held out only his left arm toward Sion. The angle was awkward. It didn't turn at the elbow; he turned the whole shoulder.

It came undone.

No blood came.

Sion looked at the place. Before, it had run down to the back of his hand. This time it only seeped slowly from inside the wound.

"I think it stopped."

Harun said nothing.

"It stopped, right?"

"Ater,"
Harun called.

Ater answered without taking his eyes off the angle.

"I heard."

"Tell him."

Ater waited a moment, then spoke.

"It bleeds less when it is healing. It also bleeds less when it is going bad."

"And now?"
Sion asked.

"Now it is the second."

It took Sion half a beat to understand that.

Once he did, he looked at Harun's hand.

It was dark under the nails. The fingers stayed half curled and wouldn't straighten. Sion pressed the back of the hand with his finger. The pressed spot went white and didn't come back.

Harun made no sound the whole time it was pressed.

"Can you feel that?"

"Feel what?"

Sion didn't ask again.

"Why'd you want it untied right away this time?"

Harun answered with his eyes still ahead.

"So you'd see."

"See what?"

"This."

Sion looked at the hand again. Three fingers that stayed curled and wouldn't straighten, and the back of a hand where the pressed mark didn't come back.

"And if I see it?"

"Then you don't have to come anymore."

"You told me to come."

"Now you don't have to."

Sion knew what that meant. Knowing it made him angry.

"Then what about the arm?"

"The arm's already settled."

"How long has it been?"

"An hour."

"Not that. From the beginning."

Only then did Harun look at Sion.

"How long?"

Sion had an answer ready. He had kept it ready all the way here. But once he was asked, it wouldn't come.

"…I don't know."

"You don't know?"

"I let it slip once."

Saying it made it worse. *Slipped* was a lighter word than *lost*, and what had been lost wasn't his.

"How much did you let slip?"

"I don't know that either."

Harun heard the answer and turned his head back toward the corridor.

"It's fine."

"It's not fine."

"I said it's fine."

"If I knew how much it was…"

"What would you do if you knew?"

"I told you not to count,"
Harun said.

"That was about how many more runs."

"Same thing."

Only then did Sion see why it was the same thing.

Whether you counted how many more times you could stand or how many hours the arm had left, once you counted, you knew when the last one was. This man had said that knowing it changes your hands.

"Tie it again."

Sion wound the cloth again. Two turns, then the knot. This time it went on the first try.

"Done."

"An hour."

"Again?"

"Again."

At that, Sion looked toward the board. He couldn't see the board from here.

"I won't let it slip this time."

"It's fine if you do."

"What?"

"If you don't let it slip, you'll know all of it."

"I'm not allowed to know?"

Harun waited a moment before he answered.

"If you know, you have to carry it out."

Sion didn't know what that meant.

He didn't know, and still his back went cold.

"Out where?"

"On the ship."

Sion stood there and couldn't say anything.

Until now, when this man talked about the ship, he hadn't split the ones leaving from the ones staying. Just now he had. And he had put Sion on the side that leaves.

"I'm…"

"Go."

"I haven't finished…"

"Watch the line."

Sion didn't move.

Harun said nothing more either. Instead he shifted his grip on the wreckage once with his right hand. His left arm stayed pressed against his body.

Sion stood beside him for a moment.

Above, Sern cut something. There was a short, high sound. It wasn't a sound he had made earlier, when he was holding on two beats. Now he was cutting on one.

"Sern, are you all right?"

"I am not,"
Sern answered.

That was all. The man who always said he was fine when asked had said he wasn't and moved on. He had no breath left for more of an answer.

Ater spoke, short.

"Up."

Sern looked up.

"Now."

Sern went in.

"Back."

Sern pulled out.

Three words. Sion remembered that this was a man who used to speak in sentences. Now only one word at a time came out.

Only then did Sion know it in his body: all three of them here had very little left.

"Ater."

"Yes."

"Why do they keep hitting up there?"

For the first time, Ater took his eyes off the angle.

"I do not know."

"How can you not know?"

"Until now, they have never struck up there."

Then he went back to the angle.

Sion held on to that answer. Something snagged, but he didn't know what.

"Still here,"
Harun said.

"Yes."

"What are you going to do here?"

"I don't know."

"Then go do what you do know."

Sion turned away.

Turning, he looked back once more.

The width Harun stood in really was one person. On the left was the collapsed wall, on the right the black body. Between them stood one person, and that person's left arm was tied right now, and below the tie the blood barely went.

If that person weren't standing there, they would come in through that width.

Sion went two steps.

Then a sound came from above.

It wasn't the sound of metal snapping. It was a low sound that dragged on and on, something that had been pressed down a long time twisting all the way until at last it couldn't hold and burst.

Sion had never heard that sound before.

For the first time, Ater's face went hard.

"Harun!"

Sern twisted around.

Sion looked up.

The metal screen that had spanned the top of the corridor was folding from the middle.

Not slowly. It folded all at once, and both ends tore out of the walls. Thick pieces ripped out along with them from where they had been anchored, and the wreckage piled beneath came pouring down whole.

Not dust. Chunks.

Below it was the width where Harun stood.

Harun didn't look up. He was looking ahead. Because ahead, a black thing was coming in, timed to that exact moment.

They had been waiting for this. This was what all the hitting up there had been for.

Sion ran.

As he ran, he saw several things at once.

Sern threw himself down from above. Ater raised his gun and lowered it. There was no angle to shoot. The falling chunk was already half a hand's span above Harun's head.

Three steps.

Three steps seemed close enough to reach, but three steps wasn't enough.

As he ran, he felt something in his hand.

The cloth he had untied earlier. The end left over after he retied it. He had kept it in his fist the whole time and never let go.

Sion ran, unable even to let go of it.
