# Episode 204. Felt from Below

The change did not come to any one person first.

In the middle of a work sector on the hidden level, Luhai stopped short, set down the debris he had been holding, and raised his head.

Sound.

It was a sound that was missing, the thing that had always been laid underneath: the breath of the restoration axes turning, the tremor of sorting lines flicking on and off, the beat of something, somewhere, being raised upward. What had been there even when you could not hear it was now broken in patches. Not completely, but there were holes in it.

He looked up at the ceiling.

"…Something's different."

Beside him, the first recovery hand stopped clearing debris and raised its optical eyes. While the second braced its straightened leg against the wall and stood, the wall-side machine's optical eye came up too, slowly.

What came to Aka, who raised her head half a beat behind Luhai, was not sound. When she drew in a breath there was the smell of collapsed places and the smell of dead light, and something else mixed in between them, so she drew in one more breath.

"Up there… something ended."

Luhai turned to look at her.

"You sure?"

Aka did not answer right away; she closed her eyes and opened them.

"Not all the way. But… the thing that was holding on broke. Something that kept things tied for a really long time."

The first and the second lifted their heads at the same moment, and the wall-side machine lifted its own. The three of them looked at the same place, at the ceiling and what lay above it.

"You feel it too?"

Luhai asked.

"The lines have been cut."

The first said.

"From the top down. One at a time."

The second looked at its straightened leg, at its own knee.

"We should go, then."

It said.

Luhai clicked his tongue.

"Then we go up."

No one objected. The Hazran people got to their feet, the man without a younger brother, the tired-faced woman and the woman who came with two children, and the old Hazran man picked up his plate. When the child with the screwdriver, the child who looks down and the little child stood beside the adults, Aka stood next to the three of them.

They went upward, pushing debris aside, lifting away collapsed panels, squeezing past blocked passages. The first hooked its arm through the arm of the second, which was the slowest, and the wall-side machine pushed from behind, and the second did not shake them off.

No one knew who was in front; they all climbed together.

Aka did not go all the way to the front. In the middle, between the stopped machines and the people, she felt at the air above.

One sector.

They passed under a dark sorting panel with no light on it.

One sector.

A passage that, before, would have shut stood stalled halfway open. Luhai stood in front of it and put his hand in, but it did not close.

"It's real."

He said it under his breath, and it was not a happy voice. He pulled his hand out and put it in once more, and still it did not close.

The second looked at that passage.

"I have never seen one like that."

It said.

"One that stays open."

One more sector up, androids came into view.

Three.

They stood in front of a dark wall, but no one raised a weapon. Luhai's hand went to his iron bar, stopped, and came down.

The three were not standing at the same angle.
One had a hand to its face.
One was touching the spot where the wall's light had died.
The other was looking at the unit beside it.
On their face surfaces were clumsy lines, not fully drawn, traces of light.

A thin breath came from where the children stood. The Hazran children watched without a word, and the little child was the first to speak, very small.

"A face…"

Everyone looked toward the androids.

The child took a step forward, and no hand held them back. Aka saw the hand of the woman who came with two children go up and come down. With dust from a crack in the wall still on their fingers, the child stopped in front of the android and looked up at its face.

"…Can I draw some more?"

The android did not answer.

A beat.

It did not lower its head. It did not turn away. It stayed where it was.

The child's eyes went wide. The child raised a hand and, in dust, drew one small curve over the wavering line of light.
Where a mouth would be.

The child holding the screwdriver came from the other side, put the screwdriver in a pocket, dipped a fingertip in dust, and drew the line at the edge of an eye out a little longer.
The android trembled, faintly.
It did not run. It let them.

The little child did not back away and stayed beside the android.

Luhai saw it and could not say anything, and then he let out a rough breath.

"Hah… After all this time, they're really finding their faces now."

It was not a sneer. He moved the iron bar to his other hand.

Beside him, watching new lines being added, Aka murmured low.

"Only now, coming back to themselves."

It was not something said to the android. Luhai looked at her but did not ask.

A little further up came footsteps, several of them, coming down in different beats. Sion came around the corner, and Seorin, Sern, Ater, Han Jiwoo, Kael and the last android followed him, and behind them came androids that had newly opened their eyes.

Luhai stopped dead in the middle.
He looked at Sion. At Seorin. At Sern and Ater. At Han Jiwoo and Kael. At the last android. At the androids behind them.
He counted.

Seven.

He counted again.

Seven.

"The machines."

He asked.

The first looked past them. So did the second, and the wall-side machine.
No one else came out.

Sion did not answer; he slowly shook his head.

"Both?"

The first asked.

"Both. Up there. Together. Stopping those."

The first looked at the androids that were gaining faces, then at Sion again, but it said nothing.

While the first's optical eyes went down and the second braced itself against the wall, the wall-side machine did not move, and kept its optical eye on the last android.

Luhai's mouth opened and closed. This was all that came out.

"…You really finished it up there."

Sion nodded, slowly, once.

Luhai could not ask anything more, and he set the iron bar down on the floor.

After that, hands moved first. While people cleared debris, the first and the second held up a collapsed structure, and the wall-side machine stood in front of a dead line. When the androids stood in front of the sorting panels, Han Jiwoo stood beside them. Sern counted, Ater called out numbers, and Kael tore things out. His ankle made a sound, but he did not stop.

While the adults lifted debris, the children got busy and went around among the androids.
Eyes, mouths.
One child put a dot of dust on a cheek.

"This is better."
"It looks like it's smiling."
"This one looks too sad."

The adults went quiet. The man without a younger brother stopped, debris in his arms. It was children's voices. He heard the sound again.

The androids responded.
One unit tilted its head each time a child's hand touched it.
One held its drawn face up to the wall to see it.
One raised its hand for the first time. It stopped near a child's wrist. It did not touch. It did not pull away either.
The child looked at that hand, then at their own wrist, and laughed.

Luhai heard the laugh but did not turn around. He picked up a panel and looked for somewhere to put it; Aka pointed to the spot beside it, and Luhai put it there.

Sion watched the scene for a while. His hand held no blade. With that hand he picked up a piece of debris.

The last android stood a little way off. The child who had first set lines on his face came over, hesitated, then stood in front of him and looked up.

"Do you… already have a name?"

Han Jiwoo, Aka, Luhai: a few people looked that way.

The last android went still and did not answer, but the child waited.
He shook his head slowly. It was the first time he had shaken his head.
The child nodded and stood beside him, and did not ask his name, having asked once.

Aka watched the two of them and blinked slowly. She did not go over; she kept watching.

Luhai hoisted a collapsed panel.

"Don't just stand there gaping. If you made it out alive, put your hands to work first."

It was rough, but no one took it as rough. More hands moved.

People.
Machines.
Androids.
Children.

Each in a different way. They lifted the same panel.
Han Jiwoo on one side, an android on the other.
It came up. A beat out of step.
They did not drop it.

The old Hazran man stood holding his own plate, sixteen lines on the front and one line scratched on the back. He looked at the back, and at the androids that were gaining faces. He did not scratch a line; he held the plate to his chest.
