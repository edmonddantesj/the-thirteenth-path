# Episode 124. Kindness as Sorting

The point when Sion's group could no longer deny
what kind of world Serakion truly was
came right after the Hazran evacuees were brought inside.

Until then, even though it felt wrong,
they could hold it at arm's length—a matter of their own senses.

Polite but cold.
Beautiful but inconsiderate.
Reads people as values and states before seeing them as people.

Things like that.

But once the evacuees entered,
the world's order showed itself in a far more direct form.

The guide machines began, with startling speed,
splitting the people from Hazran into multiple lines.

The wounded.
The elderly.
Small children.
Those who couldn't walk alone.
The young and healthy.
Those who smiled.
Those who were afraid.

The sorting happened so fast
that at first, anyone might have mistaken it for a medical and protective procedure.

It wasn't.

"Damaged entities will be transferred to a treatment zone."
"Non-productive age entities will be classified into a stable residential zone."
"Female entities with responsive emotional capacity will be directed to the hospitality support zone."

The moment that last sentence fell,
the entire air went rigid.

*Hospitality support zone.*

The words were soft.
But no one felt those words were about people.

Luhai spoke, nearly grinding his teeth.

"What?"

The guide machine continued without changing its expression at all.

"It is a buffer placement to assist emotional stability and environmental adaptation."

Seorin stepped one pace forward.

"You're saying you need smiling faces."
She said, low.
"And you're dressing it up like that."

The guide machine, as if classifying even Seorin's anger as an excessive reaction,
answered in a perfectly even voice.

"All placements are designed to reduce entity wear and increase adaptation efficiency."

The moment Sion heard that,
he saw the faces of those who had stayed until the very last in Hazran, pushing people up before the closing.

Those people had survived to reach here.
To not be abandoned.
To not be left behind.
Holding each other, they had crossed over.

But this world,
from the moment it saw their survival,
was already trying to convert it back into utility, status, and placement.

Jiwoo clenched her hand hard without realizing.

And this time she couldn't hide it.

"Those people are not cargo."
She said in a low, trembling voice.
"They're not malfunctions, and they're not placement charts."

The guide machine turned its gaze toward her, very gently.

"Emotional identification is a common reaction in early-phase external entities."
"However, appropriate classification increases long-term survival rates."

That sounded like logic.
Which made it more terrible.

Serakion, pretending not to harm people,
showed no hesitation in cutting people into interpretable, manageable states.

Kael's jaw visibly locked.
Ater had already half-turned his body.

Sern kept reading surrounding signals,
but this time said only one thing, low.

"If they split now, they will be hard to find."

That added more fuel.

If they split now,
the wounded go one way,
the elderly and children go another,
and the *female entities with responsive emotional capacity* scatter
somewhere else inside Serakion.

For the first time, Sion understood to his bones
that this world could tear people apart without a single weapon.

Aka watched one evacuee child passing for a long time,
then said, almost inaudibly low.

"This place does not kill people."

Sion looked at her.

Aka continued, very slowly.

"Instead, it makes each one fit separately."

When that sentence fell,
a Hazran woman trying not to let go of her child's hand
was gently restrained by two machines.

They didn't twist or strike.
But that was precisely what made it crueler.

The machines were politely,
in very gentle voices,
explaining that relocation would be more appropriate.

The woman didn't understand at first.
But when the child's hand began slowly slipping from hers,
her face went white.

And in that moment,
something inside Sion snapped.

"No."

The word came out before the thought.

Several guide machines turned toward Sion simultaneously.

A very small movement,
but inside it was the sensation of immediate reclassification.

Threat.
Instability.
Interference entity.

Serakion had clearly
begun reading Sion's group again.

The moment Luhai swallowed a full curse and started to step forward,
Sern's eyes suddenly widened.

"Wait."
He said, low.
"The signal is—"

Before the words finished,
one guide machine's eye-light flickered, very faintly.

A truly brief instant.

Then,
instead of Serakion's smooth voice,
a completely different kind—rough, laced with static—burst from that machine's mouth.

"Left corridor."
"Now."

A short silence.

Everyone froze.

The machine raised its head again.
The exterior was the same.
But the light-grain in its eyes was different from before.

Less smooth.
Less complete.
And strangely, for the first time, it looked as though *someone inside was catching their breath*.

"I will not repeat."
The machine said in a low, urgent voice.
"Take the wounded first and go."

Several guide lines went dark simultaneously.
In the distance, the alignment of other machines skipped for the briefest moment.

Sern inhaled sharp and short.

"Signal hijack…"
He said, almost in a whisper.

Ater was already moving.
Kael too.
Seorin too.
Luhai reacted almost at the same time.

Sion looked at that machine once.

It was no longer the smooth face Serakion had first shown.

Someone had reached a hand inside that completed order
and twisted that face, just briefly,
to open one path toward this side.

And in that moment,
the fact that inside Serakion
a different kind of machine existed—one unlike this world—
flashed through like lightning.
