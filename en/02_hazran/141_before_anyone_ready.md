# Episode 141. Before Anyone Was Ready

The next move was not decided at the end of a meeting.

It started on the side that was falling apart first.

The human they had brought back spoke properly for the first time only after the day had fully turned.

While Han Jiwoo stayed at the casualty's side all night, the two inner machines took turns watching the breathing and the bleeding. The second recovery hand leaned against one wall and never left; with the leg Han Jiwoo had fixed twice stretched out in front of it, it did not stand, only sat and watched.

The yellow lamp went off and came back on three times, and Sern wrote down the count while the machine that had opened the way looked at the record panel beside him.

"The signals have increased."

The machine said.

"Which direction?"

"Up."

Sern wrote that on his record panel. Up.

The two inner machines switched places; one machine stood and the other sat. When the sitting machine put its hand on the casualty's chest, the standing machine looked at where that hand was, and it was right. The standing machine left, but Han Jiwoo never gave up her place.

For a long while the casualty's eyes wouldn't properly open.

As dawn grew thinly light, a sound came out between the ragged breaths.

At first no one could make it out, and not the second time either. Only the third time did Han Jiwoo lift her head.

"Again."

Her voice was low.

The casualty's eyes stayed shut; only the lips moved.

"…Not… down…"

The breathing broke off once. Han Jiwoo bent lower and put her ear close to the mouth, and the second recovery hand pushed off the wall and dragged its leg closer.

The casualty spoke again.

"Not… down… Up… moving them…"

A short silence.

Han Jiwoo asked first.

"Who?"

While the casualty took a long time gathering breath, Han Jiwoo put her hand on the chest, and the left side followed half a beat late.

The casualty spoke in a sound that nearly broke off.

"The kids… the ones with good hands… first…"

The first recovery hand, near the threshold, lifted its head.

The light in its optical eyes changed: the steady glow jumped once, then came back.

Sern spoke low, almost at the same moment.

"That was the reason the deployment changed."

One of the old machines asked.

"Be precise."

On the record panel Sern opened was the one line he had drawn yesterday, the line going up.

"Did you not say it had changed from a guarding deployment to a herding one? If that is not simple containment but a reselection transfer toward the upper levels, the explanation fits."

He put one more dot near the top of the line.

The woman who came with two children heard that and held the child on her knees. When the child stirred awake, she held the child closer still, and Han Jiwoo saw it.

The woman asked Han Jiwoo, low.

"What are kids with good hands?"

Han Jiwoo couldn't answer right away.

"…I don't know."

"My kid has good hands. Takes after their dad."

The arms around the child did not loosen at all.

Han Jiwoo looked at the child's hand, asleep around a screwdriver. Thumb on top, the rest below.

"I know. I taught them."

The woman said nothing more and covered the child's hand with her own.

The casualty let out a few more words, but they didn't make a sentence. Only the sound "up" came, twice more, and after that there was only breathing.

Han Jiwoo spoke without taking her hand off the chest.

"Enough. Sleep."

The casualty's eyes closed, the closed eyes closed once more, and the breaths grew longer. Han Jiwoo took her hand away and then looked at that hand. The casualty's blood had dried on it, but she didn't wipe it off.

The first recovery hand turned toward the gap.

The old machine called out, low.

"Where are you going?"

The machine answered without stopping.

"I have to check before it is too late."

"The leg."

The old machine pointed at the second.

"I will go alone."

"The hands that went alone did not come back."

The first stopped where it was, but it did not look back.

The old machine spoke very low.

"We do not yet know whether this is a matter that ends with checking."

Only then did the first recovery hand look back.

"If even the checking comes too late, that is the end."

The two faced each other between the gap and the pillar. No one passed between them.

The second tried to drag its leg and stand.

"I can just go with it."

The second said.

Han Jiwoo answered without looking.

"On that leg?"

"Dragging or not, I will go."

The second looked down at its own leg and went on.

"I know the way. Even if the leg does not."

Han Jiwoo's hand stopped above the casualty's chest, then moved again a beat later.

"…Then I stick by your side."

The second looked at her.

"Why?"

"If it drags, someone has to fix it."

While the second stayed on its feet, Kael squatted down beside it.

"Where are the stairs?"

"Past the second fork."

Kael nodded, but he didn't get up.

Sion cut in.

"What needs checking?"

Every eye came to him, and the first recovery hand, its eyes less strange than yesterday, looked at him very briefly.

"The relocation routes below the upper levels."

Its voice was low.

"Whether we can get the children, and the humans who work with their hands, out first."

Luhai swallowed a curse.

"They're really doing it."

Aka grew quieter still, and only after looking at the floor once did she speak, low.

"So they pick them prettily."

The old Hazran man heard that, and his hand clenched on his plate.

The woman who came with two children had been looking at her child's small hand; she tucked that hand inside her clothes and hid it, and Han Jiwoo saw that.

When she got up from her place, hers was a face that had stayed up all night. Her eyes were red, but the look in them was cold.

"I'm going too."

She said it short.

The old machine objected at once.

"You must stay inside."

Han Jiwoo looked straight at it.

"They're moving the kids with good hands up first, aren't they?"

She kept her voice low.

"Then I should be the one to see which hands they pick over there."

The old machine didn't answer; it looked at Han Jiwoo's hands. The blood had dried on the hands that had pressed down on the casualty all night.

"And that one."

The old machine pointed at the casualty.

"The inside hands can watch. The breathing's back now."

Han Jiwoo looked at the two inner machines, and both nodded. One machine came and sat in Han Jiwoo's place and put its hand on the left side of the chest. Han Jiwoo looked at where that hand was, and it was right; only then did she step back.

The first recovery hand looked Han Jiwoo over slowly, from top to bottom. Its gaze stopped at her oil-stained hands.

Han Jiwoo held those eyes to the end, and the first turned its head away first.

Sern looked at the top dot on his record panel.

"When do we go out?"

"Before sunrise."

"There is no sun here."

"Before the lamp goes off."

Sern looked at the yellow lamp and wrote that down.

Ater asked the first.

"Where does the one line going up lead?"

"To where they send them up."

"People?"

"People."

Ater asked nothing more and looked at that line on Sern's record panel. There was one dot at the end of the line, and above it, nothing.

Kael asked the second.

"Which step was it where you lost them?"

"Third from the top."

"Why?"

"Only that one is a different height."

Kael nodded. Third. He counted it down on his fingers and did not straighten the folded fingers again.

Luhai came over from where the children were awake.

"The kids heard."

He said it low.

"Heard what?"

"About sending people up."

Han Jiwoo looked toward the children: one child was looking at their own hand, the one holding a screwdriver. Covering it with the other hand, the child looked at Han Jiwoo, and Han Jiwoo nodded.

Seorin came from the direction of the dark exit and took in the situation at a glance: the one lying down, the ones standing, and the machine turned away.

"You're going?"

"Going."

Han Jiwoo answered.

Seorin looked at the first recovery hand and asked.

"How many?"

"The fewer the better."

"How many?"

"Five. Counting the second."

Seorin only nodded; she didn't ask who the four were.

Sion laid his hand over his pocket. Two lines: when it is time to leave, and knowing he's not going to. He lowered his hand.

The casualty breathed, and the left side followed half a beat late. Han Jiwoo looked once more at the inner machine's hand resting there, then turned away.

The yellow lamp went out. It didn't come back on.

A beat.

It came on.

"Not yet."

Sern said.

No one asked.
