# Episode 183. One of Theirs

Preparations to leave moved fast.

No one thought there was time to spare,
and no one believed they could hold on to this short rest any longer.

Serakion would surely come again.
It would not let this failure stand.
And next time,
it would count in even the fact that an android with a will was left somewhere in this old layer
before it moved.

Everyone knew that.

So the routes were checked again,
Seorin went back over each team's role, short and sharp,
Ater quickly sorted the newly blocked paths from the gaps still usable,
and Sern weighed damage, time and the number of people who could still move
to rebuild an execution sequence tighter than before.

But even in the middle of all that,
eyes kept drifting back toward the last android.

He was still in the quiet section.

Han Jiwoo checked the last of the fixtures,
one of the old machines set newly brought parts down beside him,
and the children near him were trying hard not to be too loud.

The scene looked strangely peaceful.

And that was exactly why
Sion felt all the more sharply that this time would soon be over.

The more they wanted to stay together,
the less they could hold on for long.

One of the children crouched beside the last android
and said in a very small voice,

"Can't you stay now?"

No one could answer right away.

The question hit the exact spot everyone had been avoiding.

Why did he have to be taken?
Why couldn't they stay together?
Why did a being who had barely survived have to be moved toward danger again?

For a long while Han Jiwoo couldn't bring herself to speak.

More than anyone,
she did not want to send this being back to the battlefield now.

Only moments ago the word "hurts" had come to her far too naturally,
and she knew that now, even while repairing him, she moved her hands so as not to startle him.

Even so,
she couldn't avoid what had to be said.

"If he stays here…"
she said, very low.
"Next time they'll come to you first."

The child blinked.
It wasn't the face of someone who fully understood.

But what kind of sadness those words held,
even a very small child seemed able to tell.

Han Jiwoo glanced at the last android,
then looked back at the child.

"They'll come looking for him."
"Then this place gets dangerous too."

It could have sounded too cold,
but strangely, no one heard it that way.

It didn't sound like pushing someone away,
but like having to cut off part of your own side in order to protect it.

The child's eyes went red almost at once.

"But…"
the child managed, barely.
"It's nice when we're together."

At that,
a few people in the room turned their heads away altogether.

It was so true
that it hurt more.

In that moment Sion, too,
thought that this was how parting always came.

Being together is good.
So you want to stay together.
But precisely because of that,
you can't let danger come to the place where you are together.

The last android seemed to be listening.

His optical eye was still dim,
and his body still hadn't fully pulled itself together.

But when the child, holding back tears, looked at him,
he too moved his gaze that way, very slowly.

And for a long time,
a really long time, he stayed like that.

What passed within that stillness,
no one could say exactly.

But Sion, strangely,
couldn't shake the feeling that this being was choosing his own place right now.

Whether he wanted to stay here.
Or whether he had to leave.

It might not yet be something you could call thought,
but what he could not leave behind him,
he had already learned far too well.

Seorin came over quietly.

She crouched a little, bringing herself down to the child's height,
and said, briefly,

"I know it's good being together."
"That's why we're taking him."

The words were startlingly exact.

Not leaving him behind because it's good,
but
taking him because it's good.

So danger won't come back here.
So no target is left beside what has to be protected.
And at the same time,
because that being could no longer stand outside the next fight.

The children couldn't hold back their tears.

But strangely,
they didn't cling, or throw themselves on the floor, or cry that they wouldn't let him go.

That was probably
less because they understood
than because they were children who had already watched someone be lost once.

That holding on now might not be love,
but might be tying danger to their side again—
theirs were faces that sensed it, however dimly.

The child who had been sticking close to the last android all along
touched the faint line of his mouth one last time with a fingertip, as if pressing it on again.

"Come back."

It sounded like a request,
like an order,
like a prayer.

The last android's optical eye trembled, very faintly.

Then, a moment later,
he moved on his own.

Before Han Jiwoo could reach out to support him,
he braced a hand against the wall and slowly stood.

It wasn't a steady stance.
His balance was shaky, as if his knees might buckle again any second.

But even so,
he looked once toward the children,
then turned toward Sion's party and Seorin.

In that moment,
everyone in the room felt the same thing.

He was not being dragged.

He couldn't speak yet,
and maybe he didn't understand all of it yet.
But this movement, at least,
was surely close to something he had chosen of his own will.

Wanting to stay with them,
and so leaving with them.

That choice, which looked like a contradiction,
felt, if anything, exactly right.

Watching him, Han Jiwoo finally closed her eyes and opened them again,
and murmured, very low,

"All right."

Who it was meant for,
she herself probably didn't know.

But Sion thought he knew.

It was for that being,
for the children,
and for the whole next battle about to begin.

Because he had now become one of theirs,
in the end he could not stay only at their side.
