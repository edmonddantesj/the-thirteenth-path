# Episode 214. Folding Inward

Before dawn, the air inside the warehouse began to move again.

No one made a loud sound, yet the air changed—just from people rising, re-wrapping cloth, checking weapons, and shortening the length of their breaths.

Now it was the silence of entering, not waiting.

Sern stood as though he had been the first to finish preparing.

His face was not much different from last night, but under the faint light of early dawn, that quiet looked drier and harder.

He slowly traced his fingertip across a rough map spread on the inner wall.

Not a precise blueprint. Closer to a layered sketch—lines memorized long ago, routes updated in between, and detours shared only among the surviving.

Seorin was the first to approach.

"The way in?"

Sern spoke without stopping his hand.

"Three branches. But only two are actually usable."

Kael joined immediately.

"Why drop one."

"Possibility it has been read."

Ater asked low.

"An old line?"

"Yes."

Sern answered plainly.

"It survived too long. That is what made it a line that had to be abandoned first."

Sion heard that and dimly felt: a path inside the Empire was not simply a known route. It was a question that included how long it had been used, who had died on it, and who had not yet given it up.

Sern pressed a point on the map.

"Here is the perimeter supply line. Here is the maintenance line. And this side gets grazed by a retrieval line every now and then."

Luhai frowned.

"What is a retrieval line. Does it pick up people?"

Sern did not raise his gaze.

"Sometimes people. Sometimes what used to be people."

Luhai closed his mouth immediately.

Aka stepped closer to Sern's fingertip.

"Where we are going—is it cold?"

Sern wore the face of someone who understood the question immediately.

"Not at first. But further in, it will be."

Aka asked nothing more. Instead she kept watching the point where Sern's finger had stopped on the map.

Ater watched for a moment, then asked low.

"To reach Elia—how far should we ideally go undetected?"

Sern looked at Ater for the first time.

"Ideally, to the end."

"An impossible answer."

"Yes."

Sern said it as though it were nothing.

"That is why we choose the less bad option."

No good path. No right answer.

Getting read a little later. Getting a little deeper in. Getting someone out a little more intact. The paths Sern chose always seemed to be that kind.

Seorin crossed her arms and sorted.

"So the core is three things. How far we hide the ship. Who goes inside. And where we exit after bringing Elia out."

Kael added.

"The way out's going to be the first thing to collapse."

"Yes." Sern answered at once. "Getting in is easier than bringing someone out."

Sion looked down at the lines on the map.

There were many. Of all those lines, the ones they could actually walk were almost none.

On the surface, too many paths and too well-organized order. Yet when the moment truly came, almost no path remained to slip one person out through.

Ater said it low.

"I will take the forward position."

Sion looked at him.

Ater already wore the face of someone who had reached a conclusion.

"Reading the inner order—I will be faster."

Sern did not argue right away.

But his silence was closer to calculation than agreement.

"That is correct. But if you are in front, the moment you are read comes faster too."

Ater did not deny that either.

Sion looked between the two.

Both were right.

Ater could read the Empire's order. The Empire could read Ater just as well.

That double edge felt like the most dangerous part of this infiltration.

Then Jiwoo spoke for the first time.

"What about Serakion."

Every gaze went to the back simultaneously.

Serakion, standing in a dark corner of the warehouse, was wordless as always.

But no one saw that silence as empty anymore.

Sern glanced that way briefly, then spoke.

"He will stand out."

Luhai muttered small.

"He stands out even doing nothing."

Sern continued.

"But he can be misread. As a noble's private soldier, or special guard force."

Seorin raised her head.

"Then rather than removing him entirely, forcing him into the hierarchy so he reads as something is better."

"Yes."

Kael looked at Serakion once more and said it short.

"Then the core splits into two. The team going inside, and the team holding the lines from outside."

Luhai asked immediately.

"Where am I?"

Seorin said without acknowledgment.

"The loud side."

"That is harsh."

Aka added right away.

"It is accurate."

"You people are really too much."

Laughter was not entirely absent.

But it could not stay long either.

Brief as the sound of catching a breath before a door about to close.

Sion asked quietly.

"The side where Elia is—can Aka keep tracking?"

Aka closed her eyes briefly and opened them.

"I do not know about continuously. But it should get clearer the closer we get."

"And the other person?"

Aka could not answer right away.

"That side is strange. Not far, but stays quiet. Not hiding, but barely wavers."

Sern heard that and closed his eyes for just a moment, then opened them.

Ater said nothing either.

Sion could tell by now that both had a rough idea of who that person was, but he did not ask the name.

What mattered now was not who that person was, but whether they could reach Elia.

Sern pointed at one last line.

"We go this far first. The first door is the auxiliary corridor beside the maintenance line. Past that, everything after has to be faster."

Kael nodded short. Seorin had already trimmed down to only the necessary questions. Jiwoo wore the face of someone calculating which segments could be collapsed on the way out. Aka kept gauging the distance between the two presences she felt.

Only Luhai murmured very small.

"We are really going in…"

This time no one took the words lightly.

Sern folded the map.

"Let us go."

Short words.

The moment they fell, every silence inside the warehouse seemed to look in the same direction.

No one hesitated further.

Now it was time to go inside.
