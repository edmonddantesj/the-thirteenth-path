# Episode 191. First Gate

The first gate did not look like a gate.

The end of the gray corridor.
A wall.
A wall like every other wall.
The guide light changed.
The wall folded.
Without a sound.
It opened.
It closed.
A wall again.

The party pressed into the shadow of a corner ten paces back.
On the floor in front of the wall, three lines of boundary light.
The three lines changed color in turn.
Slowly.
Sion counted the beat.
First line.
Two.
Three.
First line.

The moment Sern saw the wall, he spoke low.

"Alignment gate."
"It opens according to reclassification of transit targets."

"Lets what through?"

Sion asked.

"Nothing, right now. When we go in, it counts us. Whether we are something to block or something to let in."

"Is there anything it lets in?"

"There is."

Sern said.

"The children going up would have passed through here."

Sion looked at the three lines of boundary light.
There were no footprint shapes.
Only lines.

Han Jiwoo looked at the lines.
Three, in a child's steps.
Two, in her own.

"How would it have counted the kids?"

She said.

"As something to let in."

Sern said.

"Children with translucent fingertips. That would have been the standard."

Han Jiwoo looked at her own fingertips.
Calluses.
Oil.
She closed her hand.

Seorin didn't look at the gate.
She looked behind it.
Threads of light running along the base of the wall.
They ran once and stopped.
Ran again and stopped.

"Don't break it."

She said it low.

"Make it open wrong."

A chill ran down Sion's back.
His hand tightened on the blade.

Ater looked at the circulating light and the rails overhead.
The light ran three times and stopped.
He held up three fingers.

"Three opening beats."
"We can make one false pass line."

"How?"

"If the left is seen first, the gate counts the left as something to block. If the right comes in during that, it counts again. The space between the two counts is one beat."

Sern took it up.
Two lines on the record panel.

"First line, deliberately slow."
"The second line has to draw the alignment calculation first."

Seorin nodded.
She summed it up, short.

"Act like you're going straight at it."
"While it's picking what to block, he shows himself once, from the side."
"Then the gate counts again for what it has to block. Sion goes in on that beat."

"What does he get counted as?"

Sion asked.

"Don't know."

Seorin said.

"Neither does it. That's why it counts again."

"How long does the recount take?"

Sion asked.

"One beat."

Ater said.

"The length of time the light runs and stops. You must be inside within it."

Sion looked at the ten paces.
To the gate.
Ten paces in one beat.

"Can't make it."

He said.

"Get to five ahead of time."

Seorin said.

"While it's counting the left. When it counts the left, it doesn't look at the middle."

Sion looked at the spot five paces out.
Just short of the second line of boundary light.

The last android heard that.
Beside Han Jiwoo.
His shoulder axis made a sound.
He looked at the wall.
The wall that ones like him would have passed through.

Han Jiwoo looked at him.

"Just be seen."

She told him.

"Don't go in."

He didn't nod.
She took that as a nod.

"Why doesn't he nod?"

Kael asked it
of Han Jiwoo.

"He doesn't."

Han Jiwoo said.

"Not once. But he does what he's told."

"That's better."

Kael said.

"Than nodding and not doing it."

Han Jiwoo didn't answer.

Kael took the left axis.
With one old machine.
On the right there was only one old machine.
Luhai's place was empty.
Kael looked at the empty place.

"You all right alone on the right?"

"It can be done."

The old machine said.

"It only has to be half a beat late. Being late takes only one."

"If Luhai were here."

Sion said.

"He isn't."

Seorin said.

"He's down below. That's a hand too."

Sion looked to the right.
One old machine.
He looked at its feet.
Heavy feet.
Good feet for being half a beat late.

The first move went in.

Sion went the five paces first.
Slowly.
Just short of the second line of boundary light.
The light didn't count him.
Not yet.

The left axis stepped in.
By the way that would be seen.
Half a step early.
Kael's foot touched the first line of boundary light.
The guide light changed color.
Beneath the gate's surface, threads of light ran.
To the left.
They stopped.
The gate was counting.

Sern folded one finger down.

The light stayed on the left.
Kael's feet.
The old machine's feet.
It counted the two.
One person.
One machine.
The light wavered over the two of them.
Block.
Block.
Kael didn't move.
Not while he was being counted.

The last android came out from the side line.
Not from the front.
Not fully exposed either.
One shoulder.
The knot.
The faint lines on his face.
Right where the gate's threads of light would reach.
He stood there.
One beat.

The threads of light under the gate stopped.
The direction they had been running wavered.
Left.
Side.
Left.
They couldn't decide.

Han Jiwoo's signal came from where she had pulled back.

"Now."

The right axis came in.
One old machine.
It took the beat it was meant to come in late on and moved it half a beat early.
A heavy foot touched the third line of boundary light.

The gate counted three at once.

Left.
Side.
Right.
The threads of light split three ways.
And stopped, split.

The alignment gate opened.

Wrong.
In the middle.
Where it should have stayed shut.
Like a passage, not a block.
One person's shoulders wide.

"In!"

Seorin's voice was short and hard.

Sion went.
His body ahead of his thinking.
Almost sliding.
Into the open gap.
The edges of the folding wall brushed both his shoulders.
Cold.
Inside.

The old machine from the left axis came in after him.
Before the gate closed.
It grabbed an inner support axle.
Twisted it.
Metal ground on metal.

The gate tried to close.
The inner axle was off by a hair.
It closed halfway.
Stopped.
The guide light wavered.
Once more.
Light trying to correct itself.
The gate stayed half open.

Sion landed on the floor inside.
He looked back.
The gate.
A gate half open, and wrong.
Beneath it the threads of light were still split three ways.
Split, and unable to run.

The corners of his mouth went up.
He pressed them down.
He reset his grip on the blade.

The inner corridor.
No boundary light.
The walls were closer.
The light came from above.
He put his back to the wall.
It was cold.
He breathed.
From outside the gate came the guidance tone.
"Recalculating alignment."
It was a phrase he had never heard it say.

Seorin came in.
Sern.
Record panel held against his chest.
Ater.
Down from above.
Kael.
The old machine from the right axis last.
Its shoulder caught on the gate.
It twisted.
It was in.

Han Jiwoo stood in front of the gate.
The last android was still on the side line.
Holding the legion's eyes on himself.
The optical eyes of two Serakion units were on him.
Not on the gate.

"Come now."

Han Jiwoo said.

He came.
From the side line to the gate.
Half a beat.
The two units' optical eyes followed him.
Reached the gate.
Too late.
He was in.
Han Jiwoo was in.
Behind them the gate tried to close.
It didn't.
Halfway.
The inner axle was still off.

The two units came up to the gate.
The half-open gap.
One put its shoulder in.
It caught.
On the misaligned axle.
It pulled back.
Put it in again.
Caught.
The two looked at each other.
At the gate.
They didn't come in.
They stood outside the gate.

"They're not coming in?"

Sion asked.

"They cannot come in."

Sern said.

"The gate cannot count its own side either. A wrong gate is wrong in both directions."

Sern wrote on the record panel.

"One."

He said.

"Nine came in. Two beats to close. It did not close."

"And if it doesn't close?"

"What is behind will follow."

Sern said.

"But it is only half open, so one at a time. They cannot come in a column."

Seorin looked at the gate.
The half-open gap.
One person wide.

"One at a time is fine."

She said.

"Moving."

"We leave it behind us?"

Han Jiwoo asked,
looking at the half-open gate.

"Can't close it."

Seorin said.

"Close it and it counts again. Leave it open and it stays wrong."

"Stays wrong."

"Leaving it wrong is our job. Today."

Han Jiwoo looked at the gate once more.
The split light.
Light that couldn't run.

Sion looked at the gate once more too.
Nothing broken.
Not a scratch.
The gate stood there, wrong all on its own.
He turned away.

The last android looked at the gate.
The gate ones like him would have passed through.
The gate that couldn't count him.
Han Jiwoo looked at his shoulder.
The knot.
Still holding.

"Let's go."

She said.

He went.
His shoulder axis made a sound.
The corridor inside the gate gave the sound nothing back.
The walls swallowed it.

Sern looked at the wall.
It was the same as the walls below.
He counted.
Nine.
One gate.
How many gates were left, he did not know.

"How many more?"

Sion asked.

"I do not know."

Sern said.

"Up to the light Aka saw. That is the end."

Sion looked up.
The ceiling.
The light was still far off.
