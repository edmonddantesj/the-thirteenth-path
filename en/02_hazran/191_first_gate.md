# Episode 191. First Gate

The first gate did not look like a gate.

At the end of the gray corridor, it was a wall exactly like every other wall.
When the guide light changed, the wall folded open without a sound, then closed.
A wall again.

The party pressed into the shadow of a corner ten paces back. On the floor in front of the wall, three lines of boundary light slowly changed color in turn.
Sion counted the beat. First line, two, three, first line again.

The moment Sern saw the wall, he spoke low.

"Alignment gate. It reclassifies what is passing through, and then it opens."

"Lets what through?"

Sion asked.

"Nothing, right now. When we go in, it counts us. Whether we are something to block or something to let in."

"Is there anything it lets in?"

"There is."

Sern said.

"The children going up would have passed through here."

Sion looked at the three lines of boundary light. There were no footprint shapes, only lines.

Han Jiwoo looked at the lines too. Three in a child's steps, two in her own.

"How would it have counted the kids?"

She said.

"As something to let in."

Sern said.

"Children with translucent fingertips. That would have been the standard."

Han Jiwoo looked at her own fingertips. Calluses, with oil worked into them.
She closed her hand.

Seorin looked not at the gate but behind it. The threads of light running along the base of the wall ran once and stopped, then ran again and stopped.

"Don't break it."

She said it low.

"Make it open wrong."

A chill ran down Sion's back. His hand tightened on the blade.

Ater looked at the circulating light and the rails overhead. The light ran three times and stopped.
He held up three fingers.

"Three opening beats. We can make one false pass line."

"How?"

"If the left is seen first, the gate counts the left as something to block. If the right comes in during that, it counts again. The space between the two counts is one beat."

Sern took it up and drew two lines on the record panel.

"The first line, deliberately slow. The second line has to be caught in the calculation first."

Seorin nodded and summed it up, short.

"Act like you're going straight at it. While it's picking what to block, he shows himself once, from the side. Then the gate counts again what it has to block. Sion goes in on that beat."

"What does the gate count him as?"

Sion asked.

"Don't know."

Seorin said.

"Neither does it. That's why it counts again."

"How long does the recount take?"

Sion asked.

"One beat."

Ater said.

"The length of time the light runs and stops. You must be inside within it."

Sion looked at the ten paces to the gate.
Ten paces in one beat.

"Can't make it."

"Get five of them done ahead of time."

Seorin said.

"While it's counting the left. When it counts the left, it doesn't look at the middle."

Sion looked at the spot five paces out, just short of the second line of boundary light.

Beside Han Jiwoo, the last android heard that. His shoulder axis made a sound.
He looked at the wall that ones like him would have passed through.

Han Jiwoo looked at him.

"Just be seen. Don't go in."

He didn't nod. She took that as a nod.

"Why doesn't he nod?"

Kael asked Han Jiwoo.

"He doesn't."

Han Jiwoo said.

"Not once. But he does what he's told."

"That's better than nodding and not doing it."

Han Jiwoo didn't answer.

Kael took the left axis with one old machine. On the right there was only one old machine.
Luhai's place was empty. Kael looked at the empty place.

"You all right alone on the right?"

"It can be done."

The old machine said.

"It only has to be half a beat late. Being late is something one can do alone."

"If Luhai were here."

Sion said.

"He isn't."

Seorin said.

"He's down below. That's a hand too."

Sion looked at the old machine on the right, at its heavy feet.
They were good feet for being half a beat late.

The first move went in.

Sion went the five paces first, slowly. Just short of the second line of boundary light.
The light didn't count him.
Not yet.

The left axis stepped in half a step early, by the way that would be seen. Kael's foot touched the first line of boundary light.
The guide light changed color. Beneath the gate's surface, threads of light ran to the left and stopped.
The gate was counting.

Sern folded one finger down.

The light stayed on the left. It counted Kael's feet and the old machine's feet, one person and one machine.
The light wavered over the two of them.

Block.
Block.

Kael didn't move while he was being counted.

The last android came out from the side line. Not from the front, and not fully exposed either.
One shoulder, the knot, the faint lines on his face.
He stood where the gate's threads of light would reach.

One beat.

The threads of light under the gate stopped. The direction they had been running wavered.

Left.
Side.
Left.

It couldn't decide.

Han Jiwoo, who had pulled back, sent the signal.

"Now."

The old machine on the right axis came in. It moved the beat it was meant to come in late on half a beat early.
A heavy foot touched the third line of boundary light.

The gate counted three at once.

Left.
Side.
Right.

The threads of light split three ways and stopped.

The alignment gate opened.

Wrong.

In the middle. Where it should have stayed shut, it opened not as a block but as a passage, one person's shoulders wide.

"In!"

Seorin's voice was short and hard.

Sion went. His body went ahead of his thinking, sliding into the open gap.
The edges of the folding wall brushed both his shoulders. They were cold.

Inside.

The old machine from the left axis came in after him before the gate closed. It grabbed an inner support axle and twisted it.
Metal ground on metal.

The gate tried to close. The inner axle was off by a hair.
It closed halfway and stopped. The guide light, trying to correct itself, wavered once, then again.
The gate stayed half open.

Sion set his feet on the floor inside and looked back. A gate half open, and wrong.
Beneath it the threads of light were still split three ways, unable to run.

The corners of his mouth went up. He pressed them down and reset his grip on the blade.

There was no boundary light in the inner corridor. The walls were closer, and the light came from above.
He put his back to the wall. It was cold. He breathed.

From outside the gate came the guidance tone.
"Recalculating alignment."
It was a phrase he had never heard before.

Seorin came in. Then Sern, with the record panel held against his chest, then Ater, down from above, then Kael.
The old machine from the right axis was last. Its shoulder caught on the gate, and it got in only after twisting its body.

Han Jiwoo stood in front of the gate. The last android was still on the side line, holding the legion's eyes on himself.
The optical eyes of two Serakion units were turned not toward the gate but toward him.

"Come now."

Han Jiwoo said.

He came. From the side line to the gate, half a beat.
By the time the two units' optical eyes had followed him to the gate, it was too late.
He was in, and Han Jiwoo was in.

Behind them the gate tried to close but didn't. The inner axle was still off, and it stayed halfway.

The two units came up to the gate. One put its shoulder into the half-open gap and caught on the misaligned axle.
It pulled back and put it in again. It caught again.
The two looked at each other, then at the gate. They didn't come in; they stood outside the gate.

"They're not coming in?"

Sion asked.

"They cannot come in."

Sern said.

"The gate cannot count its own side either. A wrong gate is wrong in both directions."

Sern wrote on the record panel.

"One."

He said.

"Nine came in. Two beats to close. It did not close."

"And if it doesn't close?"

"What is behind will follow."

Sern said.

"But it is only half open, so one at a time. They cannot come in a column."

Seorin looked at the gap, half open and one person wide.

"One at a time is fine. Moving."

"We leave it behind us?"

Han Jiwoo asked, looking at the half-open gate.

"Can't close it."

Seorin said.

"Close it and it counts again. Leave it open and it stays wrong."

"Stays wrong."

"Leaving it wrong is our job. Today."

Han Jiwoo looked once more at the light that was split and couldn't run.

Sion looked at the gate once more too. Nothing broken, not a scratch.
The gate stood there, wrong all on its own.
He turned away.

The last android looked at the gate. The gate ones like him would have passed through, the gate that couldn't count him.
Han Jiwoo looked at his shoulder. The knot was still as it had been.

"Let's go."

He went. His shoulder axis made a sound.
The corridor inside the gate didn't give the sound back. The walls swallowed it.

Sern looked at the wall. It was the same as the walls below.
He counted. Nine, one gate.
He didn't know how many gates were left.

"How many more?"

Sion asked.

"I do not know."

Sern said.

"Up to the light Aka saw. That is the end."

Sion looked up. The light on the ceiling was still far off.
