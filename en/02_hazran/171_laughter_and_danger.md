# Episode 171. Laughter and Danger

The section that day was peaceful for a remarkably long time — rare inside the old layer.

It was not a brief gap. It genuinely felt, for a while, like a time where no one had to stifle their voice.

The children were still careful at first.

Seeing the android with a face had not instantly erased their fear. But it had clearly changed something.

From the moment eyes and a mouth appeared on a smooth face, it had become slightly harder for the children to keep calling them just "those things."

So that day, when one went closer, another found courage. When one smiled, the child beside them finally smiled too.

Jiwoo watched from a distance and ended up staying close again.

Partly because her presence made the children less afraid. Partly because she herself did not want to look away from the scene.

One child declared it almost like an announcement.

"I want to draw this one's face this time."

Another child raised a hand immediately.

"I want that one."

"Then I get this one."

There was no logic or reason in those words. But strangely, who each would go to seemed already decided, as naturally as breathing.

The children stood before the androids one by one.

One child drew the eyes very large. One drew the corners lifted all the way up into a smiling expression. One drew a scrunched mouth and then laughed at their own work.

"This one looks kind of angry already."

The child beside them burst into laughter.

Another child drew a mouth too crooked, smudged it once with a palm, and drew it rounder.

"This one looks stupid."

Even from the adults' side, a tiny laugh escaped.

Luhai covered his mouth with his hand but laughed in the end. Sion noticed a beat late that he was laughing too.

Seorin did not stop the laughter. Sern held his recording tablet and for once paused his writing for a long while. Ater leaned against the wall without looking away.

Even the old machines and the Hazran people were not fully pulled back to the outside this time.

They had not come very close. But at least they were not averting their eyes.

That seemed like the biggest change in this scene.

Feeling that something frightening is frightening, yet staying in the same space and watching anyway. That was harder than it seemed.

Each time the children drew a face with their own hands, the lines surfaced one by one as light from inside the surface, with a slight delay.

Round eyes. Thin eyes. A short mouth. A long grinning mouth. A crooked mouth.

Every android's face now held a different expression.

And they, too, seemed to feel the change.

One slowly turned its head toward a metal panel where its face was reflected. One looked at the android beside it, then brought its gaze back to its own face. Another, when a child drew an extra line over the eyes, tilted its body toward the child, barely.

All the movements were clumsy.

Different from the precise beat of executing commands. Closer to something awkward and slightly slow — repeatedly checking a newly appeared self while still finding it unfamiliar.

The children watched and kept laughing.

"It is looking at itself right now."

"Wait, really."

"This one suits smiling."

"My drawing is better."

While that laughter filled the room, Sion suddenly thought he would remember this scene for a long time.

Even after everything passed later, he would probably think of these strange faces first.

There were no names yet. But there were faces. And having faces meant it was now hard to see them as completely empty beings.

Jiwoo watched and breathed very slowly.

She was still afraid. She still did not want to rashly assign meaning.

But at least, the fact that the responses the androids were showing right now could not be explained by simple system error or command residue alone — that was becoming increasingly hard to deny.

One child stood before the android they had given a smiling mouth and said.

"Do you like this?"

There was no answer, of course.

But a few seconds later, both ends of that mouth line brightened, very faintly.

The child who saw it went wide-eyed, turned around, and shouted.

"It is smiling!"

The other children erupted in laughter again.

That laughter was so unfamiliar inside the old layer that it sounded more precious and more painful for it.

One old machine watched the scene quietly from afar. Its optical eye was wavering, very faintly.

That reaction was not simple wariness. It looked closer to the pause of surprise and hesitation and perhaps the moment something lost very long ago reappeared before it.

Ater said it very low.

"They all react differently."

Sern received it quietly.

"Yes. They received the same kind of face, but they do not look at it in the same way."

Luhai murmured, still wearing a face of disbelief.

"Then really... each one is taking it in differently."

No one could confirm that. But no one could dismiss it as easily as before either.

One woman from Hazran stood behind the children, watching with slightly wet eyes, and said it very small.

"They could laugh like that."

Who the words were aimed at, the children did not know, and the adults did not ask.

It was probably aimed at the children and at herself at the same time.

That was when it happened.

Jiwoo raised her head first. More precisely, she was closer to the side that felt something changing before it arrived.

The air cooled just slightly. Before any sound reached, an unfamiliar tension was already seeping in from somewhere in the space.

Two old machines stopped almost simultaneously. One outside hand turned not toward the children drawing faces but toward the gray corridor entrance.

Sion noticed and felt the laughter dying very slowly.

Nothing had happened yet. But everyone was simultaneously sensing that something was about to come in wrong.

Seorin said it short.

"What is it."

The answer came from somewhere else first.

A man who had been among the Hazran people — clearly there just moments ago — was no longer visible.

Someone said it low first.

"Wait. Where did he go."

The instant those words fell, Jiwoo's face hardened, very faintly.

He was a man who had been rescued and brought in days ago. He had wounds. He spoke little. He always stayed close to the walls.

So quiet that his presence had blurred.

And precisely because of that, the fact of his absence reached everyone a few beats late.

Sion felt something go cold behind his back.

In that moment, the android that had just been given a smiling mouth turned its head first.

Not toward the children. Toward the darkness beyond the gray corridor, where no sound had come yet.

Then two more androids shifted their gaze in nearly the same direction.

This time it was too clear for anyone to call it coincidence.

One step at a time, very slowly, they began moving their positions — not in front of the children, but between the children and that darkness.

No command had been given. No one had told them to.

But the movement was clearly on the side of preparing.

Sion watched with held breath.

The danger was not yet visible. But the first to sense it here might have been them.

Beings that had received faces. That had received laughter. That had begun accepting strange expressions as their own. And now were beginning to stand in front of the children by their own will.

In that moment, the air inside the old layer changed completely once more.
