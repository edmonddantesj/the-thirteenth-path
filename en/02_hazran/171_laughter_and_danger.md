# Episode 171. The Children Who Laughed First

That day the sector stayed at peace for longer than the old layer usually allowed.

It wasn't a brief lull. While the circulation lines above changed twice, no one below kept their voice down.

The children were still careful at first. Even after they had seen the one android with a face, it took them half a day to come from the entrance to the inside.

Since that android's face had gained eyes and a mouth, the children would start to say "those things" and stop. One child called it "the one with a face," and another just said "that one."

That day one child stepped into the sector first. The child beside them followed, holding on to that sleeve. The first one in looked back and laughed. The one holding the sleeve laughed too.

Han Jiwoo watched from a distance, and in the end, this time too, she went somewhere close and sat with her back to a pillar inside the sector. It was where the children would see her if they looked back.

One child said it like a declaration.

"This time I'm drawing this one's face."

Another child put a hand up at once.

"Me, this one."

"Then me, over here."

None of them said why that one. None of their choices overlapped, either. The children scattered, dabbing soil onto their fingers.

The children stood in front of the androids one by one. A child who couldn't reach stood on tiptoe. When that still wasn't enough, the child beside them held them up at the waist.

One child drew the eyes very big, one pulled the corners of the eyes all the way up into a smiling look, and one drew the mouth frowning and then laughed at it.

"Because this one's kind of grumpy anyway."

At that, the child beside them burst into giggles.

Another child drew the mouth too crooked, rubbed it once with a palm to blur it, and drew it again, rounder. Where it had been rubbed, the soil smeared and stayed like a cheek.

"This one looks silly."

At that, even from the adults, a very small laugh slipped out. Luhai covered his mouth with his hand but laughed in the end, and Sion, too, noticed a beat late that he was laughing.

Seorin didn't stop the laughter. Sern, record panel in hand, stopped writing for a long while, just this once, and Ater stood leaning against the wall and didn't look away.

Even the old machines and the Hazran people hadn't pulled all the way back out this time. They didn't come very close, but they had come in as far as the wall just inside the sector's entrance, which no one had crossed until a few days ago.

Each time the children drew a face in with their own hands, the lines rose one by one, after a small delay, as light from inside the surface.

Round eyes, narrow eyes.
A short mouth, a frowning mouth, a smiling mouth stretched long and wide, a crooked mouth.

Every android's face got a different expression.

They didn't stay still either.

One slowly turned its head to the side, toward a metal plate where its face was reflected, and one looked at the face of the android beside it before bringing its gaze back to its own. Another, when a child traced its eyes over once more with a finger, leaned very slightly that way. The child's finger met its face that much more firmly.

Heads turned and stopped, stopped, then turned a little more. Then they came back a little, as far as they had turned.

The children kept laughing as they watched.

"Look, it's looking at itself."

"Whoa, it really is."

"Smiling suits this one."

"Mine's better than that."

"No, mine's better."

"Yours is crooked."

"Crooked is better."

Two children were shoving each other, and the one shoved bumped into an android. The android didn't move. The child steadied a hand against its body, stood there, and laughed.

The laughter filled the room. Leaning against the wall, Sion listened to it and looked at each face in turn. Every face was different.

There were no names yet.
There were faces.

Back against the pillar, Han Jiwoo breathed very slowly. The hand resting on her knee clenched once and loosened, and with that hand she took hold of the hem of her clothes. Each time the children touched an android's face, the hand tightened.

One child spoke in front of the android they had given a smiling mouth with their own hand.

"Do you like it?"

Of course there was no answer.

A few seconds later, both ends of the mouth line brightened, very faintly.

The child who saw it went round-eyed, then turned around and shouted.

"It's smiling!"

At that the other children burst into giggles all over again. The laughter struck the walls and came back. In the old layer, it was the first echo of its kind.

One of the old machines was quietly watching the scene from a distance. Its optical eye was wavering, very faintly. The moment the smiling mouth brightened, the machine's hand came off the wall and touched it again.

Ater spoke very low.

"They each respond differently."

Sern answered quietly.

"Yes. Though they received the same face, they do not look at it in the same way."

Luhai muttered, still looking as if he could hardly believe it.

"So really… each one's taking it in differently."

No one answered that. No one said he was wrong, either.

A woman from Hazran had been standing behind the children, and, watching the scene with slightly wet eyes, she spoke very softly.

"So they could laugh like that."

The children didn't hear it, and the adults didn't ask. The woman pressed the corner of her eye once with her hand. She was the woman who came with two children.

Her two children were up ahead. One was drawing eyes, and the other, beside them, was dabbing soil onto their fingers. She didn't go to them. She stood there, two steps from the wall.

That was when it happened.

Han Jiwoo looked up first. Her back came off the pillar.

The air cooled, just slightly. There was no sound yet. A scrap of cloth hanging on the inner wall of the sector stirred once, though there was no draft.

The two old machines stopped almost at the same moment. One outside hand turned its gaze first, not toward the children who had been drawing faces, but toward the entrance of the gray route.

Only when he saw that did Sion realize his laughter had stopped.

Nothing had happened yet. Even while the adults in the room turned toward the entrance one by one, the children were still laughing.

Seorin kept it short.

"What is it?"

The answer came from somewhere else first.

A man who had been among the Hazran people, who had definitely been right there until a moment ago, was nowhere to be seen. Where he had leaned against the wall, only the cloth he had been sitting on was left.

Someone spoke first, low.

"Wait."

"Where'd he go?"

The moment the words fell, Han Jiwoo's face stiffened, very faintly.

He was the man who had been rescued and brought in a few days ago. He'd been injured, said little, a man who always kept close to the wall. Even at meals he was last in line, and after he got his food he went back to the same spot.

One by one, people turned to look at the empty cloth. The shape of where he had sat was still pressed into it.

Sion felt something go cold somewhere behind his back.

At that moment, the android that had just been given a smiling mouth turned its head first.

It moved not toward the children, but toward the darkness past the gray route, where no sound could be heard yet.

Then two other androids moved their eyes in nearly the same direction. The third was half a beat behind.

One step at a time, very slowly, they began to move themselves, not in front of the children, but between the children and that darkness. When one stopped, the one beside it matched the spacing. The three became a line.

No one told them to.

The child who had drawn the smiling mouth saw its back and stopped laughing.

"What's wrong?"

The android didn't look back. The child pushed at its back once, but it didn't give. The child took their hand away.

Han Jiwoo took that child by the shoulder and slowly pulled them back. The child came, pulled along, eyes still on that back.

Holding his breath, Sion looked at the three backs. The faces the children had drawn with soiled fingers all faced the darkness. On the backs turned this way, nothing was drawn.

The air inside the sector cooled once more. The scrap of cloth stirred once more.
