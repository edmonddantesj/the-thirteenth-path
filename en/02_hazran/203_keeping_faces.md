# Episode 203. Keeping Faces

They went down slowly.

Three walls to a level, a panel on every wall, lines on every panel, so no one hurried.
The tips of Kael's fingers were scraped from tearing out panels, but he didn't wipe that hand.

One level further down, they came out into a wide middle section.

There were androids, more than ten of them.
They stood scattered along the alignment lines on either side, in the broken waiting grooves, in front of collapsed wall panels, under dead sorting panels.

None of them was looking the same way.

One was touching its own hands.
One was looking down at the lines of its face reflected in the floor.
One had stopped and was slowly looking at the unit beside it.
One had its palm against the wall. The wall did not light up in the shape of its hand, but it kept its palm there anyway.

Han Jiwoo slowed, then stopped, and spoke almost like a breath.

"…They really are all different."

Sern was looking too, and though he held the record panel up for a long time, he didn't write.

"Not the same beat. Response speeds all differ. They are… no longer bound."

"Did you count them?"

Sion asked.

"I started counting and stopped."

Sern said.

"Not one of them is the same, so they cannot be counted."

"Could you count them up there?"

"They were one and the same thing."

Sern said.

"However many there were, they were one."

Sion looked at the scattered ones, one by one. They were not one.

He went toward the nearest android.
It was looking at its own face reflected in a metal plate on the wall. The trace of light was unsteady, and the shape wouldn't settle.
Round light where the eyes would be, and nothing where the mouth would be.
Still, it was not a smooth blank; something was there.

The android knew Sion was coming, but it did not step back.
A beat late, it raised its hand toward its own face and laid it over the light.
It touched. As if checking that the face was its own.

Any word would have come too soon, so Sion couldn't bring one out.
He only nodded, slowly, once.
The android watched that movement for a long time, then lowered its hand from its face. After it had seen Sion nod.

"Why'd you nod?"

Kael asked.

"I don't know. I couldn't say anything, and I couldn't just leave it either."

Sion rubbed the back of his neck once with his hand.

"Did it get it?"

"It lowered its hand, didn't it?"

A little farther down, Ater stood in front of a bundle of cut approval lines.
Thin lines hung loose along the wall, half their light still left.
He felt along them with his fingertips.

A number.
A number.
A number.

"These lines tried to keep every unit as a number to the very end. Not as a name. Not as a response. As a number."

Han Jiwoo heard that, closed her eyes, and opened them. She looked at the lines under Ater's hand, at numbers she couldn't read.
One of those numbers was a hand that had been in her hand, the child at her fingertips. She clenched her hand tight.

The last android came up beside Ater and, without hesitating, took hold of one strand of the approval lines with his hand.
He bent it.
Three thin streaks of light trembled briefly and went out.
A line without light was left in his hand, and he laid it on the floor.

Ater saw it and murmured very low.

"Yes. That kind of name has to end now."

"Who are you saying that to."

Sion asked.

Instead of answering right away, Ater looked at the bent line.

"To myself."

He answered.

"I was a number too. In my house. A son, by number."

Han Jiwoo looked at Ater.

"Which number were you?"

Ater didn't answer right away.

"A place in a count. That is all I know."

Han Jiwoo asked nothing more.

Seorin was looking at the next section.

"We go further down. There's a restoration axis left."

Sern took it up at once.

"Two levels below, the central restoration line is still live. If we do not cut it now, it will push back up."

"Push what?"

"These units. By their numbers."

At that, three androids moved first. Where they had once waited for commands, they did not wait.
The three glanced at one another and clumsily split up the directions.
One to the left railing, one to the central groove, one toward the wall.
It wasn't precise. Their paths crossed, and they bumped once. They moved aside. They went on.

Watching the three, Han Jiwoo felt her chest ache, but she didn't put her hand there.
When she pressed her inner pocket, the fragment with the smiling mouth drawn on it was there.

"They went first."

She said.

"Nobody even told them to."

"They heard."

Seorin said.

"They heard, and they went on their own."

"What if they go the wrong way?"

"If they go the wrong way, they come back. Until now they've never gone the wrong way. They were set up so they couldn't."

Seorin's finger pointed toward the section below.

When Han Jiwoo looked the way the three had gone, one took a wrong turn, came back, and went again.

When they reached the section where the central restoration axis remained, the two androids that had gone down first were holding the bottom of the restoration panel.
They lacked the strength, so though the panel had come halfway down, the axis inside was untouched and light was circling upward.

Kael looked at the panel. On the level above he had only watched, but this time he went first.
He put his shoulder into the other side of the panel and took one of the two by the hand, moving it closer to the axis. Showing it where the force goes, briefly.
While Sion stepped in and gripped the side of the panel, Kael pushed on the other side.
The ankle he had turned on the level above twisted, but he didn't let go.
Sern listened to the axis turning, faster, then slower.

"Now. Once more."

Ater read the light on the wall and called out the number of the central line.

"Not the second. The third. The center always leaves one empty and uses the third as the true reference."

"You said the middle's left empty."

Kael said, both hands pressing on the panel.

"It is left empty. The one beside it is the real one."

Ater said.

"The same as what we saw up there."

"Leave the middle seat empty, and the one beside it is the real one."

Kael said.

"Was it like that with people's seats too?"

"It was like that with people's seats too."

Ater said.

"The person seated in the middle mattered least."

The last android pressed the innermost anchoring axis with his hand.

The third.

The restoration panel shook hard.
Sion's arms bracing the panel shook, and Kael's shoulder, and the two androids' hands.
Then the beat that had run long on the inside broke off all at once.
The light stopped circling, and the light that had been going upward went out where it was.

Several androids nearby went still for a moment, as if holding their breath.
Then they moved, each at a different speed.
One sat down slowly on the floor.
One leaned against the wall.
One touched its own face again.
When the two that had been holding the panel let go, the panel hit the floor, and the two looked at their own hands.

Kael, a hand on his ankle, let out an empty breath that was almost a laugh.

"Now we're getting somewhere."

One of the two tilted its head very slightly toward Kael.
There was no answer. Only that the words had come its way.

Han Jiwoo spoke very quietly.

"They're really coming back now."

No one answered right away.
Sern marked one restoration axis on the record panel, but there was no number to write, so beside it he wrote nothing.

The last android took his hand off the anchoring axis and looked at his own hand.
He looked at the android sitting down, the one leaning against the wall, the one touching its face.
His hand rose to the lines on his own face and touched them.
This time he didn't lower it right away.

Han Jiwoo watched that hand and said nothing.

Seorin looked once at the dark below.

"We go further down. Even if we don't finish today, we cut it all the way down."

Sion nodded quietly and looked down.
There were still lights to put out, lines to cut, numbers to empty.

On the way down there were more than the seven. The androids went down with them.
The one that had sat down got up.
The one that had leaned on the wall came away from it.
The one that had been touching its face lowered its hand and walked.
Each a beat apart. On the stairs there were many footsteps.

Han Jiwoo listened to those footsteps without counting them.
Every time the last android walked behind her, a sound came from his shoulder axis.
Among the other androids' footsteps, that sound alone was different.
She listened to that sound.
That one she counted.
