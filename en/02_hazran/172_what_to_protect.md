# Episode 172. What to Protect

The danger did not come as sound first.

What arrived first was the sensation that something was already aligning itself toward them.

So when the old machines and the androids who had just received faces looked in the same direction almost simultaneously, Sion — though he could not yet see anything — already felt the thought: too late.

The next moment, the sound of metal crashing in succession erupted from the darkness beyond the gray corridor.

And soon, the first line of Serakion's android force appeared.

No faces. No expressions. No pauses.

They filled the narrow corridor like water, pressing inward at a beat too smooth and too even.

Their movement was so different from the beings on this side — who had just been receiving faces from children's hands — it was hard to believe they were made from the same bodies.

"Back!" Seorin shouted first. "Get the children out!"

Jiwoo was already moving. She pushed the shoulders of the two nearest children and spun them backward. A Hazran woman grabbed a crying child almost in an embrace and passed them to the rear line.

But before the chaos, the androids had moved.

Three androids with faces stepped forward almost simultaneously.

Not exactly the same. One turned its body first. One glanced at the android beside it before moving. Another chose the position blocking the children first.

But those differences made it more visible, not less.

They were not moving on command right now. They looked like beings who had already decided, each on their own side, what to protect first.

The first clash was so fast Sion recognized it by sound first.

Metal bending. Frames splitting. Light bursting from somewhere inside.

One android that had just received a face grabbed the neck section of the first Serakion unit pushing through and twisted it down.

Simultaneously, another beside it slid low and slammed an enemy android — one skimming toward the children — into the side wall.

The other side had far greater numbers. But for the first few seconds, the feeling was that this side was cutting the battlefield.

Luhai said it almost like a breath.

"No way..."

It was not strange to say.

Beings that had been barely feeling their own faces moments ago were now moving faster than the humans, more decisively than the old machines — as though they already knew what to block first.

But Sion could tell it was not because their combat skill was superior.

They were not fighting by chasing and killing. To the end, they moved to sever the lines trying to reach the children and the people.

The objective was too simple. And therefore stronger.

Here. The children. The ones who gave them faces.

Ater, watching the upper corridor, called out low.

"It is not just the first line! There are more behind!"

Sern was already falling back, calculating where the corridor would be blocked. Two old machines began opening a gap in the rear line before Seorin even spoke.

But faster than any of those movements, one android with a face planted itself in front of the child who had drawn the smiling mouth.

Three enemy androids pushed in simultaneously.

The first with a shoulder. The second with a side. The third diving almost bodily.

That android took all three at once.

The left arm frame bent inward. The chest section half-cracked. One side of the smiling mouth the child had drawn flickered from the impact.

But the body did not retreat.

Instead, with what force remained, it wrenched the first unit's neck downward, shoved the second into the wall, and dragged the third to the floor along with itself.

One child saw it and finally broke into crying.

That crying split the room more distinctly than any sound of battle.

In that moment, Sion felt he understood without further explanation why this fight was so terrible.

Beings that had just received faces, that had been standing inside children's laughter — were now grinding their own bodies down without hesitation to protect those very children.

One unit destroyed nearly four enemy androids in succession.

Precise. Cruelly fast. Astonishingly steady.

But that precision was not the pleasure of killing. It was closer to the resolve of a side that had already decided what to keep.

One old machine murmured very low, watching.

"That is..."

But the words never finished.

Because from behind the Serakion force, the next line pushed through without slowing.

And the method was different.

Spending the front line to open a path. The rear line layering over on top.

No pauses. No retrieval. No hesitation.

It was less combat and more a way of feeding commands forward.

Jiwoo went white the moment she saw it.

"Self-detonation incoming—!"

Before the words ended, two units slammed simultaneously into the torso of the android holding the front.

Light burst. The frame crumpled inward. One eye line the child had drawn went half-dark.

The child screamed.

A Hazran woman held the child and could not swallow her own tears either. Luhai could no longer even curse. Sern nearly dropped his recording tablet for the first time.

But that android did not collapse.

Even with a half-broken body, it covered the children behind it to the very end.

And looked back, very briefly.

Sion could not be certain who the gaze was for. The children. Another android. This entire place.

But what was clear was that it was a gaze of final confirmation.

Protected. Still remaining.

And the next moment, the body tilted forward again and collapsed together with one enemy unit it held to the end.

Sion felt something inside himself tear open.

That was not the sound of a weapon breaking. It was the sound of a being that had received a face from children's hands moments ago, that had felt its own face for the first time, being destroyed in front of the ones who gave it.

And what hurt more than the horror was how clearly visible the reason was.

The children who gave faces. The people who finally looked back. The beings who said: not empty.

That simple reason was moving those bodies to the very end.

The Serakion force was still pushing in.

But now, no one in the room could see this fight as a simple collision between androids of the same kind.

One side was being pushed like commands. The other was holding by will.

And that difference, because it had been born too late, was all the more cruel.
