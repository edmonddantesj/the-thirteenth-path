# Episode 184. Moving Again

They set out quietly.

No one shouted a rallying cry. Seorin raised her hand once, and each team scattered to its own axis. Footsteps fell on the gray route in different beats. People's footsteps, the heavy footsteps of the old machines, and one set of footsteps where one side landed heavier than the other.

Seorin's plan was unchanged.

Who took which layer, who cut which route, which team handled recovery and luring. Which axis was the most dangerous, and at which moment their movements had to lock together. It was all exactly as written on the first page of Sern's record panel, and the second page had one more dot.

The last android joined the same axis as Han Jiwoo.

It was where Seorin had put the dot on the floor. The most unstable axis, the one most likely to shake.

"Why the most unstable one?"

Luhai had asked before they set out.

"So it shakes."

Seorin had answered.

"Put him on an axis that doesn't shake and only he shakes. Put him on one that does, and they can't tell what shook it."

Seorin didn't put him at the front; she put him in the middle of the team. One old machine ahead of him, Han Jiwoo behind, and behind her, two Hazran men. The two Hazran men each carried a piece of wall panel, less a weapon than something to block with.

The man without a younger brother shifted his grip on his panel. When his hands shook, the man beside him held up the bottom of the panel, and they carried it together.
One beat.
The man beside him let go. The man carried it alone. His hands didn't shake.

Han Jiwoo walked right beside him.

She didn't hold him up; the whole way, she kept to a distance where one outstretched hand could reach him at once. When he staggered, her hand came up, and when he stood straight again, it came down. Three times it came up and three times it came down, and not once did it touch him. The fourth time, it stopped halfway up. He had corrected his footing himself before he could stagger.

"Good."

Han Jiwoo said.

He didn't answer; he looked at the foot he had corrected, and on the next step, too, he corrected it before he could stagger.

The children saw them off from deeper inside until the very moment they left.

Before the last android turned away, one child waved a hand, very small. The hand that had only been held up waved this time.

He looked at that hand for a long while, then let his optical eye flicker, ever so slightly.

As the child lowered the hand, the child beside them took it. A Hazran woman put her arms around both their shoulders and turned them inward, but even as the child was turned, their head craned back. Aka stood in front of the child and blocked the view. When the child craned to the side, Aka moved to that side.
The child didn't give up. Neither did Aka.
Until they turned the corner.

The gray route of the old layer was quieter than before.

Three kinds of footsteps.
Human.
Machine.
Him.

The walls gave all three back.

The inner passages of Serakion were always like this. Too smooth, too orderly. Though the first battle had passed through here, there was no dust on the floor. Even the cross-sections of broken structures were strangely straight, and the marks of the first battle remained on that clean gray surface like stains no one had permitted.

A scratch ran down the wall from a person's height to the floor. At its end, everything had been cleared away. Only the scratch remained; whatever had made it was gone.

Luhai ran a fingertip along the scratch once as he passed, but nothing came off on his fingertip. He looked at the fingertip, then shifted his grip on the iron bar.

"Who cleared it?"

He asked.

"Them."

Sern said.

"At night. Without a sound."

"Ours."

"It is theirs. By their count."

Luhai looked again at the scratch running from a person's height to the floor, but there was nothing at its end.

Somewhere a low, polite guidance tone passed once. It was no alarm; it was announcing which paths had opened and which had closed, in the same voice as before. The path it called closed was the very path they were walking, and still no one stopped walking.

"It says this one's closed."

A Hazran man said.

"Take the closed one."

Seorin said.

"They're watching the open ones."

The man shifted his grip on the wall panel.

Listening to that sound, Sion watched the back of the person ahead of him on his team. The same path, the same plan.

On the axis to his left, the last android was walking. Footsteps that hadn't been there the first time they came this way. Not a human step, not an old machine's step, not the step of a Serakion unit. That sound, one foot landing heavier than the other, struck the walls and came back. Serakion's walls sent sound back well.

Checking the gap ahead, Ater spoke low.

"When the first reaction comes, it will change at once."

Sern added to that, very low.

"What we are aiming for is not a difference in strength. It is a difference in reaction. Not breaking a line, but making it impossible for a line to stay the same line."

Luhai nodded at that and shifted his grip on the iron bar in his hand; it was the third time.

"Why do you keep regripping that?"

Sion asked.

"My hands are sweating. Yours are too."

Sion looked at his hilt and found it wet, so he rubbed it on his trousers.

Seorin said nothing and walked looking ahead. At every fork she pointed the way with one finger, and the teams split off along that finger.

At the first fork, Luhai's team turned right. Before he turned, Luhai looked back once, toward the android. At the second fork, Ater caught the corner of a structure, hauled himself up, and looked down once.

At the third fork, the two old machines split off. Before they parted, they looked at each other once. One laid a hand on the other's chest plate and took it away, and the other made the same motion. Then they parted. Sion saw it, but he didn't know what it meant.

What remained was Seorin, Sion and Sern, and Han Jiwoo's team.

The last android was still not whole. His stride was ever so slightly slow, and the shoulder axis they had fixed made a sound once every ten steps. Each time it did, Han Jiwoo's hand came up, and each time the raised hand came down, she looked at it.

He did not stop. Without taking his optical eye off the back of the one ahead, at each fork he turned toward the side Seorin's finger pointed before the one ahead did. The old machine in front noticed and quickened its pace.

Seeing that, Han Jiwoo realized her own feet were a beat late, and she quickened them.

"You turned first."

She said to him.

"Before Seorin's finger."

He didn't answer; he looked at the back of the one ahead. At the next fork he turned half a step early again, and Han Jiwoo saw that half step.

The corridor lights dimmed once and came back. Serakion's lights were lights that never dimmed. Sern raised his head and wrote something brief on the record panel, and even then Seorin didn't slow her pace.

"What was that?"

Sion asked.

"I do not know."

Sern said.

"It has never gone dark before. I am noting it."

"Did they shake?"

"They do not shake."

Sern said.

"I am recording it as shaken."

Seorin raised her hand one last time to confirm each team's position.

Above, Ater raised a hand in answer, and Sern held up the record panel. Far off, the end of Luhai's iron bar struck a wall corner twice. The old machines put their hands to the walls at their assigned axes.

The last android, together with Han Jiwoo, stopped in front of the axis that would shake first.

The old machine in front put its hand to the wall. When Han Jiwoo stood beside it, the android stood between the two of them, and the two Hazran men stood their wall panels upright.

Far off, the circulation hum inside Serakion changed, very faintly.

The polite guidance tone cut out once. When it resumed, it was a little faster, and it said the same thing twice. Serakion's guidance tone was one that never said the same thing twice.

Sern's hand stopped above the record panel, and he listened instead of writing.

"What's it saying?"

Seorin asked.

"It says the route has been reassigned."

Sern said.

"Twice. The same route."

"Why say the same thing twice?"

"Because its first words did not take."

Sern said.

"Or because it did not believe its own first words."

Seorin looked ahead.

Feeling a chill spread down his back, Sion gripped the hilt of his blade.

The last android's optical eye flickered, very briefly. While Han Jiwoo held her breath, Seorin turned her head from the front toward the left axis.

The android took one step forward.
The old machine took its hand off the wall.
The Hazran men's wall panels came up.
Han Jiwoo's hand came up.

This time it didn't come down.

Her hand landed on his shoulder, on the knots. He did not stop. Her hand went forward with that shoulder.

One step.

She let go.
