# Episode 184. Moving Again

They set out quietly.

No one shouted a rallying cry,
and no one went on at length to stir themselves up.

They all knew it already.
What they needed now was not some inflated show of resolve,
but the precision never to miss the same gap again.

Seorin's plan was still alive, exactly as it was.

Who took which layer,
who cut which route,
which team handled retrieval and which the lure,
which axis was the most dangerous,
and at which moment their movements had to lock together.

That frame had not changed.

But once everyone actually started moving into place,
Sion could tell right away.

This was going to be a different execution from the plan they had made before.

Because this time,
the last android was among them.

He was attached to the same axis as Han Jiwoo.

The most unstable axis,
the one most likely to shake,
and so the one where a single small variable could topple the whole balance.

Seorin did not put him at the front.
Nor did she use him like the blade of a head-on breakthrough.

Instead,
she placed him where he could change the flow.

A spot the command-type androids would have no choice but to react to,
where that one reaction could throw off the whole beat.

It was less a way of throwing in strength
than a way of readjusting the battlefield.

Sion felt that the placement was right.

It did not spend the last android like a tool;
it honestly built into the plan
the change his existence had already made.

Han Jiwoo walked right beside him.

She did not hold him up completely.
Doing that seemed as if it would make him into something too weak.

But she kept, to the very end, a distance where one outstretched hand could reach him at once.

She knew he was still unsteady,
and she also knew she must not stop him from walking on his own.

That fine sense of distance
looked like the language that suited the two of them best right now.

The children saw them off from deeper inside until the very moment they left.

Their faces had not quite stopped crying.
But they did not cling and hang on the way they had earlier.

Before the last android turned away,
one child waved a hand, very small.

He looked at that hand for a long while,
then let his optical eye flicker, ever so slightly.

Whether that was an answer or simply a reaction,
no one could say for sure yet.

But at least,
no one who was there thought it was nothing.

That was enough.

All that was left now was to move.

The gray routes of the old layer were quieter than before.

Quiet was not the word for it.
The inner passages of Serakion were always like this.
Too smooth,
too orderly,
and for that very reason they set people on edge.

Even the cross-sections of broken structures were strangely straight,
and the marks of the first battle remained on that clean gray surface
like stains no one had permitted.

Somewhere a low, polite chime passed once.
It was not a loud alarm.
It was closer to a sound that re-sorted the routes
and laid out the flow anew.

Serakion was always wrong in this way.
A world that answered disturbance with tidying first,
caged people with placement before violence,
and held out order before fear.

Passing through that silence,
Sion felt something strange.

It was the same path, clearly.
The same plan.
But this time what ran through it meant something different.

Before, it had been a plan for rescue, destruction and cracks.
But now, mixed into it as well,
was the sense that they would never lose the one they had managed to protect.

Checking the gap ahead, Ater said, low,

"When the first reaction comes, it will change at once."

Everyone had already expected that.

The moment Serakion's androids
sensed the presence of the last android,
they were likely to react differently than usual.

And that very gap
was also the heart of this plan.

Sern added, very low,

"What we are aiming at is not a difference in strength."
"It is a difference in reaction."
"Not breaking a line,
but making it impossible for a line to stay the same line."

Hearing that, Sion quietly held his breath.

He was right.

This fight was not simply one the stronger side would win.
It was a fight over who would first read and use the faint tremor that comes when command meets will,
that strange slip in the beat.

That was why Seorin's plan was more like guerrilla war.
Not winning by pushing through the front,
but keeping Serakion from holding on to perfect efficiency,
while at every gap another team bit off one beat at a time.

Delaying the drop lines,
cutting off the circulation lines,
scattering into single pieces a legion that was supposed to move in one line.

And at the center of it,
walking right beside them, was the last android.

He was still not whole.
His stride was ever so slightly slow,
and the shoulder axis they had fixed did not look fully stable either.

But even so,
he did not stop.

If anything, more quietly and more clearly than before the battle,
he was looking at the path in front of him.

Watching him,
Sion felt again that the last android was no longer simply something that had survived.

He was one of the reasons for the next fight.
And at the same time,
he was the reason that fight had to be different from the last.

Seorin raised her hand one last time to confirm each team's position.

Everyone's eyes met briefly,
and the old machines also scattered to their assigned axes.

The last android, together with Han Jiwoo,
stopped in front of the axis that would shake first.

And at that moment,
far off, the circulation hum inside Serakion changed, very faintly.

The feeling of a polite flow
just now beginning to be rearranged.

Sion felt a chill down his back.

It had come.

This time we came in first,
and this time we chose the ground.

But even so,
Serakion had begun to sense something too.

The last android's optical eye flickered, very briefly.
Han Jiwoo held her breath,
and Seorin's gaze grew sharper.

Inside the old plan,
a new heart had already come in.

And now that heart
was about to strike the first beat of the second battle.
