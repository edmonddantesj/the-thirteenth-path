# Episode 109. The Ones Outside Began to Close Hazran

The ship didn't go far.

It rose about two spans off the ground, slid west, and stopped behind the shadow of a collapsed wall.

That struck Sion as strange, and he went forward.

"Why aren't we going?"

Han Jiwoo had the back of her hand against the coupling.

"To clear the heat layer, I have to pull her up."

"How long?"

"Don't know. Sixteen aboard, the left side's patched together, and the right got scraped earlier."

"Then in the meantime…"

"We hover here."

Han Jiwoo took the back of her hand away and set it back again.

"She really won't last long."

Aka answered quietly.

"She doesn't have to last long."

Han Jiwoo turned her head.

Aka spoke with her hand still on the outer wall.

"Just long enough to send us off."

It was cruel, and it was exact.

This ship didn't need to come fully back to life. For now it only had to hold long enough to carry the people who had to be sent a little farther out.

Hazran was the same. It wasn't the whole city that would live; it only had to hold a little longer, long enough to send people off alive.

Sion took that answer and looked back.

From here he could see Hazran. Two spans up, it was a little higher than where he had been standing before, and that little bit let him see all of it.

Only then did he understand.

The power this ship was raising now wasn't free. It could only raise it while the four were standing out there. If those things reached this place, it would end half-raised.

What those four were earning right now was the time it took this ship to lift.

Sern was sitting on the floor. It was the second time today he had sat down. Ater stood beside him, looking out through the gap in the entrance. It was his stance for reading angles, but even if he read them, there was nothing to use them on.

"How many can you see?"
Sern asked.

"About twelve."

"Are more coming?"

"They are."

Neither of them said what came after that. Even if they said it, there was nothing to do about it from here.

Seorin put her back to the wall and spoke low.

"So we get in like this, and that's the end of it?"

It was less a question than a sneer thrown at herself.

"It's a war we started."

No one could answer right away.

The sentence stabbed the air inside the hull, exactly.

Luhai spoke, leaning against the wall.

"Still, Zahir told us to go."

That was closer to talking himself into it, too. His eyes kept going outside.

This kid was a Hazran person. Watching your own city close from the inside tears a person apart in a different way.

"There's still the smell of people out there,"
Luhai said, low.

At that Sion broke down once more.

It wasn't just the corridor closing now. It was alleys and houses, the smell of somewhere to go back to. To Sion, Hazran was a place he had first come to three days ago, but to this kid it was where he had been born.

"What smell?"
Sion asked.

Luhai took time to answer.

"Water boiling."

"Can you still smell it?"

"No."

Then Luhai added:

"That's why I said it's still there. Because I can't smell it."

Sion held on to those words for a long time.

Nasim's voice came from outside.

"Drag the screens!"
"Pull the inside in!"
"Clear that out!"

The voice wasn't putting things in order to save people anymore. It was the sound of a city being folded shut.

Kael muttered just inside the entrance.

"The left's dead for good now."

He said exactly what he had seen with his own eyes. No lie, no comfort.

"That won't open again."

Hearing that, Sion felt it for real: Hazran was losing its functions one by one, leaving only its last order.

Harun was leaning behind the collapsed line. His body had broken down a great deal, but his gaze hadn't. He couldn't stand in front anymore, yet just his being there showed it wasn't over yet.

Sion stood just inside the entrance and looked down at his own hands.

Until a little while ago they had been hands that held people, braced against metal, caught falling bodies. Now they could do nothing.

That helplessness choked him more rawly than the debt.

We brought the war, and they are the ones closing it.

The moment Sion, without thinking, started to take a step forward, Han Jiwoo caught his arm.

"No,"
she said, low.
"If you go back out, it all falls apart."

Sion clenched his teeth.

"But they're the only ones left."

Han Jiwoo couldn't answer right away either.

That short silence was more honest than anything.

It was true. Only they were left. That was why they had to hold out in here all the harder. The ship had to stay alive, or the choice Hazran had forced on them would be for nothing.

Sion pulled his arm free.

Having pulled it free, he went nowhere. There was nowhere to go.

The inside of the hull was still cramped. Sixteen were pressed to the walls, and no one spoke. The sounds outside didn't reach in here. It was one thin layer of metal, but that one layer swallowed every sound.

That was the strangest thing to Sion. All day today he had known things by sound. Whether the ship was empty or full, how many steps the other side had come, who was striking from below. All of it had been sound.

Now there were only his eyes.

So instead, he began to count.

He had been counting for a while already. Ever since Seorin told him to count while he came up, he hadn't stopped. As long as the numbers rolled around inside his head, his hands didn't shake.

Now that was the only thing he could do.

That was when Aka spoke for the first time, low.

"Keep watching."

Everyone looked her way.

Aka was still looking outside.

"We have to watch to the end."

It was a short thing to say.

Sion thought he knew what she meant.

It meant don't live pretending not to know. It meant don't leave as if running away, and see with your own eyes, to the end, how Hazran closed and who stayed behind to bear what.

Sion was watching.

He counted as he watched.

"Sion."

Aka was looking at Sion. For the first time today.

"Yeah."

"Are you counting?"

"You are, aren't you."

"…Yeah."

"Don't count."

Sion pushed back at once.

"Harun told me to count."

"I know."

"And Seorin told me to count while I came up."

"I know that too."

"Then why?"

Aka waited a moment before she answered.

"If you count, you don't look."

Sion took those few words and couldn't say anything.

It was true.

All day today Sion had counted. He had counted from two hundred and eight down to eight, counted one hour five times, counted the gaps between the blows from below, from ten up to eighteen. Every time he counted, it became bearable.

The reason it had been bearable was that while he counted, he didn't see.

Counting had been his way of holding out, and his way of holding out was his way of not looking.

"Then what do I do?"
Sion asked.

"Look."

"What changes if I look?"

"Nothing changes."

Then Aka added:

"But they know we're watching."

At that, Sion's breath caught once.

Outside, Zahir looked this way. They were checking eyes. Whether they had really boarded, whether they were really ready to leave, whether it was now their turn to close.

Sion met those eyes.

He stopped counting.

As soon as he stopped, his hands shook. Hands that hadn't shaken all day.

He watched with them shaking.

Zahir turned around slowly.

Just before he turned, for a very short moment, he looked not toward the ship but toward the board. The board where Sion had drawn his marks.

What his face looked like couldn't be seen from here.

Then he walked toward the corridor.

Sion watched that back. He watched it without counting.

And the other three went to their places.

Nasim dragged the debris on the left, Nahira set something down low on the inside, and Harun took his body off the wall.

The four didn't look at one another. Not once.

They were people whose placement was already settled, so there was no need to check.

Even then Han Jiwoo didn't take her hand away. She kept setting the back of her hand against the coupling, taking it off, setting it back.

"What are you doing?"
Sion asked.

"Listening."

"To what?"

"For when the left changes its sound."

"And if it does?"

"If it does, we have to stop there."

At that answer Sion looked toward the entrance. Stopping here meant stopping above Hazran.

"And if it doesn't?"

"If it doesn't, we make it over."

Han Jiwoo spoke low from behind him.

"I've got her halfway up."

"How much more?"

"That much again."

Sion took that answer, started to work it out, and stopped.

That much again meant those four had to stand out there that much longer again.

If he counted, he would know how many minutes it was.

Sion didn't count.

Instead he looked outside.

The four were each in their places. The shape the four made was left, very small, in the middle of the collapsed city. Seeing it from above, he could see exactly how small that shape was.

Earlier, when he was inside it, he hadn't known its size. When you're inside, all you see is what's in front of you.

Sion kept looking at that small shape.

Aka came and stood beside him. She didn't say anything.

The two of them watched together.
