# Episode 205. Misaligned Time

Even after Serakion went quiet, the hands did not rest.

They lifted out collapsed panels, cleared away the wreckage of dead sorting lines, and pulled out anchor axes that had been driven in wrong.
People used their hands.
Machines held things up.
Androids brought the remaining alignment devices to a stop, one by one.
There were few words. No one gave orders, yet each did what was in front of them.

Humans who had come down from the upper levels were in the sector the Hazran people had been clearing. People who had been bound inside Serakion, people with marks on their necks and transparent fingertips, sat against the wall a few at a time. When the people of the hidden level brought them water, the hands that took it shook.

A commotion came first, a small one. They thought someone was hurt, but it was not that.

The one who stopped first was Han Jiwoo.

She had been looking toward a collapsed passage, and she slowly set down the scrap of metal in her hand. In front of the passage, between the debris and the dust, stood a child with a mark on the neck and transparent fingertips. The child was looking not at their own feet but to the side, and turned their feet this way, then settled them back.

Han Jiwoo's lips parted, like a breath.

"That child…"

A few heads came up.

A young man standing beside a fallen pillar froze where he stood. He was not from the hidden level but someone who had come down from above, and he could neither let go of the scrap of metal in his hand nor grip it harder. He followed Han Jiwoo's gaze to its end and saw the child, and then the man beside the child who had stopped in the middle of lifting debris.
The man without a younger brother.

"…Brother?"

The word cut through the people between them, late and careful.

Neither of them could move at first. When the man without a younger brother let go of the debris, his hands were empty, and one of them felt, out of habit, at the empty space beside him. He stopped his hand and looked at the young man.

A few steps.
Those few steps.

The younger brother moved first.
One step.
Then another.
Walking or running, there was no telling.

Unable to speak, the older brother barely lifted both arms. When the younger one crashed into them, the older brother's body shook once, hard. He held on to his brother's back and still could not say anything. The younger one did not cry either, but his shoulders shook.

The older brother swallowed a breath through a cracked throat.

"You were alive."

His hand stayed on his brother's back, but his fingers did not lock together.

A little way off, Luhai looked away and, as if it were nothing, nudged a piece of debris with the toe of his boot.

"The ones who make it always find each other way too late, I swear."

No one said anything. While the first recovery hand watched the two of them, its optical eyes stayed there.

A little further off, the woman who came with two children stopped where she stood, holding a water canister, as if she might give way. The canister tipped and a little water spilled, but she did not notice. Where she took one step forward stood the child in front of the passage, the child Han Jiwoo had seen, the child who had been looking to the side.

When the child looked up at her with a face that could not believe it, the woman took the child's cheeks in her trembling hands.
Once.
And again.
She stroked them over and over.
Then she pulled the child into her arms. Her breath gave way.

The child sobbed quietly too and could not let go of their mother's collar. The two children who had stood behind her came over, and there were three. The woman's arms could not hold all three, but she tried anyway.

Han Jiwoo watched the three of them. The child who had pushed the others through in the third rescue and stayed behind, the child who had met her eyes, was now in their mother's arms. Han Jiwoo did not go over; with her back against the wall, she pressed her inner pocket. She did not close her eyes.

The last android stood beside her and looked at the three of them.

His hand went forward, shaped like a hand pushing, and stopped.

"Yeah. The one who pushed. Pushed them through, and they came. Stayed behind."

Han Jiwoo said.

He made no sound, and looked at his own shoulder, the side that sat lower.

In a far sector, one of the people who had come down from above found his sister. Another called a comrade's name three times, and when an answer came on the third, the one who had called laughed with a dazed face. In one passage two people stood holding each other by the shoulders, unable to speak, and the people carrying debris went around the two of them. No one told them to move.

Sion was standing beside the old Hazran man. Holding his plate with its sixteen scratched lines, the man turned it over and looked a long time at the one line on the back, then set his fingernail to it and drew a crossways line over that line. It was not crossing it out; it was one more line added to a line. Turning the plate back to the front, he scratched one line beside the sixteen.

Seventeen.

"There was one child, wasn't there."

Sion said.

"There was one."

The old man said.

"From thirty-six, nine."

He held the plate to his chest.

At the edge of a collapsed hall, an aristocrat robot was lifting a metal frame alone with its long hands. Beneath it, two humans freed from conditioning, with marks on their necks and transparent fingertips, were dragging out shards of dead devices. No thanks, no apology, no words. The robot held the frame in the same posture and did not let go until the two had crawled out, until all the debris was cleared. Its arms did not shake. A robot's arms do not shake.
Still, it did not let go.

Not far off, the first, the second and the wall-side machine lay flat in front of a broken lift axis, changing an auxiliary line for upper-level traffic so that it could lift people and materials from the lower sectors. Beside them, a human who had come down from above was handing over tools, but his hands shook and he dropped a screw twice. The machines did not hurry him, and the second twisted its body on its straightened leg, picked up the screw and set it close to his hand. On the third time, the human spoke, small.

"…Sorry."

The first stopped its hands and checked the state of the axis again, then moved its body a little to one side and left room for him to go on standing there. Whether that was acceptance, or not yet. The human stood in that place and picked up the next screw, and this time he did not drop it.

Han Jiwoo watched those scenes: arms that would not let go of a frame, a shoulder that made room.

Dragging his ankle, Kael went back and forth between sectors, sorting out the repair routes. He placed people and blocked off the stretches most likely to collapse first, so that the children's paths and the androids' working space did not overlap. Whenever he stepped away it showed at once, and twice the flow on the side clearing panels tangled, but both times he reached in first and set the order straight again.

Luhai saw it and tossed out a line.

"See, if you're not around, the line always gets knotted somewhere."

"So don't let it knot."

Kael said.

Sern gave a short nod at that.

"Correct."

Ater had gathered the wreckage of record panels and pieces of approval markers dragged down from the upper level off to one side, and he was checking them one by one; now and then his hand stopped on a broken marker. Looking down at it, he spoke very quietly.

"These can approve nothing now."

Aka, beside him, turned to look, but Ater did not raise his eyes.

"And yet things like this remain for a long time. In the way people's hands shrink back before their own judgment does."

Aka pressed her lips together and did not argue, and a little later she spoke low.

"So we'll have to hold on longer, then. Till they stop shrinking back."

Ater nodded very slightly and set the marker down, broken side down.

The last android was silent, and so were the other androids.
When someone went in under a dangerous panel, they stepped in front of it first.
When heavy debris was lifted, they held up the other side without a word.
When children came close, they eased the angle of their bodies, just a little.

Luhai alone grumbled to the end. When one android stood in front of a collapsed passage and marked the danger zone with its own body, Luhai sighed as he spoke.

"Would it kill you to say something?"

The android turned its head, not toward Luhai, and pointed with one finger at a crumbled crack beneath the children's feet.

Luhai clicked his tongue and waved the children away from it.

"Not there, over here. …Ah, seriously."

The android did not lower its finger until all the children had crossed.

When the day turned over once more, a place was made in one corner of a broken sector where the children could rest.

The Hazran brothers only let go of each other late. Both their eyes were a mess, but no one teased them. The younger one, still holding his brother's sleeve, gave a small laugh.

"You really got old."

The older one answered with a face caught between crying and laughing.

"You're not much better."

A few people around them laughed, quietly and briefly, and the laughter rang on a long time in the hidden level.

Hearing the laughter, Sion looked slowly around at the empty places, at the things that had not come back. The place where the two old machines had stood was not here; it was up above. He let out a breath.

At the end of his gaze, the child who had first set lines on the last android's face was hovering beside him again today. The child did not speak to him, only stood beside him a long while, then looked up at the back of his hand and brushed the powder off their own hands. The last android did not move, and he did not leave.
The child did not leave either.

From far off, Aka watched the two of them, slowly lowered her eyes, and opened them again.

From far off came Luhai's voice.

"Don't stack it like that over there. If it falls, we start from scratch again."

Grumbling, metal knocking on metal.
Children whispering, the grinding of a machine shifting a panel.
A breath passing between it all.

The old Hazran man stood his plate against the wall with the seventeen lines showing and the back, where one line had a line drawn across it, turned to the wall. When he took his hand off the plate, the plate stood on its own.
