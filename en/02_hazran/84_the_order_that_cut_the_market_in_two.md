# Episode 84. The Order That Cut the Market in Two

Zahir was not at the front line.

He stood one section back, where the whole market could be seen. It was the same spot Sion had seen a few days earlier. A place where the return line, the outer water channel, and the center of the market were all visible at once.

Back then, Sion had thought it was a spot chosen for watching what came in.

Seeing it now, it wasn't. It was a spot chosen for seeing what could be given up so the rest would hold together.

Zahir spoke low.

"We cut the central market line."

No one understood right away.

Nasim was the first to raise his head.

"What?"

"We give up the central market line."

Zahir didn't look away.

"Cut the inner power cable and close the west corridor."

Hearing that, Sion looked toward the central market line.

A few days ago Seorin had haggled over a price there. She had argued for a long while over a single heat-shedding cord, and Nasim had laughed beside her. Stalls had stood in long rows on both sides, and people had bumped shoulders as they passed.

It was the widest line in Hazran. The place where goods passed, people passed, and words passed.

Cutting it wasn't dividing the front. It was tearing the city in half.

Nasim took a step toward Zahir.

"Cut that and the inside gets blocked too."

"I know."

"Then people can't get out either."

"That's why we get them out now."

Hearing that short back-and-forth, Sion's back went rigid.

Sion swallowed once. Even after he swallowed, his chest did not settle.

Harun had said nothing.

Sion looked at Harun's face. It was a face whose calculation was already done. It had probably been done before Zahir spoke.

"It can be done,"

Harun said, low.

Nasim turned toward Harun.

"But the people left in the west."

Nasim's voice cracked.

"We're throwing them away."

Harun didn't avoid his eyes.

"No."

"What do you mean, no?"

"We have to start getting them out now."

Both of them were right.

Sion couldn't choose which of the two was correct. If Nasim was right, Harun was throwing people away, and if Harun was right, Nasim was throwing time away. Because both were right, both had a reason to hate the other.

"How many minutes?"

Nasim asked.

Harun didn't answer right away.

"I asked how many minutes."

"As long as it takes to sweep them in."

"I'm asking how many minutes that is."

"That's for you to decide."

Nasim looked at Harun once more.

Sion knew why that answer was cruel. Harun hadn't given him any time. If Nasim swept them in fast, that many people would come out, and if he was slow, that many would stay inside. The cutting wasn't in Nasim's hands, but how many came out was.

It was time no one was giving.

"Twelve,"

Nasim said.

"Get them all out while I count twelve."

It wasn't a number anyone had counted out for him. It was a number Nasim had just set with his own mouth.

Zahir explained no further.

"Nasim."

"…"

"Sweep in everyone from the west."

Nasim chewed back a curse, swallowed it, turned, and ran.

"Harun."

"I know."

"Sion."

Sion registered his own name half a beat late.

"You hold between the inside and the west,"

Zahir said.

"Keep it linked, with no gaps."

Sion nodded and ran. It was no time to ask more.

He had thought the market would get louder, but it was the opposite.

When a truly bad order comes down, people go quiet for a moment. The people of Hazran didn't shout; each went toward what they had to do. Nobody asked.

And then the city began to divide itself.

Kael lent a hand to the side tearing off the binding plates of the central pillar. Ater pointed out where the power cable had to be cut so the other side couldn't use it. Sern was still buying time at the front, and Harun kept changing the corridor angles so he could buy one more beat.

Hazran hadn't built the central pillar.

It had been here already, and they had stood it up and used it. They had joined two old hull frames, clamped the gap between them with binding plates, and then hung everything they could on top, stall awnings and corridor roofs alike. Until a few days ago, Sion had thought it was just a pillar.

Tearing off a binding plate took six people.

Four set levers and two pressed down from above. At first it didn't come out. One more pull, and the plate buckled and one side lifted first.

Into the lifted gap, someone else shoved an iron bar.

The first binding plate came out.

As it came out, what had rested on top sank down. The sound was low and long and went on without an end. Even after the big pieces settled, small things kept sliding down for a long while.

And then something rose.

At first Sion didn't know what it was. He thought it was dust.

For dust, it glittered.

It was powder ground out as two metal plates, clamped together for a long time, scraped against each other. Pressed for years, it had been ground loose in the single moment they came apart.

The powder rose and didn't come down.

The day's heat was still rising from the floor. The powder rode on top of it and floated. Floating, it spread sideways, and the upper part of the corridor went hazy.

Sion raised his hand and looked. Nothing settled on the back of it. It only floated and didn't come down.

He saw it, but he didn't know what it was doing.

The people tearing at the plates didn't look either. They were busy tearing, and there was no one to look at that now.

Only Harun looked up.

After looking, he looked above the opposite corridor too. It was hazy there as well. It was the face of someone measuring by eye, but he didn't say what he was measuring.

Then, without a word, he went back to fixing the corridor angles.

In the inner section, Aka spoke low.

"It has to split to live."

Han Jiwoo pressed harder on the coupling.

"What's split doesn't join back."

There was the sound of the second binding plate coming out.

Sion ran to the west corridor.

It was already tangled there.

Three people who were left still couldn't let go of their baggage, and Nasim was all but tearing it from their hands and throwing it aside. Luhai pushed two small children to either side and opened a way.

Seorin was watching the rear. It was where the sound was coming from.

"If you don't go now, you get closed in with it,"

Seorin said sharply.

As soon as she said it, she turned around. She didn't look at Sion.

Sion stood between the inside and the west. It was the place Zahir had given him.

Only once he got here did he understand what "keep it linked, with no gaps" meant. When the line broke, the people behind couldn't see the front, so they stopped. Stopping meant being late, and being late meant not getting out.

Sion turned so he could see both sides from where he stood.

He raised one hand to signal the inside and turned the other way to watch the end of the west line.

It broke once.

Around the middle, one person fell and the people behind stopped. From where they stopped, everyone further back was shoved all at once.

Sion shouted.

"Keep going! Keep moving!"

The fallen person was pulled up by those beside them, and the people behind started walking again. It took a few steps to get walking again.

Those few steps grew the further back it went. Two steps' stop at the front became ten steps at the very back.

Only then did Sion understand why he had been put here.

Here, no one could keep it from breaking. Joining it again every time it broke was Sion's job.

Sion laid his hand on the shoulder of the person in front. He didn't push. He only laid it there, and the person moved half a step forward.

A person who has stopped will resist if pushed. If touched, they go on their own.

From the third time on, his hand went out before he thought.

Nasim was counting.

He counted while he shoved people in. He didn't count out loud, only moved his lips, but Sion could read the shape of them.

It was nine.

People were still coming out from the end of the west line. He couldn't see whether it was three or four.

It became ten.

Sion took two steps toward the end of the line and stopped. He couldn't leave this spot empty. If it emptied, the inside and the west would be cut off from each other.

Eleven.

The last two came around the bend. One ran and one limped.

Nasim stopped counting.

He didn't get to twelve. Sion saw Nasim's lips stop, and he didn't know whether they stopped because everyone was out or because he couldn't count any further.

Far off, Harun's side raised the signal to clear the last angle of the central line.

Sion looked back one last time.

The line where they had haggled over prices a few days ago was there. The stall frames still stood, and a few awning cords were left. Powder floated above it all, and the far end couldn't be seen.

The city was now cutting the widest part of its own body.

Sion still didn't know what it would gain by cutting it.

He couldn't see anything gained except the floating powder. That didn't even seem like a gain. It was just what had come out as things broke.
