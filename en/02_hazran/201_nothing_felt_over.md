# Episode 201. Nothing Felt Over

The highest hall was quiet.

None of the sounds they had run into on the way up were here. No metal on metal, no plates slamming shut, no guidance tone.
Only Sion's own breathing reached his ears.
He held his breath. He could still hear it.

No one moved right away.

At the summit of Serakion, they had confirmed that the figure was empty.
What should have collapsed had not collapsed, and there was nothing to charge at.

Sion was looking at the figure straight ahead, neat and exact.
Its head was still turned toward him. Because he was the last one who had spoken.
He turned his head away, but the figure's head did not follow. It was not a request.
When he looked at the figure again, its head was turned toward him; it had never turned away.
He closed his eyes and opened them.
Nothing had changed.

Seorin was the first to look away, and without managing a word, she sheathed her blade.
Even after it was sheathed she left her hand on the hilt, then took it away.
Not knowing where to put that hand, she pressed it to her side.

Sern kept looking at the figure.
He raised the record panel, lowered it, raised it again, but there was nothing to write.
He went right up beneath the figure, looked at the structure, and walked around behind it.
There was nothing on the figure's back either, not even a seam.
He came back around to the front.

"Did you think there'd be something back there?"

Kael asked.

"A line."

Sern said.

"The guidance tone was attached to the wall by a line. That is not attached to anything."

"Then how does it talk?"

"I do not know."

"Truly… shallow."

He said it very slowly. The fingers holding the record panel pointed once toward the figure, then dropped.

Ater spoke a little later.

"So an empty proxy body was kept up as if it were the approving authority, all the way to the end."

He did not take his eyes off the figure.

"I believed I had come to correct a wrong arrangement. But at the very top, there was not even anyone to answer for the arrangement."

"What about your house? Was there someone?"

Han Jiwoo asked.

"There was."

Ater answered.

"Someone wrong. Wrong, but someone was there."

"Is that better?"

"I do not know."

Ater said.

"You could tell someone wrong that they were wrong. That."

He looked at the figure.

"There is nowhere to say it to."

When Han Jiwoo dropped her gaze, both her hands were clenched.
Her nails had dug into her palms, and when she opened her hands there were four red marks. She hadn't known they hurt.

She looked at the empty space beside her, between Kael and herself.
It was where the two old machines had stood, the two that had been there the whole way up.
Now there was nothing there.
The hall's light shone on the spot. With no dust, there was no mark either.

Han Jiwoo spoke very softly.

"It wasn't supposed to end like this."

"What wasn't?"

Sion asked.

"The two of them. They burned up to show us that."

"They didn't burn to show us anything."

Seorin said.

"They burned to make it stop. It stopped."

"And after it stopped, this."

"Nobody knew what came after it stopped. Not those two. Not us."

Han Jiwoo looked at the empty space and said nothing more.

Sion looked at the empty space and at the figure, and still he couldn't say anything.

Kael's jaw and shoulders were tight. His body had not finished its fight, and there was nowhere in front of him to put his strength.
He took a few steps forward and stopped, looked up at the figure and made a fist, but there was nothing to hit.
He struck the edge of the pedestal the figure sat on, once, with his palm, and it made a small sound, but the hall did not give that sound back.
He looked at his palm. A curse should have come out, but it didn't.

The last android stood in front of the figure for a long time with his hand on his chest plate, not moving.
The light on his face was dimmer than a moment ago, but it hadn't gone out.

Han Jiwoo looked at his back, at the knot, at the one that had come loose.
She didn't go to him. Not now.

Outside the hall, the aristocrat robots were still standing.
The one whose finger had been trembling was still trembling.

"What about them?"

Kael asked.

"They're standing."

Seorin said.

"They're standing because nobody's told them to stand."

"And if they were told to go?"

"By who?"

"Us."

Seorin looked out through the door.

"We're nothing to them. We're not approval."

The one with the trembling finger turned its head inward, through the door.
Not toward the figure, but toward the last android.
Then it turned back again.

Sion looked back, at the door and at what lay below it.
Sound was still coming up, more than before.
Not one set of feet, but all different feet.

"Go down and that's what we'll hear."

Sion said.

"That sound is Serakion now."

Seorin said.

"Not this place."

Sion listened to the sound, but no tears came. They never got that far.
When he looked down at his palm, fresh blood was seeping over the dried blood.

Seorin finally spoke.

"…We have to go down."

It was short, and none of her usual firmness was in it.
Still, they were words. Words to move by.

"What does going down now get us?"

Kael asked.

"Nothing. But there are people down there. There are kids. There are the ones making that sound."

Sern asked very slowly.

"Is this… over?"

No one could answer right away.
The figure stayed where it was, and from below, sound came up.
Something in the middle of collapsing and something laid bare, both.

Ater spoke very low.

"It is over. At least, in the sense that that form can no longer imitate anyone's approval."

He paused a moment, then added more quietly.

"But it is not settled."

"So what's left?"

"The lines."

Ater said.

"Everything that thing said it approved. There will be lines left on every level below. That is empty, but the lines are not."

"Can the lines be cut?"

"They can."

Sern said.

"The same way we cut the guidance tone. Both are inside the walls."

"Then that's something we can do."

Han Jiwoo said.

"It is something we can do."

"So there's one thing."

Han Jiwoo spoke quietly.

For the first time in this hall, Sern wrote one line on the record panel.
Lines.

Sion looked back at the figure once more instead of answering.
Its head came around toward him, exactly.

"It's over, but…"

He said it very quietly.

"It doesn't feel over."

No one answered that.

Kael looked at the figure one last time.

"Does that thing have a name?"

"The President."

Ater said.

"A title. There was no name. Not in the records either."

"Not even a number?"

"Not even a number."

Kael didn't ask anything more.

While Han Jiwoo closed her eyes and opened them again, Kael let out a long breath.
Sern closed the record panel and read the figure no further.

The last android slowly lowered his hand from his chest plate.
Then he took one step back from the figure.
A sound came from his shoulder axis, and the hall gave it back faintly from the ceiling.
Kael looked up at the ceiling, then at his own palm.

That one step.

It was the first step anyone had taken backward in the hall.
Han Jiwoo saw that step and went to him.
She tied the loosened knot and gave it a tug, and it held.

"Let's go."

She said.

He turned away from the figure, but the figure's head did not turn away.
It was not a request.

When Seorin walked toward the door, Sion, Sern, Kael, Ater, Han Jiwoo, and the last android followed.

Seven.

The seven went out the door and between the aristocrat robots. The robots didn't move aside, and they didn't block them either.
The seven passed between them, past the one with the trembling finger.
Its optical eyes followed the last android, the light on his face.
One beat.
They didn't turn back.

The optical eyes Han Jiwoo saw were watching him, not the figure.

"That one too."

She said.

"Later."

Seorin said.

"For now, go down."

Han Jiwoo looked at it once more; its finger was still trembling.

The hall stayed as it was, perfect.
The figure, the hands on its knees, the exact turn of its head facing where no one stood.
The door did not close. There was no one to tell it to.
