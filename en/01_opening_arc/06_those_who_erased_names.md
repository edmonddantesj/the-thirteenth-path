# Episode 06. Those Who Erased Names

Jun Aster.

Those few letters left in the middle of the scorched record-panel fragment were enough to change all the air in the passage.

Below the name, a half-burned verdict notation was attached.

**Betrayal. Incineration.**

Sion held the fragment in his hand and could not move for a while. An erased name usually stays erased to the end. A name burned once vanishes not only from the records but from people's mouths. So a name that had been taboo for so long coming back like this, at a dead archivist's last place, was close to a nightmare.

Seorin was the first to find her voice.

"Insane."

That one word was so exact that Sion, if anything, couldn't raise his head.

Sern, standing opposite, said nothing either.
He looked at Ater before he looked at the record panel.
His lordship had gone still. It was a very short silence, but Sern knew. Ater Valkar rarely stopped like that. Least of all in front of a single name.

Ater opened his mouth, very slowly.

"Look at the fragment."

It was said to Sion, but it also sounded like something he was saying to himself. Don't get worked up, don't judge by feeling first, look at the record first: an old habit. A way he had learned until it was in his body, all his life, in House Valkar and in the Empire Approval Bureau.

Hearing that, Sion finally let out a short laugh.

"Even now, that's the first thing out of your mouth?"

Seorin took it up at once.

"A name just came back from the dead, and the gentleman says feelings can wait, the record comes first."

Sern said, low.

"Feelings prove nothing."

"Something's been proven, though."

This time Sion raised his head.
The eyes that had been on the fragment until a moment ago now looked straight ahead.

"One thing, at least. That someone tried to erase this name all the way."
Sion looked down at the edge of the fragment.
"And that instead of getting rid of it all at once, they tried to kill it by cutting it off here and there."

Ater did not answer that directly. Instead he lowered his gaze to the floor beside the body. The archivist's final posture, the broken capsule, the scratched preservation room door, the access terminal pried loose by force, and the automatic preservation device that was not yet entirely dead. Feelings aside, the scene was speaking plainly.

Someone had come here.
Someone had tried to take something out.
And the archivist had managed, to the very end, to leave part of it behind.
It was a record that would be finished if it all went over, so he had split it up to the last so it could never go over in one piece.

Sern moved first.
He knelt in front of a preservation terminal two steps from the body and checked its dying screen.

"One device is alive."

Sion shifted his eyes there at once too.
The terminal's surface was cracked and its lower half scorched, but the core inside seemed not quite dead; it was blinking a faint blue light.

Ater asked.

"Can it be recovered?"

Sern answered.

"From the device alone, it is difficult. But there are traces that someone pushed something into it at the end."

The words were barely out before Sion was searching near the archivist's hand again. From a crack in the floor caked with black ash and dried blood came two more very thin record-panel shards. One was completely dead; of the other, only a corner remained.

Looking at the body, Seorin said, low.

"He never let go, right to the end."

At that, Sion's hands paused for a moment.
Black ash was still packed between the archivist's stiffened fingers. The traces of holding on, prying loose, trying to push something in, to the last. It was the hand of a man who could not let go of the work until he died.

Sion murmured, very quietly.

"He carried all of it alone."

Seorin looked at the body, then turned her eyes away.

"If he hadn't been alone, he wouldn't have ended up here."

Sern clipped a temporary connector to the underside of the device, and Ater used his wrist terminal to force the dying authority structure awake. Sion fitted one of the shards from the floor crack into the slot Sern pointed to and pushed it in.

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then, soon after, broken sentences rose into the air above the terminal.

At first there was only noise.
Broken timestamps, collapse alarms, incomplete approval numbers, deletion-failure flags.

Only after that did a voice come in.

A rough, cracked voice, on the edge of death.

The archivist's.

"…restore… the sequence… no… the sequence has to be seen first…"

The voice broke off, then went on again.
Most of the middle was broken, and that made it all the more desperate.

"…even if the name… survives… if the sequence dies… the verdict changes…"

Something in Sion's eyes wavered.
Seorin, too, shut her mouth without a word for the first time.

Sern was reading the broken sentences floating in the air, and Ater, more slowly than him but more deeply, was taking in what the words meant.

The voice came alive one last time.

This time it was much shorter.

"…those who erased the names… the history…"

Noise.

"…wrote it."

And one last sentence was left in the air.

**Original approval sequence mismatch**
**Restoration withheld. Reason: higher seal intervention**

The last error sentence left in the air wavered a moment longer, then spread blue across the cracked terminal and was gone.

No one spoke right away.
A single name coming back was big enough on its own, and the voice the archivist had just left hinted at far more than that.
Each of them dimly felt it now: what they would have to chase might not be a single line of text, but a severed order and the scenes it had left behind.
But there was no time to put that into words here.

Suddenly a short warning tone jumped from the underside of the dying terminal.

Sern was the first to turn his eyes.

"My lord."

A single red line spread across the terminal's surface.
The playback was already over, yet the device was pushing its last remaining authority signal outward.
A signal that a long-hidden record had been accessed again.

Ater reached for the terminal at once, but he was too late.
The red marker flashed once more, then died where it was.

Sion swore under his breath.

"So we're not the only ones who saw it now."

"It is worse than that."

Sern already had his wrist terminal on.
The Empire Approval Bureau's official lines were still quiet.
But farther out than that, a thin contact network that never shows up even in official reports was stirring first.

One, two, three.

The Black Covenant descendant line.
The outer ring of the resonance-net.
Old eyes planted in the port's outer reaches.

Sern's eyes sank, very slightly.

"They are coming."

Ater read that look and asked.

"From which side?"

Sern answered without closing the screen.

"Not the Empire alone."

That one short sentence changed the air in the passage completely.

"What did you just say," Sion asked. "Black what?"

Sern closed the screen.

"You are better off not knowing."

"I hate that answer the most."

"Then I will put it this way." Sern chose his words a moment before answering. "Those names are not on the Bureau's official list. But they always move before the official list does."

Seorin took that up.

"Then they'll come from above, and from below too."

Sion looked at her.

"Below?"

"With an access like this, it won't be just the top of the Alliance Outer Route Administration moving."
Seorin spoke fast, and her head had already moved on to the next square.
"Port brokers, go-betweens, private surveillance ships, they'll all take the money and latch on. It means the ones coming to stop us aren't on just one line."

Ater said, low.

"The Empire will close the gates first."

"And the other side will seep in through the gap."

Seorin took it up at once.
She was no longer looking at the body or the record. Instead she was reading the layout of the passage, the exit at the back of the preservation room, the piping overhead, the crack in the side wall and the spacing of the footsteps outside, all at once.

"Sion."

"Yeah."

"From here on, understanding comes later. Get out alive, then read."

Sion drew a breath.
She was right. The moment he dug through more fragments here, the record might survive and the people might not.

Sern was already into a calculation of a different kind.
The time left before the Empire's official blockade line closed, the port's outer surveillance routes, the possible escape passage the Black Covenant descendants had last left open, and the odds of survival for these four moving together versus moving apart.

The answer was unpleasantly clear.

"My lord."

"Speak."

"We must leave together."

At that, Sion's face was the first to change.

"Now there's something I didn't want to hear."

Sern did not blink.

"If we move separately, we will be taken one at a time."

Seorin laughed.

"You do make it sound pretty. It just means all four of us are caught in the same pursuit net."

"That is correct."

Ater was silent for a very short moment.
The situation itself was already abnormal: a man of the Empire Approval Bureau having to slip out in one bundle with two field officers of the Alliance Outer Route Administration. But the signals outside were saying it. It was not only the official order on the move now. The official order and unofficial greed had caught the same scent at the same time.

He reached his conclusion.

"We withdraw first."

Sion needled him at once.

"Approval granted, then."

"This is not a situation to be pleased about."

"That I agree with."

The sound of metal striking metal in the outer passage came closer.
This time from several directions. One sound of something shutting on the upper level, two sets of footsteps running rough from below, and one fine vibration approaching along the outer wall at the back.

Seorin marked the directions with her hand at once.

"The front's blocked. The top's closing too. The back wall of the preservation room, there's a crack there, right? I saw it earlier."

Sion turned at once.

"The ventilation duct?"

"Yeah. Fits one person at a time."

Sern added his calculation right away.

"The end of the duct comes out under the outer maintenance bridge. The official plans show it as blocked, but on the resonance-net's old waypoint tables it is still open."

Hearing that, Sion turned to look at Sern.

"Why would you know that?"

Sern answered.

"There is always at least one person who knows a road you do not."

Then, a beat later, he added.

"About that name earlier."

Sion was already turning toward the crack.

"Is this the time to ask about that?"

"No. That is why I am asking now. Later, you would not answer."

Sion didn't answer.

Seorin stepped half a pace in between the two of them.

"You said the end of the duct is under the maintenance bridge. How many minutes."

"Four minutes."

"Then ask in four minutes."

Sern asked nothing more. He already knew he would not be able to ask in four minutes either.

Seorin looked at Sern and gave a short laugh.

"Insufferable, but competent."

"The same goes for you."

Ater was already recovering the capsule shards left by the body and the terminal core.
Sion, too, tucked the fragment bearing Jun Aster's name deeper inside. The record was a later problem now. Whether they could get it out came first.

Outside the passage entrance, someone shouted.

"Inside's open!"

Right after, an impact like a gunshot struck the metal wall.
Dust came showering down.

"Go!"

This time Seorin was the first to move.

Sion looked at her back for a moment. The justification request was still alive, and if they walked out with this, one more name would be attached to that line. And yet she was always the one who ran first.

The rust at the edge of the crack had been scraped off inward. Fresh scrape marks.

"The one who locked it went out this way too."

Sion followed her, Sern beside him found the direction in which the crack actually opened, and Ater, last of all, looked back once. The dead archivist's place, the darkened terminal, the half-open preservation room door. He could not look long at a place it was already too late for.

Without another word, the four pushed themselves into the crack in the back wall of the preservation room.
