# Episode 30. The Place Not Owned

Even when the hull came fully away from the structure's outer wall, the air inside had not yet shaken off all the tremor of the site they had just left.

Han Jiwoo moved quickly between the pilot's seat and the engine panel, reading the ship's condition. One side of the lower hull had taken residual vibration from the collapse just now, and one of the external fixing shafts was answering slower than usual. Not bad enough to blow right away, but not in a state to hold out comfortably for long either. Right now this ship was tuned for barely getting out alive; it was not a body fit for running a straight route with a pursuit on its tail.

"I can't bring the speed up yet,"
Han Jiwoo said, low.
"One more hit to the fixing shaft and the turning angle goes off."

Seorin eased off the wall and asked.

"Pursuit ships."

Sern answered at once.

"No direct reaction yet. But the resonance from the judgment unit's restart remains."

Ater added, low,

"Before any direct blockade, the surrounding approval network is likely to read the abnormal signal."

Sion only listened quietly to all of it.

There were six people aboard. A little while ago the number itself had felt abnormal, but now, oddly, no one said it aloud. Everyone was looking at other problems first. How to carry the sentences they had read forward, where to break for before a pursuit caught on, when and how to open the larger fragment Kael had brought. Problems that came before any headcount had already arisen.

Kael sat leaning in the farthest corner of the hull. He had not fully settled in, but he wasn't sitting up like someone about to jump back out either. He still hadn't let go of the fragment, and his eyes followed the movements of the people inside the hull more often than the dark beyond the slit. The eyes of someone who had leaned toward the side that survives, but had not yet given himself fully to either side.

Seorin spoke first.

"Let's cut down the options."

Han Jiwoo let out a breath that was almost a snort.

"Nothing to cut. Go into a port like this and we get flagged on the spot."

Sern lowered his head slightly.

"That is correct. If we take a fixed route after setting off a reaction like that, either the Empire or the Alliance can read the pattern quickly."

Ater put it more coldly.

"Going straight to Elia Vern's repository must be ruled out."

No one argued with that.

Sion, his back to the wall, closed his eyes and opened them. Normally, Elia's face would have been the first thing to come to mind by now. Someone who could read, who could hide things, who knew the grain of a leftover value. Until now, taking a fragment to her would have been the fastest way. But now it was also a way they couldn't take precisely because it was fast. Once a reaction this big had been touched off, someone who could read the way Elia could would be the first name in the other side's calculations too.

Seorin said quietly,

"Go to the repository and it's over."

"I know,"
Sion answered.
"That was the first thing I thought of too."

"Which is why it's even more out."

The line was less a jab than a settling of the matter.

At the end of that exchange, Kael cut in for the first time.

"Elia,"
he said, his voice low and settled.
"That the name of the reading hand?"

Sion neither denied it nor confirmed it right away. Only after a short silence did he say,

"Someone who can read."

Kael didn't ask more. Instead he ran his thumb once along the edge of the fragment, as if that short answer was enough. What mattered now was less who Elia was than the plain fact that someone like that was needed.

Ater looked at Sion.

"Is there another way?"

Sion still didn't answer right away. Instead he lowered his head a little, as if thinking, and his hand went searching through his coat pocket out of habit. His fingertips slid past once, then caught on a small piece of paper that had been pressed flat between the inner folds for a long time.

Sion's hand stopped, very briefly.

Han Jiwoo raised an eyebrow.

"What's that."

Sion didn't unfold the paper right away. The feel of it was new to him. It was paper he had no memory of putting there.

Seorin asked first.

"Is it yours?"

"I don't know,"
Sion said, low.
"I've never seen it before."

Sern spoke with his voice held down.

"When we left the repository, there was one moment when Elia Vern came close to you."

Sion looked up.

Sern went on.

"I did not realize she had passed you anything then. But looking at it now, I believe that is when she put it there."

This time Kael too took his eyes off the fragment and looked over. Ater watched only Sion's fingertips without a word, and Sern closed his mouth again.

Only then did Sion slowly unfold the paper.

Inside, with no other explanation, were just two words.

**Hazran**

And one line below it.

**Aka**

Han Jiwoo reacted almost by reflex.

"Hazran?"
She let out a breath.
"The one out in the desert belt?"

Sion didn't take his eyes off the paper.

"You know it?"

"Sure I do."
Han Jiwoo gave something close to a snort of a laugh.
"A place you'd be embarrassed to call a port. Scrap rolling around half buried in sand, and fake tags moving faster than real ones."

Seorin's expression stiffened very slightly, then eased.

"I remember now,"
she said, low.
"A frontier no one's law ever quite reaches. Sand, wrecked hulls, moving markets, smuggled fuel. Easy to hide in, filthy to get out of alive."

Ater looked from one line on the paper to the other.

"What is Aka?"

No one could answer right away.

Sern said quietly,

"It is likely a personal name. But nothing in our records matches it at first look."

Kael asked, low.

"The reading hand left this?"

Sion was silent a moment, not even able to fold the paper.

"It's that handwriting,"
he said quietly.
"But I don't remember her giving it to me."

Seorin glanced at Sion.

"She'd have known your habits."

It was short, but it was enough. The habit of his hand going to his pocket first whenever he was stuck. If she had slipped it in ahead of time, counting even on a moment like this one, then the lack of explanation was, if anything, just like her.

Han Jiwoo looked at the paper once more and said,

"One planet, one name. Damn, that's so Elia."

"Even so, we now have a direction,"
Sern said.

Ater still did not take his eyes off "Aka."

"It is too little."

"On purpose, probably,"
Sion said, folding the paper.
"Write any more than that right now, and getting caught would've come faster."

Before he had even finished, Sern's eyes jumped to the panel.

"Reaction incoming."

Han Jiwoo turned at once.

"What is."

"Approval network to the rear. Reigniting."

"Pursuit ship?"

"Yes." Sern kept his eyes on the screen. "But this is not coming for us."

Sion raised his head.

"What do you mean."

"The angle is not on us. It is on the structure." Sern's voice dropped. "What they read was not us. It was the judgment unit that came back to life earlier."

Ater understood first.

"…We are the ones who switched it on."

"Yes."

The cabin went quiet.

Sion looked out through the slit. The structure they had just escaped was already only a shape in the dark, and the reaction that had come alive so briefly somewhere inside it would have died again by now.

It was dead, and still the noise of it had carried.

"How many seconds was it on?" Sion asked.

"Eleven seconds, to be exact."

"Eleven seconds is enough to call them."

"Yes."

Kael said, low, from the corner,

"That's why I went around alone."

No one answered.

"Every time I read a fragment, it calls them this much. Bring people along and they get caught just as much."

"But today you brought people along," Sion said.

"Yeah."

"Why."

"Because reading alone, I can have it on for eleven seconds and still read nothing."

The next moment, one side of the hull shook, short and hard.

Han Jiwoo threw herself toward the pilot's seat, letting out a breath like a swallowed curse.

"Shit, they've already fired."

The warning tone sounded late. A red line spread at a slant across the wall panel. What had just grazed past wasn't a direct hit, but it meant there was no time left to wait either.

Seorin pushed off the wall and said,

"Think later. Stay alive first."

Ater was already pulling the side panel over to fold away the approval traces, and Sern had begun reading the pursuit angle. Kael got up and tucked the fragment away out of sight.

Sion, the folded paper in his fist, said,

"We go to Hazran."

Instead of answering, Han Jiwoo woke the engine roughly.

"If we're done talking, hold on tight. From here on, fall off and you really die."

The hull tilted hard once.

Moments ago they had been talking about where to go. Now there wasn't even room to worry about that. A desert planet, one unknown name, and the two words Elia had quietly left behind. The pursuit ships gave them no time to hold on to any of it longer.

All that remained was not losing their direction while they ran.

The hull slowly began to bend off its course.

Not a line running straight ahead, but a gray route that deliberately skimmed past a field of dead freighter wrecks and then cut its angle again from there. No one would call it fast. Instead it was a route that never left a single trail, a route that made straight-line pursuit miserable, a route the people of the gray zones had learned with their bodies to survive a long time.

Hazran was not close enough to reach right away. But if they abandoned the fixed route and rode the gray belt behind the heat layer, they could blur the pursuit's calculations for a while at least, as they closed in on the frontier. What they needed now was not a fast arrival, but to slip in there alive.

Beyond the slit, instead of starlight, a long band of dust streamed past.

Sion put the folded paper back in his inside pocket. Once it was in, he pressed down on it once.

Thin.

It had been like that when he first put the fragment in, at the dock. Then it had been one thin thing; now it was one thin thing with a sheet of paper added.

That was all that had grown.
