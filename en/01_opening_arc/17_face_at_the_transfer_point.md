# Episode 17. The Face at the Access Point

The shuttle stopped a little later than scheduled.

It was not a big difference, but on routes like this, that little always got on your nerves.
Sion opened his eyes the moment the engine tremor died down, and Seorin sat up almost at the same time. Sern had never fully fallen asleep to begin with, and Ater had only had his eyes closed; he was already awake.

Yona spoke from the cockpit.

"We're here."

The door had not opened yet, but the air inside the ship had already changed.
Not the sealed closeness of being in motion, but the particular, stilled tension of having docked onto some outside structure. Through the wall came the metallic pressure of an old hull and a worn docking ring locking into each other.

Sion asked first.

"How's the side we latched onto?"

Yona ran her eyes over the panel and answered.

"It's alive, at least."

Seorin made a face.

"That's not reassuring at all."

"Out here, that much is good news."

It sounded like a joke, but it was not entirely one.
An outer access point was, true to the name, less a living port than a place where routes that had failed to die were forced to keep connecting. The power not being completely out already counted as half a success.

When the door opened, cold air pushed into the narrow cabin.

Outside was even emptier than the last access level.
Only every few sections did a ceiling light still work, and on the walls of the long access bridge, faint marks remained of dock numbers erased long ago. There were almost no official signs; instead, old direction arrows and painted-over warnings were stacked layer on layer. It was the kind of space where no one would remember who had last called it an official facility. The reason it was abandoned and still not entirely dead was that connection rules and equipment from different eras had half overlapped here and held on.

Ater stopped short the moment he took a step outside.
A structure long abandoned, yet unable to die completely because it was needed.

Yona pointed ahead with her chin.

"As far as there. Less talking."

Not far off, in a dark space at the end of the access bridge, something like a waiting bay, someone stood leaning against the wall.

At first he took it for the shadow of the structure.
The light was too dim to make out a face, and the line of the body blended into the wall. Only after two more steps did Sion realize it was a person, and in the same moment he realized who.

"Wow."

A disbelieving laugh slipped out of him.

"You actually came yourself?"

The woman leaning there slowly lifted her head.

Hair cut short and pushed up, a work jacket smudged here and there with grease and metal dust, an insulating band wrapped carelessly around one wrist. Her expression was bored, but her eyes were not bored at all. They were the eyes of someone who, the instant she saw a thing, measured its price, its condition and its risk all at once.

Han Jiwoo looked Sion up and down once and spoke.

"Why do you look like that?"

Sion huffed a laugh.

"That's a cold first thing to say to someone you're glad to see."

"I am glad,"
Han Jiwoo said, dry.
"You made it back alive. But you look like you paid way too much to do it."

Seorin gave a small laugh beside him.

"Nothing but the truth, right out of the gate."

Only then did Han Jiwoo's eyes move to Seorin.

"You came too."

"Looks like it."

"Then this job really is a pain."

"Yeah. A big one."

It was only a few short exchanges, but Ater could tell right away.
This woman knew Sion well. She had known him long, had seen him many times, and knew, at the very least, what kind of face he wore when he came carrying something dangerous. And she was no stranger to Seorin either.

Sern looked at Han Jiwoo's hands without a word.
On the backs were burn marks left like afterimages and old metal scratches. The hands of someone who handled equipment herself, in the field. Not just a broker.

And only the inside of her left thumb and forefinger was thickly callused. Not the right hand. For a right-handed person to wear down just two fingers of the left like that, the right hand had to hold the tool while the left kept holding on to something.

A hand that held parts in place.

Sern started to calculate how many years she had used that hand that way, then stopped. It was not a figure he needed now.

Han Jiwoo soon moved her eyes to Ater.
For the first time her gaze changed, very slightly. Eyes that looked not at his clothes but at his posture, his silence, the interval between his breaths.

"And what's this?"

Sion answered.

"Long story."

"If it's long, I'm not taking it."

"That's why I'm keeping it short,"
Sion said.
"The kind we need if we're not all going to die together."

Han Jiwoo did not nod right away, even after hearing that.
Instead she looked once at Ater, once more at Sern, and said,

"If you're saying that, it really is a strange combination."
Taking people on at an access point meant staking her own line, and the approach to the next dead marker, along with them.

Ater's expression barely changed at that, but inwardly he sensed that this woman was the same kind as Elia. Only, if Elia was a person of keeping and reading, this one was a person of movement and equipment. Someone who looked at things and people together and calculated first whether both could hold up.

Yona spoke from behind.

"No time."

Han Jiwoo rewound the band on her wrist once and nodded.

"I know. That's why I waited."
Mixed into that was the sense that she had already run the numbers once. If she did not take them on here, she might save one route, but she could lose, whole, the larger line that the fragment they carried pointed to.

She turned and pointed into the waiting bay.
Inside were two old cargo carts, panels half torn out, and, hidden there, a mid-range transport a little bigger than the shuttle. Its hull looked even more worn, but for exactly that reason the signs of a great deal of handwork were plain. Temporary reinforcement plates, external wiring bypasses, traces of replaced manual locks. Not a dying ship forced to keep running, but a ship held together by calculation so that it would not die.

Sion laughed the moment he saw it.

"Your hands have been all over this."

"Of course,"
Han Jiwoo said.
"If anyone else had touched it, I wouldn't have let you on."

Seorin looked at the ship and said low,

"Better than it looks."

"I hate that line most of all,"
Han Jiwoo shot back at once.
"It looks plenty good, too."

Seorin huffed a laugh, and watching that, Sion briefly thought of old times.
It had always been like this. Han Jiwoo pushed people away with words, but her hands moved first. The type who cursed and said she hated it and already had the necessary adjustments done.

Sern asked quietly.

"Is this the next transport?"

Only then did Han Jiwoo look at Sern head-on.

"Yeah. But it won't be a gentle ride."
She pointed her chin at the transport inside the waiting bay.
"I'm the one who reads dead markers and still-living equipment together. This leg can't be stitched without that."
She went on.
"Of the routes into the outer cluster, two are already dead, and someone's been feeling along the third from behind for a while now. So we have to swing farther out than planned."

Ater asked, low.

"Is that possible?"

"That's why I'm here. To make it possible."

Sion asked.

"How dangerous?"

Han Jiwoo answered, completely unbothered.

"Pretty dangerous, by the standards of the people standing next to you."
Then she added,
"By yours, a little more than usual."

"That doesn't help at all."

"I didn't come to make you feel better."

A short silence passed.

In it, somewhere far off in the access point, a single sound of metal being struck rang out.
It could have been the noise of age, but Sern and Yona raised their heads at the same time. Seorin held her breath at once, and Sion, by instinct, checked first what lay behind Han Jiwoo.

Han Jiwoo spoke low.

"Yeah. That sound's why we can't stay long."

Yona swore under her breath.

"Already feeling around?"

"More precisely, they're not sure yet,"
Han Jiwoo answered.
"But two more sounds and they will be."

Sern asked.

"Can we depart immediately after boarding?"

"I made sure we can,"
Han Jiwoo said.
"But once you're on, there's pretty much no getting off halfway."

Sion did not even laugh at that.
By now they all knew too well that sentences like that were not jokes.

Seorin moved first.

"Good. Then let's get on."

Ater felt that those few words, oddly, sounded firmer than any order given so far.
Seorin was always like this. Whenever the mood dragged or a gap opened for judgment to split, she cut off exactly the length that was needed and pushed it forward.

Just before boarding, Sion brushed past Han Jiwoo and said quietly,

"That's one more I owe you."

Han Jiwoo answered without even looking at him.

"Your debts never go down anyway."
Then she added, very quietly,
"So stay alive and pay up."

Sion did not answer that.
Still, that one short line caught somewhere in his chest, oddly heavier than the hull.

Han Jiwoo counted heads in front of the ramp.

Sion, Seorin, the black coat, the quiet one beside him. Four.

Then she looked at Yona. Yona had not come into the waiting bay; she stood where she was, near the entrance to the access bridge. She was not carrying any bags either.

Han Jiwoo turned her eyes back to the instrument panel and said nothing.

She did not recount the supplies either. She had planned for four from the start.
