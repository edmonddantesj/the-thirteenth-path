# Episode 02. Order of Pursuit

Reading Room Seven of the Empire Approval Bureau was always too quiet.

In the outer corridor a single footstep spread long, but once you came inside, sound died faster instead. The record panels, sealing devices and approval-line terminals ranged along the walls were all spotless, and that made them feel colder.

Even in a room like that, Sern Barek was the one who read the smallest change first. He was doing it now.

One of the temporary retrieval lines flowing across the reading desk shivered, very briefly. Too short to call an error, and it had happened twice, too often to pass off as chance.

Sern held the screen still and turned his eyes to the side.

Ater Valkar stood across from him. Ordered uniform, ordered posture, ordered silence. He was always like that. Rather than seizing something in a hurry, he first looked at how far it had spread.

"One of the outer retrieval lines has been cut abnormally."

Even at Sern's words, Ater did not answer at once. Instead he turned part of the screen with a fingertip. Two blanks in the retrieval path, a temporary seal number reassigned, a fine collision among access-permission traces. The grain of a record someone had hastily erased, hastily covered and hastily touched again.

"From which side."

"The neutral port city line."

Only then did Ater's hand stop.

Sern did not miss that moment. It was very short. But clearly, at that point, his lordship's thinking had stopped, once.

"This is not a routine disposal case," Sern added. "A reading line went in first, and a seal line covered it after. The sequence is wrong."

Ater quietly closed the screen.

The Empire Approval Bureau had always been a place that believed in sequence. What to open, what to close, what to erase, what to leave. Its whole order began with sequence. So a record whose sequence had gone wrong was worse than a simple error.

"Barek. Prepare a field access line."

Sern bowed his head.

"An official reading, or an unofficial check."

Before answering, Ater let his gaze rest once on the surface of the closed record panel. Beneath that smooth surface, the trace of something hastily erased spread faintly.

"For now, a check."

Sern filed that "for now" away separately in his mind. His lordship always used precise language, and precise language usually came out a little later than the actual judgment.

"Understood."

Even after Sern withdrew, Ater did not move for some time. The neutral port city. A retrieval line cut abnormally. Reading traces reopened after the seal line had covered them.

This was a matter that did not yet have a name. But the kind of thing that has to be cut before it gets a name usually left a much larger name behind later.

At last Ater turned.

"Barek."

"Yes, my lord."

"First confirm who reached the scene before anyone else."

Sern laid his hand on the reading desk. The dock staff roster of the Alliance Outer Route Administration came up. The Empire Approval Bureau could not open Alliance personnel records directly, but the summaries passed over for transit screening were still on file.

Most of the names had been erased. What remained were numbers and categories.

**Outer dock operational staff · Registered: 411 · Species composition: Myojok 373 / other 38**

Sern stopped on that line for a moment.

"My lord."

"Speak."

"In the 18-B unloading crews, eight of every nine are Myojok."

Ater did not turn his head toward the screen.

"Is that needed for the judgment?"

"Yes. That side does not move by paperwork."

Ater answered a beat later.

"That may be because no one gives them paperwork."

Sern kept that sentence. It was rare for his lordship to set his words down on that side.

"Is it the Alliance Outer Route Administration?"

Ater neither denied nor confirmed it. He only said, very low:

"It seems an unordered hand has gone in."

Sern asked nothing more. Instead he turned back toward the terminal. His lordship rarely called anything "unordered." And when that word came out, things usually ran in a direction more troublesome than a seal.

He called up the outer access list he kept bundled separately outside the approval line.
There was more than one way into the neutral port city. Official envoy lines, Empire logistics approval lines, civilian contract transfer lines, and the gray routes that never made it into any record. The question was which of them had moved first.

Sern ran down the list and narrowed his brows, barely.

There was no approval trace from the Alliance Outer Route Administration.

Nothing there could mean two things. Either they had not moved yet, or they had already slipped outside the official record.

And Sern put the odds on the second.

"My lord."

"Speak."

"The Alliance Outer Route Administration will not be on an official line."

Ater looked back at him.

"On what grounds."

"Because there is nothing."

Sern's answer was short, and so it was more precise.

"The paperwork shook that badly, and there is no trace of an official movement approval.
The approval wait is in the seven-hour range, and within that window the scene closes three times. Anyone who knows that does not wait."

Ater's gaze sank for a moment.
It was a look in which displeasure and interest overlapped, very thinly.

"That is not good."

"It is not."

"But if they touched the scene first, they will have left traces, if anything."

Sern nodded.
A scene an unordered hand had entered before an ordered one always left one thing behind.
A trace no one expected.
And sometimes, a door just about to close.

Ater straightened the hem of his coat.

"We go to the neutral port city."

"What class of escort vessel shall I arrange?"

"As fast as possible, so long as it does not show."

Sern asked nothing more.
Move under the Valkar name, but keep the Valkar shadow to a minimum.
That alone was enough to read that this case was not like the others.

The collection ship was even narrower inside than he had expected.

The moment the door shut, you could feel it had never been built to carry people. A low ceiling, reinforcement plates roughly patched on, air thick with old fuel smell and metal dust. On one side of the cargo hold, metal canisters stamped for disposal were lashed down, and in the far corner a few shadows, no telling who had claimed the spot first, crouched without a word.

Sion did not much trust that kind of silence.
The quieter a place was, the more often everyone in it was already carrying too much of their own trouble.

Hearing the door close, Seorin spoke low.

"A ship where the waste gets better treatment than the people."

"Then it's the right compartment for us."

"You always put yourself down like that."

"I'm matching the classification. Drop one more compartment from here and I've got nowhere left to go."

Seorin went quiet at that for a moment.

"You know that, and you're going anyway?"

"I'm going because I know."

Sion looked at the door once more.

The eyes that had looked down from the ramp never came down. They hadn't stopped them, and they hadn't followed.

When someone can stop you and doesn't, it's one of two things. Either you're allowed to see this far, or it's better to watch you from here on.

Sion swept his eyes across the cargo hold.
On the outside this was a waste collection ship, but the air inside was more complicated than that. A ship carrying nothing but real waste had no reason to be this quiet. The way everyone avoided everyone else's eyes was far too practiced. Laborers, brokers, stowaways, or people somewhere in between. Only one thing mattered.

This was a compartment where no one asked anyone's name.

When the collection ship began to vibrate slowly, Seorin leaned her shoulder against the wall and asked.

"So, do you actually have a plan?"

"Once we're in the neutral city, we start from where the retrieval line began."

"That's not a plan, that's stating the obvious."

"Where the record was cut, the transfer belt, the dock number where the goods changed hands. Then we look for people again from there."

Seorin frowned.

"People?"

Sion picked his answer for a moment, then shrugged.

"If they carved it out this much and burned it this fast, odds are it wasn't only goods they were hiding."
Sion glanced down the end of the passage for a moment and added.
"If it's the kind of job that ends once you hand it all over at once, people get left in the middle, and pieces too. Like someone broke it up that way on purpose."

"The records clerk?"

"Them, or a courier, or a go-between. There'd have been at least one person who saw something with their own eyes."

Seorin pushed herself off the wall and stood straight.

"So in the end we have to find a person."

"Jobs always get bigger because of people."

"No. They get bigger because of you."

This time Sion smiled, just slightly.

The collection ship lurched hard once, then settled onto the long route out of Administration space.
Once the vibration evened out, the silence in the cargo hold shifted a little too. Like people who had only now accepted that they were all headed the same way, everyone let go of their tension, very slowly. Not that any of it was fully gone. Someone kept a hood pulled low; someone else pretended to sleep with both hands hidden in their sleeves.

Sion sat back against a reinforcement plate and took the burned record fragment out of his retrieval pocket again.
Scorched edges, a cut face far too straight, a surface that still shed black powder when he scraped it with a fingernail.

Seorin watched him and asked.

"Looking at it again?"

"This kind of thing's better looked at while we're moving."

"Why."

"Once we arrive, it'll get annoying."

"It's plenty annoying already."

Sion tilted the fragment under the light.
The hold's ceiling lights were so weak he had expected the grain of the surface not to show, but there were marks that showed only in that faint light. Between the burned patterns, one very thin, unfinished line remained. Too broken to call a letter, and cut too deliberately to pass off as an accidental scratch.

He rubbed that spot with a fingertip.

"Look at this."

Seorin leaned in closer.

"What."

"Here, inside the panel face. Whoever cut this didn't cut just the text."

Seorin narrowed her eyes.

"A signature line?"

Sion didn't answer right away.
There was still too little to be sure.
But the position of the cut was strange. Not in the middle of the body, but at the very bottom of the document. Where a name usually went, or an approval line, or the order.

"Could be."

"That's a sour face for a 'could be.'"

Sion put the fragment back in his pocket.

"That spot usually gets touched last."

"And?"

"Someone carved it out first."

Seorin turned that over, then muttered.

"So something got cut out before the name."

At that, Sion looked at her for a moment. Seorin took the look without changing her expression.

The collection ship slowed as it passed the outer transfer belt once more.

Above the reinforcement plate there was a viewing port about the size of a palm. It had been cut to check the cargo stowage angle, so the glass was thick and cloudy. Sion brought his face close to it.

The transfer belt slid by along the side. Hundreds of docking rings ran on like a chain, with ships clamped between them. Every size there was. The Administration supply ship was some twenty times this collection ship, and the Empire logistics ship moored beside it was twice that again. At the outer end of the rings a long stretch was left with nothing clamped to it. No lights on there either.

Sion started counting the empty rings, then gave up. Past twenty-some, it was too dark to see the rest. Even his night eyes only went that far.
It wasn't a short layover, and it wasn't a full transfer. It was the kind of stop peculiar to gray routes, loading and unloading only the cargo and people too awkward to put on record.

In that gap the cargo hold door opened once and closed again.
No one new came in. Instead, the noise outside pushed in for a very short moment.

Someone arguing in the distance.
A heavy crate being shoved.
And, only for an instant but clearly, one word.

"Archivist."

The sound cut off the moment the door shut.

But Sion and Seorin raised their heads at the same time.

Their eyes met in midair.

Seorin spoke first.

"It wasn't just me who heard that."

"No."

Sion's voice had gone much lower than before.

"Someone just said archivist."

"Then this is a person after all."

Instead of answering, Sion got to his feet.
The collection ship was slowly starting to move again.

Seorin was up right behind him.

"Where are you going."

"The forward compartment."

"Now?"

"I need to see who said that word before we get into the neutral city."

Seorin swallowed a sigh.

"Don't be the one to charge in first this time."

"When have I ever."

"You had your charging-in face on just now."

Sion didn't answer and moved into the narrow passage at the front of the cargo hold.
Rough vibration rang up through the floor, and old metal tones trembled softly in the ceiling pipes.
Through the oil smell peculiar to collection ships, other smells began to mix in one by one, growing thicker the closer the port came.

Salt, fuel, old wiring, wet steel plate, and the air you only find in a place where too many people have come and gone, each hiding a different name.

The neutral port city was getting close.

The sound leaking through the gap in the forward door came again.
Clearer this time.
Two low, rough voices were wrangling over something.

"…One archivist, and the whole line got turned upside down."

"…Shut your mouth. A name comes out, we're all finished."

Sion stopped in front of the door.
Seorin stood right behind him.

Neither of them said anything more.

Seorin asked, very low.

"Open it?"

Sion laid his hand on the door panel.
The cold feel of metal met his palm.

"We have to."

"Why."

Listening to the voices beyond the door, Sion answered.

"Because now we have to find out whether what we're chasing is a thing or a person."

At that moment the whole collection ship lurched hard, once.
Far off, a long arrival signal sounded, low.

The neutral port city.
