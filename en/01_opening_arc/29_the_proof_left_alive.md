# Episode 29. The Proof Left Alive

The sound of the collapse followed them a long way.

Even as the four of them crossed the auxiliary footplate and the broken junctions one after another and made it back out toward the outer ring, the sound of metal striking and splitting kept ringing up from the deep dark below. It was not just debris falling. It was the sound of part of the classification structure, which had barely been holding until moments ago, folding in on itself link by link as they pulled out. If not for the two lines and the piece of the approval line they had just secured, this whole site would have lost its meaning along with whatever had collapsed down there.

Sion braced himself on the outer footplate first. Right behind him Sern took his place, checking again the load lines that had not given way, and Ater, one hand against the wall, caught his breath for just a moment. Kael kept watching behind them to the very last, and only after he had pulled the larger fragment firmly in against his chest did he ease himself out.

Han Jiwoo's voice came over the close-range channel.

"You alive?"

Sion answered.

"So far."

"Then get over here fast,"
Han Jiwoo said.
"I can't keep the hull half-latched much longer."

The vibration wrapping the structure's outer wall had grown rougher than a moment ago. It meant this whole boundary section was taking both the judgment unit's restart and the aftershock of the collapse. What they needed now was not to dwell on it but to get out; only after that could anything be interpreted.

Seorin spoke low over the channel.

"Talk later. Everyone back on the ship first."

This time no one argued.

The short way back was more dangerous than the pursuit had been. Going in, it had still been more a matter of opening the structure; going out now, they had to step again on a path that had already been shaken once. Sern called the footing first, and Sion, instead of the afterglow that had already begun to die, followed the lines of damaged metal and the marks of recent shifts in weight. Ater no longer read the pattern; he watched only the angles of collapse. Kael, behind the three of them, kept choosing the spot from which he could brace at once if someone missed a step or the structure twisted one more time.

Even that short move showed that their roles had already changed.

Moments ago they had been at a distance where they could cut each other loose; now it was all but settled who had to brace whom if someone went down first. Too early to call it trust, and too quick a fit to call it mere function. But inside this site, at least, there was no denying it.

When Sion got himself into the hull first, a very faint machine heat brushed past him with the air inside. It was far from full relief, but the plain fact that the floor would not drop out into space, not right now, was enough. Sern and Ater came in one after the other, and Kael swept the outside once more, to the very last, before pushing himself into the hull.

Han Jiwoo let it out low, as if she had been waiting.

"Now breathe for real."

Seorin, standing against the wall, looked at the four who had just come in, one by one. Her look was closer to checking than to relief. Whether anyone was badly hurt, whether anyone was in no state to be pushed again right now, whether the fragment was still in hand. And last of all, Kael.

"You didn't drop it,"
she said, low.

Kael did not put the fragment down.

"If I had, it'd all have been for nothing."

Seorin did not answer that. Instead she turned her eyes to Sion.

"Explain."

With that one word, the air in the hull fell back into order.

Sion leaned his back against the wall, caught his breath, and said,

"It wasn't a location."

Seorin didn't ask him to repeat it. Instead she looked at his face for a moment.

"…That's the same face you had when you first saw the fragment."

"Yeah."

"Which means it's gotten worse."

"Yeah."

Han Jiwoo cut in from the pilot's seat.

"I only got it half broken up, so run it once for me."

Sion said it all at once.

"Opening the door didn't take a name, it took an order. What we were looking for was where to go. That thing is about how you get through."

"Is that so different?"

"A name, you just have to have. An order, you have to know."

Han Jiwoo was quiet for a moment.

"…Oh. So that's why they erased it."

"Yeah. Erase something you only have to have, and it shows. Erase something you have to know, and it doesn't."

Kael heard that and still didn't cut in. Instead, one knee up near the hull floor, he kept looking only at the edge of the larger fragment he still held against him. As if the sentences they had just seen weren't fully sorted out on his side yet either.

Ater glanced at him and said,

"What we have secured is two lines and part of an approval line. We cannot yet say we have read the whole structure."

"Still way better than before,"
Han Jiwoo said.
"Before, we were hanging on to one name. Now at least we know what got cut out."

Seorin asked.

"And one more thing."

Sion knew right away what she meant.

"Multiple approval."

This time every eye went to Ater.

Ater nodded slowly.

"At least two. One is an Empire-style approval line, that much is certain. The other was not entirely the same as the Alliance's either."

Something shifted, very slightly, in Sern's eyes.

"Then who built the structure is also still undetermined."

"Yes,"
Ater answered.
"What is certain, though, is that the way the Empire and the Alliance currently explain things cannot account for all of this structure."

Which meant that outside the official languages that ran the world now, another grammar remained, not yet dead. Erasing names, rewriting verdicts, cutting out the order of access—in the end, all of it might have been done to keep that grammar from coming back to life.

A brief silence settled in the hull.

And this time it was Seorin who looked at Kael.

"How much did you know."

Kael didn't answer right away.

Until a little while ago, at the site, that silence had looked like wariness. Now it was a little different. It was closer to a silence that was calculating whether to say more, how much to say, and what he would be giving up on his side the moment he said it.

"I didn't know it wouldn't open just by finding the name,"
he said, low.
"But I knew this wasn't only about finding coordinates."

"How?"
Sion asked.

Kael touched the end of the pattern on the larger fragment with one finger.

"Every fragment I got hold of before this was cut at the same place."

Ater looked up.

"Fragments?"

Kael stopped for a moment under that look.

"This isn't the first one."

At those words the air in the hull changed, very slightly.

Seorin didn't so much as move an eyebrow, but in that brief stillness Sion could feel her already reordering her priorities. Han Jiwoo openly narrowed her brows, and Sern, if anything, went quieter. Ater's eyes were held less by the fragment in Kael's hands than by the words that had just come out: "not the first."

Sion asked, low.

"How many."

Kael steadied his breath.

"None of them read properly. Most were dead fragments, and they went out the moment I touched them."
He stopped for a moment, then added,
"The pattern stayed, though."

"What pattern."

"That it's the order that got cut, not the name,"
Kael said flatly.
"That's why I couldn't just take this one and walk out. If it breaks off at the same place again, holding it alone means nothing."

Han Jiwoo folded her arms and said,

"Then there's only one conclusion."

When no one picked it up right away, Han Jiwoo went on herself.

"Now you can't go alone, and we can't go without you either."

Kael looked out through the slit.

Han Jiwoo didn't miss that look.

"What. Something out there you came in on?"

Kael was silent a moment, then answered low.

"There was an access craft I'd docked."

"Was?"
Sion asked back.

"It came loose just now, when the reaction woke up,"
Kael said flatly.
"Whether it broke off on its own or got swept away by the debris, there's no getting it back now."

Han Jiwoo clicked her tongue softly.

"Right. Now we really are out of options."

Seorin cut across that brief silence.

"Joining us or just working with us, decide that later,"
she said, low.
"What matters right now is not dying before then."

Whose side Kael was on, how far he would go with them, what his real name was, where the fragments he had found before this were. All of it mattered. But what mattered more right now was keeping the two lines and the piece of approval line they had just secured alive, and carrying them through to the next reading.

Ater said, low,

"First, we must read it."

Sern followed at once.

"And we must move. Now that the reaction has come alive, this will not end quietly at this site alone."

Sion raised his head at that.

The judgment unit had run again, however briefly. That could not have stayed inside the site. If other eyes had been watching this place, they might already have read the resonance, and the restart of the classification structure had very likely shaken the pursuit net farther out.

Seorin drew the conclusion at once.

"Then we go in order."
Her eyes moved from one person to the next.
"Han Jiwoo, get the hull off first. Sern, sort out the reaction from that site. Ater, don't lose a word of what you read. Sion."

"I know,"
Sion cut in first.
"I'll hear him out about the rest of the fragments."

Last of all, Seorin looked at Kael.

"You're still a guest."

Kael didn't look offended at that either.

"Fine by me, as long as you keep me alive."

"That's not your call."

The corner of Kael's mouth moved. Less a smile than an expression close to admitting that what she said wasn't wrong.

The hull slowly began to pull away from the outer wall.

A heavy shudder of metal came up once, and past the slit went the part of the structure the four of them had been clinging to a moment ago, collapsing farther down.
