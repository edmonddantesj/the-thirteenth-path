# Episode 03. Neutral Port City

When Sion opened the front cabin door, it was already empty. A partition hatch leading upward hung half open, and above it two sets of footsteps were moving away. One of them kept an uneven rhythm. The two who had brought up the archivist. Lose them now and they would be hard to find again.

From a distance, the neutral port city always looked like a floating heap of scrap.

Hundreds of access bridges and transfer belts, makeshift mooring layers patched together from old hulls, signs and dock numbers that matched no standard, cargo lights that never slept even at night. The Empire, the Alliance and the border worlds had all tried to call this place by a name in their own language, but none of them had ever managed to make it theirs. So it was always neutral, and always dirty.

The moment the collection vessel latched onto the outer mooring layer, the people who had been huddled inside began to scatter, each in their own direction. No one said goodbye, and no one looked at anyone again. In this port, that sort of courtesy was what looked suspicious.

Sion and Seorin didn't wait to be last off either.

The air changed the moment they came down the steel ramp.
Oil and salt, exhaust heat that hadn't cooled, the smell of old wiring, even some spice burning somewhere, all mixed together. Too many things always mixed here at once. Languages, cargo, people, lies.

Not long after they stepped down, Sion's terminal chimed once.

**Request for origin cross-check on classification-hold item—rejected. On-site verification not required.**
**Justification required for accompanying auxiliary inspection personnel.**

The answer had taken seven hours and forty minutes. He had known the first line since the moment he sent the request. Not the second.

Rejected meant don't. Justification required meant it had already been written down somewhere. What gets written down doesn't come out right away. It comes out later, all at once.

Seorin asked from beside him.

"What'd it say."

"Rejected."

"Not that."

Sion switched off the screen.

"Your name."

"I knew it would. That's why I wrote it myself."

With that, Seorin walked on ahead.

The dockhands on the mooring layer weren't much different from the Administration's Bay 18-B crew. Ear fur cropped short, work-suit collars pulled up to the throat, heads that turned first toward any sound. As Sion passed, a few of them looked up and then looked down again. No one greeted anyone. Recognizing each other here did no one any good.

The port was big upward, too. Twelve mooring layers were stacked one on another, and from below, the topmost was just a black band. Above it hung the belly of a large freighter in the middle of docking, as wide as a whole city block. Its shadow covered half the plaza, and inside that shadow the cargo lights had to stay on even in daytime.

Seorin spoke low.

"Perfect neighborhood for hiding."

"That's why everyone comes here."

"You too?"

Sion laughed.

"Me, I got stuck with this."

"You're the one who believes that least."

Instead of answering, Sion lifted his gaze.
The two who had gotten off ahead were already half lost in the crowd. Not gone completely. One was tall, and the other dragged his left leg ever so slightly. He didn't know yet which of them had said the word archivist, but neither of them looked like a plain laborer.

"Left."

Sion said it quietly.

Seorin didn't ask. She peeled off to the right at once.
At times like this the two of them were like old partners. When one watched the path, the other split the field of view.

Below the outer mooring layer, hand-painted guide lines outnumbered the official markers. Thin passages linking warehouse to warehouse, unpermitted connecting stairs, back doors where a face got you through better than a mooring permit. Ideal for an archivist to hide in, and just as ideal for a pursuer to blend into.

The one with the limp glanced back once.
Sion ducked his head at once and fell in with a passing crew of cargo hands. Any slower, and at that distance he would have been spotted.

Seorin clicked her tongue from the other side.

"Those two have chased people before too."

"I know."

"Real wholesome port."

The two of them met up again at the end of the mooring layer.
The pair ahead were heading down toward the lower transfer bridge. At the end of that route were the temporary lodging blocks, cheap bars and warehouses that doubled as pawnshops. A district where people vanished before the records did.

Sion looked that way and said quietly.

"If he's an archivist, that's the right place to hide."

"Why."

"It's the last place someone hunting a person would go."

"But you're going."

"I'm Bloodhound, remember."

Seorin didn't laugh.

"That's two."

"You're counting?"

"Yeah."

They took the steel stairs down to the lower level.
Every creak of the iron plate underfoot brought the noise from below up more clearly. Dice rolling, someone fighting, someone haggling over cargo tags, someone cursing in another language. This city always ran a little overheated, and that was why it swallowed so much.

Halfway down the stairs, Sion stopped short.

Seorin whispered from right behind him.

"What."

Sion pointed with his chin toward the far end of the plaza below.
Two people in gray coats were brushing past the pair from before. From the markers in their hands and the way they moved, they weren't port brokers. Too clean, and too quiet. Less like people who smelled money than like people who had already been given orders.

"And who are they."

At Seorin's words, Sion answered low.

"We're not the only ones who came."

Before he had finished, a short scuffle broke out below.
The man with the limp shook off someone's hand and turned, and one of the gray coats went straight in under his coat. Something flashed, a blade, a shock baton, something in between.

The crowd screamed and split apart.

"Run."

This time Seorin moved first.
Sion went right after her, taking the stairs two at a time.

The plaza below turned into a mess in an instant.
Cargo crates spilled, bottles broke, curses burst out at anyone in the way. The limping man forced himself into a gap in the crowd, and the two gray coats who had tried to grab him latched on from different directions.

Sion tried to keep all three in sight at once as he ran.
Who was the archivist, who was the courier, who was the pursuer, he couldn't be sure yet. But one thing was clear.

They were already chasing each other over the same thing.

Seorin cut right and shouted.

"I've got the limping one!"

"Don't push it!"

"You're the one who'd better not!"

That came flying out even in the middle of all this, and Sion nearly laughed.
It was no time to laugh, but a line like that, at a moment like this, was what kept his head on straight.

At the end of the plaza, under a rusted guide tower, the limping man lost his footing and lurched hard.
In that instant, something fell from inside his coat to the floor.

A small record tag.

Sion changed course.

He hit the floor on his knees and slid. His work trousers scraped along the wet steel plate and everything below his knees went hot. His hand went out first. Someone's boot heel half-kicked the tag away, the tag bounced once, and his fingertips reached the spot where it landed. Got it.

It was still warm. The warmth of something just out from under someone's coat.

Curses came up behind him. Close. Two steps.

Sion didn't get up. Getting up would be too late. He rolled sideways over his shoulder, and where he rolled to was a narrow gap between two warehouses. His shoulder caught on the corner of the wall and scraped hard.

He pressed his back to the wall and held his breath.

A gray coat swept past the mouth of the alley. The footsteps went three more paces and stopped. The pause of someone about to turn back. Just then, from the plaza, came the sound of a stack of cargo crates collapsing, and the footsteps veered that way.

Sion didn't move until the sound had faded completely.

His knees burned and his shoulder stung. In his hand, the tag was cooling.

Seorin slid in from the connecting passage on the other side.

"Alive?"

"Barely."

"I lost the limper."

"Where."

"Pawnshop alley. But I didn't lose him, he went in." Seorin jerked her chin over her shoulder. "He stopped limping in front of it. Limped two hundred steps, and right there, he didn't."

"So it was an act."

"Two hundred steps of acting. Right up to where someone who knew him was watching."

"I got this, though."

Sion opened his hand to show her.
The record tag looked like a cheap one for outer-reaches freight, but on one side was a scorched handprint. Part of the tag's ID face was scratched too, and along the edge, very faintly, a single small symbol had been engraved.

Seorin caught her breath and peered at the tag.

"Can you read it?"

"The face is locked."

"Nothing goes easy today."

Turning the tag over, Sion found a tiny pressed groove in a corner of the back.
Not a factory lock. More like the mark of someone who had personally sealed it once more.

He pressed the groove with the tip of his fingernail.

The tag's surface wavered, and a faint projected text came up.
It wasn't a complete sentence.
Part of a destination code, part of a transfer window, and a single short line of marking.

**Delivery on hold until archivist survival confirmed**

They both stopped breathing at the same moment.

Seorin swore first, low.

"Insane."

Sion kept his eyes on the projection as he spoke.

"He's alive."

"For now."

"Yeah. For now."

The short blue letters warped in the air and vanished.
The tag went cold in his hand again, like a dead thing.

But it was already enough.

The archivist wasn't dead.
At least in this port, he was still being treated as alive. And someone was holding something back until that survival was confirmed.

Seorin was the first to raise her head.

"So what are we chasing now. The archivist, or the package."

Sion tightened his grip on the tag.
Only if someone is alive does the next thing get passed on. A person hangs on one object, and until that person is confirmed, the whole line stays stopped.

"Both."

"This just got bigger."

"This is where it always starts getting bigger."

Outside the alley, the commotion was still going on.
Someone was looking for the limping man, someone was looking for the thing that had just dropped, and someone was already getting ready to cut only their own line and disappear.

Sion watched the gray crowd beyond the alley and let out a breath, very slowly.

And at that very moment,
a few black coats passing along the upper connecting bridge across the plaza caught his eye.

It was not the walk of port workers, or brokers, or private security.
Too orderly. The movement of people who had arrived not a moment too late.

Seorin followed his gaze upward.

"What's that now."

Sion looked that way for a moment, then closed his hand on the tag again.

"A more troublesome scent."
