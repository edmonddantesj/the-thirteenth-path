# Episode 24. The Hand Ahead of the Shadow

The scrape of metal from the darkness ahead was short, but too distinct to write off as a trick of the ear.

All three of them stopped at once. The inside of the structure had been quiet from the start, but the silence of that moment was sharper. Debris vibrating in the distance, the faint holding hum of Han Jiwoo's ship rising from the hull side, and only the certainty that someone was still somewhere in the darkness just ahead.

Sion drew the air in, very shallowly. Oil, metal dust, the old, lingering tang of ozone. Between them, very faintly, a new scent was mixed in. Not blood, and not the trace of anything burning. Closer to the dry smell of fiber from the clothes of someone who had been on the move a long time, and the smell left on the fingertips of someone who handled metal surfaces often.

He said it with little more than the shape of his mouth.

"Someone's there."

Sern answered, lower still.

"Yes."

Ater said nothing and fixed his eyes on the narrow junction deep in the darkness. The sound just now seemed less a careless noise than one made while settling back into a place to stop.

Over the close-range channel, Seorin's voice came in low.

"Close in?"

Sion shook his head at once.

"Not yet."

Bolt first inside a structure this tight, and you could end up the one caught instead of the one chasing. At this distance, what mattered was not who had come first but who made the first mistake.

Sern spoke, almost in a whisper.

"There is an empty space in the upper-right frame. Wide enough for one person to hide once and turn again."

Sion gave only the smallest nod.

"I saw it too."

Ater looked at the underside of that frame and added,

"It is not a dead end. It is a place to hide, and at the same time a place to watch the next exit line."

At that, the corner of Sion's mouth lifted for a moment.

"Now we're looking the same way, a little."

And just then, the darkness ahead moved, very faintly.

No one showed themselves. Instead, between gaps in the structure, a shadow like the hem of a coat slipped past, cutting very briefly through the afterglow of a dead light. Too brief to make out a face, or even the whole build. But one thing was clear.

Whoever it was knew they were being followed, too.

Seorin muttered, very low.

"They noticed."

Sern spoke more coldly.

"It is possible they knew from the start."

Sion didn't react to that right away. Instead he kept watching, to the very end, the spot where the shadow had just disappeared. This wasn't someone who simply hid, but the type who pulled back while factoring in even the fact of being pursued. That kind, rather than running scared, tried to make the pursuer misread them one more time.

Ater spoke quietly.

"It may be a lure."

"I know."
Sion answered low as well.
"But now that we're in this far, we can't not step on it."

That held equally for the three on site and the two left on the ship. Touch this wrong and the remaining judgment response would die, and the hand holding the larger fragment would slip away with it. Too late, on the other hand, and the other would vanish down the next exit line. At this distance, hesitation was loss.

Han Jiwoo's voice brushed through the comm-line, very quietly.

"Response is rising on your side."

Seorin asked at once.

"Which way."

"Not the one inside that died earlier. Somewhere else."
Han Jiwoo said, low.
"Waking up one at a time along your heading."

At that, Sern stopped walking.

"One at a time?"

"Yeah."

"Is there an interval?"

Han Jiwoo was quiet for a moment.

"…There is."

Sern stood where he was and looked back. The traces they had passed on the way here lined up again in his head.

The first weight-shift mark. Then the scorch from the back of a hand. Then the scrap of fiber. Then the metal scrape.

"My lord."

"Speak."

"The traces are evenly spaced."

Ater turned his head.

"Evenly?"

"Someone being chased does not leave traces evenly. They bunch up where it is urgent and vanish where it is safe." Sern's voice was half a tone lower than usual. "Here, it is the same interval from start to finish."

A brief silence.

Sion looked back, very slowly.

Now that he thought about it, it was strange. Coming this far through this narrow junction, he hadn't been confused once. At every fork there had been exactly one next trace.

On a path like this, that couldn't happen.

"Somebody left these."

"They were not wiped. They were left," Sern answered.

Ater spoke as if carrying it on.

"We are being led."

Seorin asked.

"Then do we turn back now?"

"That's worse," Sion answered.

"Why."

"Anyone who led us this far is watching for when we turn back, too. The moment we turn, our ship's position gets pinned for them, exactly."

Han Jiwoo swore, short.

"I can't change my angle right now."

"I know. That's why we're not turning back."

Sion looked ahead.

If this were someone who'd panicked and dropped the fragment, they wouldn't move like this. This was the movement of someone who, even after making off with the larger fragment first, figured they could handle pursuers well enough.

"That guy read it before we did."

"Is that a good thing?" Seorin asked.

"Not at all."

"So what are you going to do?"

Sion didn't answer for a moment.

"We follow, sure. But we don't step where that guy put things down."

"What are you talking about?"

"If we follow the traces, we go where that guy decided." Sion moved his eyes off the scorch on the wall, to the side. "But that guy's human too. Even if they picked the spots to leave things, there are spots they dodged so they wouldn't leave anything."

Sern understood at once.

"You mean we count the spots that were avoided?"

"Yeah. What's left was left to be seen. What's dodged is real."

Ater said, very low,

"That is far slower."

"Yeah."

"The other one gets farther in the meantime."

"Yeah."

"And you will do it anyway?"

Only then did Sion look at Ater.

"You said it yourself back there. Throwing something away knowing its price is different from throwing it away without knowing."

Ater was silent for a moment.

"…I did."

"This time I'll throw it away knowing. I'm throwing away speed."

Sion stepped one pace farther into the narrow junction inside the structure. The metal plates underfoot were unstable, but if he kept to the load lines Sern had marked earlier, they would hold. Sern kept watching the order of those footsteps from behind, and Ater read the wall marks and damage patterns, watching only for the next exit line the other might choose.

The three of them moved far less clumsily now than at first. It couldn't be called a team yet. But at least here, inside this narrow structure, what each of them needed to be watching had almost come into line.

At the end of the shadow ahead, metal grazed once more.

This time a very brief afterglow followed the sound.

As if some thin plate had caught the light once in the dark and vanished.

The look in Sion's eyes changed in an instant.

"You saw that."

Ater was looking at the same spot.

"Yes."

Sern said, very low,

"It is a plate."

Seorin's brows drew together.

"The larger fragment?"

"Probably,"
Sion answered.
"Not sure it's a perfect match, but nobody carries plain gear like that."

That fragment was in someone's hand, right now.

Han Jiwoo spoke again, almost in a whisper.

"Can't hold long. The structure's response is getting bigger."

Looking ahead, Sion steadied his breathing, very slowly.

Catch them here, now, and maybe at least the larger fragment would be in hand at once. But push too hard and the whole structure could shake. Go too slowly, on the other hand, and the other would slip down a deeper exit line.

At the next fork, Sion didn't go toward the trace.

He pressed against the opposite wall and swept his eyes low along it. There was nothing there. That was what was strange. At this width, a shoulder or an elbow would brush the wall at least once going past.

"Nothing brushed here."

"And so?" Ater asked.

"To get through this width without brushing, you have to turn your body. To turn, you have to shift whatever you're carrying to one side."

Sion lowered his hand toward the floor.

There was one very shallow mark in the dust. Not a footprint. It was narrow, and its end was angular.

"The corner of a plate," Sern said first.

"Yeah. Touched it to the floor once while turning."

"How large is it?"

Sion set his fingers at both ends of the mark. A hand's span and a little more.

"…The whole thing's a lot bigger than this, then."

Ater looked down at the mark. The shard left broken in the slot had been the size of a fingernail. If the plate it had been attached to was this large, what the person up ahead was holding was not a fragment but a whole plate.

"This was not left to be seen."

"Yeah." Sion straightened. "First thing that guy didn't mean to show us."

Ater spoke low.

"Sion."

"What."

"At this distance, they will stop once at the next corner."

Sion asked back.

"You sure?"

"Yes."
Ater's voice was cold and clear.
"Someone who reads that structure does not run straight on. They will stop to catch their breath once, to look at the next judgment unit."

Sern took that up at once.

"And that is when they are closest."

Sion looked at each of the two in turn. One read structure, the other read sequence. Both spoke a language different from his, and yet right now, strangely, it was all gathering into the same answer.

He said, very low,

"We take them at the next corner."

From beyond the corner, one very short, shallow breath leaked out. Not a mere footstep or metal scrape, but a living breath, the kind only someone who had stopped for a moment while carrying something heavy could make.

All three gazes gathered at once on the end of that darkness, and the silence across the channel set hard along with them.
