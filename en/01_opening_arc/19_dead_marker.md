# Episode 19. Dead Marker

For a while, Han Jiwoo's ship slid on through darkness without a single marking.

With hardly a window in the hull, nothing outside could be seen directly, but there were small changes instead.
The rhythm of the engine as it bore down, the spacing of the outside friction that brushed the hull, the short vibration that came up through the floor at every route connection.

Sion had his back to the wall. It was not a sitting position, nor a leaning one. He had one shoulder blade against the wall and kept the rest of him off it.

He had learned it on the docks. Cargo crates were faster to count with your back than with your eyes. The floor told you first which one the crane was lowering.

So when Han Jiwoo twisted the control stick a fraction and slowed, Sion, who had not been watching the instruments, raised his eyes almost at the same moment.

"There it is."

Han Jiwoo gave him a sidelong smile.

"Your sense isn't dead yet, at least."

"You knew from the instruments."

"Knowing from the instruments is my job. Knowing with your back is your lot in life."

Ater, hearing that, asked.

"What is it that you knew just now?"

"The ship switched roads," Han Jiwoo answered for him. "Onto one the official routes don't use."

"Does that show on the instruments?"

"It does. About three seconds later."

Ater looked toward Sion. Sion already had his shoulder back against the wall.

"Is it any use, knowing three seconds earlier?"

"Usually not," Han Jiwoo said, hands on the control stick. "But some people die because they knew three seconds late."

Seorin pushed off the wall and asked.

"What changed?"

Sion did not explain right away.
Instead he set his palm against the inner wall of the hull. Through the low, steady vibration, one very thin jolt was mixing in at regular intervals. Not the smooth flow of an official route, but the feel of forcing a ride over connection traces long since pushed off the standard.

"The road isn't smooth,"
he said.
"It's not a continuous line. It's the kind that forces the cut places back together."

Han Jiwoo brought up one more layer of an old coordinate grid over the instrument panel.
Not the official route display, but auxiliary markings she had drawn in herself. Dead access points, traces of closed transfers, signal shadow zones, power resonance. None of it was on a standard map, but it was the kind of map living people passed around among themselves.

Sern looked at it and said,

"Two of the three markings are dead."

"I wrote them down as dead, not as gone,"
Han Jiwoo answered.
"If they were completely gone, I wouldn't use them either."

Sion laughed low.

"The dictionary out here really is strange."

"A living dictionary always is."

The ship slowed once more.

This time everyone felt it.
Not to a stop, but to the speed of something that would need checking soon.

Han Jiwoo said,

"There's a remnant of the first marker up ahead."

Seorin narrowed her brows.

"Can you see it?"

"Not yet,"
Han Jiwoo said.
"But there's a point where things go strange before the moment you should be able to see it."

Sion nodded.
It was less an explanation than a shared sense. Between people who knew, that much was enough.

Hearing that, Ater grew oddly uncomfortable.
He still could not fully follow that language. But it was not that he understood none of it, either. And that in-between state was exactly what scraped at him. He had already followed too far to draw a line and call it not his world.

Sern asked quietly.

"If it is a marker remnant, does that mean a structure remains?"

Han Jiwoo thought for a moment, then said,

"Could be a structure, could be a signal, could be half of each."
"Out here, not much is whole to begin with."

Sion huffed a laugh.

At that moment, a warning light on the left of the cockpit blinked, very briefly.

Han Jiwoo's eyes changed at once.

"Everybody quiet."

The air in the cabin sank all at once.
Sion wiped off his smile, and Seorin had already moved her hand toward the storage locker. Sern was working out the warning light's cycle and the ship's deceleration together, and Ater, without a word, was watching the faint afterimages over the instrument panel.

Han Jiwoo tapped one side of the panel twice, and the hull's external listening device opened, very thinly.
For an instant noise flooded in, and under it something was mixed in, a regular interference tone. Not natural noise, but the sound of an artificial marker left long ago, not yet dead and still trembling.

Sion said, very low,

"This is it."

Seorin asked.

"What is?"

"The marker remnant."
Sion murmured it with the face of someone listening to the noise.
"I thought it was cut off completely, but one end is still trembling."

Sern narrowed his eyes as if he had understood at once.

"A guide signal."
"Not an official guide line. Most likely the remnant of an approach-sequencing device."

Han Jiwoo snapped her fingers.

"Right,"
she said.
"Not an official guide line. The half that was left after the cut."
"But if this is still here, either someone couldn't erase it completely, or…"
She did not finish the sentence.

Ater said low, as if picking it up,

"Someone was still using it."

For the first time, Han Jiwoo looked properly at Ater.
It was closer to a look that tested whether he understood what was being said.

Han Jiwoo nodded once.
"Now we're talking about the same thing."

Sion did not react to that.

He leaned toward the listening device and closed his eyes.

The noise kept sounding the same. At first, it did. After about forty seconds, one thing came loose from it. Just a little shorter than the rest, and just a little more regular.

He caught hold of it and began to count.

One. Two. Three. Four. Rest.

One. Two. Three. Four. Rest.

One. Two. Three. — Rest.

Sion opened his eyes.

"The third one's short."

Sern reacted at once.

"Is it short at regular intervals?"

"Yeah. Twice it's four, once it's three."

"Then it is not a malfunction."

"I know."

Han Jiwoo asked, without taking her hands off the control stick.

"If it's not a malfunction, then what?"

Sion closed his eyes again. He counted once more. The same.

"One's been taken out."

The cabin went quiet.

"It used to be four. Only the third one lost a beat. Nothing was put in where it was taken out, so now it just sounds like three."

Ater said, very low,

"Remove one, and the sequence changes."

"Yeah."

"And yet it still makes its sound."

"Yeah. That's the worst part."

Sion took his back off the wall.

"Nobody follows a broken marker. But this one sounds fine. It sounds fine, and one's missing."

Seorin glanced at him.

"Can you read it?"

"Not completely.
But this one, the marker isn't pointing at the destination. It's the kind that makes you turn off just before you reach it."

Sern asked.

"Is it a trap?"

"Don't know yet,"
Sion said.
"But one thing's for sure: whoever left this road didn't leave it straight."

Hearing that, Ater slowly lowered his eyes.

"The Approval Bureau builds them that way too."

The three of them looked at him.

"A seal does not lock a door. It changes the sequence that brings you to the door. Someone who does not know the sequence comes all the way to the door and still goes somewhere else. Because that is the cheapest way to stop them."

"Cheap?" Seorin said.

"A door can be broken. A sequence cannot."

Han Jiwoo asked, hands on the control stick.

"So is that what's in front of us now?"

"I do not know," Ater answered honestly. "But the way it was made resembles hands from my side."

Han Jiwoo adjusted the control stick again.

"Then we don't go in head-on."
"We follow the remnant, but we come in at an angle for the last approach."

Seorin nodded.

"That I like."

"If you like it, pay more."

"I'll think about it if we get there alive."

Sion almost laughed at that, but held it in.
Right now, more than laughing, the resonance of that dead marker trembling outside the hull was on his mind. It was the not-yet-dead reflected sound of a road someone had cut on purpose.

And that reflected sound was calling them somewhere now.

The ship turned slowly.
The hull, which had been heading straight on, twisted very slightly, shifting to an angle that read the signal remnant from the side as it slid past. A small change, but one everyone could feel even inside the windowless cabin. The choice mattered not because it looked good. Once a head-on approach was recorded, whoever was feeling along behind them could latch onto the same remnant.

In that moment Ater felt his breath go oddly shallow.
Soon would come the moment when he touched the real trace of a road someone had cut, not with his feet but with his own choice.

Han Jiwoo said one last thing.

"Everybody get ready."
"From here on, for real, we go in reading only what's left."

The listening device stayed open. The noise lay low beneath everything, and inside it the marker was still counting.

Four. Four. Three.

Four. Four. Three.

No one knew who had taken one away. Only that nothing had been put in its place, and so the ones that remained kept crying out in threes.

Listening to that sound, Sion realized, late, that the fragment he had held in his hand today had been crying like this too.

It sounds fine, but one is missing.
